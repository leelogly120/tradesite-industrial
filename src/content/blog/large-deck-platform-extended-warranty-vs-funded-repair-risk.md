Buy an actual written warranty extension when it transfers repair exposure your business cannot comfortably retain, covers the intended platform and destination, and has a workable claim route. Keep a funded repair reserve when major costs remain excluded or your business can carry the defined exposure more economically. In both cases, fund maintenance, inspection and operational disruption separately. Before ordering, place the priced extension beside the same ownership-period repair scenarios and retained costs. An attractive premium is irrelevant if the machine or intended use is ineligible.

<!-- audit-section: buyer-intent -->
## Buy a defined transfer of cost

A warranty extension is a contract purchase alongside the platform. Its value comes from the costs and remedies it actually accepts after the applicable initial cover, subject to its conditions. Buying it does not select the people who maintain the equipment or provide a substitute platform during a stoppage.

An owner still needs competent service resources under either option. Keep the planned maintenance organisation fixed while comparing paid cover with retained financial exposure. Otherwise the supposed saving comes from deleting necessary work rather than choosing a different way to fund defined repairs.

The lowered deck below places that continuing work beside the actual asset. Budget the service location, access and competent provider independently of the extension premium. A policy paying an eligible repair expense and a contractor accepting the physical repair task are different commitments.

## Read eligibility before comparing the premium

[JLG's warranty page](https://www.jlg.com/en/support-and-services/service/warranty) provides an actual OEM example: it describes options extending parts and labour cover, transferable coverage and claim access requiring an account, the equipment serial number and warranty documentation. Those are JLG's offerings. For the machine being purchased, use its own issuer's complete written policy and priced offer. Identify the additional remedy purchased beyond the base cover before assigning value to an extension.

Identify the platform, options, owner, destination and intended use on the offer. Establish when the extension starts and ends, whether operating hours limit it, and which registration or purchase deadlines apply. A period written from first delivery is different from one beginning when the original warranty ends. Put the actual calendar and operating basis on one timeline.

Then read the remedy. Separate eligible parts, labour, diagnosis, travel, transport and any other cost category. Mark each as included, capped, subject to a deductible or retained by the owner, using the actual clauses. Keep wear, maintenance, inspection and downtime in their stated allocation rather than assuming that a defects heading buys all ownership costs.

## Make the claim route part of the purchase

Name who accepts the claim, which provider can perform covered work and what authorisation must precede it. Put the required records with the responsible fleet coordinator: machine identity, service history, fault report and the documents specified by the actual policy. The owner must be able to follow that process in the intended destination.

Read the cost timing as well as the final allocation. A reimbursement arrangement can leave the owner paying a provider before the claim settles. Include that cash requirement in the ownership plan. A declined claim and a covered claim requiring owner cash have different consequences, even when both begin with the same fault.

Reject an extension as a purchase benefit when the offered machine is ineligible or the claimed service route has no accepted provider for the destination. Obtain another workable offer or price the retained exposure. Paying for an unusable arrangement does not reduce the equipment risk.

## Fill the cost comparison from records and actual offers

Use one platform configuration and planning period. Keep a scenario's repair amount separate from its probability. Historical records from a different machine or duty are not a reliable failure forecast for the new purchase. Without a defensible probability basis, show the scenario amounts and affordability directly instead of manufacturing an expected-value figure.

| Cost or eligibility datum | Owner entry and actual policy reference | Responsible owner | Decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Covered asset and use | Machine/options ___; destination ___; policy eligibility clause ___ | Fleet buyer and issuer | Rejects cover for the wrong asset or use |
| Matching period | Ownership dates ___; cover start/end ___; hours basis ___ | Fleet planner | Exposes uncovered periods in the comparison |
| Eligible repair scenario | Defined defect/remedy ___; provider cost basis ___; covered clause ___ | Competent service provider and issuer | Identifies the expense actually transferred |
| Owner share | Deductible ___; per-claim/aggregate limit basis ___; transport/travel/diagnosis share ___ | Finance and contract owner | Establishes cash retained beside the premium |
| Claim access | Claim recipient ___; authorised provider ___; required approval/records ___ | Fleet coordinator | Tests whether the destination route works |
| Unchanged owner duties | Maintenance ___; inspection ___; wear ___; disruption plan ___ | Maintenance and operations leads | Keeps necessary expenditure in both options |
| Paid-cover total | Premium ___; retained scenario costs ___; cash timing ___ | Finance lead | Prices the complete transfer route |
| Retained-risk funding | Accessible amount/cost basis ___; spending authority ___; replenishment trigger ___ | Finance director | Tests affordable retention over the same period |

A reserve needs accessible funds, an authorising person and a rule for replenishment after use. Merely omitting the premium from the purchase order does not create a repair budget. Test whether that accessible reserve pays the defined scenarios after allowing for other commitments drawing on the same cash.

Choose the extension when its actual transfer of material repair expense solves the owner's affordability problem at an accepted complete cost. Retain the exposure when the owner can fund the defined scenarios and the complete comparison supports retention, especially when paid cover leaves the main costs with the owner. If neither route is affordable, revise the ownership plan before buying. Keep the reasons beside the signed decision.

## Keep resale and downtime separate from repair cover

The yard platform below is a reminder that ownership can continue across locations and eventual buyers. If resale falls inside the planning period, read whether this offered extension transfers, what records are required and which party must act. Exclude an assumed residual benefit when transfer is unavailable or its conditions cannot be met.

![A green deck is raised above a crawler and deployed feet in an open yard beside a factory wall.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261029-deck-yard.webp)
*Use the offered policy's transfer terms when resale forms part of the ownership plan.*

Plan the work programme's response to a platform stoppage separately. Repair-cost cover is not an attendance deadline or a replacement-machine commitment unless the actual contract includes that additional service. Keep inspection, competent repair and return-to-service responsibilities with their appointed parties.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical coverage mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): the owner counts every stoppage cost as covered.** A contractor buys an extension for eligible defective parts and labour, then includes transport and lost work in the same assumed recovery. Its actual offer leaves those costs with the owner. Rebuild the comparison using the written allocation and fund the remaining disruption and transport exposure separately.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the fleet sells a benefit it cannot transfer.** A buyer expects to resell the platform during the coverage period but never checks the registration and transfer conditions. The offered policy requires issuer acceptance of the new owner, but the onward offer advertises cover before that step is completed. Read the actual policy before pricing resale; complete the required steps or exclude that benefit.

Use [ARC-F25](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/) as the large-deck ceiling/wall access candidate for the defined task. Request the equipment proposal and actual warranty terms as separate documents. Treat an optional extension as available only through a named issuer's accepted written offer for that machine.

Attach eight records to the RFQ:

1. Proposed platform identity, options, destination, ownership and intended use.
2. Actual extension policy, named issuer and itemised priced offer.
3. Covered parts, labour, remedies, per-claim/aggregate limits and exclusions by clause.
4. Coverage dates, hours basis, deductibles and registration requirements.
5. Destination claim route, authorised provider and preauthorisation conditions.
6. Owner allocation for transport, inspections, maintenance, wear and downtime.
7. Intended resale date and accepted transfer conditions and records.
8. Repair scenarios, cost basis, reserve funding and named decision authority.
