Choose external weather-sheet production when your customers buy the outer panels of an approved roof assembly and your proposed line produces that defined product. Choose perforated acoustic liner production only when customers have specified the inner liner and its complete acoustic assembly, and supported liner orders pay for the additional material-processing, inspection and handling scope. When liner demand does not fund that separate business, buy the specified liner and keep the weather-sheet investment focused on its own orders.

A perforated inner liner is not a substitute for the outer weather sheet. Decide which layer you sell before asking whether a forming line needs perforating equipment. Otherwise, a machine comparison starts with two different roof products presented as the same item.

## The two products occupy different places in the roof

A built-up roof combines components with different responsibilities. The outer sheet belongs to the weathering arrangement; an inner liner is a separate component facing the building interior. An acoustic specification concerns the selected assembly rather than a hole pattern in isolation.

The manufacturer-authored [Euroclad Elite roof-system document](https://products.tatasteelnederland.com/sites/producttsn/files/Euroclad-EPD-ELITE-3.pdf) describes standard and perforated liner options within its roof construction. This dated document establishes the product distinction here, not a current approval or an acoustic rating for another roof. Obtain the actual project assembly from its designer when buying production equipment.

Ask the sales team to separate current quotations into outer-sheet orders and acoustic-liner orders. For liner enquiries, keep the complete assembly reference beside the liner drawing. If the requirement is simply recorded as “acoustic roofing,” the sold component has not yet been defined. Replace that phrase with the named sheet, its location and the party accepting it.

## Choose a product business before a process route

An inline perforator versus a separate perforation operation is a later factory decision. First establish whether the investor needs to manufacture the specified liner at all. That decision uses committed products, supported order coverage, the bought-liner alternative and the cost of delivering an accepted liner package.

An investor who already sells external sheets does not automatically have customers for acoustic liners. Require the liner product schedule to stand on its own. If all liner volume depends on one uncommitted project, retain purchased supply until the business case has an acceptable order and funding basis.

## Price the liner operations that a forming-only offer omits

For a perforated liner proposal, assign responsibility for the perforated strip or sheet entering the forming process. One route buys material with the required pattern; another adds a separately specified perforating operation. Either route needs the exact perforation geometry and retained solid areas to match the liner drawing.

Then follow the material through the proposed operations. Identify where the buyer inspects the hole pattern, where process waste leaves, how surfaces are protected and how completed liners are identified. The machine supplier states the offered material and product scope. The buyer's quality lead defines the inspection against the controlled drawing and the project acceptance requirements.

Do not infer acoustic performance from visible openings or from a different manufacturer's roof. Attach the assembly reference and specify the evidence required for that assembly. If the investor changes the liner material, pattern or backing, treat that as a product-design change for the responsible designer, rather than an operator adjustment made to save production time.

## Compare accepted deliveries, not nominal sheet area

Build two independent contribution calculations. For each weather-sheet order, include the specified input, forming, cut ends, inspection, protection and delivery. For each liner order, include all purchased or owned perforation work, finish inspection, process waste, product identification and packaging, plus any backing that the investor explicitly sells.

Use accepted deliveries as the denominator. A nominal quantity that fails the specified pattern or finish is not saleable output. Compare ownership with a delivered bought-liner quotation covering the same drawing and acceptance boundary. Own liner production when that complete comparison supports the investment and its demand basis. Otherwise, purchase the specified liner and quote it separately alongside the roof sheets.

## Two mistakes that change the purchase

**Mistake: using the weather-sheet order book as the liner forecast.** A hypothetical roof-sheet manufacturer buys perforating equipment because both products appear in industrial roofs. Its customers continue buying external sheets and specify liners from an existing assembly source. Separate those order books before investment; count only the liner business the proposed supply route can actually serve.

**Mistake: accepting a pattern sample instead of the liner product.** A hypothetical buyer approves a flat perforated sample, but the sold liner also requires a defined section, solid fixing areas and finish after processing. Add the complete liner drawing and inspection sheet to the offer so the purchased process ends at an accepted formed component.

## Eight RFQ attachments for an investable comparison

1. A roof section naming the outer sheet and the proposed inner liner.
2. The designer-issued acoustic assembly reference and required evidence.
3. The controlled liner or weather-sheet drawing, including interfaces.
4. The material, finish and input-condition schedule.
5. The actual order lengths, quantities and repeat-order basis.
6. The proposed purchased or owned perforation boundary and waste arrangement.
7. An inspection and packaging sheet for the accepted finished product.
8. A dimensioned factory plan and a comparable bought-product quotation.
