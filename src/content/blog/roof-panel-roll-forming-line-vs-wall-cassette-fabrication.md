Choose a roof-panel roll-forming line when repeat orders require long sheets with a controlled repeating section. Choose a dedicated cassette-fabrication route when the order book requires individually dimensioned facade units with folded edges, corners and fixing details. If cassette orders are occasional, purchase finished cassettes from a specialist and keep the roof-panel investment focused on the products it actually manufactures.

Both businesses use sheet metal, but they sell different products and need different production plans. A roof-panel former is not a complete cassette workshop. Make the investment against accepted product drawings, a representative order mix and a process proposal that covers every required operation.

## Identify the finished item before selecting equipment

A roof-panel order normally defines a repeated longitudinal section, material and a cutting schedule. A cassette order also identifies the individual unit's edges, corners, fixings, joint arrangement and position in a facade layout. Record those distinctions from the actual project drawings instead of assuming every item called a “wall panel” follows one production route.

[Ruukki's Liberta range](https://www.ruukki.com/building-envelopes/products/facade-claddings/liberta-rainscreen-panels) describes metal cassettes folded on all sides and supplied with defined fixing arrangements. This is a useful product-category distinction, not a specification for an ARCLIFT machine or a recommendation to copy a proprietary facade system.

*Separate the repeated section from the finished unit's complete edge and fixing requirements.*

The sample photograph shows an edge comparison, not a finished cassette. Use actual, identified product samples and drawings to prepare the manufacturing brief. A familiar metal colour or a similar edge is insufficient to establish the sequence of operations needed for the customer's final item.

## Cost a complete production route for one representative order

For roof sheets, obtain a proposal covering the nominated material input, forming, cutting, receiving and inspection scope. For cassettes, list the operations required by the drawings and ask a competent fabrication-equipment provider to define the process and resources. Keep cutting, edge forming, corner details, surface protection and finished-unit inspection explicit wherever the actual product requires them.

Do not turn that operation list into a claim that one selected machine performs every step. The supplier's process proposal must identify the equipment and outside work for each operation. Count staff, movement between operations and inspection time in the same order cost. A quotation for the most visible machine is not a quotation for the manufacturing business.

Use real product quantities and revisions. A roof-sheet business with repeated sections has a different order pattern from a facade workshop processing many individually marked units. A single large project can fill a cassette order list without creating a repeat market for an unspecified standard unit. Separate the awarded project from the demand used to support the continuing business.

The decision follows the supported order route. Buy the roof line when its defined products serve the repeat orders at an acceptable complete cost. Invest in cassette fabrication when the defined project work and continuing market support the full workshop. Buy specialist output when the equipment would otherwise stand idle or the necessary process expertise is absent.

## Keep product manufacture separate from installation access

Manufacturing facade units and providing people access to install them are separate purchase scopes. A platform enquiry needs the workface, panel or unit dimensions, load distribution, floor route, obstructions and installation sequence. The finished product still needs its own handling and attachment arrangements.

![A broad low platform stands on a workshop floor near other equipment.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261109-low-deck-workshop.webp)
*Installation access belongs in its own site package after the facade product and work sequence are defined.*

The platform photograph illustrates an access-equipment category. It does not depict cassette manufacture or establish a load rating. For an installation business, price the platform, separate material handling and the competent installation resources together. Do not add access equipment to a manufacturing-only investment unless awarded work requires that service.

For long profiled wall sheets rather than cassettes, review the actual section drawing with the line supplier. The name “wall” does not force a cassette process, and the name “panel line” does not promise cassette capability. Treat each finished-product drawing as the starting point for its own proposal.

## Two failures caused by shortcuts in the equipment budget

**Hypothetical failure case: a roof former is purchased against a facade render.** The buyer assumes sheet-metal panels share one manufacturing method. The ordered facade includes individually folded units and corner details outside the line proposal. Obtain the marked product drawings and a complete operation-to-equipment matrix before buying the main machine.

**Hypothetical failure case: one large facade order becomes assumed repeat demand.** The workshop budget includes the first project's quantity but omits the different unit details, handling and follow-on market. Compare a specialist finished-product quotation with the full owned-workshop route for that order, then assess continuing demand separately.
