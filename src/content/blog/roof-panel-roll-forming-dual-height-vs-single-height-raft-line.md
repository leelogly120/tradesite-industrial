Choose a supported dual-height line when keeping two fixed profile trains installed avoids a raft-exchange resource conflict and the complete entry, cutting and receiving arrangement accepts both pass levels. Choose a single-height rafted line when a fixed pass level is necessary and the funded handling, preparation and storage arrangement makes tooling exchange practical. When both fit, award the lower complete cost over the same two-profile order sequence. Put both material paths on a supplier-issued elevation before accepting either machine price.

The business has already chosen one shared line for these two products. That allocation stays fixed here. The new purchase concerns two physically different ways to keep their tooling available: both trains resident on the host, or an identified train removed while another occupies the production position.

## Follow two panels through the proposed machine

Start with released section drawings for Product A and Product B. Add their actual material specifications, cut requirements and accepted finished lengths. Have each bidder mark the complete route from its proposed input to the receiver. Number the relevant guides, tooling trains, cutters and receiving objects so every price can be related to one physical arrangement.

[Bradbury's roll-former range](https://www.bradburygroup.com/bradbury-group-roll-formers) describes upper and lower resident tooling paths selected through the entry arrangement, and separately describes removable subplates carrying stands, shafts and tooling. These establish different machine architectures. Compare the offered roof-panel configuration against its own product drawings and supplied objects.

The roller row below shows working contours along a full forming bed. For a resident-level proposal, the drawing must locate both required trains. For a rafted proposal, the configuration schedule must connect each removed assembly to the complete product it produces.

## Two resident levels change more than the stand count

A dual-height proposal has two tooling paths at different levels. Trace the sheet into each path and follow its finished product through cutting and receiving. Record the supplied configuration for switching between the paths, including all product-specific items required outside the forming stands.

[ASC's panel-line description](https://www.ascmt.com/panelrollforminglines) explains different output levels on its dual-height equipment and identifies product-specific entry and cutting arrangements. Its subplated range uses removable groups of stands and tooling with separately resourced handling. The purchasing lesson is to obtain the whole route, rather than adding another roller train to an unchanged single-level quotation.

A shop with a fixed receiving interface should settle that geometry first. If the two-level proposal cannot serve that interface through an accepted supplied arrangement, retain the eligible single-height route. If an adjustable or separately arranged receiver is offered, include its actual equipment, space and change work in the dual-height price. For the single-height route, obtain the actual pass and outlet datum for each raft configuration too. Select that route for a fixed receiver only when the complete supplied configurations meet the receiver requirement.

Keeping both tooling trains installed also means keeping their service access within the layout. Request the maker's access and removal envelopes for the complete machine, including the inactive tooling path. A compact production rectangle is useful only when the owner can perform the specified service without occupying a required neighboring route.

## Put the physical choice on one purchase sheet

| Decision record | Fillable input and datum | Owner | Purchase consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed products | A/B section revisions ___; material and finish ___ | Product lead | Qualifies the same two products |
| Actual sequence | Order IDs ___; switching pairs ___; release windows ___ | Planner | Establishes switching frequency and concurrency |
| Pass geometry | Upper/lower or single level ___ from machine-base datum ___ | Equipment designer | Tests the complete elevation |
| Input and cutting | Guides/conveyor/cutter IDs ___; required product-specific items ___ | Line integrator | Completes both material paths |
| Receiving | Exit levels ___; inspection/support/pack positions ___ | Plant engineer | Rejects an unserved finished-panel route |
| Removed objects | Raft outlines/masses ___; handling route and storage positions ___ | Handling and maintenance leads | Funds off-machine exchange capability |
| Accepted change | Last accepted A ___; first accepted B ___; sample criteria ___ | Quality lead | Measures a complete usable transition |
| Complete cost | Installed equipment ___; exchanges/service ___; common period ___ | Buyer | Compares qualifying architectures |

Use the issued machine-base datum for equipment positions and the released section datums for panel measurements. Preserve their relationship on the installation drawing. A finished panel width and an assembly-handling width are different entries with different purchasing consequences.

## A rafted host purchases an off-machine operation

Identify what leaves the host, what remains installed and what must be prepared before the next product begins. Price the maker-defined handling, competent work and accepted restart. Include any product-specific cutter or guide that changes outside the raft boundary. The tooling set becomes a usable product configuration only when those associated items are ready too. Keep every required guide, cutter and connection identified against Product A or Product B in the supplied assembly list.

Reserve a real preparation and storage position for each removed assembly. The provider supplies its handling dimensions, mass and care requirements. The owner assigns custody, identification and the resources needed to preserve readiness. Keep those costs with the raft proposal even when the preparation occurs before production stops.

The loose tooling below occupies floor space beside machine structures. Use the actual proposed raft outlines to allocate complete storage and movement routes. A row of available shelves or an unbooked corner is insufficient when the exchanged objects have no defined home.

## Judge a switch through accepted output

Select an A-to-B transition from the order book, then a return transition where it occurs in normal work. Use the same input materials and panel acceptance criteria for both proposals. Compare from the last accepted output of the old product through the first accepted output of the new one. Record preparation performed elsewhere separately from the time the host is unavailable.

For dual height, include the offered changes to input, cutting and receiving as well as selecting the resident tooling path. For the raft route, include the complete assembly exchange, associated equipment and inspection. A short demonstration of moving one item cannot represent the entire product transition. Price the host and both complete product kits under each route. Add the dual-height receiver and service provisions to that bid; add raft handling, preparation, storage and exchange labour to the other. For shared handling equipment already required by both routes, charge the additional reservation, attendance or displaced work attributable to exchange, rather than buying the same resource twice.

Both arrangements share the purchased production route. If the order calendar requires A and B at the same time, resolve the uncovered work through the [shared-versus-dedicated line decision](/blog/multi-profile-roof-panel-roll-former-vs-dedicated-lines/). Buying two resident tooling levels alone leaves the shared entry or output dependencies in place.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical architecture mistakes

**Mistake: the upper product has no receiver.** A buyer orders two resident tooling levels while retaining the old fixed-height receiving table unchanged. The second quoted product reaches a different exit level. Obtain the full elevation and price its accepted receiving route before choosing the resident-level package.

**Mistake: a raft exchange competes with coil delivery.** Another buyer treats the workshop crane as freely available, although every planned exchange overlaps committed coil receipt. Stored assemblies also occupy the same material aisle. Reserve a workable handling and storage arrangement, price its resources, then repeat the comparison against resident tooling.

## Eight attachments for a two-profile line RFQ

1. Controlled A/B section drawings, material grades, gauges and coatings.
2. Actual order lengths, quantities, switching sequence and release windows.
3. Coil packages and product-specific entry and cutting requirements.
4. Plant plan and elevation from floor and machine-base datums.
5. Receiving, inspection and packing positions with fixed-height constraints.
6. Handling resources, competent attendance and raft preparation/storage areas.
7. Requested complete alternative configuration schedules and representative accepted-transition protocol.
8. Destination utilities, installation/support boundary and common costing period.
