**Build an in-house maintenance team when recurring workload supports competent staff, equipment-specific technical information and suitable facilities. Use a qualified service contractor when those resources are absent. A mixed arrangement works when routine duties and specialist jobs are assigned explicitly; operator training alone does not qualify someone to repair the machine.**

This is an investment decision about the organisation behind the platform. Buying tools and holding spare parts are only part of that organisation. Before ordering a ceiling platform, decide who can perform the documented maintenance tasks, who takes responsibility for specialist work and how the business keeps operations running during downtime.

## First purchase a capability, then compare its annual cost

List the work the delivered equipment requires from its maintenance documentation. Assign each task to a role with the relevant competence, information and equipment. Keep routine operator checks, maintenance, repairs and any required examinations distinct. Completing one does not automatically satisfy the others.

The general principle is well established: maintenance requires competent personnel, with specialist assistance for work beyond the internal team's competence. [HSE's work-equipment maintenance guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/maintenance.htm) describes both competent in-house staff and specialist contractors in its Great Britain context. Use that distinction to structure the purchase, alongside the requirements applicable at the destination.

The tracked base and supports in the photograph below explain why a generic vehicle workshop description is inadequate. The service scope must identify the actual assemblies and the technical information needed for their assigned tasks.

If the proposed internal team lacks the information or competence for a particular task, allocate that task to a qualified provider. Do not count a future training intention as current capability when pricing the first operating period.

## Complete this make-or-buy worksheet

Use the same planning period and fleet population in both columns. Record actual provider quotations and payroll/facility costs; this worksheet does not assume a universal fleet size at which in-house maintenance becomes cheaper.

| Required input | In-house entry | Contractor entry | Owner and effect on the decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment population | Enter machine IDs, configurations and locations | Enter the same covered machines and locations | Fleet manager fixes the service population |
| Documented workload | List tasks and intervals from each machine's instructions | List tasks included in the provider's offer | Maintenance lead establishes comparable work |
| Competence | Name competent roles and equipment-specific training | Name provider capability and assigned specialist scope | Technical lead rejects gaps before operation |
| Information access | List manuals, diagrams and diagnostic information available | List information the provider needs and receives | Equipment supplier and service lead establish workable support |
| Facilities and tools | Enter suitable workspace, tools and required test equipment | Enter provider resources and site resources still required | Facilities manager identifies investment and site dependencies |
| Availability | Enter staffed periods and cover for absence | Enter contracted attendance scope and exclusions | Operations manager prices actual coverage |
| Annual cost basis | Enter staff, training, premises, tools and outside specialist work | Enter fixed fees, call-outs, travel and excluded work | Finance compares the same period and task scope |
| Downtime arrangement | Name the isolation, repair and return-to-service responsibilities | Name the site/provider handover responsibilities | Site manager prevents an unassigned equipment hold |

Treat competence, information and a safe workplace as entry conditions. Only compare cost after both routes meet those conditions. A low-cost option with nobody able to perform a necessary task is an incomplete offer.

For an internal route, include cover for leave and the time technicians spend away from the platform on other equipment. For an external route, distinguish a booked inspection or service visit from an attendance commitment after a fault. Use the provider's stated coverage; geographical proximity does not itself create availability.

## A mixed model needs a clear dividing line

An owner can retain documented operator checks and defect reporting while purchasing scheduled technical work from a specialist. A larger fleet can employ a maintenance team for specified tasks and contract out jobs requiring expertise or equipment the team does not hold. In either arrangement, write the boundary by task, not by an informal label such as “small repairs.”

Give the site one named coordinator for defect records, equipment withdrawal and communication with the competent service party. The coordinator does not acquire repair competence from holding that role. Define who authorises return to service after the required work and checks are complete.

The raised platform below shows the whole asset whose availability the business is funding. Buying maintenance capability is about keeping that defined asset supported through its life, rather than assuming a mechanic can attend any unfamiliar machine.

![Green crawler equipment with a wide elevated deck, lifting arms and extended ground supports outside a factory.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261029-deck-yard.webp)

*Price support for the complete delivered configuration, including the assemblies that connect the deck to its supporting base.*

Keep access to current manuals and equipment records in the ownership plan. Staff turnover and contractor changes should not leave the machine without its maintenance history or the documents needed by the next competent provider. Agree the records delivered after each service task and keep them linked to the machine ID.

## Two ownership mistakes

**Mistake: counting an operator as the maintenance department.** A hypothetical contractor buys one platform and assumes its trained operator will handle every fault. When a technical problem appears, there is no competent repair provider or appropriate technical information. The purchase budget omitted the support organisation. Allocate technical tasks and obtain the provider's accepted scope before placing the equipment order; keep operator checks within the operator's training and instructions.

**Mistake: funding a workshop for work it cannot cover.** A hypothetical fleet owner purchases tools and hires a general mechanic, then discovers that essential equipment-specific work remains outside that employee's competence and information access. External costs continue alongside the new overhead. Build the task-and-competence matrix first. Invest internally only in the workload the team is equipped to undertake, and price specialist work separately.

## Eight attachments for an ownership-ready RFQ

1. Intended platform tasks and work locations, with floor-to-task dimensions and access plans.
2. Proposed machine population and deployment calendar for the planning period.
3. Internal staff roles, relevant competence and any equipment-specific training already held.
4. Available maintenance workplace, tools and access arrangements described by the facilities owner.
5. Task allocation showing operator checks, internal technical work and contracted specialist work.
6. Destination and identified external providers, with the actual service scopes requested.
7. Required technical documentation, machine records and instruction language.
8. Itemised purchase boundary covering equipment, training, documentation, parts and separately priced service.
