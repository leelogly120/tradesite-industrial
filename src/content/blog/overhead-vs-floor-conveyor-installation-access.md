**Choose an overhead conveyor when the specified product can use its proposed suspended carrier, the building accepts the supports, and the route preserves required floor circulation. Choose a floor conveyor when the process needs base-supported product presentation or the overhead route cannot meet those conditions.** Compare complete qualifying systems through the same transfer stations, including installation and service access. Floor area saved along a track is only one part of that purchase.

The production team first defines what moves, where it must arrive and how it is presented at each process. The conveyor provider then proposes a complete route. Personnel-access equipment follows the installation and maintenance tasks on that route; it does not choose the conveyor architecture for the factory.

## Follow the product through every transfer

[Pacline describes overhead conveyor layouts](https://www.pacline.com/enclosed-track-conveyor-advantages/) that use space above the production floor. Its [layout guidance](https://www.pacline.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/PAC-LINE-DetailedConveyorSpecs.pdf) starts with the path, workstations, aisles, load and unload areas, material flow, carriers and building attachments. Those elements belong in the buyer's comparison before either layout is priced as equipment alone.

For an overhead route, identify the actual suspended product and carrier envelope, each transfer and the required supporting arrangement. Trace loading and unloading back to the workstations that people use. A clear overhead line between two departments is incomplete if the product cannot enter and leave that line in the required condition.

For a floor route, identify the actual product support, conveyor footprint, crossings, process interfaces and receiving positions. Use the completed operating plan, including storage, machines and circulation. A floor system that fits during construction still needs room to operate when those other assets arrive.

The photograph captures an unfinished hall, not a conveyor installation. It shows why the construction route and completed operating layout need separate records. The programme owner must state when access equipment can enter, which support areas are available and when process equipment closes those positions.

Product presentation is a real selection condition. If a process requires the item to stay supported from below, retain that requirement in the floor proposal. An overhead alternative needs a specified carrier that delivers the accepted presentation at every station. Reject a route that solves travel between departments but leaves a separate, unpriced conversion of the product position at each process.

Compare both routes at the same output boundary. For example, a transfer delivered beside a process and a transfer that presents the item in the required process position are different purchases. Keep any extra handling equipment and staffing in the option that needs them.

## Decide the route before counting installation days

The conveyor designer supplies the component arrangement and building interfaces. The installer supplies the assembly and access stages. Combine them with the structural review and the factory's occupation programme so each offer includes the same completed system.

Overhead installation creates work at tracks, supporting members, connections and other positions specified by the selected system. Floor-mounted equipment has its own assembly and interface tasks. Where the accepted floor route leaves no elevated conveyor work, remove the large ceiling-platform allowance from that package and price only the access duties that remain.

Give component handling its own work package. Conveyor sections, supports, drives and other assemblies need the actual presentation arrangement specified by their installers. A shared personnel deck is a working surface for the agreed task; its role and permitted items come from the offered configuration.

Use actual offers and the factory's records for interruption, labour and continuing-care costs. Avoid assigning an automatic efficiency or payback advantage to either architecture. The winning purchase must deliver the required product flow through the real building and leave a workable service arrangement.

## Preserve the later access route as part of the layout

An overhead conveyor still needs loading and unloading space, building supports and maintenance positions. Include those places in the operating plan before calling its floor area available for something else. Show how personnel reach the actual service faces once production machines and stock occupy the building.

![A red broad mesh deck stands raised on crawler equipment and support legs in an open workshop yard.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-deck-raised-yard.webp)
*Retained maintenance access needs the full base and support footprint as well as the elevated working surface.*

Use the visible arrangement as a reminder to draw the complete proposed machine, not just the deck outline. The equipment provider supplies the actual geometry and support reactions. The facility supplies the future route, floor records and available occupation windows. Compare routine access with the separate resources required to replace a drive or another named assembly.

Keep installation access and recurring maintenance as distinct orders. A machine that was useful during an open construction stage is not automatically an economical retained asset. Ownership requires enough supported work and a real operating route; a defined service arrangement deserves a quotation for occasional tasks.

## Two hypothetical layouts that lose their advantage

**Failure case: overhead track is compared with a complete floor line.** A buyer accepts the lower track quote but leaves out loading stations, transfers, supporting work and installation access. The line reaches the department without presenting the product to the next process. Reprice both architectures through the same finished transfer points, with the extra resources assigned to their actual route.

**Failure case: the saved floor area absorbs the maintenance position.** The production layout fills the space under the overhead route with permanent equipment. A later service task has no accepted personnel setup or component-removal route. Reserve the provider's actual service positions in the final layout and price a workable alternative before committing that space to another asset.
