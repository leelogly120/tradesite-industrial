Two opposite faces of one building can have the same roof level and very different forming-equipment requirements. A road may serve one face while a narrow service strip serves the other; the intended receiver and material side can also change. The buyer needs **two destination briefs before asking how equipment might move between them**. A crawler base does not make the second face a copy of the first.

The system considered here raises a compatible roof-panel roll former. The [relocation guide](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-work-zone-relocation/) examines a proposed sequence between work zones. This article prepares the two destination geometries that must exist before that later movement discussion can be meaningful.

## Describe each building face as its own destination

### Give the faces stable project references

Use a grid, cardinal direction or another agreed building reference, then label the actual roof receiving area on each face. Front and back can change with the reader's viewpoint. A common project convention lets the equipment party relate each destination to the same whole-building plan. The brief should describe where the first emerging panel is expected to be received, rather than assuming that every point along an eave represents the same work task or that the opposite side has a mirrored receiving arrangement.

### Name the candidate ground positions separately

Show the proposed stand area for each face and identify the ground reference relevant to it. Equal roof elevations do not establish equal outlet-to-support relationships if the surrounding ground differs. A hard-looking road and a similar-looking paved service strip can also require different support evidence. The drawing identifies where those questions arise; the [ground-support review](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-ground-support-review/) addresses the separate specialist assessment. Do not transfer a conclusion from one face to the other based on surface appearance.

### Associate the actual panel task with each face

State which panel requirement and output direction belongs to each roof receiver. If the same former is proposed, identify that shared basis without implying that its orientation is the same at both destinations. If the project changes panel requirements between faces, preserve the change in the brief. The buyer is defining the two tasks, not authorizing a different tooling or machine setup. A supplier response can then explain which actual proposed configurations address those tasks and which questions remain unresolved.

## Compare what the shared roof height omits

### Keep inlet and outlet directions visible

On each destination plan, label the former's inlet and cutter or outlet against the named receiver. Mirroring the building plan does not establish that the equipment can be mirrored with it. The feed-side space could face the road on one side and the building on the other. Keep the actual material interface question visible rather than rotating only the roof-direction arrow. The [mounting-interface guide](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) develops physical machine connections; here the task is retaining both ends of the intended arrangement in each destination brief.

### Describe access to the destination, not just around the building

Identify the route that reaches each candidate position, including the actual endpoint and the construction stage when it is expected to be available. A route around a building does not automatically connect to the required position beside its opposite face. Another contractor's storage, an access restriction or a disconnected service strip can create a different destination problem. Record the location and known geometry without prescribing a driving or transfer method. The [first-arrival survey](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-access-route-survey/) supplies the corresponding route inputs.

### Compare local activities at the same project stage

One face may have continuous deliveries while the other contains facade work, drainage or stored material. Describe the actual expected activities rather than assuming the quieter-looking side is available. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/workplacetransport/information/safeworkplace.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE workplace-route guidance</a> addresses obstructions and changing site routes. It provides context for describing both destinations at the intended stage, without approving any machine access, occupied work area or proposed transition between them.

## Test two face-specific enquiry briefs

### Keep the hypothetical roof task constant

Imagine a **hypothetical hall** with equal eave elevations on its east and west faces. The east brief proposes a base beside a delivery road, with an identified receiver. The west brief proposes a base beside a service strip, but its receiver is marked only as opposite side. Both initially ask for the same compatible former. The shared roof height is real within the example, yet only the east task has a named outlet-to-receiver relationship. The first useful action for the west brief is to identify that destination, not to assume east's equipment response applies.

### Expose the different interface questions

At the east face, the controlling issue may be whether the project can make the proposed area available beside deliveries. At the west face, the receiver and the material-side location both need definition. The trade-off is not east versus west as inherently better positions. It concerns the different information and local commitments required before either concept can be compared. Ask the equipment party to respond to the two completed destination briefs, with each local dependency visible. A single height-based reply would otherwise conceal the unresolved west-side task.

### Retain differences when one proposal covers both

A supplier might later identify one actual equipment arrangement for discussion at both faces. Keep the separate destinations and their assumptions in the comparison. Coverage of two described positions does not establish the route, permitted equipment state or work method between them. The next relocation enquiry can use the completed destination information without treating it as movement approval. The table illustrates what belongs in each destination row before that next question is asked.

| Destination comparison | East-face brief | West-face brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receiver identity | Named bay and first receiving area | Must be named before the opposite-side label is useful |
| Ground relationship | Candidate position beside the road | Separate position and ground reference in the service strip |
| Outlet direction | Shown against the identified receiver | Needs its own direction rather than a mirrored assumption |
| Material-side interface | Related to the road-side space actually proposed | Requires a location in the west-side occupied layout |
| Site availability | Delivery interaction needs a project response | Service-strip activities and later access need their own response |
| Later transition | Supplies a described departure destination | Supplies an arrival destination, without authorizing the route between them |

## Prepare the destination pair for equipment review

### Ask for coverage of two named tasks

Use one whole-building orientation plan and two detailed destination views. Identify which information is common and which changes. The [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) is one architecture to investigate; a truck-mounted proposal should answer the same two tasks using its own actual arrangement. Request a clear statement of the positions addressed and the geometry still needed. That is more useful than a general promise to work on both sides of the building, because it gives the buyer an equipment response that can be compared with actual project locations.

- Name the two roof receivers and associate each with a stable building reference.
- Connect the requested outlet height to each candidate ground position and local datum.
- Show the panel material requirement, output direction and separate inlet-side space.
- Identify the access route ending at each destination, with unresolved connections marked.
- State the transport handoff and the construction stage affecting the two destination areas.
- Keep local support, shared-site activities and any excluded equipment interfaces separate between the briefs.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize when one brief cannot represent both faces

A common proposal may not fit both tasks when it relies on a mirrored machine arrangement, an undefined second receiver or a copied support assumption. A height match does not close those gaps. If one destination changes, revisit the affected brief without silently changing the other task. This page supplies no relocation method, travel permission or number of required positions. The buyer's output is the destination pair; the actual transition, configuration and work arrangements require the responsible equipment and project parties.

## FAQ

#### Can the second face simply use a mirrored drawing?

A mirrored building view can help presentation, but it cannot establish a mirrored equipment configuration. Label the actual inlet, outlet and receiver on the second destination plan. The material-side space and surrounding activities may differ. Ask the equipment provider to identify the arrangement it actually proposes rather than treating a graphic operation as a technical answer.

#### Does the same roof height mean the same equipment question?

No. Ground reference, location, direction and intervening geometry can all differ. Keep the height datum with each destination and show the actual requested outlet relationship. Equal eave elevations can be common project information while the two equipment-interface questions remain different. The comparison should preserve those differences instead of asking a single nominal height to represent the whole task.

#### Is the route between faces part of this brief?

The destination pair should identify its connected access endpoints and dependencies, but it does not select the intervening movement method. That needs a separate relocation review using actual equipment states and the relevant construction stage. Clear departure and arrival tasks make that later question better defined; they do not authorize any transition or establish how many moves are needed.

#### What if only one face has a complete receiver?

Ask a bounded equipment question for that task while identifying the missing receiver on the other face. Do not copy the first answer across the building. The project needs to define the second destination sufficiently for its own response. Keeping the partial scope clear can move the enquiry forward without implying that both faces have already been resolved.
