Buy a specialist access package matched to the facility's hazardous-area requirements when its issued area schedule places the task or equipment route in a classified zone. Compare an ordinary ceiling platform only for a work package whose location and conditions the responsible facility team has accepted for ordinary equipment. An electric drive or a production shutdown is not a substitute for that decision.

This choice begins with the workplace, not the platform's paint color or catalog height. The purchasing team needs the facility's defined equipment requirements and the exact area in which the machine, its supplies and its supporting activities will be located. A low ordinary-platform price is irrelevant when it addresses a different environment.

## Two eligible packages, rather than a cheaper unqualified machine

One route retains the work in its classified environment and buys equipment and services suitable for it. The other changes the actual project package through a facility-designed arrangement, such as moving eligible work to an accepted location or using an approved different work period and conditions. Compare the second route only after the facility has accepted its defined conditions.

The site owner and appointed specialists determine whether that alternative exists. Facility specialists define the isolation, ventilation, testing and environmental decisions required by their actual work arrangement. A contractor's promise to stop work nearby is not the facility's completed environmental decision.

Great Britain's [HSE explanation of explosive atmospheres](https://www.hse.gov.uk/fireandexplosion/atex.htm) discusses workplace zones and suitable equipment. Its [DSEAR overview](https://www.hse.gov.uk/fireandexplosion/dsear.htm) identifies fire and explosion risks from dangerous substances. These are jurisdiction-specific sources for the category distinction. The destination's responsible specialists establish the applicable requirements for the actual facility.

Avoid purchasing “explosion-proof” as an unqualified adjective. Ask for the actual equipment configuration and evidence corresponding to the facility's stated requirements. A protection claim for one motor or component does not describe the complete working package, its tools, supplies and supporting activities.

## An environmental boundary belongs in the purchase schedule

Complete the fields below from the facility's issued documents. Procurement records the requirements; it does not assign the area classification or decide that a shutdown removes it.

| Buyer input | Project entry and reference | Responsible owner | Purchase decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task location | Building/grid, workface and height from setup floor ___ | Surveyor and maintenance lead | Identifies the actual ceiling-access duty |
| Environmental requirement | Applicable area document, revision and equipment requirements ___ | Facility's appointed responsible specialist | Defines which equipment offers are eligible |
| Package boundary | Machine, tools, supplies, support area and routes within the review ___ | Contractor and facility reviewer | Prevents a compliant-looking component from standing for the whole package |
| Specialist offer | Exact configuration and supporting evidence against each requirement ___ | Proposed specialist equipment provider | Accepts or rejects the classified-area equipment package |
| Alternative work conditions | Issued location/period, accepted conditions and authorised owner ___ | Facility manager and appointed specialists | Establishes whether an ordinary-equipment alternative exists |
| Structural interface | Offered support reactions, floor records and approved positions ___ | Equipment provider and structural engineer | Tests physical suitability independently of environment |
| Programme and services | Access window, facility attendance, handling and removal scope ___ | Planner and actual service providers | Completes the cost of each eligible route |
| Release boundary | Accepted document set, change triggers and responsible project sign-off ___ | Project manager and facility owner | Keeps the ordered scope aligned with the accepted work package |

The alternative needs a real delivered outcome, not a note saying “work during shutdown.” Include the facility's retained services and the cost of making that work period available. If the alternative does not have an accepted environmental basis, remove it from the ordinary-equipment comparison.

Structural suitability remains a separate test. A platform can match an environmental requirement and still be unsuitable for the floor, entry route or required work positions. Conversely, fitting through the door and reaching the ceiling does not answer the environmental question.

## Compare the complete work package over the same maintenance scope

Price the specialist route with the actual equipment, required services, mobilisation and completion boundaries. Price the accepted alternative with the facility work it depends on, the access package, component handling and reinstatement obligations. Use the same completed ceiling or maintenance task in both columns.

Do not claim a universal saving for either route. A long occupation window, specialist availability and the facility's own programme can dominate the result. Record actual offers and agreed facility costs. If a required service has no identified provider or accepted scope, that package is incomplete and should not win on price.

## Two rejected purchasing shortcuts

**Failure case: electric power is treated as environmental approval.** A hypothetical buyer selects an electrically powered platform for a classified production area without an offer addressing the facility's equipment requirements. The drive type has been mistaken for suitability of the whole package. Remove that offer from the eligible list and obtain the specialist equipment and supporting evidence the facility requires.

**Failure case: the shutdown calendar replaces the facility's decision.** Another buyer schedules an ordinary platform while production is stopped and assumes the area requirements disappear. The facility has issued no accepted alternative conditions for that package. Keep the specialist route in the budget until an actual facility-approved alternative exists; procurement cannot create one by changing a date.

These are hypothetical tender errors. Correct them at package selection, before equipment is ordered against the wrong environment.
