**Remain supply-only when your strength is controlled roof-panel production and dispatch. Expand to supply and install when repeat awarded work supports competent installation supervision, a complete site-resource plan and the funding to carry the job through its accepted completion. Buy equipment for that added work only after identifying which resources the business must own and which it will purchase as defined services.**

A roofing installer who needs reliable panels and a panel producer who wants installation revenue start from different capabilities. This guide concerns the second business. A former increases manufacturing capability. It does not provide a roofing crew, site access, material positioning or responsibility for the completed roof.

## The customer is buying a different endpoint

Under a defined supply-only order, the producer makes the accepted panel product and delivers it to the agreed handover. The commercial scope should identify profile, material, lengths, quantities, inspection, packing and delivery. Name the receiving party and what happens after that boundary.

A supply-and-install order extends the commitment to completed work at the building. It adds a site programme, competent installation resources, handling and access arrangements, component coordination and project handover. Write those duties into a project capability plan before counting the whole installed-contract value as demand for manufacturing equipment.

Supply-only can include a specified accessory basket when the seller has accepted that scope. It still needs an explicit boundary. Delivering panels and clips does not by itself mean that the seller has priced installing them. The [toll-forming versus own-material sales guide](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-toll-service-vs-own-material-sales/) covers a different decision: who supplies and owns the input material during production.

## Keep a factory case and a project case

First test the manufacturing investment against panel orders the factory can fulfil. Then test the installation expansion against the additional work and resources needed at each project. A viable factory does not automatically support a viable installation operation, and an attractive installation order does not establish the workload for owning every machine.

Use actual quoted costs and contract milestones in the finance row. Compare the total funded work period, including waiting for the released site and completing inspections. Do not treat money associated with materials, labour and services as machinery margin.

## Installation growth has an intermediate route

The producer does not have to own every site resource at the first installed order. A defined installation partner, specialist closure service, qualified haulage provider or project-specific equipment package can fill identified gaps. The producer still needs a complete commercial and delivery plan for the scope it sells.

Choose that route when the partners' accepted scope and programme create a deliverable project and repeat demand does not yet support ownership. Buy an asset when the recurring duty, compatible configuration and competent support justify keeping it between jobs. Compare those routes against the same installed endpoint.

If installation capability and supervision are absent, keep the current offer supply-only or purchase a complete supported installation scope before promising the broader result. A machine-only quote does not close the gap. Equally, avoid buying a forming lift simply because a subcontractor already has personnel-access equipment: those resources perform different jobs.

## Place production only after mapping the site

An installation business can still use factory-made panels. Compare their delivery and receiving route with the project lengths and programme. On-site production becomes an equipment candidate when the accepted panel product, site layout and material supply support it. Roof-level forming is a further specific arrangement that needs the former, lift and roof receiver to be reviewed together.

## Two hypothetical expansions with incomplete equipment cases

**Failure case: installed-contract value is counted as former utilisation.** A producer justifies another line from the full value of a roofing contract, although much of that order funds installation and purchased services. Separate the committed panel output from the remaining work. Invest in the actual constrained resource instead of allocating the whole contract to manufacturing capacity.

**Failure case: a forming lift replaces the missing installation plan.** Another producer buys elevated forming equipment but has no accepted panel-receiving, personnel-access or specialist closure arrangement. The order can produce panels without completing the roof. Price the missing duties and assign competent providers before selecting the equipment basket.
