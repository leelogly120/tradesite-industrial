<!-- audit-section: buyer-intent -->
Choose a ceiling-platform control proposal only when its documented control locations and permitted functions suit the visibility needs of every planned workface. If a duct, column or completed wall blocks a critical view, change the access layout or work sequence before accepting the proposal. A wireless option alone cannot solve an obstruction between the operator and the object that must remain visible.

An open first bay and a congested final bay deserve separate review. Name them on the drawing, identify the objects involved and require a supplier response for the difficult bay.

## Three views belong in the same discussion

The task view concerns the fixing line, panel edge or service being worked on. The platform view concerns deck boundaries and their relationship to nearby structure. The surrounding-area view concerns the base, other equipment and people using adjacent space. A photograph that shows one of these clearly often leaves another hidden.

In the side view below, the deck edge, roof beams and crawler base occupy different parts of the scene. Use that distinction to prepare the review drawing. The proposed control position must come from the offered equipment information and site assessment, rather than the convenient spot from which a photographer took a picture.

![Side view of a red large-deck crawler platform below white roof beams in an unfinished hall](/images/equipment/buyer-20261027-ceiling-side.webp)

*Record the overhead workface and the space around the base separately; seeing one does not answer the visibility question for the other.*

## Compare the view before the controller option

Use the [remote-control planning guide](/blog/remote-control-aerial-platform-safety-planning/) for the broader control package, then use this workface schedule to assess visibility at each location.

Use a common workface schedule to compare suppliers. First identify the control locations and functions actually offered for the intended equipment states. Then relate those locations to the building section and task. This puts a proposal with fixed control positions and one with an offered remote option on the same procurement basis.

Retain the simpler proposal when its documented arrangement meets the task at every listed workface. Consider an additional control option when it addresses a named visibility constraint within its stated permitted use. Request the applicable instructions and a site-review record for that change. A longer claimed radio range has no purchasing value when the unresolved problem is a solid wall hiding the deck edge.

The trade-off is between equipment scope and the building's work sequence. Completing the open ceiling area before installing a partition can preserve a useful view, but that choice needs agreement from the construction scheduler. If the partition must be installed first, compare access layouts against the completed geometry. Budget the resulting equipment or scheduling change rather than assuming the crew will find a new control position later.

[HSE's MEWP guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm) identifies entrapment near fixed structures and collision among the hazards to address during selection and planning. That Great Britain guidance supports including overhead geometry in the procurement review; the destination's competent project team determines the applicable site arrangements.

## Make a workface record that survives a bay change

Complete one copy of this table for each materially different bay. Use building grid references and the actual setup floor as the height datum. Add a section through the obstruction: a plan view alone leaves the vertical relationship unclear.

| Buyer field | Entry and measurement datum | Responsible party | Decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workface identity | Bay/grid ___; fixing-line height ___ from setup floor; task ___ | Surveyor and installation supervisor | Group matching bays and separate different tasks |
| Critical objects | Deck edge ___; fixing line ___; adjacent beam/duct ___ marked on section | Installation supervisor with site safety lead | Define the objects the proposed arrangement must address |
| View obstruction | Object ___; outline and height ___; drawing revision ___ | Surveyor and services coordinator | Change layout or sequence where the object blocks the required view |
| Control position and state | Offered location ___; relevant function/state ___; instruction reference ___ | Supplier supplies documented scope; site reviewer assesses position | Accept the offered scope or request a different arrangement |
| Task materials and phase | Panel length ___; storage location ___; partition/duct phase ___ | Contractor and programme coordinator | Review the view with the planned task load and completed surroundings |
| Review outcome | Bay ___; accepted layout revision ___; required change ___; reviewer/date ___ | Procurement lead records supplier and site decisions | Release the matching package or exclude the affected workface |

The wide hall scene below illustrates the reason for a bay register: repeated columns and roof members give the building a regular appearance, while the surrounding floor and adjacent construction differ. Record those differences instead of declaring every bay equivalent.

![Broad red deck above an unfinished hall aisle with repeating roof beams and adjacent dark steel framing](/images/equipment/buyer-20261027-ceiling-wide.webp)

*Use grid references to connect each visibility record to a specific bay and construction phase.*

## Reject these two shortcuts before purchase

**Failure 1: a successful open-bay review is copied to a ducted bay.** In a hypothetical warehouse, the enquiry photographs show the first installation area. A later bay contains a cross-duct that conceals the far deck edge from the proposed position. The supplier priced the open-bay arrangement, leaving the contractor to revise the sequence after delivery. Prevent this by including a section through the worst obstruction and requiring a separate outcome for that bay.

**Failure 2: wireless control is accepted as a visibility solution.** In a hypothetical wall installation, the buyer selects a radio option because the next face is behind a partition. The handset changes the control interface, while the partition still blocks the intended view from the reviewed location. Prevent this by requiring the proposed location, permitted function and relevant sightline on the same drawing before accepting the option.

A proposal does not fit when the required view is blocked and neither an accepted layout nor an agreed sequence change resolves it. Exclude that workface from the order until a workable access arrangement is selected. Do not count a different operator position as a solution without the corresponding equipment and site review.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Build the RFQ around eight attachments

- Workface register with grid references, task descriptions and measured height from each setup floor.
- Ceiling and wall sections showing beam geometry, ducts, columns and the critical objects to be viewed.
- Annotated photos with camera locations marked, including the most obstructed bay and its next construction phase.
- Crew, tools and material schedule, including panel dimensions and planned staging locations on the deck.
- Floor and access-route records covering setup areas, openings, nearby traffic and the route between workfaces.
- Required control functions and equipment states, with proposed locations clearly identified for supplier review.
- Construction sequence identifying when partitions, services and temporary works change each workface view.
- Destination, transport arrangements and documentation requirements, including the named site acceptance and familiarization reviewers.
