Name each workface and what the operator must see there before you argue about a controller. A view of the platform base is not a view of the deck edge. Take that list to the [crawler under-ceiling buyers guide](/blog/crawler-under-ceiling-platform-buyers-guide/). Wider control questions stay in the [remote-control planning guide](/blog/remote-control-aerial-platform-safety-planning/).

| Face you can mark | What to write | If the drawing is unfinished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face A, the first bay | The object that must stay in sight | Mark it on site |
| The beam or duct that hides the edge | Which face it blocks | Confirm on RFQ after the photo |
| Where the operator stands | On the deck, beside the base, or unknown | Do not invent a legal standing place |
| The next face after a move | What changes: light, duct, or opening | Measure the move on site |

Two ways this goes wrong. One photo of the hall is treated as the view for every face, and the duct that hides face B never reaches the supplier. Or a radio-control option is accepted as proof the operator can see the deck edge, when the person with the controller is still beside the base under the steel.

Copy these lines onto the buyers-guide sketch:

- Face names and the object that must stay visible
- What the steel, duct, or light hides
- Operator position: deck, base, or unknown
- Catalog height class to screen: 20m, 25m, 31m, or 35m
- Floor under the tracks
- Destination country
