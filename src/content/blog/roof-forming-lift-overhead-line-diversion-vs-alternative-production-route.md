Use a forming position affected by overhead lines only under the network owner's agreed arrangements and a competent project plan covering the entire work period. Choose a different production and delivery route when isolation, diversion or another owner-accepted intervention cannot serve the required sequence. Reserve the alternative before committing the lift.

## Give the network owner the real project, including removal

Purchasing needs a decision on two construction routes, not an informal opinion about the apparent distance from the eaves. The site manager should engage the network owner and competent specialists with accurate drawings of the proposed work. Include arrival, establishment, forming equipment, coil deliveries, roof reception, panel movements, changes of position and demobilization. The controls and any necessary utility work belong to those responsible parties. Obtain their decisions and dates in writing before pricing the affected route as available.

Great Britain's Health and Safety Executive advises considering avoidance of overhead lines and contacting the line owner about measures such as diversion or isolation where work cannot be avoided. Apply the governing requirements and network-owner process at the actual site. A power interruption elsewhere on the premises, or the plant supplier's view from a photograph, provides no authorization for work near a line. Start the purchasing comparison with a route the project can avoid and a separate, conditional route that depends on specialist release. [HSE guidance on overhead lines](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/overhead.htm)

## Price each route through the last panel

For the utility-dependent bid, record application, design, network work, any outage planning, attendance, formal release and restoration as quoted by the responsible organizations. Determine the work periods actually available to the roofing contractor and what happens if the network changes its program. A permanent diversion and a time-limited isolation are different offers. Put their charges, dates and residual site constraints in separate columns whenever both are proposed. Give no credit for additional time the network owner has not agreed.

| Project entry | Who supplies it | Decision the buyer can make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify the line owner, site reference and affected roof areas | Client or site manager | Open the formal route for the actual asset. |
| Record all equipment and panel movements in scope | Forming and handling suppliers | Submit a complete activity description for assessment. |
| Enter the owner's offered intervention, release and restoration periods | Network owner | Define the usable project window and costs. |
| Map alternate forming sites, transport routes and roof receiving points | Project logistics lead | Test an independently deliverable alternative. |
| List panel marks, handover dates and held or split areas | Roofing contractor | Allocate each bay to a viable supply route. |
| Enter booked cost, cancellation terms and alternative availability | Buyer | Choose the commitment date and fallback. |

For the alternative bid, identify where panels will actually be made and how they will reach the workface. Shifting a machine to another side of the building can remove one exposure while leaving a panel transfer, crane route or return journey within the same affected area. Have the competent team assess the entire revised route. Include finished-panel lengths, transport, receiving plant, holding space and any applicable permissions. The buyer's comparison ends after both routes have supplied the same accepted roof panels and returned the site.

## Keep network dependencies out of unconditional plant commitments

Use the commercial terms to protect the sequence. If the exceptional intervention remains conditional, request an option on the machine or a later production booking and obtain a firm price for the independently accepted alternative. Compare the cost of making that reservation with the real cost of rebooking a full roof team. Where only part of the roof depends on utility work, release the independent zones first if project design, access and installation sequence allow it. Keep their purchase quantities separate from the held area.

**Mistake: The machine is booked before the owner releases the work period.** A project expects a planned outage to coincide with installation and orders a full campaign of lift days. The owner confirms a much shorter window containing only part of the proposed work. Rebuild the schedule around the written terms and price an alternate supply route for the remaining zones before issuing the purchased dates.

**Mistake: The revised plan moves the former but leaves the transfer unchanged.** A private yard lies away from the proposed equipment position. Long panels must still be brought around the same side of the structure to reach the roof. Ask the specialist project team to assess the entire revised handover and obtain a different receiving point or production route before awarding it.

Choose the utility-dependent alternative only for an owner-released period with a feasible complete schedule and defensible installed cost. Choose avoidance when a workable alternate chain removes the dependency at a better project outcome. If neither chain has an accepted basis, reserve the unaffected fabrication work and leave the affected zone out of the machine purchase commitment until the responsible parties release a route.

## Eight RFQ inputs for the two route options

1. Attach current site and overhead-asset drawings from responsible parties.
2. Identify the network owner and formal project contact.
3. Describe equipment states, delivery, panel movement and removal.
4. Provide proposed work areas, roof sequence and required dates.
5. Attach the owner's offered intervention scope and release terms.
6. Show the complete independently assessed alternate production route.
7. Request separate equipment, handling, delay and reinstatement costs.
8. Name the parties authorizing commencement, handover and restoration.
