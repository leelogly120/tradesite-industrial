Pay for an elevated-travel capability when the actual equipment documents permit the required movement, the site accepts its complete route and repeated task transitions create a measured commercial advantage. Choose a repositioned arrangement when the work consists of separate released positions or the route cannot support that travel duty. A tracked base alone answers neither question.

An extended ceiling strip can contain many short tasks. The productive fixing time is only part of the access cost: the crew also replenishes materials, changes position and waits for released work. Buy a platform against that complete cycle rather than a demonstration of one movement in an empty yard.

## The useful comparison is a completed work strip

Define one representative strip with an issued drawing, a start boundary and a finished acceptance condition. Ask bidders to describe how their offered arrangements serve all its positions. The question is not which machine moves most impressively. It is which accepted package completes the strip with the required resources and least complete cost.

Separate three equipment states in the proposal: the working arrangement at a position, the permitted arrangement for relocation and the transport arrangement for delivery. A photograph of a folded machine illustrates its physical envelope. It does not supply the transition between those states.

![A green crawler machine stands in a low folded arrangement with its support feet extended on a paved yard.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261029-deck-folded.webp)
*Ask for the documented relocation arrangement rather than treating a low equipment photograph as a travel sequence.*

The [IPAF ground-assessment guidance page](https://www.ipaf.org/en/operator-feeds/do-you-know-what-look-when-assessing-ground-conditions) includes both operating areas and travel routes in the assessment. Apply that distinction to the equipment quotation. A position accepted for stationary work is not automatically an accepted connecting route.

Manufacturer instructions also matter by configuration. [Genie's published cribbing discussion](https://www.genielift.com/en/aerialpros/cribbing-instructions-for-boom-lifts) describes arrangements with an elevated-drive lockout. Its example demonstrates why movement permission cannot be generalized across equipment or site arrangements. Do not transfer a Genie instruction or a demonstration machine's capability to an ARCLIFT catalog model.

## Fill in the transition model before ranking a drive option

Use the actual supporting surface as the height datum and drawing grids as position references. Enter the equipment provider's proposed configuration and the site review for each route, rather than a generic “indoor floor” description.

| Buyer input | Entry and reference to attach | Input owner | Effect on the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Completed strip | Grid limits ___; accepted installation output ___ | Installation supervisor | Keeps both offers on the same finished work |
| Work positions | Position IDs ___; task heights from setup surface ___ | Surveyor and installer | Establishes the positions each arrangement must serve |
| Movement permission | Equipment configuration ___; permitted relocation state and document revision ___ | Equipment provider | Admits only an actually offered movement capability |
| Route conditions | Surveyed surface, openings, obstructions and route limits ___ | Facility engineer and site reviewer | Accepts or excludes that equipment/route combination |
| Transition demand | Actual change count and measured time/resource records ___ | Planner and installer | Tests whether movement savings affect the programme |
| Replenishment | Material presentation, crew access and replenishment events ___ | Installation lead and handling provider | Prevents counting uninterrupted work that the task cannot sustain |
| Concurrent activity | Released route windows and other occupations ___ | Main contractor | Reveals pauses that neither drive system removes |
| Complete cost | Equipment, mobilisation, staffing, transitions and removal ___ | Estimator with actual provider offers | Ranks qualified packages over the same work strip |

This is a purchasing model, not a movement procedure. The equipment documents and site method define how a transition is carried out. The estimator records the time and resources that accepted method requires.

Do not use the drive speed in a brochure as the duration of a workface transition. Material preparation, permitted equipment state, route occupation and the next task's readiness all sit outside that isolated figure. Obtain a representative cycle basis from the actual proposed arrangement and use it consistently.

## Separate a route problem from an equipment premium

If the proposed strip passes through unavailable floor space, buying a drive feature does not release that space. Compare a different work sequence, a different access arrangement or genuinely separate work positions. Keep a platform out of the shortlist when its required route is absent from the accepted plan.

Conversely, a continuous released strip deserves more than a comparison of purchase prices. If one qualified package removes repeated costly transitions, include that measured benefit over the committed work. Use documented project cycles and actual offers; do not invent a universal payback period or assume a premium feature recovers its price on every ceiling.

The low platform in the hall below shows the base, support legs and the surrounding floor as part of the same work area. Map this footprint at every proposed position. Draw the connection between positions as a separate item, with its own condition and occupation record.

![A red crawler work platform stands low in an unfinished hall with support legs and the surrounding floor visible.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261101-deck-hall-low.webp)
*Show both the stationary footprint and the connecting route on the work-strip plan.*

Compare alternatives over the same period. Include the cost of establishing the route when the project retains that work, as well as inspection, mobilisation, task-related resources and removal. Where a hire alternative is being considered, use the actual provider's scope rather than assuming a feature is available locally.

The result can favor either arrangement. A machine with more permitted movement has value when the project uses that capability. A simpler position-by-position package remains a sound purchase when its accepted transitions fit the programme and its total cost is lower.

## Two estimates that purchase the wrong advantage

**Failure case: continuous travel is budgeted against a stationary-duty offer.** A hypothetical buyer takes a high-level movement demonstration as evidence that the quoted crawler deck can perform the same duty. The price schedule then omits repositioning resources. Replace the assumption with the actual offered operating-state documents and rebuild the cycle estimate for that configuration.

**Failure case: a movement premium serves disconnected workfaces.** Another buyer pays for elevated travel, but the project contains isolated tasks separated by occupied routes and incompatible working arrangements. The premium does not connect those positions. Compare separate accepted setups over the real release calendar before approving the purchase.

These examples concern equipment selection and estimating. They are not instructions to move a platform, change its supports or alter a control function.
