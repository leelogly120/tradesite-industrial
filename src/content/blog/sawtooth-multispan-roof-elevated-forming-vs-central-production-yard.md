Choose repeated elevated forming positions for a sawtooth roof only when the complete offered arrangement connects to every assigned receiver and its material supply route. Choose a central ground production yard with separately planned panel distribution when internal receivers cannot be served from those accepted positions. Where only some roof runs suit elevated production, price a defined mixed package rather than extending one lift's claimed coverage across the roof.

A sawtooth or repeated multi-span roof creates several panel destinations within one building. The perimeter eave is not automatically a receiver for every internal roof run. Decide where panels will be made and how each released run reaches its installation workface before selecting a chassis or comparing nominal height.

## Draw the receiving topology before counting machines

Start with the roof designer's plan and sections. Mark each run by grid, direction, specified panel lengths and first receiving point. Identify ridges, valleys, changes of level, rooflights and other intervening construction that the actual supply concept must address. Do not turn the plan into a proposed handling method; the appointed receiving and lifting specialists provide that design.

Then add candidate equipment positions on surveyed ground or floor references. Connect the former's proposed outlet point to the specified receiver in both plan and section. A lower internal eave can remain inaccessible from the perimeter, even where its elevation is below a catalogue height class.

## Compare two complete production-and-distribution arrangements

The repeated-position option includes the compatible lift and former, accepted setup locations, coil supply at those locations, separately defined receiving resources and the moves between released work areas. The equipment providers identify the offered configurations for production and relocation. A crawler base alone supplies no permission for loaded or elevated travel.

The central-yard option includes the former, its working space, coil and finished-panel handling, identification by roof run, and distribution from the yard to each receiver. Obtain the distribution specialist's proposal for the actual lengths and site constraints. A yard near the building is useful only when those onward connections exist.

Use the same accepted panel schedule for both. If one route needs shorter panels, new end laps or a changed roof detail, obtain the roof designer's accepted alternative and price it separately. It is a product change, not a concealed logistics allowance.

The useful comparison ends at the same receiving boundary for each run. A line price ending at the ground outlet and an elevated package ending near the roof are different scopes until the missing distribution and receiving work is included.

## Let the difficult internal run decide the missing package

Do not select the overall arrangement using the easiest perimeter bay. Review the internal receiver that creates the most consequential gap: an enclosed approach, an unavailable setup area or a route crossing another active work zone. If that run needs a separate supply method, carry its actual cost and programme into the whole-roof comparison.

That does not require rejecting elevated forming everywhere. A mixed arrangement can use accepted perimeter positions for suitable runs and a separately priced central-yard or delivered-panel route for others. Allocate each panel mark to one route so material quantities and responsibilities are not counted twice.

## Keep production release connected to installation demand

A central yard creates the opportunity to group production, but the installation team still releases roof runs in its own accepted sequence. Identify storage, panel labelling and the party authorising production for each destination. A large total roof area is not an instruction to form every panel before a receiver is ready.

Repeated elevated positions have a different dependency: the next setup area, coil handoff and roof crew must become available together. Price waiting, return visits and exception supply from the actual programme, without inserting a universal relocation or production rate.

The long ribs in the photograph show why each roof run needs its own identity. Actual drawings supply the lengths and boundaries; distinguish them in production and dispatch records so material reaches the correct internal destination.

## Two hypothetical whole-roof failures

**Failure case: the perimeter position is priced as coverage of every valley.** A buyer selects a high catalogue class but the offer has no connection to an internal receiver beyond an intervening roof run. Obtain a receiver-specific equipment response or add a designed distribution package before counting that area as served.

**Failure case: a central yard is ordered without its onward handling.** Panels are formed to their correct lengths, but the accepted route from the yard to several internal destinations was never purchased. Compare complete production-to-receiver packages and retain a supplied-panel alternative for any remaining inaccessible run.
