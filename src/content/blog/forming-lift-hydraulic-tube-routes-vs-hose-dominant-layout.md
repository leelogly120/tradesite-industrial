Choose a provider-designed tube-dominant hydraulic package for stable fixed runs when its supported installation and formed-tube replacement route fit your fleet. Choose an accepted hose-dominant package when real movement, vibration isolation or constrained routing makes flexibility valuable and the complete protected route remains inspectable and replaceable. Keep the flexible transitions required by the machine design in either offer. Give both bidders the same forming-lift duty, ownership period and delivery condition. Choose the lower complete supported total among layouts that meet those requirements.

This is a purchase decision for a new forming lift with its required functions already defined. It does not begin with a mechanic replacing an inconvenient line on an existing machine. The equipment provider accepts the complete circuit, component identities, routing and operating states. Procurement selects between actual supported configurations and funds the resources each requires.

## Compare complete routes, not lengths of material

A forming lift positions a compatible roof-panel former. Identify those two assemblies and their hydraulic boundaries separately. One machine can have its own power unit while the other has another circuit. A shared frame or adjacent controls cannot turn them into one hydraulic system.

Request each offered routing package as a complete installed object: tubes, hoses, connections, supports, guards, accessible inspection points and the relevant handover records. Keep the required functions, actual former and site programme unchanged between bids. A tube price excluding its formed assemblies and supports is not comparable with a finished hose package.

The workshop scene below contains forming stations, separate power units, black hoses and metal connections. It provides a useful physical reminder that the connection path occupies space around the machinery. Use the offered lift's own route drawing to locate its components and maintenance access.

## Separate fixed pathways from interfaces that move

[STAUFF's comparison of tube, pipe and hose](https://stauff.com/en/blog/knowledge/tube-and-pipe-vs-hose-which-is-the-right-choice-for-your-application) distinguishes rigid fixed pathways from flexible connections accommodating relative movement and vibration. It also identifies the fabrication work and vibration transmission associated with rigid routes, and the abrasion and environmental exposure relevant to hoses. The buyer should therefore compare a mixed machine layout rather than demand one material everywhere. Mark tube-to-hose transitions on the offered drawing and identify the provider accepting each interface.

For a tube-dominant proposal, identify the stable routes, their supported mounting and how the completed assembly remains accessible for replacement. A tube can be a well-defined manufactured spare. Its value depends on having the correct geometry, material, connections and supply route available to the owner.

For a hose-dominant proposal, identify the required flexibility and the installed protection and access. A locally available length of hose is only one input to a finished assembly. The exact fittings, supplied assembly identity and machine-specific acceptance remain part of the replacement purchase.

The [Parker Safety Guide, sections 2.8–2.13](https://www.parker.com/content/dam/Parker-com/Literature/Parflex/Parker-Safety-Guide.pdf), treats routing, environment, mechanical loads, component movement and future inspection and replacement as selection considerations. Those are procurement requirements to assign to the actual designer. They are not dimensions or assembly instructions for the buyer to invent.

## An eight-input routing purchase record

Complete one record for the offered configuration. Retain the drawing revision beside each answer, especially when the former and lift come from different providers.

| Input to complete | Actual datum or record | Owner | Purchase decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Complete hydraulic duty | Lift/former identities; functions and project sequence ___ | Machine provider and fleet engineer | Holds both offers to the same work |
| Fixed and moving interfaces | Numbered interfaces, route revision and relevant machine states ___ | Machine designer | Separates fixed runs from necessary flexibility |
| Vibration and exposure | Actual duty, surroundings and accepted configuration reference ___ | Fleet lead and machine designer | Defines routing and protection requirements |
| Supports and guards | Issued mounting, protection and access drawing ___ | Machine provider | Completes the installed package |
| Service accessibility | Documented service state and accessible item list ___ | Maintenance manager | Rejects an impractical maintenance layout |
| Destination replacements | Assembly identities and actual compatible supply offers ___ | Parts buyer | Prices supported tube and hose replacements |
| Traceable handover | As-built route index, parts records and change owner ___ | Supply coordinator | Connects the delivered machine to its spares |
| Ownership total | Common period; configuration, care and replacement resources ___ | Procurement and finance | Ranks eligible complete packages |

A tidy drawing is useful only if it describes the equipment being purchased. Have the provider reconcile revised guards, a relocated power unit or a changed former against the same record. These changes can alter service access even when the machine's working duty is unchanged.

## Make destination support a priced resource

Ask the fleet manager to trace one replacement request from the machine record to a real source. For a formed tube, identify who supplies the correct finished assembly and what stock the owner must hold. For a hose assembly, identify its accepted complete specification and the source actually able to supply it. Keep the provider's instructions and competent maintenance responsibility with both routes.

[STAUFF's hydraulic-noise discussion](https://stauff.com/en/blog/applications/reducing-noise-and-vibrations---stauff-sets-new-standards) treats the complete line system and its supports together. A neat fixed route still needs its vibration and support arrangement; flexibility still needs controlled routing. Compare the actual accepted design rather than awarding a premium for appearance alone.

![A red articulated lifting structure with visible cylinders supports an enclosed upper assembly beside a building under construction.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-crawler-roof-side.webp)
*Tie service-access drawings to the same complete equipment identity and documented machine states.*

Use a common ownership period and the same required duty. Include the actual configuration price, specified care, identified spare assemblies, competent attendance and any extra destination service resources. Charge both routes for work they genuinely require. Choose the lower complete supported total when both packages qualify; exclude a cheaper route whose required support cannot be provided.

## Two hypothetical purchases that omit the real layout

**Mistake (hypothetical): metal tube is requested across a moving interface.** A buyer sees orderly tubes on a fixed installation and requests the same appearance throughout its forming lift. The request ignores an articulated relationship. Correct it by returning the actual machine states to the designer, retaining the accepted flexible transitions and comparing the complete offered layouts.

**Mistake (hypothetical): a nearby hose shop becomes assumed fleet support.** An importer accepts a lower package price because local hose is stocked, but the local offer covers bulk hose while the required finished assemblies use unidentified end fittings. Obtain the accepted complete-assembly identities and an actual supply offer. Price the guards, service access and competent work before treating nearby stock as a saving.

## Eight attachments for the forming-lift RFQ

1. **Equipment scope:** crawler or vehicle-mounted lift, included former and destination.
2. **Former interface:** complete installed mass, included assemblies, mounting, profile, feed and utility documents.
3. **Work positions:** outlet elevations from supporting-ground datum and roof receiver/run-out drawing.
4. **Site route:** ground/support records, setup areas, travel constraints and transport boundary.
5. **Hydraulic duty:** named circuit functions, project sequence and environmental exposure.
6. **Routing alternatives:** provider-issued fixed/moving routes, protection and service-access scope.
7. **Ownership resources:** accepted spare assemblies, actual sources and maintenance responsibilities.
8. **Complete quote:** factory handover, common ownership period and itemised supported totals.
