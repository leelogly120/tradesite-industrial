**Buy an air-freight route when an identified, applicable part is available, the carrier accepts the packed cargo, and the earlier complete recovery justifies the additional transport cost. Use planned ocean replenishment when the required stock date allows its accepted whole route and no current repair depends on earlier receipt.** If diagnosis, part availability or qualified fitting controls recovery, resolve that constraint before paying for a faster main transport leg.

This decision starts after the responsible equipment provider identifies the item. The [critical-spares stock guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-critical-spares-stock-decision/) decides what to hold. Here the buyer selects transport for an actual order: an urgent replacement, replenishment of stock already used, or an initial item scheduled ahead of maintenance.

## Buy time at the real bottleneck

Write a dated recovery chain: diagnosis, approved part identity, supplier availability, packing, carrier acceptance, origin processing, main carriage, destination release, final delivery, competent fitting and return-to-service checks. Obtain the actual providers' responses for each stage. Compare air and ocean routes against that complete chain.

[DHL's heavy-equipment shipping guidance](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-gb/logistics-advice/essential-guides/heavy-equipment-shipping) discusses air services for time-sensitive components and ocean routes for larger machinery with more time available. Use that distinction to request real offers. It supplies no delivery promise for an ARCLIFT part, and a generic air-transit figure is not a ready-to-fit date at your site.

Separate the cost of a stopped duty from the machine's booked revenue. The operations lead should establish which contracted work is actually lost, which can move to another machine and which costs remain unavoidable. The finance lead compares that supported difference with the complete incremental cost of the air route over the accepted ocean route. Do not multiply an invented failure duration by a headline daily hire rate.

The visible rolls and adjustments show the value of precise identity. Ask the equipment provider to match the requested item to the actual machine and current parts record. Use photographs to locate a component alongside the provider's identifiers; a photograph alone is an inadequate basis for an urgent substitution.

## Air acceptance is an item-specific gate

Give the forwarder the packed dimensions, gross mass, contents and any relevant safety-data or dangerous-goods information. Batteries, fluids and other regulated contents need the carrier's actual response. The provider packing the part must state what is present. Do not assume a small package is eligible for every air service.

Compare the full destination service as well. A part reaching an airport still needs import release and delivery to the named service location. Identify the consignee, broker, receiving hours and people authorised to accept it. Obtain an ocean alternative on the same declared item and destination rather than comparing an airport quote with a site-delivered sea quotation.

## Keep emergency freight separate from the normal stock plan

An emergency order should not automatically redefine every replenishment as air freight. After an applicable stock item is used, the buyer can set a new required-stock date from actual workload and the accepted replenishment proposal. Transport routine replacements by the service that meets that date at a justified total cost.

Equally, an ocean restocking plan is unsuitable when its actual ready-to-use date misses a committed service window and the business has no alternative. Compare an accepted air route, temporary replacement equipment or a revised project allocation. Choose the complete feasible recovery, not the mode with the lowest freight line.

The separate workshop components show why receipt needs an allocation. Mark an arrived component against its intended repair or stock location. Obtain the part provider's storage conditions and shelf-life requirements where applicable. An unopened urgent package placed in general storage does not complete the intended equipment recovery.

## Two hypothetical expedited-order mistakes

**Air freight advances the airport arrival but not the repair.** A buyer pays for an earlier flight while the competent fitter and required equipment checks are unavailable until the following service slot. Compare the complete recovery dates before authorising the premium. Accelerate the stage that actually controls recovery.

**An emergency part is ordered by appearance.** A familiar housing is sent rapidly, but it belongs to a different machine revision. The stopped equipment gains no availability. Have the responsible provider approve the exact item before dispatch and reconcile the received identity before fitting.

## Eight attachments for the part and transport enquiry

1. Installed equipment identity and current assembly revision.
2. Responsible provider's applicable part designation and quantity.
3. Diagnosed service requirement and approved repair resource.
4. Supplier availability and packing-ready response for the actual item.
5. Packed dimensions, mass, contents and relevant cargo declarations.
6. Destination consignee, import specialist, receiving hours and final address.
7. Required fit/stock date with the real service and workload dependencies.
8. Complete air/ocean quotations and the supported cost of changed availability.
