# Fall Protection for Ceiling Work: What Every Contractor Must Know

I've been in the ceiling work industry for over a decade. In that time, I've seen three preventable fall incidents that could have been avoided with better planning. This guide distills those hard lessons into actionable safety practices.

## The Harsh Reality

Fall incidents in ceiling work aren't theoretical. They happen when:
- Rushing to meet deadlines
- Skipping pre-work inspections
- Using wrong equipment
- Inadequate training
- Poor communication

**The statistics are sobering:** Falls account for 33% of construction fatalities. In ceiling work specifically, the risk is elevated because workers often operate at heights for extended periods.

## What Actually Works

### 1. Platform-Based Protection (Best Option)

The safest approach is working from a properly equipped platform rather than relying solely on personal fall arrest systems.

**Why platforms are superior:**
- Guardrails provide continuous protection
- No harness required for most tasks
- Worker can move freely within the platform
- Material handling is safer

**ARC-F series platforms include:**
- 1.1m guardrails with mid-rails
- Toe boards to prevent dropped objects
- Non-slip floor surfaces
- Self-closing gates
- Emergency stop buttons

### 2. Personal Fall Arrest Systems

When platform access isn't possible:

**Essential equipment:**
- Full-body harness (not waist belt!)
- Shock-absorbing lanyard
- Self-retracting lifeline
- 5,000 kg anchor point

**Common mistakes I've seen:**
- Using damaged harnesses
- Improperly adjusted straps
- Wrong lanyard length
- Insufficient anchor points
- No rescue plan

### 3. Tool Tethering

Dropped tools kill. Period.

**Every tool at height must be tethered:**
- Hand tools: Wrist lanyard or tool lanyard
- Power tools: Tool-specific tether
- Materials: Secure before lifting
- Debris: Use drop cloths or debris nets

**Real example:** A 2kg wrench dropped from 15m hits with 300kg of force. That's lethal.

## The 5-Minute Pre-Work Check

Before anyone goes up:

1. **Platform inspection** — guardrails secure, floor clean, controls working
2. **Weather check** — wind speed <10 m/s, no lightning, visibility good
3. **PPE verification** — harness inspected, tools tethered, hard hat on
4. **Communication test** — radio check, hand signals confirmed
5. **Emergency plan review** — everyone knows the rescue procedure

**This takes 5 minutes. It can save a life.**

## Emergency Response

### If Someone Falls

**Immediate actions:**
1. Call emergency services (911/120)
2. Do NOT move the casualty (spinal injury risk)
3. Check breathing and consciousness
4. Control bleeding if visible
5. Keep casualty warm
6. Monitor until help arrives

### Rescue from Height

**If suspended in harness:**
- Suspension trauma can be fatal in 15 minutes
- Rescue must be immediate
- Have rescue equipment on-site
- Practice rescue procedures regularly

## Training Requirements

### Who Needs Training

- All workers who work at height
- Supervisors who oversee height work
- Rescue team members
- Equipment operators

### Training Content

**Initial training (8 hours):**
- Hazard recognition
- Equipment use and inspection
- Emergency procedures
- Hands-on practice

**Annual refresher (4 hours):**
- Update on regulations
- Incident review
- Equipment changes
- Practical exercises

## Documentation

**Keep records of:**
- Training certificates
- Equipment inspections
- Incident reports
- Safety meetings
- Corrective actions

**Why documentation matters:**
- Legal compliance
- Insurance requirements
- Incident investigation
- Continuous improvement
- Worker accountability
