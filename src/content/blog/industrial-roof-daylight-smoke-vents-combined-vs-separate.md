Choose combined daylight and natural-smoke units when one accepted product and layout satisfy both functions, including controls and roof interfaces. Buy separate units when the required smoke positions, daylight distribution or envelope requirements cannot share that arrangement. When both complete packages qualify, choose the lower installed-and-maintained total. First overlay the accepted daylight and smoke designs; reducing the number of roof components is useful only after both outcomes are retained.

This purchase starts after natural smoke exhaust has been selected and useful daylight has been specified. The buyer is deciding whether those functions share roof hardware. The smoke engineer, daylight designer and roof designer retain their separate design responsibilities while procurement reconciles their outputs into one deliverable roof package.

## Two functions need two acceptance records

A glazed opening admits light; an accepted smoke vent supplies its specified smoke-control function in an identified system. Record the daylight criterion at the actual working plane separately from the smoke scheme's required performance and locations. Visible rooflight area should never replace the smoke designer's issued requirement.

[VELUX Commercial's smoke-ventilation range](https://commercial.velux.com/products/smoke-and-natural-ventilation) includes rooflight products incorporating natural smoke and heat exhaust ventilation. Its [UK product overview](https://commercial.velux.co.uk/products/additional-products/smoke-and-natural-ventilation) distinguishes comfort ventilation, smoke products and components integrated into particular rooflight families. That distinction matters when a quote simply calls every opening unit a vent.

Require the offered product identity and evidence for the actual combined functions. A familiar glazing family does not make every opening variant part of the specified smoke system. Procurement should connect each offered unit to both acceptance records before assigning it a combined role.

## Where the two layouts coincide, combine the real scope

Plot the daylight design and natural-smoke scheme on the same roof grid. Add structural supports, panel seams, rooftop plant and other issued exclusion zones. Mark positions where a specific combined product meets both briefs with an accepted roof detail. Buy combined units there; retain separate daylight or smoke units at the remaining required positions. The project can contain both arrangements without compromising either design.

For the combined offer, price the complete product configuration, supporting opening, weathering, operating components and required completion work. Identify who supplies each interface and who delivers the accepted operating result. One delivered rooflight package can still depend on separately purchased electrical, control or roofing work.

Keep the selected configuration intact during commercial comparison. A cheaper lid, actuator or base detail changes the proposed product. Refer that change through the responsible acceptance before preserving the combined-function credit in the price schedule.

Compare completion and care at those actual positions, rather than demanding one product architecture across the whole roof. Obtain the actual care and replacement tasks for each offered unit's glazing, operating components and associated system. Price the internal and external access duties retained by the selected arrangement, including the objects that a handling provider must present or remove.

## Where the functions diverge, separate them deliberately

Daylight distribution follows the accepted occupied layout. Smoke positions follow the accepted fire scheme. When those maps do not coincide, separate specialist units can preserve both outcomes without forcing one function into the other's preferred location. The complete comparison then includes both supplied populations and their roof interfaces.

[Colt's Apollo product information](https://www.coltinfo.ae/apollo.html) illustrates a useful product distinction: the maker identifies light-transmitting and non-light-transmitting lid alternatives within a natural-vent category. Light admission therefore belongs to the selected configuration, rather than following automatically from the word ventilator.

An opaque vent arrangement and separately located daylight units need their own accepted envelope details. Have the responsible designer address thermal, solar-control, appearance and weathering requirements for the actual products. Use those issued requirements to price complete alternatives instead of assuming a generic advantage for either a combined or separate layout.

## An overlap-to-order worksheet

Complete this schedule with the actual design and provider records. It should produce combined positions, separate positions and one coordinated metal-panel schedule.

| Decision input and datum | Fillable project record | Responsible owner | Order consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Useful daylight | Working-plane height, occupied layout and analysis revision ___ | Daylight designer | Fixes the required light result |
| Natural smoke exhaust | Zone plan and performance-schedule revision ___ | Fire engineer | Fixes the separate smoke result |
| Offered combined product | Product/configuration ID and evidence references ___ | Rooflight/smoke provider | Admits a dual-function purchase |
| Shared or separate locations | Opening IDs, coordinates and coordinated drawing revision ___ | Design coordinator | Assigns each position to combined or separate supply |
| Roof assembly | Support, seam, base and weathering detail revisions ___ | Roof and structural designers | Completes construction scope |
| Envelope constraints | Thermal, solar-control and appearance records ___ | Envelope designer | Retains the accepted configuration |
| Completion and care | Controls, checks, replacement objects and service routes ___ | Integrator and facility manager | Prices handback and ownership |
| Metal-production release | Section/material revision, piece quantities/lengths and receiver datum ___ | Roof detailer and producer | Releases only the coordinated metal-forming scope |

Compare total installed roof packages over the same owner-approved period. Include the actual unit supply, supporting construction, controls, specialist installation, personnel access and planned care. Count a shared item once and retain every separate dependency. A lower product count alone does not determine the lower completed-project cost.

## Two hypothetical combined-function mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): translucent area becomes smoke capacity.** A buyer releases ordinary opening rooflights for the warehouse's smoke zones using visible glazing area as the quantity. No offered configuration supplies the accepted smoke duty. Stop that release; obtain the named dual-function configuration and its system acceptance, or restore separate specialist vents.

**Mistake (hypothetical): all daylight follows the smoke zone.** The combined units light the storage aisle while the accepted daylight brief covers the packing benches elsewhere. A lower component count sacrifices the purchased outcome. Retain separate functions at the non-overlapping positions and revise their opening IDs and surrounding sheet lengths before metal production.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the metal-forming scope

1. Coordinated accepted opening layout and surrounding metal-panel piece marks.
2. Released panel-section drawing and profile revision.
3. Metal grade, gauge, coating and required sheet width.
4. Panel quantities, length mix and production release dates.
5. Coil-to-discharge layout and handling responsibilities.
6. Forming/lift footprint, power supply and receiver heights from stated datum.
7. Proposed sample, dimensional and finished-surface acceptance record.
8. Destination, delivery configuration and separately supplied specialist-system boundaries.
