A pitched roof is not described adequately by its highest point. The buyer needs to explain **which point on the sloping workface must relate to the former's outlet**. A roll-forming lift carries a compatible roof-panel roll former toward the intended receiving level. Neither that architecture nor a height label establishes that the former tilts to the roof angle or that an emerging panel follows the slope.

This guide prepares two roof sections and a list of named receiving relationships. The [height-datum guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-height-specification-datum/) reads equipment height labels; the task here is describing the sloping roof requirement before requesting a configuration response. It is a buyer brief, not a roof or panel-support design.

## Describe the sloping task before the machine

### Name the first receiving location

Identify the point or area where the panel is intended to enter the project's receiving arrangement. Its position may be near an eave or elsewhere on the roof; do not select it from a convenient drawing height. Give it a grid location and associate it with the intended panel direction. The final installed position of a panel can differ from that first receiver. Keeping those roles distinct prevents a ridge dimension from becoming a supposed outlet requirement without an explanation of the intervening relationship.

### Record slope as project geometry

Provide the relevant roof section and the project definition of pitch or slope, with units and direction clear. Use the actual drawing reference rather than estimating the angle from a photograph. Where several roof planes exist, identify which one belongs to each receiver. The roof slope describes the building task. It is not a specification for machine articulation, a permissible panel angle or a statement about how the panel should be supported between the former and the workface.

### Keep the panel task beside its location

State the panel profile and material requirement, intended length schedule and orientation for the named roof area. The supplier needs to know which product task the outlet relationship represents. Do not assume that two visually similar roof planes use identical panels or receiving arrangements. If these inputs remain provisional, identify the particular uncertainty. A clearly bounded preliminary enquiry can still ask for the information needed next, without treating an unselected panel or receiver as a confirmed equipment requirement.

## Put roof and outlet references on matching sections

### Connect the ground reference to the roof view

Show the candidate ground position and receiving location in the same section, tied to the common project datum. Ask the equipment party to identify its own support and outlet references on the proposed arrangement. A vertical dimension measured from another ground position may not answer this section. Preserve the distinction between the machine-supporting platform and the cutter or outlet above it. The resulting drawing pair should make clear which relationship comes from the site and which still needs a supplier response.

### Locate the intended output line without inventing its shape

Mark the requested connection between outlet and receiver as a question for review. Do not draw the emerging panel bending into the roof plane or assume a straight, unsupported line is acceptable. The actual arrangement depends on the panel and external support scope. The [panel handover-zone guide](/blog/elevated-roll-forming-roof-panel-handover-zones/) develops those separate responsibilities. Here the section supplies the relevant endpoints and roof geometry, so a supplier cannot mistake the roof pitch annotation for an already approved production or transfer arrangement.

### Ask about machine orientation explicitly

If the proposed geometry appears to require a particular former orientation, ask the provider to state what its actual configuration does and which information supports that answer. Avoid wording such as set the former to match the roof. That suggests a capability and an operating instruction not established by the enquiry. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/roofwork.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE roof-work guidance</a> provides context about access and roof hazards, but does not establish a forming-machine tilt function, panel path or arrangement for this roof.

## Compare two receiving-point assumptions

### Hold the roof plane and panel constant

Consider a **hypothetical pitched-roof enquiry**. Section A identifies a receiver near the eave; Section B identifies a receiver farther along the same sloping roof plane. The panel requirement remains unchanged. The two receiving points therefore have different locations and may require different outlet relationships, even though both drawings show the same roof pitch and ridge. The example does not establish that either receiver is suitable. Its purpose is to reveal that a shared roof-height label can conceal two distinct equipment-interface questions.

### Request the different missing answer in each section

In A, the equipment proposal may identify the outlet level but omit the horizontal relationship to the eave-side receiver. In B, the project may have marked a point on the slope without defining the external receiving arrangement. A needs a supplier geometry response; B needs the roof-side task developed before the equipment relationship can be assessed. Asking both bidders for a greater height figure would leave those different gaps intact. Treat a changed receiver as a changed project assumption, rather than a harmless adjustment to a drawing.

### Interpret a response that names no tilt function

A supplier can answer with a proposed outlet relationship without offering a slope-following former. The buyer should read the actual response, its panel-support boundary and any remaining conditions. If it does not describe a required orientation, keep that question open. The trade-off is between preserving a convenient roof-pitch shorthand and obtaining a more precise, potentially narrower equipment proposal. Precision may reveal an unsuitable concept, which is useful information before buying. It does not oblige the project to adopt an alternative that has not been assessed.

| Section detail | A useful answer establishes | It does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eave-side receiver | The named location and its required relationship to the outlet | A complete external support or installation method |
| Receiver farther along the slope | A different location on the same identified roof plane | That one eave-based configuration automatically covers it |
| Pitch annotation | The direction and slope definition of the project roof | A machine tilt setting or articulation capability |
| Outlet and support datums | Which points the equipment proposal measures | A conversion from personnel working height |
| A revised proposed arrangement | The specific configuration now offered for review | Approval of earlier assumptions that the revision has changed |

## Ask a supplier to answer the actual relationship

### Send two sections with one common task statement

Ask for a response identifying the actual proposed former, lifting arrangement and outlet point against each section. State whether the project is comparing receiving locations or requesting coverage of both. Those are different enquiries. The [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) can be discussed using these inputs without converting a building angle into an equipment specification. A useful reply explains the configuration basis and the particular project information still needed; it need not pretend that a whole roof method can be established from two sketches.

- Identify the roof plane, slope direction and source drawing for each receiving location.
- Connect outlet height and ground reference to the corresponding section rather than the building's maximum height.
- State the panel profile, material requirement and intended output direction used for the comparison.
- Mark the candidate base position and access route that locate the equipment below the section.
- Identify which receiver is required and which is an alternative still under consideration at the destination.
- Ask for the actual machine orientation and the external support boundary, with missing information kept explicit.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize a slope description that cannot select equipment

A proposal may not fit the task if it relies on a presumed tilt function, an undefined receiver or a panel path that no responsible party has described. Roof pitch alone does not close any of those gaps. A new receiver or another base position may deserve investigation, but it changes the comparison. This article calculates no angle, panel deflection, load or support spacing, and gives no instruction for setting the former. The relevant technical and roof-work parties must assess the actual configuration and receiving concept.

## FAQ

#### Should the ridge height be used throughout the enquiry?

Only where the ridge is actually the relevant identified point, which needs to be stated. Other receivers on the roof plane have their own locations and elevations. Describe the actual outlet-to-receiver relationship instead of applying the largest building dimension everywhere. This preserves the task the supplier needs to answer and avoids implying that height alone establishes usable geometry.

#### Does a pitched roof require a tilting former?

That conclusion is not established by the roof's appearance or this guide. State the actual receiving requirement and ask the supplier what configuration it proposes. Keep its answer separate from the roof slope annotation. Any claimed orientation capability needs attributable equipment information and project review, rather than an instruction inferred by the buyer from an inclined roof line.

#### Can one section cover both roof slopes?

It can show both planes, but the receiving locations and proposed equipment relationships must remain individually recognizable. Opposing slopes can have different receivers, panel directions or ground approaches. A shared section is a presentation choice, not proof of common equipment coverage. Ask which named tasks the proposal actually addresses and retain a distinct response where the physical relationship changes.

#### Is the output line a panel-support design?

No. In this buyer brief it identifies the relationship that requires assessment. The panel system, actual equipment and responsible external-support and receiving parties must establish the applicable arrangement. A line drawn from outlet to roof cannot specify allowable panel span, contact locations or a handling method, even if it appears to match the roof plane neatly.
