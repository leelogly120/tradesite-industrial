A roof-forming lift enquiry needs to say **where the forming outlet must be, not just how high the roof is**. The system carries a compatible roof-panel roll former toward the intended receiving level. Its base, lifting structure, machine support and cutter occupy different positions. A distance from a truck bumper to a wall cannot stand in for the horizontal relationship between that machine outlet and the receiver.

This guide turns a setback question into a paired plan-and-section request. The result is a comparison of differently labelled offer dimensions at one unchanged site position, with the missing geometry identified. The [broader roof-zone survey](/blog/roof-zone-survey-roll-forming-lift-selection/) covers the whole site brief; here the decision concerns one base-to-outlet relationship.

## Give setback two named endpoints

### Choose a recognizable base reference

Start by asking what the equipment proposal calls its base reference. A vehicle outline, crawler centre, support location and lifting-structure reference are different things. Label the point or line used by the supplier rather than selecting whichever edge is convenient to measure on site. On the building drawing, connect that reference to a fixed grid or surveyed feature. This makes later comparisons repeatable even when one bidder illustrates a truck and another illustrates a crawler.

### Locate the cutter rather than the platform edge

The machine-supporting platform does not itself identify where a panel leaves the former. Ask for the cutter or outlet reference relative to the proposed support and machine orientation. If the line is existing equipment, its actual arrangement matters; an outline of a different former cannot establish that relationship. The [mounting-interface review](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) addresses the machine connection. For setback, carry its confirmed outlet point into the site drawing without assuming that the frame and cutter share an endpoint.

### State which roof feature is the destination

A roof edge may mean a structural grid, fascia, gutter, parapet face or first receiving location. Name the feature that the requested offset actually reaches. Record any second feature separately. A dimension ending at the wall can be accurate yet leave the outlet-to-receiver gap unspecified when the roof projects beyond it. The buyer's useful first sentence is therefore a relationship: proposed base reference to identified outlet, then outlet to named receiver. It is a geometric requirement, not a claim that a selected machine can achieve it.

## Connect the plan to a roof-edge section

### Preserve along-edge and across-edge directions

A single setback dimension usually describes only one direction. Show where the candidate base sits along the building as well as its distance across the roof edge. Place the intended panel direction on the same plan. An outlet can have the desired perpendicular offset while facing a different bay or meeting a column farther along the facade. Keep the full proposed equipment outline separate from the outlet point so a reviewer can examine both the required position and the space occupied around it.

### Use the same location in section

Draw the section through the actual proposed outlet and receiver, with its location marked on the plan. Include the relevant ground level and the roof-side feature that defines the offset. A typical section taken elsewhere may omit a projecting canopy or a different ground elevation at the candidate position. Name the outlet height reference explicitly. Platform height and personnel working height answer other questions; neither supplies the missing horizontal geometry or identifies the point where the panel is intended to emerge.

### Keep the intervening space visible

Locate the eave, gutter, parapet and other features within the proposed relationship. Show observed boundaries and areas not measured, rather than adding an assumed clearance band. The <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/roofwork.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE roof-work guidance</a> discusses roof edges, openings and access in Great Britain. That provides context for keeping the receiving-side problem visible; it does not define a forming-lift clearance, panel support or safe route across the edge. Those remain project-specific matters for the responsible equipment and roof-work parties.

## Normalize unlike offer references at one site position

### Hold the site position and receiver constant

Consider an **explicitly hypothetical comparison**, not an ARCLIFT project. The site drawing identifies one candidate position on grid G, one roof receiver R and one matching section. Offer A labels setback from a vehicle bumper to the facade. Offer B labels it from a lifting-structure reference to the roof edge. Both use the word setback, but neither label by itself locates the forming outlet against R. The task and site position remain unchanged throughout this example.

### Identify the missing reference chain in each offer

For A, ask how its bumper reference relates to the equipment base and actual outlet in the proposed arrangement. For B, ask how its lifting reference relates to the same outlet, and which roof-edge feature its dimension reaches. Do not convert either label with a guessed body length or a photograph. A may have an identified bumper-to-facade dimension while B has an identified lifting-reference-to-edge dimension, yet both can still lack the relationship the roof task actually needs.

### Return a common relationship without inventing a conversion

Ask each provider to locate its own equipment reference and outlet on the unchanged G/R drawing pair. The trade-off is between accepting an apparently comparable label and obtaining an attributable geometric response. The result is not a new calculated setback: it is a drawing comparison showing which reference chains are complete and which link remains missing. If B provides the outlet-to-R relationship while A returns the same bumper label, B has answered that particular geometry question more clearly; this does not select or approve B's equipment.

| Offer observation | Why the figures remain unlike | Specific clarification against the unchanged site drawing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A starts at a bumper | A vehicle edge is not yet the named equipment base | Locate bumper and actual base reference against grid G |
| A ends at the facade | The facade is not automatically receiver R | Identify the facade-to-R relationship on the site drawing |
| B starts at a lifting reference | Its relationship to the actual outlet is not shown | Locate that reference and outlet for the proposed configuration |
| B ends at a roof-edge label | The label may describe another feature than R | Name the actual edge feature and keep R separately visible |
| One response identifies only the supporting platform | The former outlet remains a different point | Show the actual cutter/outlet relationship rather than substituting platform edge |
| Both responses use the same word setback | Shared wording does not create shared endpoints | Return the same G/R plan and section with attributable equipment references |

## Turn the comparison into a useful enquiry

### Send the smallest complete drawing pair

A preliminary drawing pair need not imitate an equipment designer's finished drawing. It must make the required relationship legible: unchanged site position, each offer's named references, receiver, relevant edges and unresolved measurements. Label dimensions as survey observations, design requirements or supplier information. A photograph with arrows can help explain location, but it cannot supply an unseen distance. Send the underlying plan or section reference with the image so the equipment party can identify which relationship its response is expected to address.

- Name the site grid and fixed feature locating the unchanged candidate site position.
- Ask the supplier to identify its base reference and show its relationship to that grid.
- Mark the actual forming-machine outlet separately from the supporting platform outline.
- Keep the same roof receiver, panel material requirement and intended panel direction for both offer responses.
- Connect ground level, roof feature and outlet height to the matching section location and access route.
- Identify which reference link is missing in each offer and request it without a guessed dimension conversion.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize when setback is the wrong unresolved question

A setback proposal may not fit the intended task while its endpoints remain undefined. If the machine itself has not been identified, its outlet reference may still depend on the forming-line choice. If the roof-side receiver has no defined location, an equipment setback cannot close the production layout. Those are upstream gaps, and a larger catalogue height does not resolve them. Likewise, available space does not establish ground suitability. Use the [ground-support review](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-ground-support-review/) for the separate support question. This article supplies no reach curve, support spacing, operating movement or minimum clearance for either position.

## FAQ

#### Is setback measured from the truck body?

Only if the particular comparison explicitly uses that reference and connects it to the relevant equipment points. A body edge is not automatically the base reference used by the lifting-equipment drawing. Ask the supplier to identify its convention and show how the outlet is located relative to it. Otherwise two apparently equal setback values may describe different relationships.

#### Does more lifting height resolve a larger horizontal gap?

That cannot be inferred from a height label. The required outlet point has a location and an equipment state, and the proposed configuration must be assessed against both. Ask for the arrangement corresponding to your candidate base position. This guide makes no relationship between advertised height and horizontal capability, and does not select a machine from those labels.

#### Can a photograph provide a preliminary setback?

It can identify visible objects and help explain where a question arises. Perspective, unknown dimensions and hidden features prevent it from establishing the actual proposed relationship. Tie the photograph to a plan location and provide relevant surveyed or required dimensions separately. A reference machine in another scene also cannot establish your proposed machine's base-to-outlet geometry.

#### Must the closer position be preferred?

No. A shorter offset can coexist with an unavailable lane, a different support question or an unresolved receiving arrangement. Compare positions against the same task and obtain the missing site and equipment responses. The purpose of the paired drawings is to expose those differences before choosing, rather than treating distance alone as a selection rule.
