**Retain an identified ceiling grid when the responsible ceiling specialist accepts its condition and compatibility with the replacement panels, services and required performance. Purchase a complete replacement system where that acceptance is absent or the required layout changes beyond the retained system's accepted scope. Then buy access against the actual survey, removal, installation and inspection tasks in each zone.** A mixed package is sensible when some zones qualify for retention and others require renewal.

New tiles alone and a complete suspended ceiling are different purchases. The distinction changes the material order, specialist work and platform occupation. Counting the whole room as tile replacement before evaluating its grid produces an unreliable equipment brief.

## Recover the identity of what the project hopes to keep

Obtain the existing ceiling records and a specialist condition survey. Identify the system, panel module, edge arrangement, suspension components and relevant previous alterations. Record missing identity or condition information as a survey task with its own access requirement. Do not put unassessed areas into the retained quantity simply to complete a tender column.

[Kanopi by Armstrong's ceiling-selection guide](https://kanopibyarmstrong.com/pages/how-to-choose-ceiling-tiles) distinguishes a new suspended ceiling from replacement tiles in an existing grid. It states that a replacement tile's edge profile needs to fit that grid. Physical fit is therefore a necessary purchasing input; the appointed specialist must also decide whether the retained assembly satisfies the project's complete requirements.

The buyer should receive a zone schedule with a clear outcome: retain the accepted system, retain with specified repairs, or replace. Where opening-up is required before that outcome, purchase the investigation separately or include a precisely bounded investigation stage. A full-area commitment is premature before the retained scope is defined.

## Three different construction orders sit behind one new ceiling

**Panel renewal on an accepted retained grid.** The contract removes the identified old panels and installs compatible replacements. Define any specialist repairs, service changes and inspection requirements separately. Price the access tasks that actually remain rather than importing a complete new-grid installation allowance.

**Selective renewal.** Some zones retain the system, while others receive specified repairs or complete replacement. Give each zone its own material references and released scope. The programme must allow the relevant inspections before the next work hides those locations.

**Complete suspension and panel replacement.** The package supplies the specified new system, including its supports, perimeter and service interfaces. The installer needs access for those tasks as well as panel placement. Coordinate the removal and new installation stages with the building team before reserving equipment through the whole interval.

## Issue a retained-and-new scope schedule

Fill the schedule by room or construction zone. The ceiling specialist owns the retention decision; the buyer uses the resulting quantities and tasks to compare supply and access offers.

| Decision field | Buyer entry | Source or measurement datum | Owner and effect on the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing identity | System and component references: ___ | Existing records plus identified field locations | Ceiling specialist; establishes what is actually proposed for reuse |
| Retention outcome | Retain, specified repair or replace by zone: ___ | Signed survey tied to the room/grid reference | Responsible technical lead; fixes material and work quantities |
| Panel compatibility | Module, edge and specified replacement reference: ___ | Measured panel/grid features and selected product documents | Ceiling supplier; determines the actual panel order |
| Required performance | Accepted assembly and relevant requirements: ___ | Project specification and specialist decision | Designer; prevents physical fit replacing system acceptance |
| Changed services | Lights, diffusers, access panels and revised locations: ___ | Coordinated reflected ceiling plan | MEP coordinator; identifies retained-grid conflicts and extra tasks |
| Task positions | Survey, support, panel and inspection faces: ___ | Levels above the local supporting floor and named building datum | Surveyor and installer; defines equipment work positions |
| Materials on the deck | New and removed objects present together: ___ | Component dimensions, masses and planned distribution | Installer and access provider; defines the task-load proposal |
| Access period | Released stages, waiting interval and return visits: ___ | Main contractor's accepted programme | Equipment buyer; compares retention and separate mobilisation costs |

An acceptable retained grid does not automatically mean the old service layout remains acceptable. Carry the actual new ceiling and MEP drawings into the retention review. If the revised arrangement requires a different technical outcome, update the construction scope before changing the equipment booking.

## Platform capacity follows the stage, not the room area

During investigation, the work can be limited to selected survey locations. During panel removal, the crew handles old material and packaging or collection equipment. During new support installation, the required faces and tools differ again. Give the supplier a separate task-load description for every stage the proposed platform must serve.

For repeated work across a cleared hall, compare a shared large deck against those complete tasks. For isolated repairs or separated rooms, compare localized access and its full mobilisation scope. A broad deck is a candidate when its working arrangement and route serve the actual zones; floor area alone does not settle the purchase.

## Two hypothetical replacement contracts with missing work

**Failure case: a matching module is treated as complete compatibility.** A buyer orders tiles from their face dimensions alone. The selected edge detail and required ceiling assembly differ from the retained system. Have the ceiling specialist and selected product supplier release the actual compatible replacement before purchase; replace the system where the required retention acceptance cannot be established.

**Failure case: tile-only access is booked before the survey outcome.** Another buyer hires a platform for panel exchange, then receives a survey requiring suspension renewal in several zones. The order omits support installation and its inspection visits. Finish the zone decision first or purchase a bounded investigation stage, then obtain the revised installation and access package.
