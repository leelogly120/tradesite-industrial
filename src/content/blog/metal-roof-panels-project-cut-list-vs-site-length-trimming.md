Release a cut-to-length kit for measured roof areas where the final geometry, panel marks and permitted cutting process are settled. Buy overlength or standard-length pieces for the specific areas whose final dimensions will arrive later, provided the roof system accepts field trimming and the installer has priced the complete finishing task.

## Identify where each final dimension becomes available

Mark the roof plan with the physical points that determine the finished dimensions. Where drawings fix a grid dimension but construction still determines the abutment, ridge or verge, hold that final dimension. Release an adjacent bay earlier if its actual reference points are complete and measured. Treat these as separate production groups. The decision concerns where to commit the final length and pay for the consequence of an error. The machine's ability to follow a variable cut list is only one input.

## Ask for a marked schedule that the installer can receive

Give each panel group an order mark, measured length, permitted end condition and release date. Enter who turns a field measurement into a purchase instruction. On a phased project two lengths can be correct on different drawing revisions. A fabricator needs one accepted schedule for the released phase and a clear hold on the rest. An installer receiving standard blanks needs a location to measure, process, segregate and protect them before delivery to the workface. Include that location in the proposed route.

| Enter reference points, measured run and inspection date | Site survey lead | Release exact lengths only for physically settled areas. |

| Enter maximum permitted shipping length and unloading position | Logistics lead | Check that the chosen stock lengths reach the crew. |
| Enter change cutoff, remake price and earliest replacement slot | Fabricator | Value the exposure of early final-length commitment. |

Quote the same installed area in both packages. For a cut kit include surveying, detailing, checking, shop cutting, marking, packing in erection order and replacement of incorrect pieces. For a site route include purchased stock length, offcuts after permitted reuse, handling, cutting equipment, labour, finishing and waste removal. An offcut has value only when another released mark can use it with the correct orientation, finish and useful length. Credit actual reallocation to the next mark once. Keep spare stock for possible damage separate from normal cutting loss.

## Buy flexibility only where dimensions remain exposed

For repetitive completed bays, a correct marked kit can eliminate a second measuring and sorting operation. For an unfinished abutment, delaying the final dimension can preserve the installation date even when a shop replacement would arrive too late. Check whether a later supplier cut release can serve the same need more economically. Choose the later shop release if its confirmed return slot and second delivery cost less than the complete field station and meet the installation date. Distinguish that genuinely available slot from a verbal expectation of quick replacement.

**Mistake: The kit matches an obsolete drawing.** An estimator releases every bay from an early roof plan before the end wall is installed. The end condition changes on a short row and the precut pieces are too short. Restrict the first release to measured bays, name the person authorized to release the end row and attach the replacement terms to the purchase order.

**Mistake: Every angle is charged as an ordinary cross-cut.** The buyer orders exact panel lengths, expecting the valley to arrive finished. The supplier has priced square ends and the installer has allowed only for fixing panels. Compare explicit shop-shaped pieces with permitted field finishing and include labour, access and disposal before selecting the award.

A mixed schedule often gives the clearest purchase. Freeze long, repeated measured runs; retain a separately priced local finishing allowance for genuinely late pieces; give the customer one dated packing list showing both. Compare the line investment against the volume of panels the shop will actually form, using saleable installed lengths and the full coil consumed. This keeps an inexpensive standard blank from looking cheap merely because the downstream contractor inherited the work.

## Eight inputs for a cut-kit or field-trim RFQ

1. Attach separate measured and unreleased roof areas.
2. Give panel profile, material, finish, orientation and quantity.
3. List final dimensions, reference points and responsible measurer.
4. Identify square cuts, angled cuts and end treatment for every group.
5. State permitted site operations, finishing and handling requirements.
6. Show shipping length, unloading and the site processing space.
7. Provide release dates, changes, spare stock and replacement terms.
8. Request comparable finished and installed quantities with waste allocated.
