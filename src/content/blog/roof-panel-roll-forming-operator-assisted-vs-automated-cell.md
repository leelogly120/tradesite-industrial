Buy an integrated automated cell when its demonstrated input-to-output route serves the actual order mix and listed exceptions, competent support is available, and the same-order cost comparison favours that complete cell. Choose operator-assisted production when the proposed automation cannot serve those orders or its full operating cost exceeds the accepted assisted route. Compare accepted output and assigned tasks, rather than the former's motion alone.

Both routes require competent people and a documented supplied configuration. Automation changes which tasks those people perform. It does not eliminate material replenishment, inspection, planned maintenance, exception handling or the employer's machinery responsibilities.

## Describe the order route before counting people

Follow one representative order from its released job data through material presentation, forming, cutting, inspection, receiving, packing and the agreed output handover. Record which activities are automated, which use people and which depend on separately supplied equipment or services.

Choose the endpoint deliberately. A demonstration ending with an unlabelled stack is not equivalent to an offer ending with checked, labelled and packed order groups. Keep the full route visible when comparing staffing and elapsed time.

## Give routine work and exceptions equal attention

List the routine product mix: section revisions, materials, lengths, quantities and delivery groups. Then identify the exceptions that actually occur in the order book, such as a changed material lot, a short urgent order, a rejected piece or a revised packing group. Obtain the provider's documented scope for handling those events under the proposed arrangement.

An automated cell earns its place when the supported routine flow and accepted exception route fit the business. Operator-assisted production is a valid outcome when varied work needs flexibility that the proposed automated route does not provide economically. Do not price unsafe workarounds as flexibility or assume operators can bypass the supplied configuration to cover an omitted task.

Keep the competence requirement explicit. Identify production supervision, quality, material control, maintenance and technical support for both routes. If the proposed automation depends on support that is not available under an actual arrangement, include the cost and lead-in needed to establish it before committing the cell.

The measured trial period should include the tasks the provider has agreed to demonstrate, with those tasks recorded. Keep quoted maximum machine speed separate from accepted order output over that period. Do not turn one selected demonstration into a universal staffing or throughput claim.

## Automate the constrained route, not a disconnected island

When the business already runs a line, use actual interruptions and accepted-output records to identify the constrained part of its route. A receiving or inspection bottleneck does not disappear because the former accelerates. Conversely, buying an automatic output module is not enough if input supply remains the limiting activity.

The [runout-table versus stacker guide](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-runout-table-vs-automatic-stacker/) covers one component decision. This broader comparison asks whether the complete cell, its people and its exception route are ready for the intended level of automation. Keep individual module proposals inside that operating model.

## Two hypothetical automation disappointments

**Failure case: a stacker is counted as a complete staffing reduction.** The quote automates receiving but leaves material replenishment, inspection and packing unchanged. The business removes those duties from its budget anyway. Rebuild the task allocation against the complete accepted order route before comparing operating costs.

**Failure case: the trial excludes the normal order exceptions.** A demonstration uses one long repeated batch while the business mainly serves mixed short orders and urgent replacements. Require representative product and event coverage in the acceptance plan, or choose a cell whose defined assisted route serves that work.

## Request a function-by-function production offer

Attach eight items to the RFQ:

1. Released profiles, materials and representative length/quantity mix.
2. Actual input and accepted-output handover definitions.
3. Routine production sequence and assigned tasks.
4. Normal exception events from the order records.
5. Existing material-handling, inspection and packing resources.
6. Site layout, utilities and equipment interfaces.
7. Competent staffing, maintenance and support arrangements.
