Build a roof-panel roll-forming business when your committed customers buy defined roofing profiles and you can deliver accepted panels in their required order lengths. Build a light-gauge steel-framing business when customers buy identified framing components or assembled frames, and your funded offer includes the design-to-production information, feature tooling and assembly responsibility that those orders require. Without a defined framing customer and that complete delivery chain, keep the first investment on the roof-panel side and buy framing from an established source.

Both businesses start with metal strip. They finish with different customer obligations. A roof-panel order is not a frame drawing, and a collection of formed studs is not an assembled wall ready for the customer's next activity. Decide the sold product before comparing machines.

## Start at the customer's receiving point

[Howick's machine buyer guide](https://www.howickltd.com/resources/roll-forming-machine-buyer-guide/howick-machine-buyer-guide) distinguishes framing supply routes ranging from component production to panelised construction. Its [frames and trusses overview](https://www.howickltd.com/applications/frames-trusses-joists/frames-trusses) provides a manufacturer example of equipment for that separate category. These are framing-business references; they are not capabilities assigned to an ARCLIFT roof-panel former.

Write one accepted delivery on the front of the investment file. On the roofing side, state the panel drawing revision, material, finish, ordered lengths, quantities and receiving location. On the framing side, state whether the customer receives loose identified members, organised frame kits or assembled frames. Name the party supplying the structural design and the production information issued from it.

That last distinction changes the factory. Loose members require a reliable identification and dispatch sequence. Assembled frames add working space, connection operations, any fixtures required by the selected assembly system, and inspection before transport. If the sales plan promises assembled frames while the machinery budget stops at formed lengths, the investment is incomplete.

## A worksheet for the first real order

Fill this with an actual prospective customer's order, not a catalogue's maximum product list.

| Buyer input to complete | Roof-panel route | Framing route | Responsible owner and purchase effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sold output and receiver: ___ | Accepted roof sheets at ___ | Members, kits or assembled frames at ___ | Sales lead fixes the revenue boundary and included work. |
| Controlled design reference: ___ | Profile drawing and roof-system requirements ___ | Design model, member schedule and connection references ___ | Customer's designer issues the basis used for machine evaluation. |
| Information handoff: ___ | Released length and quantity schedule ___ | Member identities, feature locations and assembly sequence ___ | Production planner maps each design revision to an identifiable output. |
| Completion operations: ___ | Cut, receive, protect and bundle ___ | Form, identify, prepare connections and assemble as sold ___ | Operations manager prices the work after forming. |
| First-order layout: ___ | Long-panel discharge and collection envelope ___ | Member storage, assembly bays and frame dispatch envelope ___ | Factory engineer measures both from the same building plan datum. |
| Acceptance and rework: ___ | Panel inspection and rejected-length handling ___ | Component inspection and assembled-frame checks ___ | Quality lead sets the accepted-output denominator for costing. |
| Funded order coverage: ___ | Supported panel orders over ___ | Supported framing orders over ___ | Finance lead compares the complete business against actual demand. |

Do not divide the framing-line price by metres and compare it with a roof-line price per metre. The saleable outputs differ. Cost the accepted order, including bought material, preparation, labour, rejected work, packaging and delivery. Add the fixed costs each business introduces before calculating its required contribution.

## Framing adds an information and assembly business

For a framing proposal, follow one revised opening through the whole order. The designer changes the opening; the production planner issues a new member schedule; the operator produces the correct revision; the assembly team places the matching members; dispatch sends the right package. Identify who withdraws obsolete files and who prevents mixed-revision parts entering the assembly.

Price the actual handoff, including any quoted software, file preparation, training and recurring charges. Specify which of those services the machine supplier includes and which the investor procures elsewhere. Do not describe an unpriced software interface as a completed design service.

Then walk the frame assembly area using the largest planned frame and its connection sequence. Measure the space needed for the quoted assembly method, people, stored members and completed output, including any fixtures that method requires. Assign handling and transport independently from the machine purchase. A framing business that sells assembled output needs those activities even when the forming operation is compact.

## Roofing wins when the business really sells panels

For a roofing distributor expanding into manufacturing, a roof-panel line fits when customers already buy the offered profile and finish and when the investor can serve the order lengths profitably. Make the comparison against continued purchase of finished panels. Include delivery, minimum-order consequences, stock protection and the labour needed to supply small orders.

![A ribbed silver sheet with repeated indentations projects from blue and orange machinery.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-profiled-sheet-machine-outlet.webp)
*The formed sheet at the outlet illustrates the finished-output boundary used in a panel-production brief.*

The photograph supports a discussion of formed-sheet output; the purchase still uses the customer's actual profile. Reserve receiving space from the proposed outlet datum to the point where each long panel is collected and packaged. A machine that fits inside the workshop still needs a workable finished-panel route.

Select the roof route when its accepted-order contribution supports the complete investment and its delivered scope matches the customer's roof product. Select framing when the independently costed framing offer clears those same commercial tests and includes the design, identification and assembly obligations you sell. If neither clears them, continue bought supply rather than buying either machine to create demand.

## Two expensive business mismatches

**Mistake: treating frame production as longer roof-sheet production.** In a hypothetical start-up, the investor budgets a former and coil stock but sells assembled wall frames. Connection operations, assembly work areas and drawing control have no owner or allowance. Correct the plan by costing the sold frame package from design release through dispatch before placing the equipment order.

**Mistake: choosing framing to satisfy an existing roofing customer list.** A hypothetical distributor purchases a framing line because its catalogue looks broader, while customers continue ordering roofing profiles. The relevant orders never enter that production route. Keep the roof business on the panel comparison and require independently supported framing demand before adding the second business.
