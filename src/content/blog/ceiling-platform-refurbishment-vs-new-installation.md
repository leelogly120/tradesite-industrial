A platform brief for a new ceiling installation should not be copied unchanged into a refurbishment purchase. Existing ceiling fabric, services, partitions, building use and removed material can change what the access proposal needs to address. Conversely, an open new-build workface still needs actual task, floor, route and material information; an unfinished building is not automatically unrestricted.

This guide compares the purchase briefs for two explicitly hypothetical projects. It provides no building-condition diagnosis or removal method. Use the [ceiling maintenance checklist](/blog/ceiling-maintenance-safety-checklist/) for an individual maintenance task. Here the outcome is a side-by-side scope comparison that shows why two superficially similar ceiling heights can require different supplier questions.

## Define the result for each project type

### Describe new installation as a task, not an empty room

For a new installation, identify the ceiling or wall areas, fixing positions, materials and work period the proposed equipment must serve. Name temporary works, stored components and other trades relevant to the area. “Open hall” is a description that needs a date and task context.

A broad deck may be worth investigating for a continuing workface, but the task still needs defined geometry. The [crawler ceiling-platform route](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms) supplies an access concept to discuss, not a finding that an unfinished structure suits a particular machine.

### Describe refurbishment beyond a replacement label

For refurbishment, ask the responsible project team to identify the intended result and the information available about existing fabric and services. Distinguish an inspection scope from a defined replacement purchase. Do not assume that the equipment provider can identify concealed building conditions from a general photograph.

The enquiry should say which task requirements are known and which await the building or specialist trade's assessment. A platform proposal can be preliminary while making those dependencies explicit. It should not be described as a complete answer to an uninvestigated building condition.

### Keep removed material in the purchase boundary

A refurbishment brief may need provision for components, packaging and removed items that differ from a new installation's incoming material demand. Ask which provider addresses each service and what equipment information it needs. No material should disappear from the purchasing discussion because it is labelled waste.

The [material-replenishment guide](/blog/ceiling-platform-material-replenishment-supply/) helps define the supply boundary. It provides no permission to carry or lower removed components. The access proposal and the specialist material/task arrangements must address their respective scopes.

## Identify what the building already contains

### Separate observed geometry from missing information

Mark known beams, services, partitions and ceiling features around the proposed task. State where survey coverage is incomplete. An existing ceiling can conceal information that the project must obtain through its responsible specialists; the equipment enquiry should identify that gap without diagnosing it.

For new installation, the same discipline applies to planned services and temporary works not yet present during the visit. Record the construction stage for which the proposal is intended. A photograph of the current open space cannot establish the future arrangement when the platform is expected.

### Compare access over the whole work period

A refurbishment task can take place in a facility with continuing operations, while a new installation can also share access with other contractors. Identify the actual period and space the site can release. Do not assign an occupied-building assumption to every refurbishment or an empty-building assumption to every new build.

The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) connects these simultaneous space uses. The purchase brief should state which workfaces and routes are available, what changes later and who controls those changes. It is not a programme authorising simultaneous activities.

### Request current floor and support information

Ask the building owner or project team for the information needed to examine the proposed route and support positions. Existing use, a successful previous delivery or a visibly sound surface cannot establish suitability for a different machine. An unfinished slab likewise needs its actual records and review.

Provide the [floor-review inputs](/blog/indoor-aerial-platform-ground-pressure-guide/) alongside the candidate-specific equipment information. Refurbishment and new installation can differ in available evidence, coatings, local features and surrounding occupation. Those differences should change the questions asked, rather than become broad claims about which building type is safer.

## Compare two different platform briefs

### Test an open installation workface

In the first explicitly hypothetical project, a contractor wants a broad platform considered for a continuous ceiling installation in an unfinished hall. The workface is described, but later service installation will change nearby clearance and material space. Ask the provider which building stage and material arrangement its proposal addresses.

The missing purchase decision concerns coverage through the intended period. The project may need a revised work window or another proposal once the surrounding installation changes. This example claims no rate of work, reduced crew, useful deck size or suitable configuration.

### Test refurbishment beside continuing building use

In the second hypothetical project, a facility plans replacement work in selected bays while other areas remain in use. The project team can identify the bays but has not yet described the removed components, material provision or complete survey coverage around existing services.

Ask the responsible trades to define those inputs before treating an access quote as complete. The trade-off is between a proposal for a well-defined initial task and a wider purchase whose information is still incomplete. A larger machine or broader service label cannot resolve unknown building and task conditions.

| Brief item | New-installation example | Refurbishment example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended result | Defined new ceiling workface and material requirement | Identified replacement scope with specialist building inputs |
| Surrounding geometry | Current structure plus planned services/temporary works | Observed existing fabric plus explicitly uninvestigated areas |
| Material services | Incoming components, packaging and replenishment scope | Incoming replacements plus removed-item destinations |
| Available work period | Construction-stage access shared with other trades | Facility-agreed bay windows and continuing operations |
| Floor information | Records for actual route and proposed work positions | Current building records and changes relevant to the candidate |
| Scope not yet covered | Later-stage obstructions or changed material areas | Missing task/building information or unpriced removal services |

### Keep the source within personnel-access planning

Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE MEWP guidance</a> discusses task/site selection, overhead conflicts and material considerations. It supports giving a provider the actual conditions and work requirements. It does not diagnose existing building fabric, identify hazardous materials or approve a removal task.

Specialist investigation and any required building-service decisions belong to the appropriate project parties. An equipment supplier should receive the resulting relevant requirements, not be assigned a building assessment through an access enquiry. Keep sensitive building information in the project's appropriate channels.

## Prepare the right scope for quotation

### Ask each provider to address the actual project stage

Give every provider the same marked workface, building period, material description and known constraints. If the information will change, identify the expected change and the question it creates. A quotation based on the early open structure should not quietly become the proposal for a later enclosed bay.

For refurbishment, separate the task currently defined from contingent work that has not been scoped. Ask for the actual proposed coverage and exclusions. This lets procurement obtain a useful initial response without implying that unknown conditions or future work are included.

### Compare site provision with supplier services

Ask what the platform quote includes and what the site must supply: receiving arrangements, relevant surveys, power information, material services and nominated project coordination. Keep these assumptions beside the price. They can differ between providers even for the same project.

The [support-purchase guide](/blog/ceiling-platform-purchase-service-support-package/) helps clarify local attendance and advisory scope. Support cannot replace the specialist task definition. A supplier's willingness to answer equipment questions does not establish competence or an appointment to assess existing ceiling fabric.

### Assemble two distinct enquiry sheets

- State whether the brief concerns new installation, defined replacement or exploratory assessment.
- Mark the task height, ceiling/wall geometry and exceptional positions.
- Identify incoming material, packaging and any removed-item services needed.
- Provide access/transport routes for the relevant building stage and work period.
- Attach floor or ground records and identify missing support information.
- Request candidate-specific outrigger/support and occupied-space data where applicable.
- State available power, control-related restrictions and relevant site requirements.
- Identify destination, building ownership and the appropriate documentation contacts.
- Name wind/environmental questions for exposed areas and specialist inputs still outstanding.

### Record what would change the platform question

A revised ceiling scope, newly identified service, changed floor area or different material object may alter the enquiry. Describe the physical change to the provider and ask what additional information is needed. Do not reduce it to a date amendment if it changes the task itself.

For example, a hypothetical inspection enquiry later becomes a component-replacement proposal. The previous task location can remain useful while the material, removed-item and simultaneous-work questions change. Carry forward the known geometry, but request the new task scope rather than treating the earlier inspection response as a replacement-work answer.

### Keep a useful partial answer within its boundary

A provider may address one accessible bay while another remains dependent on investigation. Preserve that partial coverage without presenting it as whole-building suitability. The buyer can then decide which further project information to obtain and whether a different method is needed for the exception.

The [platform method comparison](/blog/crawler-platform-vs-spider-lift-vs-scaffolding/) can support that next discussion. It should compare the same known task and site facts. Changing the method does not eliminate missing building information or authorise work in an uninvestigated area.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise when the brief is not ready for a full offer

A proposal may not fit when its building stage differs from the actual work period, existing conditions are assumed, removed material has no defined service, or a partial bay response is treated as complete coverage. This guide provides no condition diagnosis, hazardous-material advice, removal method, structural finding or work authorisation.

## FAQ

#### Is refurbishment always a harder access task?

No universal ranking follows from the project label. Compare actual geometry, building information, materials, work windows and candidate equipment. A new installation can also contain complex temporary works or shared-space constraints.

#### Can the platform supplier identify unknown ceiling conditions?

Do not assume that role is included. The building owner and appropriate specialists must define the task and relevant building information. The equipment provider can then address its stated proposal against those inputs.

#### Can an inspection quote cover later replacement work?

Only the actual provider's revised scope can establish what is included. Replacement can introduce material and removed-item questions absent from inspection. Keep unchanged geometry while requesting a response to the changed task.

#### Should removed components appear in the platform brief?

They should appear as identified task and service requirements where relevant. That does not authorise placing or handling them on the platform. The responsible task and equipment parties must define the applicable arrangement.
