**Buy fleet telematics when compatible equipment can supply the specific readings needed for recurring dispatch or maintenance decisions, and a trial shows that acting on those readings pays for the system. Keep disciplined dispatch and meter records when the fleet is small, movements are known, or the supplier cannot provide reliable data for the machines and locations in question.** Test the missed collection, unnecessary visit or late service you intend to eliminate before buying subscriptions for an entire fleet.

Start with three different clocks. A rental contract has a chargeable period. A dispatch record tells you where a machine was sent and when it was returned. A meter records a defined kind of machine use. The readings answer different questions. A platform can earn a hire day while standing ready during a construction delay. A depot can accumulate machine hours while checking a returned unit.

The green deck in the yard below is visibly elevated. A photograph cannot reveal its contracted hire status, operating hours or location history. Give the photographed type of asset an individual fleet identity in the trial and reconcile each reading to its real dispatch and return records.

## Make a list of decisions the dispatcher will actually change

Review recent fleet transactions with the person who schedules collections and the person who schedules service. Pick examples with a real cost: a truck sent to the wrong site, a technician sent too early, a due-service machine dispatched again, or a unit recovered too late to take a confirmed booking. Record the cost of each event once. A map with dots on it is worth money only if it changes one of these decisions while there is still time to act.

[JLG describes its ClearSky telematics system](https://www.jlg.com/en/directaccess/5-questions-about-jlgs-clearsky-telematics-system-answered) and gives examples involving usage and service planning, including engine hours. That describes its named product. Ask each provider which fields, update intervals and export options are available on the actual machines in your fleet. A battery-powered platform and an engine-powered unit can count use differently. A location tag alone cannot supply a manufacturer's service meter.

For a records-only proposal, define who records the asset ID, site, on-hire and off-hire time, departure and return meter values, defects and next service point. Give disputed or missing records an owner and a correction rule. A paper or spreadsheet process that already performs reliably can be a sensible investment baseline. Compare it with the cost of operating a connected system, including the staff who still act on exceptions.

## Trial the awkward machines as well as the easy ones

Select examples of the oldest supported model, a current model, a unit working indoors and one moving between depots. Obtain the manufacturer's position on retrofit hardware where relevant. Specify installation responsibilities, data access, subscription periods and what happens to hardware and records when a machine is sold. Define appropriate permissions with the customer and service provider. Buyers need records about equipment and legitimate business events; the contract should define who sees them and for how long.

Have one dispatcher work from the proposed alerts while another retains the baseline record. Reconcile disagreements at the end of each hire. A missing signal is a data gap, not evidence of a stolen unit, a completed collection or zero use. Retain the site's agreed off-hire channel. The workshop photograph shows a low deck inside a large industrial building, a useful reminder to include indoor reporting conditions in the trial.

Compare hardware, installation, subscription, staff training, integration and exception handling against verified avoided visits and recovered contribution. Do not credit a recovered booking at its gross invoice if extra transport, service or other costs come with it. Keep speculative future functions outside the base case.

## Two common mistakes in a fleet technology tender

**Mistake one — billing idle time as machine use.** A hypothetical operator reads very low activity during a correctly booked hire and treats the contract as unearned. Reconcile hire terms, availability and agreed off-hire notices separately from operating hours.

**Mistake two — purchasing a subscription for unsupported units.** A promising demonstration uses a current connected machine while much of the fleet produces only location data. Price the compatible group separately and retain meter-based service records for the rest.
