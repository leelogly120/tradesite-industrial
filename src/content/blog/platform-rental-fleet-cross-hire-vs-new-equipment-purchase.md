Cross-hire a missing platform for isolated customer work when an identified upstream offer covers the accepted task, dates and service package and the customer price covers the complete upstream hire, delivery and support costs plus the firm's required contribution. Buy another asset when repeat committed hires support its full ownership and delivery costs and the business can maintain and support that equipment. Do not turn an enquiry list into an investment forecast.

The buyer here is a rental company filling its own fleet gap. This is different from a contractor deciding whether to rent or buy for one installation, or a main contractor combining several trade rentals. The rental company must connect its upstream equipment supply with the service it actually sells to its customer.

## First qualify the customer duty, then source the missing asset

A fleet shortage is meaningful only after the work is defined. Identify the working surface, positions, crew and materials, access route and supporting conditions. Ask which actual equipment configuration serves them. A partner's machine in the same advertised height class is not necessarily an equivalent substitute.

[JLG's explanation of re-rental](https://www.jlg.com/en/directaccess/what_are_the_benefits_of_working_with_a_re_rental_company) describes rental companies obtaining equipment from another rental business to supplement their fleet. Use that sourcing architecture, while obtaining today's actual equipment and services from the proposed partner.

The raised deck below illustrates a complete physical asset, including the base and supports. Keep the identified configuration on the customer offer and the upstream equipment record. Describe separately any transport, handover or support services included in the arrangement.

![A red large-deck platform stands raised in a yard above its crawler base and extended supports.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-deck-raised-yard.webp)
*Match the same identified equipment configuration across the upstream offer and the customer duty.*

The [IPAF Rental Standard](https://www.ipaf.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/IPAF%20Rental%20Standard%20%28Including%20Guidance%20for%20Rental%20Companies%29%20RP-3-EN--V3.1-20231208.pdf) discusses cross-hire as a transaction involving the customer, rental company and rental partner. Its supplier-management and communication guidance is useful when setting up the chain. A partner's ownership does not erase the rental company's part in supplying its customer.

## Test the two agreements over the same occupation period

Place the upstream offer beside the proposed customer agreement. Identify the actual start and end events for charging, the equipment and service inclusions, permitted sub-rental arrangements and the people authorised to change dates. Resolve material mismatches before accepting the customer booking.

A customer who needs a short work period can still create a longer upstream occupation through transport, receiving and collection. Compare those periods explicitly. Also identify who receives fault reports, who provides the response included in the offer and what the agreed substitution process covers.

These inputs belong in the commercial assessment before a headline hire rate is compared with a purchase price.

| Buyer input | Entry and supporting record | Responsible owner | Decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer duty | Accepted task, site and equipment configuration ___ | Rental technical lead and customer | Defines the actual fleet gap |
| Committed demand | Signed bookings, dates and cancellation basis ___ | Sales and commercial manager | Separates real occupation from enquiries and optional extensions |
| Upstream availability | Identified asset, period and partner acceptance ___ | Procurement lead and rental partner | Establishes whether cross-hire can fulfill the booking |
| Charging boundaries | Start/end events on both agreements and transport occupation ___ | Commercial manager | Exposes paid upstream time not recovered from the customer |
| Delivery and support | Actual carrier, handover, response and collection scope ___ | Logistics and service managers | Completes the offered customer service |
| Asset ownership | Purchase, preparation, care, storage and disposal evidence ___ | Fleet manager and finance team | Completes the owned-asset alternative |
| Fleet conflict | Concurrent accepted jobs and required independent assets ___ | Fleet planner | Prevents one new machine being counted on overlapping hires |
| Comparable result | Customer receipts less complete route costs; required contribution over the same programme ___ | Finance team using actual offers | Ranks eligible sourcing routes without a universal payback claim |

Use the same completed customer service in both columns. Include actual upstream charges and services on the cross-hire side. Include the acquisition and continuing costs of ownership, plus delivery and customer support, on the owned side. Keep any assumed disposal proceeds visible with their evidence rather than using a speculative resale figure to make the purchase win.

## Look beyond the first profitable booking

A new asset occupies storage and support capacity between hires. Count its committed calendar, not the total number of quotations issued. Several enquiries for the same dates do not create several successive bookings. Separate competing requests from customers who have actually committed to different periods.

Cross-hire can also be a poor fit for a repeat service requirement. If the partner cannot commit the required configuration and dates, a low indicative rate is not usable capacity. Compare a documented alternative partner or a supported own-asset offer. Decline the uncovered service rather than accepting a job that neither route can fulfill.

![A red upright work frame rises in a narrow passage between industrial buildings.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261029-wall-corridor.webp)
*Keep the customer's workface and route on the sourcing brief; a broad equipment-class label leaves the real gap undefined.*

For repeat work, use the service records from completed hires to improve the forecast. Record actual transport occupation, support attendance, unused periods and the configurations repeatedly requested. That evidence can support a later fleet purchase without pretending that every first enquiry requires an owned machine.

For an immediate shortage, buy equipment only when its actual delivery and acceptance programme fits the customer commitment. Ownership is not a solution to today's gap if the purchased asset arrives after the required work. Compare actual offers rather than a generic delivery assumption.

## Two fleet decisions that misread demand or margin

**Mistake: customer off-hire is treated as the upstream billing end.** A hypothetical rental company prices a short customer task but ignores the partner's actual return and collection boundaries. The asset remains on an upstream charge after the customer work ends. Align the agreed events or include the extra upstream hire days in the customer-service calculation before accepting the hire.

**Mistake: quotations are counted as repeat orders.** Another business buys a specialist platform from several enquiries, although the requests compete for the same short project window and none provides a later committed booking. Rebuild the calendar from accepted hires and test the complete ownership cost against that programme. Use a qualified cross-hire offer for an isolated gap when its actual terms fit.

These are hypothetical sourcing errors. The correction is to compare deliverable capacity and complete cost, with the actual demand and agreement boundaries visible.
