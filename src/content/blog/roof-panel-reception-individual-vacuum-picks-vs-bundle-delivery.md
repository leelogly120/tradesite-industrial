Choose individual vacuum picks when the panel and handling suppliers accept the exact sheet and an installation crew can receive each piece at the agreed workface. Order whole bundles only where an engineer accepts the landing position and the project funds onward distribution to the installers. For either bid, price the cost of one accepted panel at its installation position.

## Trace one delivery beyond the roof edge

Start with a drawing of one complete bay. Mark the physical end of the supplier's delivery, where responsibility changes, where a panel waits and the location of its final handover. On an irregular roof the paths for long field sheets and short infill pieces can differ substantially. When a crane lands a bundle outside the released bay, map and price each onward movement before comparing the direct handover. If the installation front advances beyond the original receiving point, identify the next accepted point and include the cost of changing position.

Have the panel supplier state which handling methods it accepts for each profile, finish, length and protective film condition. Have the proposed handling-equipment provider identify its equipment and the material conditions under which it accepts the duty. A statement about vacuum handling of flat sheet is insufficient evidence for a deeply profiled surface. Packaged units have different lifting, bearing and unpacking requirements. Use the current instructions for the ordered product. For example, Ceco's CFR installation manual describes handling its bundles and precautions for its panels. Those instructions belong to the named product. [Ceco CFR installation manual](https://www.cecobuildings.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Ceco-CFR-Installation-Manual.pdf)

## Complete the receiving schedule with the roof team

Use a different row set for each group of identical conditions. A buyer can request a bundled base bid and an individual-pick alternate for the same zone. If the layout permits only one of them, mark the other route unavailable for that group and explain why. Compare a mixed award with a single-method award after counting additional mobilizations and changeovers.

| Buyer entry | Who supplies the evidence | Decision it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter each sheet mark, length, mass, finish and orientation | Panel supplier | Separate handling groups and units per delivery. |
| List the pack contents and installation order by mark | Fabricator and installer | Identify pieces that must be uncovered or moved twice. |
| Mark individual drop locations, crew position and working dates | Installer | Establish the usable direct handovers. |
| Enter engineered bundle positions and permitted occupancy periods | Project engineer | Exclude unaccepted storage or landing positions. |
| Record the offered attachment, plant and surface conditions | Handling specialist | Qualify the actual material at each transfer. |
| Name the party for unpacking, distributing and taking away protection | Site manager | Compare completed work at the same endpoint. |

Ask each bidder for a worked delivery shift using the project's sequence. Count the number of accepted panel handovers, the waiting periods between bay releases and tasks that continue after the crane or former leaves. Use the same representative shift for both offers. If the installer works from two faces, one crane moving between them can interrupt a direct-feed sequence. Conversely a bundle intended to feed two faces can divide poorly when panels for the second face are underneath those required immediately. The best order for loading the truck can differ from the best order for laying panels.

## Account for interrupted installation

Price one credible interruption. Ask where an already released sheet goes if the installer cannot accept it and who authorizes restarting production. Ask what becomes of a delivered bundle if a structural connection or adjoining trade keeps the next zone closed. A planned ground hold can cost less than purchasing roof storage that the engineer cannot release. Give credit for work completed elsewhere only when the replacement workface and the crew are genuinely available in that period.

**Mistake: A pack arrives in delivery order.** Suppose transport groups the longest panels at the bottom for the journey. The bay starts with those longest panels. The purchaser includes one lift for the bundle and omits sorting and redistribution. Submit a packing list matched to the installation marks, obtain a distribution price and compare it with loading in installation order or receiving those panels individually.

**Mistake: The direct-pick quote assumes a different surface.** A specialist prices an attachment on the basis of bare smooth sheet. The purchased profile has a different surface and film. The buyer books the crane before the specialist accepts the sample. Provide the actual profile and surface information with the request, require a written acceptance for the proposed duty and keep a separate price for a permitted alternative.

Award by released zone. Put the third-party material-handling package, temporary storage, distribution labour and installation waiting time against that zone; count the forming equipment separately. Where an elevated former presents sheets individually, compare its output position with the agreed receiving method. A machine able to form the required section supplies useful work only for the sheet groups whose complete handover the project can accept.

## Eight RFQ inputs for an installable handover

1. Attach the released roof plan and bay-by-bay dates.
2. Send the profile drawing, finish, film and longest actual panel.
3. Provide individual and packed dimensions, masses and packing list.
4. Mark the former outlet, transport arrivals and offered receiving positions.
5. Identify the engineer's accepted bundle landing areas and limits.
6. Name the panel and lifting specialists responsible for method acceptance.
7. List crew scope, onward movement, interruptions and empty-pack removal.
8. Request separate completed-work prices by accepted sheet group and date.
