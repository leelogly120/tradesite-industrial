Choose temporary overseas deployment when a defined project needs your existing roll-forming package, its destination use and customs route are accepted, and the return programme fits the next committed job. Choose a destination-based fleet when repeat contracted work and local operating resources justify keeping equipment there. If neither has a complete costed scope, buy the required panels or production service for that project instead of sending an unsupported asset abroad.

The decision is larger than whether the equipment fits a container. A returnable package ties up the line, lift and supporting resources throughout outbound transit, site work and the return process. A destination fleet commits capital, storage, people and maintenance resources beyond the first roof. Evaluate both across the same set of accepted projects.

## Count the whole period away from home

Build a calendar from withdrawal from the home fleet to readiness for the next home job. Include preparation, packing, both transport legs, destination handling, assembly, work, dismantling and receiving inspection. Use written carrier and contractor schedules for each leg. A roof completion date alone is not a machine-availability date.

Keep the initial baseline and a project-overrun scenario side by side. Price the actual alternative for a home job that overlaps the return window: another available package, purchased panels or a rescheduled contract. Do not insert an invented daily loss rate. The buyer's committed programme and quotations supply the cost of the conflict.

Port handling is a separate procurement interface. In the photograph, equipment, a cargo vessel and a crane arrangement share the frame. The forwarder must describe the actual cargo, handling and responsibility chain for your shipment; the production team must describe the later site setup.

![Green equipment on a cargo-vessel deck with crane gear above and a truck at the quay](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-port-handling.webp)

*Separate port handling from site assembly and roof production when allocating the logistics budget.*

## A temporary shipment is not automatically a carnet shipment

Assign the customs route before calculating the deployment price. Equipment use matters as well as the intended return. For example, the [Swiss customs authority's ATA guidance](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/en/ata-carnet-importation) excludes construction machinery and equipment used for professional manufacturing from that carnet route and directs those uses to another procedure. That is a Swiss example, not a rule for every destination.

Give the appointed destination customs representative the equipment identities, ownership, actual work, users, destination and return intention. Require a written route for that exact use, including declarations, security or payment requirements and closure responsibilities. Put the resulting cash requirements and administration into the comparison. Do not budget a production machine as an exhibition item because both will eventually leave the country.

The alternative, establishing a destination fleet, needs its own import and business arrangements. Permanent placement also needs storage, service responsibility, operating personnel and an accepted local equipment configuration. Treat a destination chassis as a specific supplied item with its own integration scope; a photographed truck is not a promise that a locally sourced chassis will match.

## Fill one worksheet for both asset strategies

| Input the buyer fills | Evidence owner and measurement basis | Temporary deployment | Destination fleet | Choice it controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contracted workload: ___ | Commercial manager: named awarded jobs and work windows | Overseas job plus next home commitment: ___ | Repeat destination jobs: ___ | One campaign versus continuing use |
| Complete asset scope: ___ | Fleet manager: serial identities and actual lift, line, auxiliaries | Items travelling and returning: ___ | Items retained locally: ___ | Prevent missing modules and duplicate capital |
| Unavailable period: ___ | Planner: withdrawal through return-to-service dates | Outbound, work, return, inspection: ___ | Import, commissioning and local readiness: ___ | Test programme conflicts |
| Customs and funds: ___ | Destination representative: written procedure for actual use | Outward/return obligations and cash: ___ | Destination placement obligations and cash: ___ | Compare real administration and tied-up funds |
| Local operations: ___ | Operations manager: named people, facility and service scope | Visiting/local responsibilities: ___ | Permanent responsibilities: ___ | Reject an asset plan with no operating organisation |
| Lifecycle cost: ___ | Buyer: same awarded workload and currency basis | Both freight legs and replacement work: ___ | Placement, support and residual plan: ___ | Select the lower complete viable cost |

Use the worksheet as an investment comparison, not a scoring contest. An unavailable operating team or an unacceptable equipment-use route removes an option regardless of its freight price. Among viable options, choose the one whose complete scope best supports the actual contracted work. Keep speculative future sales outside the committed-work total.

## Two export mistakes to remove before ordering

**Mistake 1 — the roof finishes, but the next job starts before the machine returns.** In this illustrative programme, the estimator counts overseas production days but excludes dismantling, vessel scheduling and receiving inspection. The same package is booked at home during those omitted stages. Rebuild the calendar through readiness at home and price an actual substitute for the overlap before choosing temporary deployment.

**Mistake 2 — a destination fleet exists only in a sales forecast.** In a second illustrative proposal, one awarded roof is used to justify permanent placement, while storage, operators and maintenance are left outside the budget. Subsequent work is only prospective. Compare a temporary package or purchased production against that single awarded job; approve a permanent fleet only after its operating resources and committed workload support it.

The transport photograph below shows a long blue forming machine carried on a truck. The carried asset and its transport vehicle are separate scope items. For the investment schedule, split the lift, line, chassis, auxiliaries and packing items into identifiable assets. Returning only the main machine while leaving required interfaces or documents at the site does not restore a usable home-fleet package.

![Long blue forming machine carried on a truck outside an industrial building](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-truck-dispatch.webp)

*Identify the carried machine and transport vehicle separately, then list every returning asset in the deployment inventory.*

## Eight RFQ attachments for the equipment side

1. Awarded project locations, production windows and the next committed use of the same package.
2. An equipment inventory identifying the lift, line, chassis, auxiliaries, serial records and ownership.
3. Roof receiver coordinates and height measured from the proposed equipment-support surface at each job.
4. Controlled panel drawings, material specifications, coil data and production quantities for those projects.
5. Destination port and site route, with the forwarder's outward and return handling and transport scope.
6. The customs representative's procedure for the stated equipment use, including return or permanent-placement obligations.
7. The local operations plan naming assembly, operators, electrical supply, storage and service responsibilities.
8. A comparative budget and calendar covering both transport legs or permanent placement, with home-job conflicts shown.
