Deploy a portable field brake when late site dimensions recur, blank material can reach the work area and representative parts fit the offered equipment and skills. Order finished shop trim when dimensions can be released early and long, complex or finish-sensitive parts travel successfully to their installation positions.

## Move the point of dimensional commitment

Assume the contractor can buy either service or can staff both from its own organization. The choice is where the final geometry becomes irreversible. In a shop route, measured dimensions travel to a fabricator and shaped pieces return to site. In a field route, flat blanks, a qualified crew and suitable portable equipment travel to the dimensions. If a nearby shop can measure, form and deliver the revised part inside the available installation window, compare that quoted service with the complete site station. Ask both providers for dates tied to the project before buying a mobile brake.

## Sort the trim list by when the measurement can be released

Mark rows that can be detailed from approved dimensions before panel delivery, those that need as-built measurements after another trade and those that vary piece by piece. Ask the installer whether a site measurement changes only the final cut length or the folded cross section itself. A shop-formed continuous section that only needs permitted length trimming presents a strong third option. A dimension that changes the return or mating surface can make a delivered finished shape unusable. Keep that distinction visible in the estimate.

For the mobile route include transport of the brake, set-up and storage, blanks, working space, competent staff and labour to finish and identify each part. Include idle time when another trade has not released the measurements. For the shop route include accurate site dimensions, detailing time, shop production, protective packing, transport, unloading, storage and rapid remakes where available. Compare accepted parts at their installation positions. An apparently inexpensive finished length that cannot negotiate the building access route is not a deliverable piece.

## Keep a local brake busy with eligible work

Replay a real project week. On which days can the field mechanic measure and complete enough eligible pieces to justify mobilizing the station? On which days must a specialist shop still make long or complex work? If an installer is reassigned to operate the field station, include the displaced installation work or replacement labour. If weather or the site owner restricts the workspace, price a covered local base or timed shop releases. A mobile station can be valuable for late closures even when most of the perimeter is shop-formed.

**Mistake: All perimeter length is treated as mobile-brake work.** A contractor adds the full perimeter to an investment forecast. Several long flashings have return geometry and finish requirements beyond the field station's demonstrated envelope. Trial the difficult parts, assign those the station can complete and price the shop-only balance before approving the purchase.

**Mistake: The delivered trim follows an early dimension.** A shop releases all folded closures before adjoining cladding is installed. A later measured return differs, and the finished pieces cannot be adjusted under the approved detail. Hold that family until site release, stock suitable blanks locally or reserve a later shop slot with a priced delivery.

Choose the field route for the specific families where late dimensional information, accepted portable fabrication and recurring work make it valuable. Choose delivered finished trim for stable, complex or long items where the workshop and transport route are a better match. Issue a split schedule when appropriate. The distinction from choosing a folder or press brake inside a workshop is practical: this decision changes which form of inventory crosses the site boundary, who measures it and when it becomes a finished part.

## Eight RFQ inputs for delivered trim and a field station

1. Attach actual flashing sections, lengths, material and finish.
2. List quantity by part family and the final measurement trigger.
3. Identify the roof detailer and site measurement release party.
4. Provide representative difficult parts and acceptance criteria.
5. Show field equipment access, blank storage and working space.
6. Give shop manufacturing dates, delivery limits and replacement terms.
7. List permissible final trimming, joints and finishing responsibilities.
8. Request delivered accepted parts and field-completed parts on equal terms.
