Charge and park the platform only if the offer actually includes a rechargeable arrangement. Put the bay, the charger, and the floor owner on the same sketch you send with the ceiling-platform shortlist. This page keeps the old URL. The buying decision lives in the [crawler under-ceiling buyers guide](/blog/crawler-under-ceiling-platform-buyers-guide/). Energy choices in general stay in the [dual-power selection guide](/blog/dual-power-crawler-platform-selection/).

| What you can see | Write it as | If you do not know yet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A battery or charger named in the offer | The exact model words from that offer | Confirm on RFQ; do not infer chemistry |
| The overnight bay on the plan | Door, column, and who owns the floor | Measure the bay on site |
| A fire lane, aisle, or wash-down in that bay | The conflict in one sentence | Ask the building owner before you shortlist |
| Hours the bay is free | The clock times, not "overnight" | Leave the hours blank |

Two ways this goes wrong. The quotation says dual power, the hall bans exhaust, and the battery cannot finish the bay because the charger was never in the price. Or the only flat corner is a fire lane, so the machine that fit the height class has nowhere legal to sit until morning.

Copy these lines onto the buyers-guide sketch:

- Offered battery and charger, or "not in this purchase"
- Overnight bay, floor owner, and the hours it is free
- Whether wash-down, dust, or a fire lane reaches that bay
- Catalog height class you want screened: 20m, 25m, 31m, or 35m
- Door and route from the street
- Destination country
