If an actual ceiling-platform proposal includes a rechargeable power source, the buyer needs a facilities answer as well as an equipment answer. Identify the offered battery and charger through the provider's records, then ask the site owner what information it needs to consider the proposed parking and charging location. An unused corner or an available overnight period does not establish a suitable facility.

This guide is conditional: it does not claim that a rechargeable option is available for every [crawler ceiling platform](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms). The [dual-power selection guide](/blog/dual-power-crawler-platform-selection/) compares wider energy choices. Here the useful output is a facilities enquiry that exposes the scope outside the machine purchase without providing charging instructions.

## Confirm that the rechargeable arrangement is actually proposed

### Identify the battery through the equipment provider

Ask the provider to identify the rechargeable equipment included in its actual proposal and the applicable records. Do not infer battery chemistry, capacity or availability from an electric label. If the quotation only mentions a possible option, keep it conditional until the supplier defines the offered configuration.

The facility reviewer needs to know what equipment is being considered. A generic platform family name cannot supply that identity, and information for another machine should not be substituted merely because it appears to use a similar energy source.

### Ask which charger is included or required

Request the actual proposed charger identity and its relationship to the offered equipment. Ask whether it is included, separately supplied or still to be defined. The buyer should not assume that a rechargeable machine price includes every item needed at the site.

This is a purchase-scope question, not a recommendation to select a charger independently. The equipment provider must identify the applicable arrangement and information; the site's responsible parties then assess the facility requirements relevant to that actual proposal.

### Distinguish parking from charging

The project may need a place for the platform when it is not being used and a separately assessed arrangement for charging. Ask the facility whether the proposed location is being considered for one purpose or both. Do not merge them simply because the buyer expects the machine to remain there between shifts.

This distinction can reveal an early planning gap. A storage permission might address space and access while leaving power, equipment requirements and other charging-related questions unanswered by the responsible site reviewers.

<a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg139.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE's rechargeable-battery leaflet</a> treats workplace batteries and charging as subjects needing their own assessment. This Great Britain context supports requesting the actual battery and charger information for facility review. Its general guidance is not a specification for the offered equipment, a charging-location approval or a basis for assuming battery chemistry or charging time.

## Give the facility a complete location question

### Identify the actual available area

Mark the proposed area on a current layout, including the access relationship and adjacent building uses that the facility wants considered. Ask who controls that space and whether its availability matches the project's intended period. A contractor's preferred parking position is not the same as the facility's accepted provision.

Do not use this article to choose a separation distance, protective arrangement or charging layout. The useful enquiry identifies the place and its owner so the actual equipment requirements can be reviewed against the site's conditions.

### Keep the equipment footprint and floor review separate

Request the configuration-specific information needed to understand the platform in the relevant parked state. The facility's floor and access reviewers need the actual equipment basis, not a deck photograph or a model suffix. A space that appears large enough can still have unanswered support or route questions.

The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) addresses broader spatial relationships. For this brief, identify the proposed parking area and the responsible review rather than declaring it acceptable from an outline alone.

### Describe the time assumption accurately

State the period during which the buyer expects the area and any proposed supply to be available. Label it as a project assumption until the facility confirms it. Do not translate an overnight gap into a charging duration or assume it will meet the equipment's energy needs.

Ask the provider for the applicable requirements and evidence for its actual configuration. Ask the facility whether the assumed location and services are available. These are separate responses, and neither should be rewritten as a runtime or productivity promise.

## Compare two proposed parking locations

### Follow a hypothetical hall and service-area comparison

Imagine a buyer proposing to leave a platform in a work hall between task periods. The hall owner permits temporary parking but has not reviewed charging. A second possible location is a service area with a documented site supply, but its access and floor questions for the actual platform remain unresolved.

Neither location is established as suitable. The hall option lacks the charging-facility response, while the service-area option lacks physical access and support answers. The buyer should send the actual equipment and charger information to the relevant reviewers instead of choosing the location with the nearest visible outlet.

### Use a conditional facilities enquiry table

| Facilities question | Work-hall proposal | Service-area proposal | Information needed next |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Purpose of permission | Parking only in the example | Proposed parking and charging | Facility response to the intended purpose |
| Equipment identity | Actual platform record needed | Same actual platform record needed | Supplier's configuration information |
| Charger scope | Not yet reviewed | Not yet matched to equipment | Actual included or required charger record |
| Space and floor | Hall-owner information required | Support question remains open | Appropriate site review for the actual state |
| Access relationship | Within current work area | Route to separate area unresolved | Current route and equipment information |
| Time and supply availability | Buyer assumption | Facility response requested | Attributable availability statement |

This is a worked comparison of unanswered questions, not a charging-location recommendation. The table intentionally supplies no electrical connection, fire separation, ventilation or battery-handling specification.

### Interpret the price together with facility scope

An equipment offer that includes a charger can still leave substantial facility questions outside the price. Conversely, a separate charger line should not be judged as a disadvantage until its actual purpose and scope are understood. Compare what the provider supplies and what the site must arrange for the same proposal.

The trade-off may involve using one area for multiple purposes or keeping parking and other facilities separate, but the actual site review must determine what is appropriate. This article does not establish a preferred arrangement or the availability of any facility service.

## Resolve the facility question before relying on the option

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise when the rechargeable proposal remains conditional

The proposal may not fit a final project decision when no actual battery or charger identity has been supplied, the facility has not considered the intended purpose, or the route and floor basis remains unresolved. A vacant area and a power outlet cannot close those questions by themselves.

This guide contains no charging steps, chemistry recommendation, charge duration, fire separation distance or ventilation design. It also does not establish that the platform can serve the intended work period. Those questions need actual equipment information and responsible facility assessment.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Prepare the charging and parking brief

- Confirm that the actual offered configuration includes rechargeable equipment and request its provider-issued identity and records.
- Identify the included or required charger separately, without assuming every electrical accessory is part of the platform price.
- State task height and ceiling geometry as context for the platform selection, while keeping parked-state information distinct.
- Provide material storage, floor and access constraints for the proposed area and identify the responsible facility reviewers.
- State destination, transport arrival and intended parking periods so temporary receiving space is not mistaken for a settled facility.
- Ask for the equipment power requirements, facility response, permission scope and documentation needed before relying on the proposed arrangement.

Keep the facility's answer attached to the actual location and purpose. If the machine will be parked elsewhere later, the earlier response should not be carried across as if the space and conditions had remained unchanged.

## FAQ

#### Does every electric platform need this brief?

This article applies only when an actual proposal includes a rechargeable arrangement. An electric description alone does not identify the source. Ask the provider what is offered before creating a facilities requirement around an assumed battery or charger.

#### Is permission to park also permission to charge?

Do not assume that the permission covers both purposes. Ask the facility to identify what it has considered and what remains open. The actual equipment requirements and responsible site review must address any proposed charging arrangement separately from a general space allocation.

#### Is an overnight period enough for charging?

This guide supplies no duration or energy conclusion. State the actual availability period and ask the provider for applicable information for the offered configuration. The facility must also confirm the assumed place and services; elapsed idle time alone does not establish suitability.

#### What if no charging location has been accepted?

Keep that dependency visible in the equipment comparison and ask the facility what information is needed. Do not invent a temporary method or assume another area can be used. The buyer may need a revised actual proposal or a resolved facility arrangement before selection.
