**Choose an articulating boom when the accepted route to the work position needs a jointed arm to reach up and over an obstruction. Choose a telescopic boom when the workface has a clear approach from an available stand-off position and the manufacturer's working envelope covers it.** Compare a floor-supported large deck separately for continuous work by adjacent technicians where the floor directly below can be released. Select the machine that reaches, works and withdraws within the accepted configuration.

A façade contractor can receive two quotations showing the same headline working height and very different coverage. One building has a low annex between the standing area and a high window. Another has a clear elevation but only one usable setup area across a service lane. Draw both sections from the actual support surface. The obstacle, offset and approach path matter as much as the final height.

The yellow assembly in the yard photograph has lifting members, a vertical work frame and deployed supports. It illustrates the room a complete machine can occupy. Its geometry is specific to that equipment. Obtain separate manufacturer diagrams for the actual articulated and telescopic boom candidates being priced.

## A point inside a diagram is only the start

[Genie explains the difference between articulating and telescopic boom lifts](https://www.genielift.com/aerialpros/articulating-vs-telescopic-boom). Articulating machines use jointed sections to help reach over or around obstacles. Telescopic machines extend a boom along a more direct line and are often considered for horizontal outreach. The relevant purchase evidence is the exact model, configuration and working diagram for the load and position in question. The longest reach listed separately in a brochure cannot simply be combined with its highest position.

Give each bidder a plan and section of the same work point. Mark the nearest permitted machine centreline, obstacle height and depth, final basket orientation, required approach to the fixing and neighbouring obstructions. Include the swing of the base or counterweight where it matters. The provider should show a feasible path to and away from the task. A section capturing the final basket position can hide interference halfway through the movement.

For a sequence of wall openings, group the points by real setup position. Identify positions requiring a machine move, closure of a vehicle lane or separate access. Ask the site authority whether the standing area remains available through both installation and later snagging. An articulation advantage at one difficult corner does not automatically justify buying that arrangement for an entire uninterrupted elevation.

## Count the positions finished during the closure

The useful price unit is a completed group of tasks within the released area. Count transporter and mobilisation costs, setup, moves, temporary closures and exception access. A long-reach machine can justify one remote setup if it safely serves several points without occupying a busy apron repeatedly. An articulated machine can justify extra setup time if its accepted envelope reaches a significant number of points behind obstructions. Use the supplier's drawings and a site-specific assessment to substantiate these savings.

A broad deck serves a different work pattern. The exterior photograph shows a long horizontal working surface over a crawler structure. A crew fastening a continuous run of wall material can benefit from working alongside one another, provided the offered platform, distribution of tools and approach to the wall are accepted. A wide working surface cannot reach around a canopy simply by virtue of its area. Keep awkward exceptions as individually priced tasks.

Ask the buyer responsible for the completed facility whether any temporary obstructions will disappear or permanent ones will be introduced. A boom selected for construction access around a scaffold can be excessive for annual cleaning after the scaffold leaves. A simpler construction position can disappear behind new landscaping. Base purchase on the periods of work the owner actually intends to fund.

## Two reach-selection mistakes

**Mistake one — applying the largest stated outreach at maximum height.** A hypothetical buyer marks a point using two brochure maxima. The offered work envelope at the required height and crew load stops short. Request the appropriate diagram and reassess the setup or machine class.

**Mistake two — drawing an elevated basket behind an obstacle without a path there.** The endpoint sits inside a nominal envelope but the boom, basket or base interferes with a projecting roof during movement. Have the competent planner review approach and withdrawal, or purchase a different accepted route.
