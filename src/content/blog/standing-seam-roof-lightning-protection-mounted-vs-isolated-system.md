Buy roof-mounted lightning-protection components only within a qualified specialist's accepted whole-building concept and the roof provider's compatible attachment details. Buy an isolated protection package when that concept requires separation. Release standing-seam tooling after the roof product and relevant interfaces are coordinated. A metal covering alone is not a completed lightning-protection system. Keep the specialist-selected protection concept in the base purchase; compare attachment products only within its accepted roof and building interfaces.

The buyer is choosing a designed specialist package, not designing electrical protection from a roof photograph. The appointed protection designer establishes the concept for the actual building, services and rooftop installations. Procurement ensures the roof and equipment orders preserve that accepted concept and include every assigned interface.

## Commission the protection concept before selecting attachments

Give the specialist the building geometry, intended use, structural and roof information, service connections and planned rooftop equipment. Include future installations that are already part of the client's brief. Obtain the accepted concept and scope matrix identifying design, components, installation, inspection and handover responsibilities.

[DEHN's rooftop-system guidance](https://www.dehn-international.com/en/master-lightning-and-surge-protection-rooftop-pv-systems) discusses different protection arrangements and the need to coordinate roofers, protection installers and electrical/PV trades. Its examples underline that separation and connections follow a specialist concept. Have the appointed protection specialist select the protection class and connection design for the actual building.

The feed-entry photograph below shows a defined material entering production. The panel profile and material leaving that process must remain the product reviewed by the roof and protection providers. Keep any attachment-related product requirements in the tooling documents rather than relying on an informal roof description.

## Roof-mounted route: buy compatible components and the rest of the system

Where the accepted concept uses roof-mounted components, obtain the actual roof attachment details from the responsible providers. Identify the panel profile, material, completed seam and any product-specific conditions relevant to the selected component. Record the roof provider's acceptance of the mechanical roof interface separately from the protection designer's acceptance of that component within the complete protection system.

Keep those two decisions visible in the purchase record. A mechanically suitable clamp is not the entire electrical-protection design. Match the protection component to the actual standing-seam identity and obtain the roof provider's accepted attachment detail.

Include conductors, connections, other specified equipment and installation only as assigned by the specialist's schedule. Map what the roof contractor supplies and what the protection contractor supplies. Price the inspection and completion evidence identified by the accepted design rather than buying a box of components and assigning the gaps to site labour.

Choose this route where the whole concept and roof interfaces accept it. Any change to panel section, material or rooftop layout goes back to the relevant designers before the buyer accepts the changed product.

## Isolated route: reserve the designed arrangement

An isolated protection package has its own physical interfaces and spatial requirements established by the specialist. Give the building coordinator those requirements so rooftop plant, solar equipment, access and replacement routes are coordinated. Procurement needs a drawable, buildable arrangement rather than a single word in the tender.

Obtain the specified supports and connections to the building under the accepted structural and protection designs. Identify installation dates, access occupation and any associated roof weathering work. Isolation does not automatically mean the roof contractor has no interface or that the building needs no supporting work.

Buy this route where the specialist's concept requires it or where an accepted alternative offers the preferable complete project arrangement. Do not move components or reduce reserved space merely to fit a later plant purchase. Re-coordinate the changed building through the responsible specialists first.

Record locations on the building's common grid and stated roof datum. Leave electrical calculations, separation requirements and protective measures with the qualified design team. The purchaser's useful action is to preserve their issued references across all affected orders.

## What the forming enquiry actually needs

The former supplier needs the controlled panel drawing, material, finish, output condition and acceptance method. Attach the relevant roof-interface requirements without asking the machinery bidder to undertake the protection design. If a different profile is proposed, obtain the responsible providers' accepted revised combination before changing tooling.

The roller photograph below illustrates the physical tooling sequence that produces the roof section. Keep the accepted section revision tied to the offered equipment and trial material. Product identity must survive the move from design coordination to a manufactured sample.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the cheapest clamp is treated as the protection package.** A roof buyer orders seam-fitting conductor holders for a solar-equipped warehouse, but the protection concept, wider electrical interfaces and completion scope have not been commissioned. Commission the specialist package and price its actual components, installation and evidence before placing the roof-interface order.

**Mistake (hypothetical): later rooftop plant occupies the isolated arrangement.** A mechanical order changes equipment positions without consulting the protection designer. Return the combined layout for specialist review and obtain the accepted revised arrangement before buying affected supports or roof pieces.

## Eight attachments for coordinated procurement

1. Accepted whole-building protection concept and responsible designer.
2. Building geometry and coordinated rooftop layout.
3. Roof-product identity, material and finished-seam detail.
4. Relevant mounted or isolated component-interface references.
5. Structural supports and roof weathering responsibilities.
6. Controlled panel drawing, lengths, quantities and trial criteria.
7. Installation dates, production location and access occupation.
8. Destination, power and design/installation/handover scope matrix.
