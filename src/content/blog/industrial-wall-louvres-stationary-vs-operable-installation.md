**Choose stationary louvres when the accepted normal-ventilation design requires a continuously open air path and the selected product meets its weather-exposure requirement. Choose an operable arrangement when the design requires closure or modulation and the offered complete assembly satisfies that duty.** Issue the airflow, exposure and operating sequence to the ventilation designer, then compare complete wall assemblies and their installation access. A larger nominal opening is not a substitute for the selected product's performance.

This decision concerns ordinary industrial wall intake or exhaust openings. The responsible project specialists separately determine any fire or smoke-control duty. Keep those functions in the actual accepted system scope rather than assuming an operable blade is automatically a fire or smoke damper.

The wall-corridor photograph shows restricted space beside an unfinished elevation. A louvre can need inside and outside tasks even where its visible front is one rectangular opening. Mark both sides and the crew's approach before allocating a single exterior platform.

## Decide whether the opening must close

The ventilation designer supplies the required airflow and allowable pressure loss for the actual operating modes. The owner supplies operating hours and closure requirements. If the brief calls for a fixed open path, compare eligible stationary assemblies including the specified accessories. If the brief requires controlled closure, define the complete louvre/damper/control arrangement before comparing bids.

[Greenheck's operable-louvre information](https://www.greenheck.com/products/louvers/operable) describes blades that open and close, commonly with electric actuators. It also distinguishes ambient-rain protection from wind-driven-rain performance in the open position. Select weather performance for the actual open operating state; closure capability answers a different part of the brief.

The same provider's [louvre FAQ](https://www.greenheck.com/resources/faqs/product-faqs/louvers-faqs) notes that accessories affect the offered package and that published raw-louvre airflow ratings exclude screens. Obtain the selected assembly's actual response with the required screens, filters or other accessories. Do not compare its bare product data with another bid's fully equipped opening.

## Treat weather exposure and airflow as one selection

Show the opening orientation and surrounding building geometry to the designer. Record the accepted weather-performance requirement and the operating modes during exposure. The provider should identify the exact louvre, accessory and drainage details responding to that brief.

A stationary external louvre with a separate internal control device is another possible complete arrangement when the responsible design accepts it. Compare it with an operable combination as an explicit alternative, including the additional component, connections, space and service positions. Do not quietly add an unpriced damper after declaring the stationary bid cheaper.

Retain any required acoustic function in the selected product schedule. An industrial opening next to occupied areas can have more than one performance obligation. The appointed designer decides those requirements; the buyer's role is to obtain a coherent package meeting all of them.

Use one row for each opening type. Several similar-looking units can have different orientations, operating sequences or service sides. Group them only when those relevant conditions match. The resulting quantity schedule gives the access contractor real repeated work rather than an averaged wall-area allowance.

## Receive the assembly, not an assumed one-piece delivery

Obtain the actual section sizes, masses and supplied state. A multi-section louvre creates field joints and handling objects different from a single assembly. Include support angles, fasteners, perimeter details and other items that the actual contract assigns to the installer.

[Greenheck's installation instructions](https://content.greenheck.com/public/DAMProd/Original/10016/485607StationaryNonDrainable_iom.pdf) distinguish single and multiple section deliveries and direct handling through frames or supports rather than blades. Use the chosen product's issued instructions through the responsible installer. The purchasing consequence is to obtain actual receiving and handling scope, not to infer it from opening area.

The yard photograph below shows an upright access assembly with its crawler and supporting feet. The wall-side arrangement occupies ground space independent of the louvre's own handling area. Coordinate both resources with the permitted inside and outside access zones.

## Schedule control handover before releasing access

For an operable assembly, identify who completes and accepts the control sequence. Include access for connection, inspection and any required completion attendance in the programme. A factory-mounted actuator does not complete its building power and control interfaces.

For either arrangement, obtain the product-specific cleaning, screen removal and other adopted maintenance tasks. Show how those faces remain accessible after fit-out and landscaping. Where a broad installation platform cannot return, buy a defined service route suited to the completed building rather than assuming the initial machine remains the answer.

## Two hypothetical louvre comparisons

**Mistake (hypothetical): Operable means weatherproof in every state.** A buyer selects a closing louvre but omits the required open-state wind-driven-rain response. The provider's actual assembly does not meet the issued exposure brief. Compare the nominated product at the operating state the building needs, then obtain an accepted revised assembly if required.

**Mistake (hypothetical): The stationary quote excludes its control device.** Another comparison prices a raw stationary louvre against a complete operable assembly, while the plant sequence requires closure. Add the separate accepted damper, controls and access to the stationary route before ranking complete totals.
