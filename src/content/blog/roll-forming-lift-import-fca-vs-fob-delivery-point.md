**Use an FCA discussion for containerised or multimodal equipment delivered to the buyer's nominated carrier at a precise agreed place. Use an FOB discussion for sea or inland-waterway cargo where the seller actually undertakes delivery on board the nominated vessel at the named shipment port.** Agree the complete term, named point and Incoterms® 2020 reference with the contracting parties. A familiar three-letter abbreviation is inadequate when it describes a handover nobody performs.

This choice concerns the delivery boundary of a defined machine shipment. It follows the decision about [modular container versus out-of-gauge transport](/blog/roll-forming-lift-container-vs-out-of-gauge-shipping/). The cargo format and the sales delivery term answer different questions: one identifies what travels; the other allocates specified delivery responsibilities for the sale.

## Start at the place where the seller hands over the cargo

Draw the proposed chain from equipment preparation to the buyer's receiving facility. Mark the seller's actual delivery point and the carrier that accepts the cargo there. For a container shipment, that point is often before vessel loading. Ask the parties to match the sale term to that handover instead of using an on-board event merely because the main journey is by sea.

The [ICC's Incoterms checklist](https://library.iccwbo.org/content/clp/Others/incoterms_2020_checklist_2024-update.pdf) directs users toward FCA for containers, multimodal transport and terminal delivery, and toward FOB for cargo delivered directly on board a ship. Its [FCA/FOB explanation](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/) distinguishes FCA at the seller's premises, loaded on the collecting vehicle, from FCA at another agreed place, ready for unloading from the arriving vehicle. Use the official rules and transaction advice for the actual agreement.

Name the point precisely enough for both logistics teams to book the same work. A city name alone leaves uncertainty about a workshop gate, consolidation warehouse, container terminal or vessel. A port name on an FOB sale still needs the operational arrangements for nominated vessel loading and notices.

The truck and workshop in the photograph represent an early transport interface. They help the buyer separate equipment preparation, collecting-vehicle loading, terminal work and sea carriage. Obtain an actual handling scope for each stage. No delivery term selects the machine's lifting points or an unloading method.

Fill this worksheet using the proposed sale term and the forwarder's matching quotation. Ask the commercial advisers to settle the applicable allocation; ask the equipment and transport parties to demonstrate that they can perform their allocated tasks.

Keep equipment acceptance, ownership and payment terms in their own contract provisions. The delivery term does not replace those records. A buyer should know separately when the equipment meets the agreed technical tests and when a specified transport risk changes hands.

## Machine scope is not hidden inside the trade term

An FCA offer for a lifting assembly is still an offer for that assembly. It does not acquire a chassis because the buyer writes a final site address. Likewise, an FOB quotation for a former says nothing about whether the separate lift and local integration are included. Preserve the machine and package list through every commercial revision.

For [ARC-T25HQ](/products/arc-t25hq-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift-40hq/), request a cargo-specific container packing response where that route is relevant. Compare [ARC-C25](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/) only against its actual offered transport configuration. The product names identify enquiry shortlists; the supplier's issued package data determines the goods in the delivery-term discussion.

The stored frames and components show why “one machine” is too short a cargo description. Identify packages, masses, dimensions and supporting/handling requirements from the equipment supplier's records. The forwarder then quotes the actual allocated transport work at the named place.

## Where two hypothetical orders go wrong

**FOB is copied onto a terminal-handover container order.** The seller's quotation covers delivery to a container terminal, while the purchase order says on-board delivery. A loss or charge between those events exposes the mismatch. Resolve the named event with the parties and advisers before booking; use a term matching the intended handover.

**FCA is written without its place.** The buyer prices collection from the supplier's premises, but the seller's amount assumes a different consolidation warehouse. Both totals appear competitive until the missing transfer is added. Put the complete address and actual point in the sale and obtain matching carrier quotations.
