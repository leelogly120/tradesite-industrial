**Buy a used large-deck ceiling platform only when its identified configuration, complete records, competent condition assessment and funded repairs support the first job, and its complete handover cost is lower than a comparable new offer or it meets a required date that the new offer cannot meet. Choose the new configuration when the used route lacks those entry conditions or advantages. Compare the same task and handover boundary before placing either order.**

This comparison concerns the asset being acquired. A machine's sale amount and the cost of obtaining supported access for the first contract are different totals. It differs from deciding whether an owner should employ a maintenance team or purchase a service contract. Establish which candidate is acceptable before building the ongoing support organisation around it.

## Put one identified machine on each side of the comparison

For the used route, record the maker, model, serial identity and actual fitted assemblies. Keep the seller's photographs, equipment records and condition-assessment report attached to that identity. A brochure for a similar machine is background information, not the record of the offered unit.

For the new route, identify the proposed configuration, included deck and accessories, documentation and delivery boundary. Compare both candidates against the same ceiling assignment and required receiving date. Assess the actual site and task for a new machine as well as a used candidate; use that assessment to select the arrangement.

[IPAF's guidance on buying a pre-owned MEWP](https://www.ipaf.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Guidance%20On%20Buying%20a%20Pre-Owned%20MEWP%20%28TE-2053-0426-1-en%29.pdf) identifies machine-specific instructions, relevant documentation, modification records and matching equipment identification as purchasing concerns. Apply the requirements relevant to the candidate and destination with competent local specialists. Keep a platform's records separate from those for a different machine carried in the same shipment.

## Complete the acquisition worksheet before accepting a sale amount

Use actual documents and attributable proposals in both columns. Give missing essential records a clear commercial consequence: remove the candidate from the purchasable shortlist until the seller supplies the required identified package. Do not replace absent history with a buyer's visual guess.

| Purchase input | Used candidate entry | New candidate entry | Owner and buying decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact configuration | Serial ID, fitted deck, accessories and modifications: ___ | Quoted model/configuration and included assemblies: ___ | Equipment lead fixes what is actually purchased |
| Attributable information | Applicable instructions, equipment identification and supplied records: ___ | Documentation schedule for the proposed configuration: ___ | Technical buyer rejects mismatched or incomplete packages |
| Condition and outstanding work | Competent assessment, defects and defined repair scope: ___ | Delivery inspection and acceptance scope: ___ | Appointed specialist establishes the handover requirements |
| First assignment | Actual ceiling/wall tasks, crew, tools and materials: ___ | The same controlled assignment: ___ | Installation lead prevents unlike duty comparisons |
| Site eligibility | Route/setup drawing and candidate loading information: ___ | Route/setup drawing and proposed loading information: ___ | Site engineer assesses the named positions and floor datum |
| Support resources | Identified parts, information and competent service route: ___ | Actual included support and separately offered resources: ___ | Owner establishes an operable support plan |
| Ready date | Repair, documents, transport and acceptance milestones: ___ | Production/supply, transport and acceptance milestones: ___ | Project manager tests the actual required handover date |
| Complete acquisition cost | Sale, assessment, repairs, transport and handover items: ___ | Quoted equipment, transport and equivalent handover items: ___ | Buyer compares the same funded endpoint |

Keep the seller's current condition statement separate from the appointed specialist's assessment and the receiving project's acceptance. Each record answers a different question. A trial on a seller's premises is not the structural assessment of the buyer's receiving floor.

## Price repairs and support as real purchases

Obtain a defined proposal for outstanding work before including the used candidate in the funded plan. Identify who performs the work, which parts and technical information it requires, and what completion records reach the buyer. Do not treat an unpriced repair list as a fixed acquisition budget.

[Genie's pre-owned aerials discussion](https://www.genielift.com/en/aerialpros/pre-owned-aerials) distinguishes documented used-equipment buying from an uncertain ownership-cost assumption. Use the actual seller's offer and machine history for this purchase. Any refurbishment, attendance or parts commitment belongs to its named provider and stated scope.

Assess the owner's service route for either candidate. A new machine's supplier contact is not the same as a local maintenance agreement. An experienced workshop also needs the information, competence and parts applicable to the identified machine. Use the [in-house versus specialist-service comparison](/blog/ceiling-platform-in-house-maintenance-vs-service-contractor/) for that separate ongoing ownership decision.

Apply the same required date to both totals. Include records, contracted repairs, loading/transport, any separately provided receiving work and the agreed acceptance endpoint. Use actual quotations and the owner's planning period. Include a supported service and interruption plan for that period, with any resale assumption shown separately.

## Two hypothetical failures when buying on appearance

**Hypothetical failure case: a fresh finish hides an incomplete purchase.** A buyer accepts a clean-looking machine without matching the instructions and modification record to its actual configuration. The receiving team cannot establish the intended supply and acceptance scope. Hold the transaction at the identified-records gate and use a complete alternative proposal instead of inventing the missing history.

**Hypothetical failure case: repairs consume the first contract's mobilisation window.** The buyer budgets only the sale and transport amounts. Essential repairs and their documented completion were never assigned. Build an attributable repair and handover programme first; choose the new candidate when the used route cannot meet the supported date and funded scope.
