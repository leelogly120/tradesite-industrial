Buy automatic packaging when repeat roof-panel pack families account for enough dispatched orders to keep the complete packaging cell economically occupied. Staff a flexible packing station for mixed lengths, unusual profiles, split-site deliveries and changing protection details when those orders would continue to need a separate crew alongside automation.

## Ask the customer what constitutes a finished pack

Start at collection. A tied stack can still need edge protection, a base, end protection, identification, waterproof transit packaging or a documented pack split by roof bay. Get the customer's accepted pack specification for the particular panel. Ask the carrier how it will receive the packed load and ask the installer which marks must be accessible at unloading. These three parties can require different information from the same package. Put the reconciled specification in the machine inquiry before asking for a strapping cycle.

A packaging cell offered for a particular product shape requires its own acceptance for long nested roof sheets, varying counts and vulnerable finishes. ## Build a pack census rather than a tonnage total

Choose an order period with everyday work, small orders and awkward project deliveries. Count actual dispatch packs by profile, size range, weight, package materials and label type. Distinguish repeat packs from manual exceptions and record the reason for each exception. A manufacturer that ships most of its area in long straight panels can still ship most of its individual packs as small job lots. Labour and label changes typically follow pack events. Transport appointments follow completed consignments.

| Dispatch record to complete | Record owner | Purchase consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter pack dimensions, mass, sheet count and finish | Production planner | Qualify repeat pack formats. |
| List bases, separators, corner pieces and wrap by order type | Packaging buyer | Compare running costs and stock changes. |
| Name the required ID, documents and mark visible after loading | Dispatch lead | Determine printing and checking scope. |
| Enter the shortest mixed run and the largest eligible batch | Shift supervisor | Model changeovers without averaging away exceptions. |
| Record the reshipment, rejection or repacking reason and expense | Quality manager | Identify defects the proposal actually addresses. |
| Map pack outfeed, holding locations and collection windows | Warehouse manager | Check whether faster packing frees dispatch capacity. |

Price the packaging route on a full collection day's orders. Give both bidders the same cut list and the same vehicle appointments. Ask them to include staging and sorting, first-pack setup, changes in materials, label verification, finishing odd packs, change to the next run and clearing the completed pack. Include people who replenish consumables or remove full packages even if the brochure calls the main station automatic. On the manual route include its equipment, space, training and peak staffing rather than comparing machinery with an unpriced labour allowance.

## Allocate the exception work before placing the order

Record where a part-finished pack goes when a panel is missing, a label is wrong or a job is split at the customer's request. Ask the provider to show an accepted recovery route with representative material during the agreed trial. If the automatic cell must stay occupied while a missing piece is produced, include the displaced packs and the cost of catching up. If a staffed exception station can complete that pack independently, budget its footprint and retained personnel. Treat these as alternative installed layouts with different costs.

**Mistake: Buying for sheets per hour.** An estimator multiplies rated line output by working hours and values all those sheets as automatic packs. Half the transport appointments contain mixed panel marks with special separators. Recalculate the share of complete eligible packs and retain the people and supplies needed for the rest.

**Mistake: A secure pack arrives in the wrong order.** A crew prepares robust bundles but combines two roof areas in an order that requires both to be opened for the first delivery. The customer incurs extra sorting and asks for separate labels and collection groups. Add the split and accessible identification to the dispatch acceptance trial before buying the automatic station.

Prefer automation if the tested repeat families, retained exception work and real collection windows leave a measurable saving after ownership and service. Prefer the staffed route when orders pay for flexibility and dispatch already keeps pace with accepted production. Where the constraint is the absence of a reliable stack or pack support, price that shorter improvement separately and compare its benefit on the same order period.

## Eight RFQ attachments for dispatch-ready output

1. Provide actual pack records and each customer's accepted format.
2. Attach panel drawings, lengths, finishes and pack masses.
3. List required protection materials, separators and identification.
4. Show the stacking handover and dispatch holding area.
5. Give a representative mixed shift and collection appointments.
6. State exception packs, short orders and destination splits.
7. Describe the acceptance check on delivered appearance and identification.
8. Request installed and operating costs for all retained manual work.
