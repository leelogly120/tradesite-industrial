Choose a defined controls retrofit when the existing line produces the required panels, its mechanical condition supports continued use, and a competent provider offers an integrated renewal with documented acceptance. Choose a replacement line when the required product, mechanical condition or complete renewal cost makes retaining the old equipment unattractive. If the problem is not yet diagnosed, commission a documented assessment before ordering either option.

This is a capital decision, not an electrical repair guide. The buyer defines the required output, complete equipment boundary, outage and acceptance result. The competent machinery provider defines and validates the engineering work. Replacing a display or controller alone is not evidence that the whole line is fit for continued production.

## Separate the reason for renewal from the equipment's age

Write down the actual business problem: an unsupported control component, repeated stoppages, lost job records, unreliable accepted lengths, a different product requirement or worn mechanical equipment. Keep symptoms separate from a provider's diagnosis. A stopped line and a panel-quality problem do not automatically have the same cause.

[Formtek's rebuild and retrofit service](https://www.formtekgroup.com/service-and-support/machine-and-equipment-rebuilds-reconditioning-retrofit/) describes engineering review of existing machinery as part of its own renewal offering. [Beck Automation](https://www.beckautomation.com/services) also identifies roll-forming control systems as a specialist service category. These examples establish that renewal is a defined provider scope, not that ARCLIFT supplies those services or that either provider accepts your particular machine.

## Define the complete renewal boundary

Inventory the existing line by its actual equipment identity and available documents. Include the input equipment, former, cutting arrangement, receiving equipment and interfaces used in production. Identify what the provider will retain, replace, re-engineer, inspect and test. Request the corresponding drawing, software/documentation and handover deliverables in the written proposal.

If the former operates with a lifting system, include that arrangement in the assessment boundary. A control change on one machine does not authorise altered behaviour of another machine or the combined installation. Have the responsible providers identify the integrated acceptance and safety review needed for the agreed configuration. Do not use this investment comparison as permission to modify interlocks or operating limits.

Establish what support remains after handover: controlled backup files, documented restoration arrangements, identified hardware, user instructions and the provider's actual service scope. Put those deliverables into the quotation. A new screen with undocumented software creates another unsupported dependency rather than solving the original business problem.

Keep the decision conditional on those records. Retain the line when the renewal provider covers the necessary work, the retained mechanics serve the product, and the complete operating case beats replacement. Replace it when those conditions fail and a defined new line provides the required output within the buyer's commercial plan.

## Plan the outage before committing the purchase

The comparison period starts when the line stops serving orders and ends when accepted production resumes. Include assessment, design work, parts, installation, trials, staff familiarisation and handover in the provider's actual plan. Use quoted dates and responsibilities; do not substitute a generic promised number of days.

Price the order-cover route separately. It can be pre-produced stock, an identified outside supplier or another approved internal line. Check the exact profile, material and order dates against that route. A general statement that “another factory can help” is not a deliverable order-cover plan.

## Avoid two false economies

**Hypothetical failure case: the control panel is renewed while the wrong product remains.** The business needs a new panel section, but the budget assumes a control upgrade will make the retained tooling suitable. Define the new product first and obtain a process/tooling proposal. If the existing line cannot serve it under a competent renewal proposal, compare a new line or purchased panels.

**Hypothetical failure case: the lowest quote omits integrated restart.** The offer replaces specified electronics but leaves drawings, interfaces and production acceptance outside its scope. The buyer budgets only the component work and discovers the missing responsibilities during shutdown. Compare complete handed-over production results, with the assessment and acceptance responsibilities written into each offer.

## Send a renewal decision pack

Attach these eight items to the RFQ:

1. Existing machine identities and the available original documents.
2. Required panel drawings, material specifications and order mix.
3. Accepted sample and recent quality/reject records.
4. Fault and stoppage history, including the actual support problem.
5. A competent mechanical-condition assessment.
6. The complete equipment and interface boundary.
7. The outage window and priced order-cover arrangement.
8. Required handover documentation and production acceptance criteria.
