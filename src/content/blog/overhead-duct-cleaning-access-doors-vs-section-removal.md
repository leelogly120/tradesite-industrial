**Install accepted reusable service openings when the duct assembly permits them and the specified openings provide the required internal cleaning and inspection coverage. Buy sectional removal when openings cannot deliver that result or the accepted refurbishment scope already requires replacing the affected sections.** Have the cleaning and mechanical specialists mark the access locations and restored condition before ordering a cleaning campaign or platform. The comparison ends with clean, closed and accepted ductwork.

Reaching a duct's outside surface is different from entering tools and inspecting its inside. A personnel platform solves the installer-position problem. It does not create an acceptable service opening, determine the cleaning method or reinstate insulation and fire protection. Allocate those tasks within the actual mechanical package.

The deck-to-roof relationship below shows why an opening needs a working-face section. The cleaner needs its accepted tool path and the installer needs space to work on the closure, not merely a point directly below the duct.

## Follow the internal cleaning coverage

Obtain a located inventory of the ductwork and components included in the campaign. The cleaning specialist identifies the required result, method, tool-entry positions and inspection coverage. Separate excluded or inaccessible components explicitly so their treatment has a provider before the wider order is awarded.

[NADCA's general specification](https://nadca.com/sites/nadca/files/NADCA_2021_General_Specification.pdf), under HVAC system preparation, calls for existing or new service openings suited to tool entry and visual inspection. It also addresses retaining structural, thermal and functional system integrity. That specification supports using suitable existing openings first and pricing accepted new openings or a defined mechanical alternative where coverage remains incomplete.

Ask the mechanical/system specialist to accept the proposed opening locations and closure construction for the actual duct assembly. Include insulation, lining, fire-rated construction and other relevant functions in that review. An opening suitable for one ordinary duct section is not automatically suitable for every system on the route.

## A permanent door buys repeated access

A reusable door or panel is useful where the adopted service plan needs the same location again. Obtain its accepted product detail, opening dimensions, service clearance and restoration scope. Record who installs and inspects it, then transfer its location and care requirements to the facility's asset records.

Tie the permanent opening's value to the repeated service task it actually serves. A door that admits one tool but leaves a required branch unserved does not complete the cleaning purchase. The specialist should connect each opening to its coverage on the route drawing. Where the project needs extra access, price it before crediting the permanent-door solution with whole-network coverage.

For sectional removal, identify the exact objects to be disconnected, handled, cleaned or renewed and reinstalled. The mechanical contractor defines the assembly work and accepted reinstatement. Component handling, temporary support and waste responsibilities need separate providers where the actual work requires them. A platform should not inherit those functions by default.

## Compare repeated care with a one-time construction scope

Where the facility has an adopted recurring cleaning plan, obtain actual door and removal-route prices for the specified events. Include the space released, access mobilisation and reinstatement each requires. Use the owner's supported interval rather than inventing a lifetime cleaning schedule.

For a one-time refurbishment, recurring access still deserves a named decision. If reusable openings serve no retained future requirement, compare their initial cost against the complete removal alternative. If recurring service needs them, include their delivered drawings and maintenance responsibility in the project handover.

## Price the actual released building condition

The operator identifies the work zones, isolation and production arrangements accepted for the campaign. Obtain the proposed personnel equipment's loading and dimensions for the responsible floor/route review. Include movements between locations, waiting areas and withdrawal after reinstatement.

A long sequence of adjacent opening tasks can justify comparing a large deck with localized alternatives. A small number of dispersed points can justify a task-specific access service instead. Decide from the coverage map and retained physical tasks, not the total duct length alone.

Completion needs a clear boundary. The cleaning provider supplies the specified result evidence; the mechanical contractor restores the assembly; the appointed quality owners accept their work. Schedule personnel access through the actual elevated checks that remain. Do not assume every acceptance activity occurs at floor level.

## Two hypothetical internal-access mistakes

**A door is counted as whole-branch coverage:** A buyer orders one new panel at a convenient underside location. The cleaning provider's method cannot serve the far side of a branch from that opening. Have the specialist map tool and inspection coverage before awarding the door locations and access period.

**Dismantling excludes restoring the assembly:** Another bid prices removal and cleaning but leaves reinstated joints, insulation and protection to an unnamed trade. Compare the fully restored mechanical result with the reusable-door route, including those actual costs and required checks.
