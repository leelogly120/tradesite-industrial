**Choose a bay-wide overhead crane package when the process needs the accepted load routes across that bay and the complete runway and supporting structure can be delivered. Choose freestanding workstation cranes when the required lifts remain within defined cells and their columns, foundations and transfer interfaces fit the production layout. Buy personnel access for the installation and service positions in the selected package.** A larger ceiling platform does not replace either material-handling system.

The factory buyer is purchasing a future production arrangement, not deciding where to repair an existing crane. The key output is a map of loads moving between actual process positions. Floor space, building work, product transfers and later access all belong in that investment. An isolated crane price omits the physical system that makes it useful.

## Follow the product through the building

Ask the process team to list the objects that move, the start and receiving positions, the required movement paths and the work that occurs at each station. Keep actual object masses, presentation requirements and frequency in the specialist handling brief. The crane supplier designs and offers the handling equipment against those inputs; personnel-access equipment is a separate purchase.

A bay-wide proposal needs the supplier's actual coverage map and complete support route. Obtain the crane, runway and building interfaces as one coordinated scope, with the relevant responsible designers identified. The [Konecranes overhead-crane range](https://www.konecranes.com/equipment/overhead-cranes) illustrates the equipment category. Select a configuration from the real process requirement, rather than treating a manufacturer category page as a project design.

For freestanding workstation systems, draw the cells and their support columns inside the production layout. [Gorbel describes freestanding bridge cranes](https://www.gorbel.com/products/cranes/workstation-cranes-and-monorails/free-standing-bridge-crane) supported from the floor. That changes the building interface; it does not remove the need for an accepted foundation and supporting arrangement. The specialist proposal determines the actual reactions, columns and coverage.

Compare the two systems against the same complete process. If a workstation route ends before a required transfer, include the actual resource and receiving interface that completes it. If a bay-wide crane supplies movements outside the business requirement, keep their value separate from the mandatory work. Extra coverage earns its place only through a defined use.

## Price the supports and the interfaces before ranking crane prices

An overhead package can involve structural work at the runway and building. A freestanding package occupies floor positions and needs its own supporting design. Price the actual work issued for both alternatives. Do not assume that all overhead cranes use the existing roof, or that a freestanding system can be anchored anywhere on the existing slab.

The facility operator should mark forklift routes, storage, maintenance areas and future expansion positions. Add the proposed crane columns, service boundaries and any excluded areas to that map. An available square of floor at tender can already have a production purpose. Include the actual relocation or lost-use cost where a support arrangement occupies it.

## Purchase a usable installation sequence

Have the crane installer identify which activities require personnel at each location and which need specialist component handling. Those resources often meet at an interface, but they remain different functions. A broad work deck is purchased for the crew, tools and permitted task materials in its offered configuration. Crane assemblies and production loads retain their own designed handling route.

Include the order in which the structure, handling system and production equipment enter the bay. Price actual access before and after permanent machines occupy the floor. An installation plan that relies on a clear hall must name the date and possession that make the hall available. Later specialist checks and service visits need actual working positions too.

## Two capital purchases that leave the process unfinished

**Mistake: workstation coverage stops at the cell boundary.** A hypothetical factory prices local cranes for separate operations but has no funded transfer between them. The final process still needs a load movement that neither accepted offer serves. Add an actual inter-cell transfer proposal or choose the bay-wide system whose approved coverage completes the route. Rank the resulting complete totals.

**Mistake: the bay crane is bought without its supporting construction.** Another buyer compares an equipment-only price with a freestanding system that includes its structure and installation. The owner later has to purchase runway and building work outside the accepted budget. Align the support, installation and acceptance boundaries before deciding that either crane architecture is cheaper.
