An equipment access route can reach the middle of a courtyard while the proposed **panel output route remains trapped inside it**. A roof-forming lift raises a compatible roof-panel roll former toward the intended receiving level. The machine's presence at that position does not establish that the emerging panel has a connected destination, or that the equipment can later leave through the space remaining around it.

This guide develops a courtyard plan with four separate boundaries: arrival, forming position, panel corridor and later withdrawal dependency. The [crawler access-route guide](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-access-route-survey/) follows the arriving equipment. Here the buyer's additional question is what the bounded courtyard does to the output concept.

## Map the courtyard as connected spaces

### Locate the only connected entry

Identify each genuine connection between the external site and the courtyard, including a passage, unfinished wall opening or other proposed access. Do not draw an entrance merely because the architectural plan contains a door symbol. The equipment party needs the actual proposed state and route geometry for its assessment. The courtyard drawing should show where that arrival route ends and how it relates to the intended forming position. Otherwise a surveyed entrance and an independently drawn machine position can conceal a missing connection between them.

### Keep the courtyard boundaries recognizable

Name the enclosing building faces, projections, columns and fixed features that define the space. Tie them to project references rather than treating the courtyard as an empty rectangle. A canopy can occupy space above an apparently clear ground strip, while a projecting facade feature can affect the relationship at another level. Show the relevant section locations on the plan. The useful buyer input is the real bounded volume requiring assessment, not a general claim that there is ample space inside the building perimeter.

### Identify the roof destination separately

Mark the first panel receiver and the roof area it serves. The intended panel route may cross a boundary of the courtyard even though the forming equipment remains inside. That relationship needs to be shown in both location and level. Avoid assuming that an open view of the sky establishes a usable output path. The [roof-panel handover guide](/blog/elevated-roll-forming-roof-panel-handover-zones/) covers external support and receipt; the courtyard plan should reveal the place where those separate functions must connect to the outlet.

## Separate the machine from its output corridor

### Draw the line's emerging-panel direction

The former has a material inlet and an outlet. Ask for their orientation on the proposed equipment arrangement, then place the intended emerging-panel corridor on the courtyard drawing. The equipment outline and that corridor answer different questions. A machine can be illustrated within the available rectangle while its output points toward an enclosing wall. Do not resolve the mismatch by assuming reverse feeding, machine rotation or a particular transfer technique. Request the actual configuration response that relates the line to the roof receiver.

### Show occupied space at the shared endpoint

Identify the expected coil-supply location, separately arriving modules and other project resources only to the extent their proposed arrangement is known. Their route may share the same courtyard entrance used by the crawler. A plan prepared before the machine arrives can overstate the space available for those later items. Compare the occupied destination, not merely the empty arrival route. This does not select a delivery order; it identifies the need for the responsible parties to reconcile their actual handoffs in the same physical area.

### Record the building stage at closure points

A courtyard may be accessible through an unfinished opening that will later be closed. Name that dependency and the expected construction stage, without assuming that removing finished work will be an available solution. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/workplacetransport/information/safeworkplace.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE workplace-route guidance</a> discusses obstructions and changing temporary routes. The relevant lesson here is to describe the route when it is needed, including its later condition. It does not establish whether this machine may pass through the opening or prescribe how it should leave.

## Compare two courtyard proposals

### Keep the project task identical

Consider a **hypothetical courtyard** with one connected access passage and an intended receiver on the eastern roof. Proposal A places the machine near the passage, leaving a shorter equipment arrival route but directing the forming outlet across the courtyard. Proposal B places the machine nearer the eastern face, changing both the machine's arrival endpoint and the space left around the former. The trade-off concerns the two different connections, not the apparent convenience of either position. These descriptions are alternatives to investigate, not feasible arrangements established by this article. Both should use the same panel task, receiver and building stage.

### Follow the physical difference beyond arrival

For A, ask how the emerging-panel corridor connects to the eastern receiver and which other courtyard uses it crosses. For B, ask what route connects the entering machine to the eastern position in its proposed state, and what space remains for the agreed material interface. A convenient entrance does not answer A's panel question; proximity to the receiver does not answer B's machine route. The comparison should preserve those two different gaps rather than merging them into a single statement that courtyard access is pending.

### Compare the later boundary as well

Suppose the passage at A remains open but the area beside B will contain completed building work before equipment departure. That difference belongs in the proposal comparison now, even if the project has not yet established withdrawal arrangements. It can alter which concept is worth developing. The table shows what the buyer can ask without selecting a manoeuvre, handling process or construction sequence. The outcome is a set of connected location questions for the equipment, logistics and roof-work reviewers.

| Courtyard relationship | Proposal A question | Proposal B question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry to proposed machine position | Does the stated arrival endpoint leave the intended output direction meaningful? | Which actual route connects the passage to the more distant equipment position? |
| Outlet to eastern receiver | What external corridor crosses the courtyard and who defines it? | What receiver relationship remains between the nearer outlet and roof? |
| Material supply after machine arrival | Which courtyard uses share the cross-courtyard space? | What occupied-space drawing describes the material interface beside the machine? |
| Separately delivered components | Where do their journeys meet the equipment already present? | Does their nominated handoff remain accessible in the proposed occupied condition? |
| Departure at the later building stage | What passage and surrounding area remain available? | Which completed work changes the earlier arrival assumptions? |

## Prepare a bounded layout enquiry

### Send one connected courtyard drawing

Use one location plan to connect the external handoff, passage, candidate machine position and roof receiver. Add section references wherever overhead or vertical relationships matter. Label the proposed equipment state from actual supplier information rather than drawing a generic folded machine symbol. The [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) explains the architecture being considered. Its tracked base does not remove the need to establish how the complete arrangement relates to the bounded courtyard and the intended material route.

- Name the actual courtyard connection and the external transport route leading to it.
- Identify enclosing faces, projections and relevant overhead features on shared references.
- Mark the proposed ground position, outlet height requirement and inlet and outlet directions separately.
- Show the first roof receiver and the full requested emerging-panel corridor.
- Describe the occupied destination for material and separately delivered assemblies.
- Identify any construction closure or completed work affecting later withdrawal review.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize a disconnected concept

A courtyard concept may not fit where its connected routes cannot be described. It remains incomplete where the machine route ends outside its proposed position, the panel corridor ends before the receiver, or later construction removes the only described connection. Those are different discontinuities. A wider gate does not necessarily answer the panel route; a higher outlet does not create departure space. Do not infer a remedy involving removal of components, overhead lifting or passage through completed work. Each would be a new proposal requiring its own actual scope and technical review, rather than an adjustment authorized by a courtyard sketch.

## FAQ

#### Does a machine entering the courtyard prove the concept works?

No. Entry describes one journey. The former's outlet must still relate to the roof receiver, and the occupied courtyard must be considered for material interfaces and later dependencies. A complete enquiry keeps these relationships distinct. It does not turn a successful passage measurement into a conclusion about production, support, receiving work or future equipment departure.

#### Is an open courtyard automatically clear above the machine?

No such assumption should be made. Relevant projections, enclosing structures, services and the required panel path need their own location and level information. Describe what has actually been surveyed and mark missing areas. A photograph looking upward or an architectural courtyard label cannot establish the usable space for the proposed equipment and emerging-panel arrangement.

#### Can a later wall closure be treated as a site issue only?

It is a project responsibility, but it also affects whether the proposed equipment concept has a described later boundary. Tell the equipment and logistics reviewers which opening changes and when. They can then assess the actual proposal with the responsible site party. This article does not authorize delaying the closure or altering completed construction to release equipment.

#### Is this the same as a general access survey?

It uses the access survey's arriving-state information but adds the courtyard's output and occupied-destination relationships. The distinctive deliverable is one connected plan showing where the machine journey meets the panel journey inside a bounded space. Detailed turning, unloading and withdrawal arrangements remain outside this buyer comparison and need the actual equipment and appointed parties.
