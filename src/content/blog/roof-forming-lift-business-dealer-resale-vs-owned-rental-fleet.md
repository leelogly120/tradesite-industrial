Choose dealer resale when your business can specify, supply and hand over machinery but cannot fund and resource a complete hire cycle. Build an owned fleet when accepted compatible bookings cover that cycle's cost over the defined planning period, named providers cover inspection, maintenance, transport and staffing, and the asset calendar has no conflicting commitments. Use a mixed model only with separately identified sales and fleet assets, commitments and costs.

The decision is not whether one demonstration machine is useful. It is which continuing business your capital, people and customer promises will support. A roof-forming lift raises a compatible former; it is not a general personnel-access machine or a substitute for the roofing contractor's complete installation package.

## Define the customer's purchased result

A resale order delivers identified equipment and its agreed documents, configuration and handover. The seller needs an accurate product brief, delivery scope and a defined route for support responsibilities included in that sale. Keep those responsibilities in the actual offer rather than copying a manufacturer's service network into the dealer's claims.

A hire-fleet order makes an owned asset available for a defined project duty over an agreed period. Specify whether the purchased service is equipment only or a wider supported production arrangement. Identify the former, material handling, operators, receiving, inspection and return responsibilities that the fleet business actually undertakes.

The customer endpoint determines the required resources. A machine-only hire and accepted panel-output service are different offers. The [hire versus operated-service guide](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-machine-hire-vs-operated-service/) explains that customer scope; this investment decision asks whether your business can support repeated fleet cycles rather than sell machines.

## An owned fleet continues working after the customer returns it

Budget the full cycle from accepted dispatch through delivery, project use, return, inspection, any required work and release for the next compatible duty. Give every activity an actual owner and provider. Include transport and between-job space rather than treating all non-hire days as cost-free availability.

Match the proposed fleet configuration to recurring project briefs. Compare former interfaces, outlet geometry, site routes and support arrangements for the actual intended customers. A list of unrelated enquiries is not a fleet duty profile. The business needs a defined way to accept or decline each job against the asset's documented configuration.

## Complete a resale-and-fleet responsibility map

| Business input | Dealer resale entry | Owned-fleet entry | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer result | Equipment, documents and handover endpoint ___ | Defined equipment/service duty and return endpoint ___ | Commercial lead; identifies the actual sold scope |
| Supported demand | Named equipment orders and release dates ___ | Compatible project periods and accepted duties ___ | Sales lead; separates orders from enquiries |
| Configuration | Customer-specific equipment schedule ___ | Fleet configuration and job acceptance basis ___ | Technical lead; defines product eligibility |
| Continuing resources | Included sales/support responsibilities ___ | Dispatch, inspection, maintenance, staff and support ___ | Operations lead; prices what remains after delivery |
| Transport | Named delivery handovers and providers ___ | Outbound, between-job and return routes ___ | Logistics lead; establishes the complete movement cycle |
| Material interfaces | Supplied former/module boundary ___ | Former, coil and receiving responsibilities per hire ___ | Project coordinator; prevents an incomplete production offer |
| Between orders | Stock and sales commitments ___ | Storage, required work and release for next duty ___ | Asset manager; identifies non-customer occupation |
| Complete economics | Actual purchase, selling and support cost classes ___ | Same period with hire-cycle costs and accepted bookings ___ | Finance lead; tests the selected operating model |

Use matching periods when evaluating the two models. Keep resale stock and fleet equipment separately identified in the records. An asset committed to a hire is not simultaneously available for an unrestricted sale and another project booking.

## Test a mixed model without double-counting the asset

A dealer can own a demonstration or fleet machine, but each use needs a defined priority, availability and handover boundary. Record how a sale affects existing bookings and how project use affects the condition and records of an asset offered for sale. Have the commercial and finance advisers establish the actual arrangements rather than assuming every machine remains interchangeable stock.

Likewise, fleet operation does not require owning every project resource. Defined haulage, specialist support or a separate accepted production service can be purchased from identified providers. The fleet business still needs a complete costed offer for the customer endpoint it sells.

If the continuing resource case is absent, retain a defined resale model instead of promising fleet availability. If repeat compatible duties and the full cycle are funded, buy the configuration supported by those duties rather than the largest catalogue class.

## Two hypothetical business-model failures

**Failure case: selling resources are mistaken for rental resources.** A dealer has quotation and delivery staff but budgets no return inspection, maintenance or between-job transport. Price the full owned-asset cycle before advertising a fleet service.

**Failure case: the same unit supports every forecast.** The plan counts one asset as sold stock, a demonstration and two overlapping hires. Separate the commitments by equipment identity and dates, then choose the operating model that the real resources can support.

## Build the equipment investment brief

Attach eight inputs to the RFQ:

1. Resale, owned-fleet or separately defined mixed-business scope.
2. Supported customer orders or compatible project-duty schedule.
3. Actual panel profiles, former arrangements and material interfaces.
4. Outlet/site geometry and reviewed support requirements for the intended duties.
5. Vehicle, carrier and complete transport scope.
6. Named inspection, maintenance, staffing and support responsibilities.
7. Dispatch, return and between-job release arrangements.
8. Destination, quantity, documentation and equipment acceptance requirements.
