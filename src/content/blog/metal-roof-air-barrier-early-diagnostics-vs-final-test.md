Buy targeted early air-leak diagnostics alongside the required final enclosure test when critical roof-to-wall junctions will be buried before the building can be tested. Keep the final test and specified inspections when the contract permits that scope, the critical junctions remain accessible for correction and the schedule allows a retest. Have the enclosure designer mark the junctions and their concealment dates before comparing prices.

## Specify the result you need from each visit

A hold point at a roof-to-wall junction is useful when the roofing and wall trades can still reach the connection and act on the result. Have the enclosure consultant identify the representative detail and test objective. On a large roof, a documented defect and correction at the first accessible section can inform the same junction in the next bays. Where the geometry, adjoining layer or installation team changes, ask the consultant whether another location warrants a visit. The proposed early test must be feasible at that stage of construction. If the test setup needs a more complete enclosure, price documented junction inspections before concealment and book the first valid whole-enclosure test date.

The U.S. Unified Facilities Guide Specification for building enclosure commissioning describes inspections while the air barrier remains accessible and gives examples of smaller tests for early construction. It also sets out a separate completed-enclosure pressure test and distinguishes the reporting for pressure tests and diagnostics. Use that structure when scoping bids. Take the actual thresholds, methods and responsible parties from your project specification. [UFGS 01 91 19 Building Enclosure Commissioning](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/DOD/UFGS/UFGS%2001%2091%2019.pdf)

Ask for two deliverables with different reporting purposes. The early visit identifies the detail inspected or tested, where defects were found and what must be repaired before that detail is built out. The final report records the measured air leakage of the contracted test boundary and compares it with the specified criterion. The ASHRAE Handbook discusses ASTM E3158 in its guidance on testing tall, multizone buildings. Have the test provider price the method the project specifies. Retain the final result in the base scope and use the early report to direct repairs while the work remains accessible. [ASHRAE Handbook, commercial and institutional infiltration](https://handbook.ashrae.org/handbooks/F25/IP/F25_Ch16/F25_Ch16_ip.aspx)

## The roof test date depends on more than roof progress

Trace the air barrier on a section down to the adjoining wall and around the tested space. Use the line the designer identifies on the issued drawings, even if another layer is the visible roof finish. At an eave, establish which party completes the connection between the roof and wall, which party inspects it and what work would make it inaccessible. On a building with multiple areas or allowances for temporary openings, have the test specialist set out the test boundaries and preparation according to the project method.

Give both test bidders the same marked boundary drawings and readiness assumptions. Use the area basis the project method specifies for that boundary. List which loading doors, louvers or temporary openings the test agency expects the site team to prepare, and assign this work in the quote. Include equipment access, power, the report and any return rates. Tie each early visit to the last accessible date and each final visit to the agreed enclosure-ready milestone.

In the tender return, ask the specialist to break out the early diagnostics and final measurement, including any observation or support work that the contract already requires. If every critical connection remains accessible and the specification allows final testing alone, use the lower-cost qualifying visit plan. If a failed final result would require opening finished roof or wall work, compare the early visit with the cost of removal, repair, reinstatement and retesting. Include both the roofing and wall trades in that estimate.

## Two mistakes that obscure the test result

**Mistake one.** An owner hypothetically requests a roof-only price for a building-level acceptance test. The quote includes a look at roof junctions and omits the pressure test and boundary preparation called for in the contract. Name the final test as a base deliverable and price the local visit with its own defect report.

**Mistake two.** In another example, a team uses the first eave inspection to release a lower roof where the wall layer follows a different path. The next junction gets covered without a planned review. Mark each recurring detail family and issue a separate release where the approved connection changes.

## RFQ for the commissioning visits

1. Contract test method, acceptance limit and required reporting.
2. Issued boundary drawings and area calculation basis.
3. Roof-to-wall sections and repeated detail locations by grid.
4. Planned sequence and last accessible date for each connection.
5. Scope and report requested for each early visit.
6. Opening preparation, temporary works, access and power allowances.
7. Repair responsibility, report closure and prices for return visits.
8. Roof panel release dates, crew handoff and any forming requirements.
