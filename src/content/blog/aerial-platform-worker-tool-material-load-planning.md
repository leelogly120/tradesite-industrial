A ceiling inspection and a component replacement can need the same crew while presenting very different platform requirements. Inspection may involve handheld tools at defined points. A replacement proposal may place the new component, the removed component, packaging and tools in the task area together. A useful task-load schedule shows those actual objects and their proposed positions before anyone compares a platform offer with the work.

This guide concerns the people, tools, materials and accessories present together in a particular task state. It supports discussion of a [crawler ceiling platform](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms) or another documented work-deck proposal. A roof-former lift has a different equipment duty; its lifting figure cannot supply a personnel-platform limit. The schedule helps request the right configuration information and makes no capacity calculation or material-handling permission.

## Define the task and platform arrangement together

### Distinguish the work being done from the crew count

“Two workers” is not a load description. Record what those workers wear or carry, which tools operate at the same time, which materials and containers are present, where they are positioned and whether anything changes during entry, travel, setup, work or withdrawal.

Break the job into stable task states. Inspection with handheld instruments, panel replacement with packaged components and service work with powered tools can place different demands on the work area even with the same crew. The [ceiling-platform project-data guide](/blog/crawler-ceiling-wall-panel-platform-project-data/) provides the wider project-input structure; this article focuses only on the task-load schedule.

### Identify the actual main, extension or removable deck

Limits can depend on the exact configuration, platform arrangement, permitted attachment, task state and instructions. Record the document identifier and applicable configuration beside every comparison. If the schedule and approved document do not use the same definition, stop and ask the responsible technical owner to reconcile them.

Identify whether the documents describe the main platform, an extension region, a removable platform or a combination explicitly covered by one configuration. These are separate descriptions, not interchangeable names for the same usable area. A large removable platform is not automatically an option for every host machine. The [under-ceiling platform buyer guide](/blog/crawler-under-ceiling-platform-buyers-guide/) explains how to establish that procurement boundary.

### Use task and equipment records that describe the same proposal

Give every input a source, date, owner and revision. People counts should come from the approved method; tool and material information should come from controlled task records; configuration limits should come from current approved equipment documents. Mark estimates as estimates and replace them before release.

A preliminary task brief can identify missing material or tool information without supplying a guessed allowance. Keep the actual requirement beside the unanswered configuration question. The aim is to compare a proposal with the work it must support, rather than choose a platform from a photograph or an unrelated rating.

## Show the people and objects present at the same time

### Record occupants by task role

Record each role present in the task state, including workers, an instructor or specialist if that person is physically present, and any other occupant permitted by the approved method. List task-related protective equipment when its mass or placement matters to the review.

Do not publish personal names, medical information or individual identifiers. Use role labels in the schedule and keep training and authorisation records in the employer’s controlled system. Crew count remains a project input until the exact instructions and site method confirm it.

### Include the whole tool and material requirement

Identify each tool, battery, cable, hose, test device, consumable, replacement component, fastener, waste item and container. Record quantity, individual source value where controlled, combined task quantity, dimensions and any storage or securing method proposed by the task owner. Identify objects that extend beyond the platform, with their proposed connections and support locations; do not omit them because only part is visible in the work area.

Do not assume that a tool bag, rack, overhanging item or long component is permitted. Do not use lifting-system tonnage as a platform limit. If an item requires a fixture or attachment, list that interface separately and hold the task until the approved documentation addresses it.

### Distinguish a simultaneous load from shift consumption

The schedule should show what is present at one time, not the total material consumed during a shift. Mark where people and items are intended to be placed and how the arrangement changes during the task. Include removed components and waste if they remain in the work area.

This positional record does not calculate distribution. It lets the competent reviewer identify concentrated, offset, unsecured or changing conditions that need exact technical information. Treat any uncertain position as an open item.

## Compare coexistence, position and external connections

### Keep replacement and removed material in one snapshot

In a hypothetical ceiling-maintenance proposal, a replacement component is present before the old component leaves the task area. A schedule that lists only the replacement understates that proposed coexistence; a shift-total list can make the opposite mistake by counting every component as present together. Ask the task owner to describe the actual simultaneous proposal, including containers and retained packaging, without treating the schedule as permission to perform the replacement.

That snapshot describes a proposed coexistence, not a work sequence. The equipment and task-method parties still need to resolve the transfer arrangement. Keeping both components visible in the enquiry does not establish how either may enter or leave the platform.

### Treat a changed region as a changed proposal

A second hypothetical comparison keeps the same replacement component and tools but assigns a component to a documented extension region instead of the main platform. The total item mass is unchanged; the physical location being reviewed is different. Retain the first proposal and identify the changed region on the second, together with the relevant configuration drawing. Do not treat an unchanged total as evidence that the earlier assessment covers both states.

Use an item identifier across the snapshots so a repeated component is traceable without being counted twice in one simultaneous state. “Location unresolved” is more informative than assigning it to the centre for convenience. The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) discusses spatial organization; this schedule records which people and objects are actually proposed together in each documented region.

### Describe fixtures, cables and items crossing the deck boundary

Record proposed racks, holders, fixtures, power leads, tool tethers, material supports and other interfaces as unresolved until exact documentation confirms their status. Include how their position changes and whether they remain attached during transport, setup, task or withdrawal.

A service hose or cable may connect a tool in the task area to equipment outside it. A long material item may also cross the platform boundary in a proposed arrangement. Listing only the length visible on the platform, or assigning an assumed fraction of the object's weight to it, leaves the external relationship unexplained. The schedule needs the object identified as a whole, its proposed route or position, where it connects, and who defines any support or attachment. Unknown connections and positions remain explicit questions.

Ask the equipment and task technical owners which effects of that relationship need assessment for the documented task state and what information is missing. This is not a method for distributing weight, calculating forces or treating the building as an available support. A drawing that shows an external contact or a line leading off the platform does not authorize leaning, suspending, pulling or supporting material there.

| Proposed task condition | What belongs in the simultaneous description | Question for the configuration review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspection at a defined point | People, instruments and other items actually present | Which documented platform region serves that task? |
| Replacement material arrives at the work face | Component, tools, containers and retained packaging | Is its proposed presence and position covered? |
| Old and new components coexist | Both components alongside the continuing people/tools | Has removed material been omitted from the proposal? |
| An item moves to an extension region | The same item with its revised location | Which region-specific and simultaneous-use conditions apply? |
| A tool connects outside the platform | The whole tool/cable relationship and proposed connection | What attachment or external-interface information is missing? |
| Material remains after a task | Continuing items plus the next task's additions | Which assumptions change before the next comparison? |

## Ask for the configuration answer behind each limit

### Clarify what a quoted limit includes

U.S. OSHA <a href="https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.453" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">29 CFR 1926.453(b)(2)(vi)</a>, in its U.S. construction provisions for extensible and articulating boom platforms, requires observance of manufacturer-specified boom and basket load limits. That bounded rule does not classify an ARC-F reference, establish that the rule applies to it, or provide a permitted capacity.

For the project, obtain the current manufacturer instructions and approved configuration records. Verify document revision, equipment identity and task state. A source about a general class cannot replace those exact records.

Ask the technical document owner to identify what each figure includes: task payload, platform self-weight, installed fixtures, or another defined quantity. Check whether a removable platform or attachment is already accounted for in the relevant record. Do not add or subtract its weight from a published limit based on an assumed definition. Likewise, a support-reaction record serves a different comparison from a task payload limit. Both need matching configuration references; they are not interchangeable figures.

### Separate task-load review from floor and transfer decisions

The competent reviewer should compare the full schedule, positions, attachments, task sequence and transfer method with the approved documents and site method. Separate equipment-limit review from floor support, manual handling, dropped-object control and work-at-height planning.

The [indoor floor and ground-pressure guide](/blog/indoor-aerial-platform-ground-pressure-guide/) addresses the support-path interface. Do not assume that a task-load comparison settles floor reactions or route suitability.

### Collect the task state, region and item information

- Site and destination jurisdiction, work-zone height, ceiling geometry, task owner and revision
- Access and transport route, floor or ground status, staging point and withdrawal path
- Exact task state, work sequence, simultaneous activity and change trigger
- Documented main, extension or removable platform region, including unresolved locations
- People by role, protective equipment where applicable, training-record owner and source date
- Tools, batteries, cables, hoses, test devices, consumables and controlled quantities, including any object crossing the platform boundary
- Materials, components, containers, removed items, dimensions, locations and securing proposal
- Attachment, rack, holder, fixture or tether status, external connections and proposed support locations, with the exact approved document reference or unresolved question
- Entry, transfer, staging, task and removal method with each responsible role
- Exact configuration identifier, manufacturer instruction, applicable limit and document revision
- Definition of each stated limit and confirmation of which platform or attachment masses it includes
- Unresolved dynamic, concentrated, offset, travel or transfer condition routed to competent review
- Site communications, rescue access, emergency interface and stop-work trigger

U.S. OSHA’s <a href="https://www.osha.gov/etools/scaffolding/aerial-lifts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Aerial Lifts eTool</a> uses workers, tools and materials as load considerations in U.S. occupational-safety context and cautions against using an aerial lift as a crane. It is an adjacent planning source, not an ARC-F classification, a capacity table or a statement of project applicability.

### When the proposed arrangement may not fit

The task proposal may not fit when the exact platform arrangement or limits are unidentified, a fixture is unresolved, or people and items cannot be assigned to documented regions. A spare-looking area or a favorable arithmetic total cannot resolve those conditions. This guide authorizes no lifting, transfer, load distribution or travel state. Refer the actual proposal to the responsible equipment and project reviewers before relying on a task-load comparison.

## FAQ

#### Why is crew count insufficient?

Because tools, materials, containers, attachments, positions and task states can change while the crew count stays the same. A reviewer needs the complete simultaneous task load and its sources.

#### What belongs in a task-load schedule?

List people by role, relevant protective equipment, tools, materials, containers, removed items, attachments, quantities, positions, task states, transfer method and source records.

#### Who confirms limits?

The exact manufacturer instructions supply configuration-specific information, while the employer and competent project reviewers decide how it applies to the task and site method.

#### Can main and extension platform ratings be added?

Separate ratings do not establish an allowable combined total. They may refer to different positions, arrangements or conditions. Obtain the exact document that explains their relationship for the proposed simultaneous state; if that relationship is absent, the comparison remains unresolved.

#### Does an unchanged total mean the previous review still applies?

Only if the relevant configuration, positions, attachments and task conditions remain within the documented review scope. Moving the proposed location of a component to another region changes the question even when its mass is unchanged. Record the revised state and refer it to the responsible reviewer.

#### Can the platform be treated as a crane?

Not from this article, a category label or a reference photograph. Any lifting or transfer proposal requires express exact-equipment documentation and project approval; otherwise the task remains on hold.
