**For a pre-engineered building project, shortlist a roll-forming line for panel production, a forming lift for an elevated panel outlet, and a ceiling/wall platform for crew access. Buy only the duties your contract needs.** When roof production and ceiling installation share a building programme, assign a separate equipment duty to each.

A contractor buying delivered roof panels should price personnel access for its installation tasks without adding a forming line. A roofing specialist responsible for site production should price the line and add a forming lift when the accepted layout requires an elevated outlet, with personnel access assigned separately. A contractor carrying both production and cladding work should compare a coordinated package with distinct machines and clearly priced interfaces.

## Start with the building package you actually hold

PEB contracts can divide roof sheets, wall cladding, ceilings, services and installation among different parties. Mark which tasks are yours and which arrive as purchased products or subcontracted work. The building's steel-frame height alone does not define the equipment package.

| Project duty | Equipment purchase to consider | Separate scope to keep visible | Buyer input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Produce the specified roof panel | Roll-forming line and matched tooling | Coil, material checks and finished-panel acceptance | Roof designer: supplies approved profile/material; production buyer uses these to request tooling |
| Place a compatible former at the required outlet | Truck or crawler forming lift | Ground support, receiver and any separate coil handling | Site surveyor: measures support surface to required outlet in the roof section; supplier selects the lifting arrangement |
| Give a crew access to a broad ceiling face | Large-deck work platform | Task materials, floor review and site work method | Ceiling supervisor: supplies crew/task loads and floor-to-workface dimensions to size the access enquiry |
| Give a crew access along a wall face | Suitable platform arrangement | Panel delivery, presentation and any dedicated handling aid | Wall supervisor: marks workface offsets and floor-to-task dimensions to select the wall arrangement |
| Supply bought-in roof or wall panels | Finished-panel procurement | Delivery, storage and onward transfer | Logistics lead: enters product schedule and delivery endpoint to price bought-panel handling |

Treat these rows as a purchasing map. Where a combined offer covers several rows, it must identify the equipment performing each duty. A brochure image showing several machines near the same building is not an equipment list.

## From coil to roof: specify the production half

For site-made roof panels, begin with the product drawing and material. Then establish the line footprint, installed mass, feed arrangement, power and outlet direction. These inputs connect the forming-line proposal to the lift review.

The lift selection comes from the proposed setup position and the required outlet-to-roof relationship. Provide the support area, access route and roof receiving arrangement together. A crawler base can enter the shortlist for a defined site layout, but the track arrangement still needs ground and support information.

*The production package connects incoming coil, the supported former and the outgoing panel; assign a provider to each interface.*

The photograph brings those three interfaces into one view. Use a site plan to add the parts outside the frame: coil delivery, the complete panel path, space for other trades and the next receiving point.

If the roofing subcontractor already owns a former, request integration against that identified machine. If panels are purchased complete, remove line ownership from the base offer and keep the finished-panel delivery route in the project scope. This avoids buying production equipment simply because it appears in a suggested building package.

## Under the roof and along the wall: specify crew access

A broad deck is worth shortlisting where a crew needs adjacent working positions along a ceiling or wall face. Describe the actual operation: installing a long ceiling element, aligning adjacent wall elements or working on a repeated strip creates different positions and material demands.

The platform offer needs the people, tools and materials present in each task state, as well as the deck geometry. Keep dedicated material-lifting or panel-positioning functions separate unless the equipment proposal explicitly includes them. A large deck area alone is not a panel-handling specification.

Floor and access information belongs in this part of the package. Send the route from delivery to workface, openings and turns, slab information, joints and overhead restrictions. Check the building condition expected during the task; use the service and partition layout expected when the equipment must enter and leave.

![Red work-deck assembly rotated upright beside a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261027-wall-frame.webp)

*Wall-oriented work changes the relationship between the deck and the workface; show the required positions in a wall section.*

The upright assembly in the photograph explains why a ceiling-only plan is insufficient for a wall enquiry. Identify the face, edge, corner and top-junction tasks separately, then ask which proposed platform arrangement covers them. Keep exceptional points visible instead of assuming the main deck reaches everything.

## Three sensible purchase packages

**Roof-production contractor:** compare a specified forming line plus compatible lift. Retain crew access, roof-side receiving and separate material handling as explicit project purchases. This package is strongest when repeated site production belongs to your contract.

**Cladding installer buying finished panels:** prioritize the work-platform enquiry and the panel-delivery arrangement. A forming line belongs in an optional expansion proposal only if you intend to take responsibility for producing the panels.

**Contractor delivering both production and installation:** compare the line, lift and work platform together, with separate scope and acceptance rows. Coordinate their occupation of the site, but preserve each machine's technical review. One supplier can make commercial coordination easier without turning different duties into one interchangeable machine.

## Where a combined order can fail

**Failure case: a forming lift is counted as the crew-access solution.** The procurement schedule lists a lift beside the roof and assumes it also covers ceiling installation. The crew-access duty remains unpurchased. Resolve it with an actual work-platform proposal or the site's separate access provider, including its floor and task requirements.

**Failure case: every machine uses the same staging area.** The forming package needs coil supply beside the building, while the wall crew's access and panel delivery are assigned to that same strip. Individually complete offers become incompatible in the programme. Compare a coordinated site layout and occupation sequence before choosing the combined package.

For parallel work, ask the project team which areas can be released independently. For sequential work, record the handback condition that lets the next trade enter. Do not purchase a second machine solely on the assumption that both can occupy the same area at once.

### Attach eight items for a coordinated quotation

- Your contract task list: roofing production, wall work and ceiling work.
- Roof plans and sections with the required outlet and receiving positions.
- Ceiling and wall drawings showing actual crew workfaces.
- Approved panel profiles, material, lengths and quantities.
- Proposed crew, tools and material loads for each access task.
- Site access, floor/ground information and a shared-area layout.
- Existing equipment, power, destination and chassis/carrier requirements.
- Requested supply boundaries, trade sequence and acceptance deliverables.
