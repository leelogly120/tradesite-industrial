Buy a purpose-designed permanent facade maintenance system when its accepted building interfaces and complete route serve the recurring tasks and its ownership case justifies installation and continuing care. Use ground-based personnel equipment when the actual industrial-wall work positions, approach and support areas fit the offered configuration at a better complete cost. If a ground arrangement cannot reach the task, remove it from the comparison; commission a suitable access design rather than assuming a larger deck solves the geometry.

The choice is between an installed building-access asset and recurring deployment of separate equipment. It concerns facade coverage, travel and servicing outside the building, not simply the maximum height on an equipment brochure. A single building can require different methods for different elevations.

## Trace the whole facade, including the return route

[JOMY's building-maintenance-unit range](https://www.jomy.be/en-BE/building-maintenance-units) describes permanent working structures moving over fixed rails attached to the building. Its examples include gantries and working cages for different surfaces. That supports a distinct permanent-access category; it does not establish coverage or structural suitability for your facade.

Mark the required working positions on elevations and sections. Include recesses, projecting canopies, changes of plane and the locations of recurring maintenance objects. The access designer defines the offered route around those features. Identify where people enter and leave the system and how the actual tools and replacement items reach each work position.

For a permanent route, request the building attachment and travel layout from its actual designer. For a ground route, request its approach, setup and offered work envelope. Put both onto the same issued building geometry so two bidders cannot claim coverage of different wall areas.

## A permanent asset creates building obligations

The permanent-system proposal must assign its building connections, support structure, travel infrastructure, installation and acceptance. The structural designer supplies the accepted supporting arrangement. The access-system provider identifies its operating, inspection, maintenance and replacement requirements.

Price the access needed to maintain that system itself. A permanent working structure does not eliminate every elevated intervention. Locate service points, drive or travel equipment where applicable, and any tasks outside the offered system. The asset owner needs a named organisation and funded route for those duties.

Also identify storage or parked positions and their effect on building operation. Include any facade work left outside the system's coverage as an explicit exception package. Do not describe an entire industrial complex as covered because one facade has a permanent installation.

## Ground access needs a usable site every visit

Use the completed yard layout, not just the construction plan. Vehicle traffic, parked trailers, landscaping, utility structures, loading areas and retained stock affect where equipment can approach and stand. The facility operator supplies the actual site use and available possession windows.

The equipment provider states transport geometry, setup geometry, task limits and support requirements for the offered configuration. The site engineer assesses the identified ground and supporting construction. A route wide enough for arrival still needs usable positions beside every work area included in the order.

## Compare an accepted maintenance programme

Complete the worksheet for the same tasks, ownership period and building condition. Count recurring mobilisation and permanent-system care as real costs, using provider offers and facility records.

Select the permanent option when its accepted coverage and full ownership case justify it for the specified programme. Select mobile deployment when its actual work envelope and recurring site occupation satisfy that programme at the better complete cost. A mixed arrangement is appropriate when each defined task has an accepted route and the combined plan is funded.

## Two hypothetical facade investments that miss the work

**Mistake: the permanent system reaches the flat wall but misses the recessed task.** The buyer compares one broad elevation drawing and omits a recess behind a canopy. Require working-position coverage on the actual sections, then fund the identified exception instead of calling the whole facade accessible.

**Mistake: a construction-yard route becomes the lifetime platform plan.** The facility purchases ground equipment before the delivery bays and landscaping are fixed. Later visits have no permitted setup positions. Assess the completed operating layout and the actual possession plan before selecting recurring deployment.
