**Buy a wet-pipe package when the fire designer accepts water-filled pipework for the actual building conditions. Buy the defined preaction package when the accepted design specifies that releasing architecture for the documented risk. Shortlist ARC-F25 for repeated adjacent pipe and associated ceiling tasks where its offered arrangement fits; use separate access for obstructed or isolated positions. Where both designs are accepted, choose the lower complete installed and maintainable cost over the same stated period. First obtain a coordinated pipework, detection and acceptance task map.**

A preaction valve assembly and a completed protection system are different purchase boundaries. The access budget has to follow the whole accepted design, including the equipment and field work supplied by other contractors. Price qualified fire-system installation and its personnel resources together through the agreed acceptance event.

The yellow access frame in the hall photograph shows a different personnel geometry from a broad horizontal deck. Use the designer's device and pipe positions to decide which type of working surface each task needs, with actual machine geometry and floor conditions.

## Release the system choice before the equipment booking

The owner supplies the actual occupancy, stored goods, operating temperatures, water-sensitive assets and relevant insurer and authority requirements. The qualified fire designer determines eligible systems and their project-specific design. The buyer compares those accepted alternatives, with their real supply and maintenance commitments. Ceiling-platform selection follows that decision.

An ordinary wet-pipe arrangement retains water in its sprinkler piping. Preaction adds a specified release arrangement and supplemental detection. The selected interlock and release type matters: the word preaction alone is insufficient to define the system being purchased. Obtain the actual design and identified products rather than comparing two generic equipment allowances.

[Viking's current VXD single-interlocked electric-release product information](https://www.vikinggroupinc.com/products/valves-systems/deluge-preaction-systems/vxd-valves-systems/vxd-preaction-systems/model-vxd) describes supervised piping, a release-control panel and compatible detection. It expressly lists the panel and detection as separately sold. That is a concrete scope gap for the buyer to close. For this identified single-interlock example, detection operates the valve to fill the sprinkler piping; discharge still requires an open sprinkler. This explains the distinct releasing-detection purchase. Identify the actual interlock and release product together in the accepted system schedule before pricing its associated field work.

## Price the pipe network and the releasing work together

For the wet-pipe proposal, obtain the complete accepted pipe, support, sprinkler, riser, alarm-interface and completion scope. Use the designer's actual schedule. The absence of a preaction release network is not a reason to assume that every other control or alarm task disappears.

For the preaction proposal, retain the specified pipework together with the releasing detection, controls, supervision, air supply and other equipment named in its design. Identify which parts arrive assembled and which need field installation. A packaged riser can reduce riser assembly work while leaving a distributed detection installation and its cable or other designed routes elsewhere in the building.

Have the fire contractor identify field tasks by location and trade. Record the personnel positions for pipe supports, joints, sprinkler work, detector or other specified release-device work, connections and accepted completion visits. The fire designer supplies the accepted detection locations. Price personnel access to those issued positions, including a separate resource where the main deck cannot serve them.

The same contractor or appointed integrator must identify the system interfaces and who completes them. An equipment price covering the valve is an earlier endpoint than an accepted operating installation. Add complementary quotations for excluded work before ranking the total. If ownership costs are included, use the same stated period and each provider's adopted service tasks and current prices. Keep unsupported service frequencies out of that calculation.

## Two networks do not automatically need two owned platforms

The pipe and releasing-device tasks can share an access resource where the same documented configuration serves their actual positions and dates. Use task IDs to show that coverage. If electrical and mechanical contractors require it concurrently in different areas, include the resulting second resource or a programme change. A common building address is not a common available machine.

For repeated adjacent ceiling work, compare a broad shared deck against the retained crew and tool layout. For an isolated detector face behind a fixed obstruction, obtain another suitable access proposal. Carry that cost beside the main platform offer rather than pretending the broad deck serves every point.

The narrow passage photograph illustrates why wall-side access needs separate geometry. A clear central hall says nothing about the approach between a perimeter structure and an installed building face. Measure the actual entrance, lateral space and support positions needed by the proposed equipment.

## Two hypothetical sprinkler buying mistakes

**The complete riser is priced as the complete preaction system.** A buyer accepts a packaged valve offer and books access only for pipes and sprinklers. The specified release panel and distributed detection have no purchase owner or installation period. Reconcile the bill of materials with the issued release design, appoint the integrator and price its actual ceiling visits.

**The pipe contractor's finish date becomes the platform departure date.** Another project removes the equipment after mechanical installation while the accepted programme retains elevated device work and a later specialist return. Add those located tasks and dates to the common access schedule before choosing retention or remobilization.

## Eight attachments for the personnel-platform RFQ

1. Accepted fire-protection brief and actual system design references.
2. Pipe, support, sprinkler and riser layout with component identities.
3. Selected preaction release/interlock type and detection/control scope where applicable.
4. Supplier inclusion/exclusion schedule and named integration responsibilities.
5. Mechanical and device task positions, measured from supporting-floor datums.
6. Concurrent people, tools, permitted items and separate pipe-handling records.
7. Measured entry routes, setup plans, floor records and site-release windows.
8. Installation, acceptance and return dates, adopted maintenance-access tasks, available site power, destination and delivery boundary.
