**Choose a raised-access-floor service route when the released design provides the required void, floor interfaces and access to components below. Choose overhead distribution when a raised floor conflicts with required thresholds or room height, and the coordinated overhead zone provides the specified clearances and maintainable work positions. When both routes fit, compare installed cost and planned service changes over the same period.** Buy a ceiling platform for the selected design's retained overhead work.

This is a building-services architecture decision. A cable tray and a busbar can both sit overhead; choosing between those components does not decide whether services belong above or below the occupied floor. Equally, a raised floor for selected power and data does not automatically remove overhead lighting, fire protection or other retained work.

## Draw the two finished facilities

Start with the same room, required service outputs, equipment positions and operating requirements. The responsible designers issue an overhead alternative and an underfloor alternative that actually deliver those outputs. Where a service must stay in one location, show it as retained scope in both alternatives rather than inventing a completely clear ceiling.

[Tate's raised-access-floor range](https://www.tateglobal.com/apac/products/raised-access-floors/) describes a panel-and-support system above the structural slab, with a service void beneath the finished surface. That supports the architectural distinction, not a load rating for an access machine. The facility's selected floor system and the actual equipment reactions require their own engineering assessment.

Put the finished-floor elevation, thresholds and building interfaces on the alternative drawings. An underfloor proposal consumes real vertical space. The buyer needs coordinated doors, ramps or other designed transitions, equipment delivery interfaces and the remaining room clearance. An overhead proposal consumes another real volume: the supports, service zones and future work positions above the room.

The yard photograph below shows a low folded deck, deployed supports and a side boom. Obtain the supplier's actual transport drawing to establish which assemblies fold or are removed for delivery. Measure that offered transport state against the coordinated route. A delivery that fits a bare structural slab can face a different opening or supporting arrangement after the floor system is installed.

## Price future changes at the location you selected

Ask the facility operator to identify the expected change tasks: which circuits or services will be accessed, where work can occur, and what equipment or operational space must remain available. Use the operator's actual forecast and current restrictions. Do not attach a generic saving percentage to either architecture.

Tate's [discussion of underfloor service distribution](https://www.tateglobal.com/amer/insights-resources/knowledge-hub/sensible-building-construction-solutions-raised-access-flooring/) explains that selected services can be placed below the floor instead of above the ceiling. For procurement, turn that feature into a project-specific access plan. The proposal needs identified service locations, access-panel zones and the responsibilities for restoring the finished surface after an intervention.

For overhead services, map the future personnel positions against the occupied layout. Equipment cabinets, fixed production cells and partitions affect where an access machine can stand. A route available during an empty-shell fitout is not necessarily available during operation. Include the actual retained access solution and any required relocation in the system comparison.

For underfloor services, show how the responsible trades reach the specified components and where floor openings intersect occupied routes or equipment. Include the operator's isolation, occupation and reinstatement arrangements through the appointed project process. A removable panel is an architectural component, not the complete service-change contract.

## Keep the working floor separate from its rating label

The structural slab, raised floor panels and support system are different parts of the load path. Put the actual proposed travel and standing positions on the coordinated drawings. Request configuration-specific equipment reactions and the floor system's relevant information for the responsible specialists. Do not convert an area-load description into permission for a particular wheel, track or support leg.

A temporary equipment route established before the raised floor is installed needs a physical entry, exit and programme. Identify which overhead tasks can be completed in that period and which return visits remain. Compare the remaining tasks with the actual finished-floor arrangement, rather than assuming that a convenient construction sequence eliminates all later access needs.

## Two layouts that hide a later purchase

**Mistake: moving cables below the floor removes every overhead allowance.** A hypothetical estimator deletes the entire ceiling-access budget, although the final design retains lighting and other overhead services. Separate displaced systems from retained tasks. Obtain a platform or other accepted access quotation for the actual remaining work and later changes.

**Mistake: the construction route is mistaken for the operating route.** Another facility uses a broad deck on an accepted structural-floor route before installing the raised floor. Its maintenance estimate carries that same machine into the finished room without assessing the new supporting system or thresholds. Price the accepted finished-state access solution and retain any separately designed route or alternate equipment in the lifecycle total.
