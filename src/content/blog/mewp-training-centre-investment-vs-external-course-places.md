**Buy places from an approved external training centre when operator demand is intermittent, several equipment categories are needed, or the business cannot sustain an audited training operation. Invest in your own approved centre when recurring eligible demand justifies qualified instructors, suitable facilities, equipment and continuing oversight.** Buying a fleet of mobile elevating work platforms (MEWPs) does not make a rental depot an approved training provider.

Start by counting learners who actually need a course. Separate new recruits, planned renewals, contractors trained by other employers and prospective public customers. Assign each to a location, accepted course category, language and feasible date. A headcount of everyone who has ever hired equipment at the depot greatly overstates the available training business.

The scheme owner determines the requirements; the fleet manager must show who can release the space and machine for each course.

## Price the establishment separately from each course

[IPAF's training centre application information](https://www.ipaf.org/en/become-training-centre) describes membership, facility requirements, application and inspection, the centre agreement and an approved instructor. It also describes auditing instructors, facilities and equipment. These are requirements of the named scheme. Obtain current requirements and charges from the relevant office for your country and intended courses. A different approved programme or employer requirement needs its own assessment.

Identify who will run the centre when its principal instructor is absent. Include course administration, records, approved materials, equipment condition, facilities, oversight and any fees in the provider proposal. Ask when training can be offered under the intended approval. A member application, instructor course or rented classroom on its own is insufficient evidence of authorisation to operate the centre. Budget external places until the centre can legitimately deliver the required training.

For the external option, request quotes for the actual course mix, delivery locations, available dates and languages. Include employee travel and paid time unavailable for normal duties. Compare public classes, agreed blocks of places and an eligible external provider delivering at your premises where offered. A provider coming to the depot is still an external provider; the two arrangements carry different staffing, governance and approval costs.

## Build a course calendar that survives rental peaks

For each proposed course date, reserve an instructor, appropriate course equipment and the required facilities. Check the calendar against the rental fleet's confirmed bookings. A demonstration unit that leaves for a paying customer on the same day either displaces the course or requires a replacement machine. Price that conflict directly. Compare the real cost of releasing depot equipment with the saving in external travel and purchased course places.

Use one decision horizon for establishment and external provision. For the centre case include fixed facilities and oversight plus variable course delivery and registration costs. Credit avoided external course charges for eligible staff. Credit public sales only at expected net contribution for bookings the business can substantiate. A staff member receiving internal training is an avoided purchase, not a second commercial sale. Keep an optimistic open-market course scenario separate from the committed business case.

## Two mistakes in a depot training investment

**Mistake one — treating operator familiarisation as a saleable approved course.** A hypothetical dealer counts each handover demonstration as an approved training place. Separate machine-specific information, employer authorisation and scheme-approved operator training. Forecast revenue only for courses the approved provider can deliver.

**Mistake two — filling every class with forecast customers.** The owner includes public learners who have neither committed nor paid, then overlooks the cost of the instructor and machine during quiet periods. Reprice the base case using actual internal demand and firm external bookings. Purchase external places if the centre cannot carry its fixed obligations.
