**Choose a separately staffed second shift when the current line is the proven capacity constraint, its permitted duty supports the programme, and material handling, quality control and dispatch can cover the extra hours. Add a second line when operating hours are fixed or simultaneous product duties justify parallel production. If the shortage is at coil supply, panel receiving or installation, fix that constraint before buying either expansion.**

This is a capacity-expansion decision for an established product range. It follows the [production bottleneck review](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-production-bottleneck-planning/); it does not replace it. Start with evidence that demand exceeds the accepted output available from the present forming operation.

## Give both options the same output target

Build the comparison around accepted panels delivered to a named endpoint during the same calendar period. Use panel area for a mixed width range, with the cut list alongside it. Use length only when the product mix makes that unit comparable. Retain rejected output and waiting periods in the production log rather than counting them as delivered capacity.

A second shift extends the time over which the existing route operates. A second line adds another production route during the available hours. Neither option automatically doubles accepted deliveries. Setup, inspection, material supply and downstream limits still apply.

The paired machines in the photograph represent separate physical production positions. When budgeting such an arrangement, draw both coil routes and both outputs. One shared handling resource must have enough time to serve the combined programme.

## The extra-hours route

Choose a second shift when enough committed work exists outside the present production window and the complete operating team can be staffed. Include material-handling staff, the appointed supervisor, quality release and maintenance response. A machine operator alone is not an additional production system.

Use a planned shift arrangement rather than treating repeated overtime by the existing crew as equivalent to another staffed shift. [HSE's fatigue guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/fatigue.htm) explains that poorly designed shifts and excessive working time affect performance and risk. The employer must establish the appropriate local working arrangement; a capacity spreadsheet is not a working-hours instruction.

Reserve maintenance and product-change periods explicitly. If the proposal occupies every available hour with saleable output, it has omitted the work that keeps the production process available. The equipment provider should address the intended duty in the offered configuration and maintenance programme.

## The parallel-line route

Choose another line when the extra staffed hours are unavailable, the site restricts operating periods, or committed products require parallel resources. A dedicated second profile line also deserves comparison when actual changeovers consume enough capacity to affect delivery, but use the measured product mix rather than assuming all changeovers disappear.

Budget the complete installation: supporting floor area, incoming services, coil positions, feeding and cut-off arrangements, runout, storage and quality resources. Keep the two outputs from competing for one occupied dispatch aisle or one receiving team at the same time.

For roof-level production, a second ground former is not equivalent to a second elevated production package. A simultaneous roof location requires its own suitable lift, complete interfaces and receiving arrangement, unless the programme deliberately keeps production sequential. Distinguish factory capacity from jobsite simultaneity before choosing the quantity.

Write down the deciding condition. For example: choose the second shift if the separate team and supporting services cover the required period and its accepted output meets the due order mix. Choose a parallel line if that period is unavailable and the fully resourced parallel layout meets the same orders. When neither meets demand, change the delivery agreement or source the shortfall externally.

## Make the trial representative

Include the actual start-up, changeovers, coil replenishment, quality release and handling route. Time from the agreed beginning to the agreed endpoint, not just the interval when the rollers turn. Record which work happens outside that observation so it is not silently excluded from the commercial comparison.

The roller train below illustrates the equipment at the centre of the additional production period. Include its required setup, inspection and maintenance work in the comparison, whether the new period is later in the day or on another line.

### Two expansion plans that fail at the deadline

**The second shift has no receiving route.** In this illustrative factory, forming operates later but panel storage fills because packing and dispatch end with the first shift. The line stops with work in progress. Either staff the supporting route and provide its approved space or calculate the output using the real storage limit.

**Both lines depend on the same unavailable resource.** A buyer purchases another former but schedules both coil changes and panel removal against one already occupied handling team. Rework the shared-resource calendar before attributing the entire shortfall to the machines. The second line should be priced with the supporting additions it actually needs.
