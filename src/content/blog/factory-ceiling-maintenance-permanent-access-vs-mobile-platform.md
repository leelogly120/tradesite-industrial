**Commission a permanent-access design for frequent work at stable overhead locations when the building can support the infrastructure and its route serves the tasks. Choose a mobile platform for changing work locations where the factory retains a usable floor route and approved support positions.** Use permanent access for the repeat points and a separate mobile arrangement for exceptions when neither route covers the whole maintenance programme.

Before investing in either option, ask the responsible designer whether the task can be moved to ground level. An accessible service point or equipment designed to lower for maintenance changes the purchase more fundamentally than choosing a different platform. The access decision follows the maintenance task, not the building's maximum height.

## Begin with a maintenance map that will survive fit-out

List the actual overhead assets the factory must inspect, service or replace. For each one, record the task location, expected visits, people, tools and replacement components. Separate recurring work from future possibilities that have no defined task or location.

Use the completed production layout. Machinery, storage, conveyors and process boundaries determine whether a mobile platform can occupy a useful position later. A construction-stage photograph of an open floor is insufficient for an equipment investment intended to serve an operating factory.

Fixed access has a different dependency. A walkway or other permanent route needs a building-specific design that serves the actual work positions, including the tools and components used there. Reaching the general ceiling area is not the same as providing a working position beside each asset.

## Compare infrastructure with repeated mobilisation

Permanent access and a mobile platform are different capital purchases. One becomes part of the facility's access infrastructure; the other remains equipment that must be brought to, set up for and maintained for the task.

Assign a named owner to each row. If neither route reaches a required point, change the design or purchase a defined additional access arrangement. Do not label the whole maintenance programme covered because most points have a solution.

## Put repeat visits into the cost comparison

Use the maintenance programme to count the work the business actually expects. Obtain quotations for the permanent design, installation and continuing care. Compare those with the mobile equipment, its support arrangements and the factory work needed for each planned visit.

For mobile access, include clearing the route, preparing the agreed work area, moving materials, reinstating production space and storing the machine between tasks. Use the factory's own records or project-specific quotations. A purchase price alone omits the recurring work needed to use the asset.

For permanent access, include the installation's effect on the building and production programme. Ask the designer to identify what happens if the serviced asset or process line moves. Fixed access is a deliberate commitment to a particular route and task arrangement, not a promise of coverage for every future layout.

Avoid a universal payback threshold. A factory with the same service points used repeatedly has a different decision from one with infrequent work spread across changing positions. Choose using the identified tasks and full proposals for that factory.

## A mixed arrangement often has the clearest boundary

Separate stable repeat work from occasional replacement or expansion tasks. A permanent route can serve the defined recurring points while a separately purchased mobile-access arrangement addresses the exceptions. Name those exceptions so they do not disappear from the capital budget.

Also keep material lifting separate from personnel access. A maintenance visit to inspect a component and a later visit to replace a large assembly are different tasks. The replacement plan needs its own handling resources even if the personnel can use the same route.

In Great Britain, [HSE's work-at-height guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/faq-height.htm) places avoiding work at height before choosing equipment for it. Use that principle to challenge the maintenance design first. The project's designers and responsible specialists must establish the applicable access and equipment arrangements for the destination.

For the proposed machine, obtain its storage envelope and permitted storage arrangement from the equipment supplier. If it serves several buildings or leaves the factory between visits, include an actual carrier arrangement and receiving resources in the ownership comparison.

## Two investments that leave the service point unreachable

These illustrative purchasing mistakes test whether each investment covers the defined maintenance work.

**Failure case: the production line occupies the platform's only position.** A factory buys mobile access from the construction layout, then installs process equipment beneath the maintenance point. The route available during construction has disappeared. Evaluate the completed layout before purchase and commission a different access design where the mobile position cannot be preserved.

**Failure case: the walkway reaches the area but not the replacement task.** A permanent route serves inspection visits, while the replacement component needs a separate handling and working arrangement. Include the actual replacement task in the design brief or retain a separately priced exception package. Inspection access alone is not complete maintenance coverage.

Attach these eight inputs to the RFQ:

- Overhead asset locations and the actual maintenance task at each point.
- Expected visit programme and defined exceptional replacement work.
- Working positions measured from the supporting floor or access level.
- Completed production layout and preserved mobile routes/setup areas.
- Crew, tools, replacement components and separate handling requirements.
- Floor and building records with the responsible structural reviewer identified.
- Permanent-access proposal and the tasks it covers or excludes.
- Mobile equipment destination, storage, transport and support arrangements.
