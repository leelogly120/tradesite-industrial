Choose prefabricated roof cassettes when the accepted building design accommodates the supplied assemblies and their complete delivery, handling, connection and closure package beats site assembly. Choose separately installed roof layers when that accepted route fits the building, access and programme better. Order a roof-forming lift only for the metal-sheet production retained in the selected package; cassette placement belongs to a separately designed handling operation.

“Prefabricated roof” is a supply boundary, not a promise that every roof layer arrives complete. One proposal includes insulation and inner components while leaving the outer weathering sheet to site work; another has a different factory scope. Compare the actual assemblies through the same finished roof.

## Draw the factory boundary through the roof section

Start with the accepted section and colour the components delivered as one assembly. Mark the remaining layers, joints, penetrations, edge details and temporary protection. This drawing becomes the commercial comparison between cassette supply and site-built construction.

[DOMICO's Element-Dach information](https://www.domico.at/produkte/element-dach/) describes project-specific factory assemblies containing supporting profiles, cassettes, mineral insulation and holding profiles, with defined openings. Its information separately identifies the outer shell. That is a useful example of why a prefabricated element and a completed weathering roof need different entries in the purchase schedule.

The buyer should obtain the offered assembly drawing and its connection design for the actual building. The responsible designers coordinate supports, bearings, interfaces and the construction states. Price any changes to the building structure before comparing assembly labour. Moving work into a factory also moves design decisions earlier: openings and interfaces need release before the relevant assembly is manufactured.

Site-built construction keeps more layer installation at the project. It still requires a coordinated accepted roof system, material deliveries and specialist installation resources. Compare both routes through completed connections, weathering, inspection and handover, including the work that continues after the large components are placed.

## Decide who owns each handover

The prefabricated route has a factory release, delivery acceptance, placement handover, connection release and roof-completion stage. Assign each to an actual party. A cassette supplier's delivery price and an installer's finished-roof price describe different purchases.

Choose a complete assembly-and-installation offer where one contracted party takes the stated interfaces and the price supports the project. Choose separate supply and erection contracts where the buyer has the coordination resources and the responsibilities are expressly allocated. In either case, name the party completing the outer skin and all interfaces between units.

For the site-built alternative, identify who supplies and fits each layer, who receives long metal sheets and who releases each area for closure. A roll-former investment is relevant only where the chosen route includes buyer-controlled manufacture of the specified outer or other compatible sheet product.

The related [built-up roof sourcing guide](/blog/built-up-insulated-roof-own-sheets-vs-system-package/) examines outer-sheet manufacture within a site-assembled system. This decision comes earlier: it changes what arrives as an assembly and how the whole roof is put together.

An assembly proposal that fits the completed building can still conflict with the construction sequence. Use surveyed support positions and the actual arrival state: unfinished ground, temporary storage, cranes and other trades all occupy space. Release the package when its erection and completion routes are both funded.

## Retain the work that actually remains

A prefabricated roof can leave substantial external weathering work. Count the outer-sheet quantities and their installation sequence independently from the cassette-placement schedule. Where long sheets are formed at roof level, align production with the released receiving zones rather than the truck delivery count.

If the chosen assemblies arrive with the relevant sheet already incorporated and the remaining work is limited to joints and edges, omit a production lift from that project's equipment budget. If the roof still needs repeat compatible sheets across large zones, compare bought panels with a complete forming proposal using those actual quantities.

The delivery plan should include a response to a held or rejected unit. Decide where it waits, who investigates the issue and how the roof sequence continues. A replacement arrangement needs to preserve the accepted system and the construction plan, rather than improvising site-made pieces around a missed delivery.

## Two hypothetical package failures

**Failure case: “complete cassette” omits the weathering layer.** A buyer compares cassette delivery with a fully installed site-built roof, then discovers that outer sheets, edge details and their access remain separate. Redraw the factory boundary, add those operations and compare both proposals through the same weatherproof handover.

**Failure case: the unit fits the roof but not its arrival route.** A project releases factory production before allocating unloading and placement space. The delivered assemblies have nowhere to wait while another trade occupies the only planned position. Resolve the delivery sequence, storage and handling arrangement before releasing that batch.
