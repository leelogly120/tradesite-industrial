Choose an OEM remanufactured exchange when the installed pump's exact application is eligible, the replacement and test basis are attributable, and its complete cost remains attractive after realistic core credit and return obligations. Buy a matched new unit when no supported exchange exists, the core terms erase the saving or the actual service requirement favors new. Compare both routes through the same competent repair and return-to-service scope.

This decision begins with a competently diagnosed pump replacement on a retained forming lift. Hold the confirmed surrounding repair requirement fixed when comparing a matched new unit with an eligible OEM exchange. Obtain both actual offers through the installed maker's attributable support route.

<!-- audit-section: buyer-intent -->
## Establish eligibility before requesting a discount

Start with the asset's configuration record and the pump identification obtained through the maintainer's permitted process. Retain the manufacturer's reference, revision and application information needed to match the replacement. Record associated controls or supplied assemblies that affect that match. A similar housing or a familiar brand name is insufficient purchasing evidence.

Ask the actual OEM or authorized channel to identify a matched new unit and any eligible exchange offer. Keep its attributable application response with each quotation. Where a superseded reference is proposed, retain the issuer's explanation and the actual additional items or work included. The buyer should understand whether both prices reach the same installed repair boundary.

![A red crawler structure supports a long raised enclosure beside a building and construction ground.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-crawler-roof-side.webp)
*Keep the lifting structure and supported upper equipment in the application record used to match the pump.*

For an exchange offer, connect the purchased remanufactured unit to the returned used component, called the core, and the provider's actual acceptance terms. That return obligation distinguishes the exchange transaction from an outright remanufactured-unit purchase. Establish who owns the core, which identity must be returned and which condition determines the credit. Keep a third-party rebuilt or used offer in its own category unless it actually belongs to the identified OEM program.

[Caterpillar's hydraulic support page](https://www.cat.com/en_US/products/new/parts/hydraulics/pumps-motors.html) distinguishes new parts, remanufactured replacements and other repair levels in its own range. That is an example of a real OEM-supported choice. It establishes no Cat pump fitment or commercial relationship for an ARCLIFT product. The installed maker's response controls the actual forming-lift purchase.

## Put the core into the purchase account

Obtain the invoice structure before comparing net figures. An exchange quotation can include a core deposit or show a price conditional on later credit. Record when cash leaves, which event starts the return period, when and where the old unit must be received, who assesses it and what happens if the return is late or unacceptable. Use the actual terms rather than assuming full credit is certain.

Caterpillar's [remanufactured hydraulic-component brochure](https://caterpillar.scene7.com/is/content/Caterpillar/CM20150526-53488-16062) describes inspection and testing of returned components and relates exchange cost to core condition. Use that historical OEM explanation to recognize the core-credit mechanism. Obtain the actual current program identifier, eligible replacement reference, core assessment terms and availability from the provider for today's installed pump.

| Common repair work | Removal/fitting and other required work ___ | Competent maintainer defines both routes |
| Remedy and timing | Actual coverage ___; supported supply date ___ | Buyer checks the repair plan and retained cost |

Calculate each route from the full amount payable, actual eligible credit, freight, packing, applicable charges and route-specific work. Keep costs common to both repairs visible but count them once in each route. If the credit is unresolved, compare the supported credit case with the lower-credit outcome the owner would have to fund.

The deposit can affect working capital even when a later credit makes the exchange attractive. Show peak cash required and expected final cost separately. A business unable to carry that temporary outflow needs a different arrangement, rather than treating the expected refund as cash already available.

## Compare restored availability on the same basis

Ask the provider what accompanies the replacement: unit identity, applicable test or release information, instructions within its supply scope and the actual remedy terms. A broad remanufactured label alone does not identify those deliverables. Equally, a new unit still needs a confirmed application and competent integration into the repair.

Keep the maintainer's diagnosis and required surrounding work common to both bids. Contamination or another unresolved cause can leave the purchased replacement exposed under either route. Obtain the competent repair scope and price its assigned work; procurement should not invent a cleaning procedure or convert a component warranty into permission to restart the machine.

![A long blue and orange machine assembly rests over a green-sided flatbed truck under workshop cranes.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-former-truck-workshop.webp)
*Allocate any equipment movement and off-site work within the repair budget before comparing replacement prices.*

Use actual availability and repair appointments in the calendar. An exchange described as stocked elsewhere is useful only when the provider supports a supply commitment for the required unit and destination. Include any dependency between removing the old pump, receiving the new one and returning the core. If one route shortens the outage, enter the actual outside-panel purchase or delayed-order cost it avoids. Count that consequence only for work on the supported production calendar.

Choose between the fully matched routes after checking those conditions. If the exchange is unavailable or the core is ineligible, the choice reduces to the supported new offer and any other separately assessed repair route. A nonexistent exchange price should not remain the target against which a real replacement is judged.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical exchange-purchase mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): spending the maximum core credit in advance.** An owner approves the exchange using full advertised credit, then returns an incomplete or mismatched unit after the provider's deadline. The final cost exceeds the funded repair budget. Obtain the actual core basis and model the retained exposure before placing the order.

**Mistake (hypothetical): matching the outside shape.** A purchaser accepts a remanufactured unit because its housing resembles the removed pump, while the application record specifies a different configuration. Stop the purchase at the identification stage and obtain the installed maker's attributable match, including any required additional scope.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Eight attachments for the replacement RFQ

1. **Asset identity:** private machine serial and controlled configuration record.
2. **Installed pump:** maker reference, revision and identification photographs.
3. **Repair requirement:** competent diagnosis and confirmed replacement boundary.
4. **Actual offers:** matched new and eligible OEM exchange quotations.
5. **Core record:** identity, ownership, condition information and included parts.
6. **Return terms:** credit basis, receipt deadline, packing and freight responsibilities.
7. **Supply evidence:** applicable test/release, remedy terms and supported delivery.
8. **Repair plan:** assigned fitting, surrounding work and competent release responsibilities.
