**For a full-height non-loadbearing partition with both wall systems already accepted by the designer, buy masonry access when its unit supply, construction-stage restrictions and finishing campaign give the lower complete cost. Choose metal-stud drywall when its complete framing, board-presentation, closure and finish package costs less through the same accepted finished wall and meets the required dates. Shortlist ARC-F25 only for the actual elevated personnel tasks its offered configuration serves. Next, mark both wall faces and every required return before ordering access.**

A finished wall is the comparison endpoint. Buying a platform against wall height alone leaves the material supply, unfinished second face or final head detail outside the equipment budget. Fix the accepted wall specification first, then price how each construction route brings people and materials to the required positions.

## Start with the work on both faces

Draw the partition as a construction sequence, with the supporting floor and wall face identified at every elevated task. Locate ends, openings, columns, service interfaces and the head. Include the finishing and inspection work that remains after the main wall area is erected.

For masonry, obtain the actual unit and mortar delivery plan, retained personnel tasks and the responsible contractor's construction-stage stability arrangements. Separate material delivery to the work area from the working stock permitted on the personnel platform. Identify any restricted regions that change where the platform stands or when the crew returns. The access buyer purchases the resulting work positions; the wall contractor and designer retain the wall's construction requirements.

For drywall, long framing components and board-sized objects create a different presentation assignment. [Gyproc describes its drywall construction](https://www.gyproc.in/professionals/drywalls) as a steel frame with plasterboard on both sides, followed by taped and finished joints. Turn those stages into actual access purchases: framing, first face, retained internal work, closure and finish.

## Board closure changes the access calendar

The wall is not complete when the first boards are fixed. Identify which services, insulation or inspections precede closure in the accepted project sequence. Give the second face its own dates and access route. If another trade occupies that face between operations, the purchase needs a real return allocation rather than continuous machine availability assumed at no cost.

[British Gypsum's partition reference](https://www.british-gypsum.com/documents/white-book/british-gypsum-wb-gypwall-single-frame-with-introduction.pdf) identifies non-loadbearing framed partitions and system-specific construction details, including interfaces at the head. Use the actual specified detail to locate personnel work, including a high-level return after the main face is closed when that detail's project sequence requires one. The existence of a drywall family does not accept an arbitrary high wall or substitute one detail for another.

For either method, obtain the actual individual material dimensions and mass before selecting presentation equipment. A board carrier or masonry delivery resource is a separate purchase unless expressly included in the accepted machine configuration. Keep the provider's material-presentation assignment separate from the crew's fixing position. The personnel deck is not the wall's temporary support or an unspecified material hoist.

## Count occupied positions through final acceptance

The masonry package includes the actual material-distribution resources, personnel access for the stated wall operations, finishing and any retained inspection returns. The drywall package includes framing and board presentation, the separate faces and layers specified for the project, internal-work releases and joint/head/perimeter finishing.

Compare complete costs through the same finished and inspected partition. Do not compare a masonry finished-wall offer with a drywall frame-and-board offer. Give waiting, relocation and later high-level finishing a named resource and date. If only one accepted method meets the required wall handback date, use that complete method; if both do, choose the lower complete finished-wall cost.

Access to one face does not establish access to the opposite face. A platform that fits the open hall at the start still needs an available route when the new division is in place. Mark the final equipment-removal opening and any material route that must remain usable during the second-face campaign.

## Two unfinished-wall purchase mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): drywall access ends at first-face boards.** A buyer prices the framing campaign and assumes the same platform allocation completes the wall. The services contractor releases internal work later, when the access machine is committed elsewhere. The wall remains open. Correct the offer with separate closure, head/perimeter finish and inspection dates. Allocate funded access to those actual high-level positions after the main wall campaign, including the route available at that time.

**Mistake (hypothetical): all masonry stock is assigned to the personnel deck.** A tender includes a unit-delivery price but no local presentation resource, then places the working stock in the crew's planned region. The simultaneous load and available working area no longer match the personnel offer. Obtain the actual delivery/presentation proposal and revise the crew-and-stock layout against the offered configuration before buying.

## Eight attachments for a quotable wall-access RFQ

1. Accepted full-height wall elevations and assembly revision, including head, end, opening and service-interface references.
2. Marked personnel task positions on both faces, with floor datums, heights, offsets and required working lengths.
3. Simultaneous people, tools, consumables and material schedule with their proposed platform positions.
4. Actual unit, framing and board product schedule, individual dimensions/mass and separately purchased presentation scope.
5. Wall-side routes, entrance dimensions, turns, obstructions and planned closure/removal sequence.
6. Ground or slab records, support-position drawing and the engineer receiving offered equipment reactions.
7. Construction restrictions and dated first-face, internal-work, closure, finishing and inspection releases.
8. Destination, site power and transport boundary, requesting offered working-region geometry, simultaneous task/load limits, reactions and equipment acceptance records.
