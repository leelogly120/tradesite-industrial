Choose a rooflight scheme when the designer's daylight study supports openings in the roof and the roof package includes their complete interfaces. Choose high wall glazing when the accepted design meets the same occupied-space brief from the facades. For the panel producer, the purchase follows the resulting sheet schedule and contract boundary. Gross floor area is a poor basis for buying a roof former.

Suppose an industrial client wants daylight over particular work zones. One bidder offers rooflight strips and another quotes high wall windows. Their prices refer to different parts of the building. Ask for two coordinated envelope offers tied to the same room use, roof form and design conditions before converting either option into a metal-panel order.

## Establish what daylight the client is buying

Mark the areas that need daylight on a floor plan. Show high racks, partitions, mezzanines and adjacent buildings as the design team requires. Record occupied hours and the owner's requirements for glare, shading and maintenance. Future fit-out matters if a proposed high window faces a rack or a partition absent from the initial drawing.

The Rooflight Association explains that roof and vertical apertures see different portions of the sky; the orientation and obstructions matter. This supports comparing daylight distribution in the actual space. The responsible designer should give the client a decision for each submitted layout. Equal areas of roof and wall glazing are not interchangeable evidence of performance. [Rooflight Association daylight white paper](https://rooflightassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/RA-Daylight-White-Paper-2025.pdf)

Exclude a scheme from machine comparisons if the building team has rejected it. When both are acceptable, record the assumptions and drawing revision on which each result depends. The roofing contractor can then compare the metal quantities on two real alternatives without undertaking the designer's daylight calculations.

## Convert each accepted scheme to saleable roof pieces

For rooflights, place the released layout over the roof panel setting-out. Identify full runs, interrupted runs and any purpose-made sheets or trims supplied under the rooflight package. Obtain the roof system provider's details for the junctions actually shown. A continuous strip down the slope and a band interrupting several runs produce different cutting lists, even if their daylight areas are similar.

For high wall glazing, obtain the corresponding roof setting-out rather than assuming the whole roof has become a rectangle of uninterrupted metal. Existing service penetrations, roof zones and other designed openings remain. Record the separate wall glazing, perimeter and access package under its actual supplier. The roof producer's retained order consists only of the sheets or flashings that the contract assigns to that producer.

This is an earlier purchase decision than checking a selected rooflight against a metal profile. Once the client selects a rooflight layout, the [rooflight and roof-panel compatibility guide](/blog/rooflight-compatible-roof-panel-roll-forming-package/) covers that junction. The immediate investment question is how much released metal production and which installation work your company will retain under each complete building option.

## Put the same commercial finish line on both bids

Ask for a weather-complete roof and enclosed wall for each alternative, together with the owner's agreed future maintenance provision. For the rooflight option, identify the rooflight supplier's included junctions, fabrication drawings and delivery sequence. For the wall option, identify wall openings, glazing and perimeter details and who releases them. Price cleaning and replacement access from the actual accepted surfaces. These are package costs even when they sit outside the roofing subcontract.

Translate the roof subcontract into machine days using the firm's own trial or production records. Group by profile, metal, cut length and batch release. On a rooflight layout, a greater count of short pieces can consume order handling without increasing sheet area. On a wall-glazing layout, longer roof runs might justify local production if the receiving route excludes finished sheets of those lengths. Obtain an alternative supply price before adding the whole building to a former's investment forecast.

## Two hypothetical tender mistakes

**Mistake: deleting a strip of roof area from the coil order.** An estimator subtracts the glazed area in square metres and leaves every remaining sheet at its original length. The approved detail actually interrupts several runs and requires separate upper and lower pieces. Reissue the cutting schedule against the opening IDs and assign the interfaces before ordering material.

**Mistake: keeping the facade work in a verbal allowance.** A high-window scheme looks cheaper because its roof has fewer specialist junctions. The comparison omits the wall openings, glazing order and future access along an occupied facade. Obtain those prices at the same completion boundary as the rooflight offer, then compare the owner's accepted alternatives.
