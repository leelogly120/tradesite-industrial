For an occupied factory, shortlist a roll-forming lift only when a protected ground position, a panel receiving route and the agreed roof-work window can operate together. It is a strong purchasing candidate for repeated long-panel replacement zones. For isolated repairs, very short closure windows or a site that cannot release the forming area, start with delivered panels and a separately planned installation route.

The decision is about keeping the replacement sequence supplied without disrupting the factory. A forming lift solves one part of that sequence. Roof removal, weather protection, people access and protection of the activity below remain separate parts of the contractor's plan. Price these alongside the equipment before comparing offers.

The photo below shows why the ground and roof activities belong on one drawing. Coils, forming equipment and the sheet route compete for space around the building. On an occupied site, mark the factory's vehicle and pedestrian routes on that drawing too.

## Let the closure plan determine the production route

Divide the roof into the actual zones the owner will release. For each zone, identify when removal can begin, when new panels can be received and when the building must be handed back. A large total roof area can still produce a poor equipment purchase if the available zones are small, scattered and separated by long waits.

Use the table to compare the same replacement zone under different supply routes. Put the owner's agreed limits in the last column; the equipment quotation can then address a defined job.

| Decision for one replacement zone | On-site forming with a lift | Delivered finished panels | Buyer entry / confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Available closure window | Includes setup, forming, handover and release | Includes unloading, staging and lifting | Factory owner: enters released start and handback times to choose the feasible supply route |
| Ground occupation | Lift position, coil supply and protected material route | Delivery vehicle, panel storage and handling space | Site surveyor: records available length, width and route clearances at the proposed position |
| Panel length and sequence | Produce against the released zone's cut list | Order and label against the same zone | Roof designer: issues profile revision, cut lengths and panel IDs for both supply quotations |
| Existing roof condition | Independent roof survey still needed | Independent roof survey still needed | Roof assessor: supplies condition survey and permitted work zones to define the replacement method |
| Equipment package | Lift, compatible former and agreed interfaces | Handling equipment and installation access | Buyer: lists lift, former, chassis, receiver and access providers with priced inclusions/exclusions |
| Interruption recovery | Preserve access to the next coil and work zone | Preserve access to correctly identified bundles | Factory operations lead: enters permitted restart window and available storage to plan interruption recovery |

## Protect the factory's operating space before choosing the chassis

A truck-mounted package is worth comparing where a defined approach and setup position can be released for the agreed work. A crawler package deserves comparison where the project includes several reviewed setup locations and a suitable route between them. Neither arrangement makes an occupied loading yard automatically available.

Check the complete route: delivery arrival, unloading, setup, coil replenishment and departure. If finished goods occupy the surveyed position during the work window, that position is unavailable for forming. Obtain the owner's permitted occupation plan rather than relying on an empty-site photograph. Include fire access and emergency routes in the site team's review.

## Two ways a promising re-roofing purchase can fail

**Failure case: the forming station blocks the factory's only dispatch lane.** The buyer selects the lift using roof height and panel length, then discovers that coil delivery occupies the same lane as finished-goods trucks. Increasing forming capacity will not fix this conflict. Reposition the proposed station or agree a workable occupation window; if neither is possible, compare an alternative panel supply route before buying.

**Failure case: new panels arrive faster than the replacement zone can accept them.** The equipment plan assumes uninterrupted forming, while removal, inspection and weather protection release only small sections. Panels accumulate without an agreed storage position. Base production batches on the released installation zones and include temporary holding arrangements in the contractor's plan. Do not use the remaining roof as an assumed storage area.

These are planning examples, not reported ARCLIFT customer projects. Their purchasing lesson is practical: compare accepted, installed work within the closure window, rather than the former's advertised output alone.

Existing roof condition can stop either route. The UK HSE's [guidance on fragile surfaces](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/fragile.htm) identifies rooflights and corroded metal sheets among the hazards and stresses coordination with the occupier. Use the site's competent roof assessment and applicable local requirements to determine the work method.

The exposed steel framing and safety netting in this photograph show the structural bays to locate in a receiving-area survey. Record openings, retained sheets and proposed replacement zones on the project drawing; a new roof-panel machine is not a structural assessment.

## A focused equipment shortlist

Start with the **[ARC-T18 catalog model](/products/arc-t18-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/)** for a truck-mounted proposal in its catalog 18.5 m reference band, or **[ARC-C25](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/)** for a crawler proposal in the 25 m reference band. Confirm the actual required outlet position, support arrangement and approved configuration on RFQ; the band alone does not select the machine.

If the job separately requires crew access from inside the building, compare the **[ARC-F25 large-deck platform](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/)** against that task and floor route. Keep the access-platform scope separate from the lift carrying the roll former. Do not assume that buying one supplies the function of the other.

## RFQ attachments for an occupied-factory project

- Roof plan showing replacement zones, retained areas and the current survey reference.
- Section with the required panel outlet datum, roof slope and receiving position.
- Profile drawing, panel lengths, cut list and installation sequence for the first zone.
- Owner-approved work windows and the required handback condition.
- Ground layout with dispatch lanes, emergency routes and proposed equipment positions.
- Access photos, measured route dimensions and available ground or floor assessment.
- Coil and former data, including whether an existing former must be integrated.
- Destination, power supply, and a clear list of lift, chassis, former and crew-access items required.
