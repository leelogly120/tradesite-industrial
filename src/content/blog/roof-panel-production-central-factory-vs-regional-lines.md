Keep production in one central factory when its delivery routes serve the required panel lengths and repeat regional demand does not support a staffed second operation. Add a regional forming line when confirmed local orders, practical coil supply and a complete local production team justify manufacturing near those customers. Open a sales or dispatch depot first when the factory can meet the orders but customers need better local service.

This decision is about the manufacturing network, not where to park a lift fleet. A regional sales address, a finished-panel stock point and a working factory are three different investments. Select the smallest complete operating model that serves the actual order book, then add production where its commercial case is independent and measurable.

## Identify what the region cannot get from the central plant

Start with orders the central factory cannot fulfil competitively under the existing delivery arrangement. Name the problem: finished-panel length, route access, delivery frequency, damage and replacement handling, or a genuine capacity shortage. Use actual quotations and order records rather than drawing a general delivery circle on a map.

If customers need a nearby contact, technical coordination or stocked standard items, a depot with a defined supply schedule is the first route to price. A forming line adds material stock, operators, utilities, tooling, inspection, maintenance and dispatch. Local presence alone does not require all of those functions.

If the central line is full, compare increasing its usable output with opening another plant. Use the [production-bottleneck worksheet](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-production-bottleneck-planning/) before calling the constraint a regional problem. If the true difficulty is transporting the required finished lengths, compare regional manufacturing with an identified project-specific site-production service as well.

## Give each candidate region its own demand case

List customers, controlled profiles, material families, expected order sizes and required delivery dates for each region. Separate awarded orders from repeat historical business and uncommitted sales prospects. Use the first two categories to build the base plan and show prospects separately as an expansion scenario.

Measure the share of orders that the nominated local line can actually produce. A regional plant that handles one recurring profile still needs a route for the rest of the catalogue. Price central supply or an identified outside provider for those products. Do not assume every region needs every profile or that one standard machine covers every customer requirement.

Also identify who buys the local coil and who owns slow-moving stock. Producing closer to customers moves material responsibility as well as production. A regional site with irregular demand can hold partial coils while the central plant holds the same material family for other customers. Include that duplicate inventory in the investment comparison.

The choice is regional when the complete local operation serves the actual orders better than the other priced routes and can be staffed and supplied. Keep central production when those conditions are absent. A depot is the correct outcome when it solves the delivery-service problem while avoiding an unsupported manufacturing investment.

## Standardise the product before duplicating the machine

Customers ordering the same panel from two plants need the same controlled product definition and acceptance basis. Establish the profile drawing, material specification, inspection method, sample identification and revision owner before buying the regional line. The machine model names alone do not ensure that output from different locations is interchangeable.

Give the quality manager authority over product revisions. When a profile or material changes, identify the affected orders and production locations, issue the controlled change and retain the relevant acceptance records. Separate local scheduling decisions from product-definition changes so urgent orders do not silently create a second version of the product.

The regional equipment proposal should include its own product trial. Use material representative of the region's nominated supply route and apply the same acceptance criteria as the central plant. Count the necessary inspection resources in the startup cost. Do not rely on the central team's availability if travel and remote support have not been included in the operating plan.

## Expand one region at a time

Make the first regional plant a defined investment with its own order scope, cost record and acceptance evidence. Track accepted output, delivered-order cost, stock tied up, rework and customer delivery performance against the central or depot alternative over matching periods. Compare the same customer mix; a region serving difficult exceptional orders will not have the same cost pattern as a plant serving long repeat runs.

Use those measured results to decide whether the next region needs a production line, a depot or project-specific service. A successful first plant is evidence for its own operating conditions. Expansion needs a new local demand, staffing and logistics case rather than automatic duplication of the first site's equipment list.

For unusually long project panels, the [fixed-factory versus site-production guide](/blog/fixed-roof-panel-factory-line-vs-mobile-site-production/) provides the alternative route. A temporary site arrangement serves an awarded project; it should not be counted as a permanent regional factory unless the continuing operating requirements are separately met.

## Two expansion mistakes to avoid

**Failure case: every sales enquiry becomes assumed factory demand.** A distributor buys regional lines using a combined national prospect list. Each location receives too little repeat work to support its own staff and stock. Separate confirmed and historical demand by region, then price a depot or central dispatch for regions that lack a manufacturing case.

**Failure case: matching machine names replace matching product records.** Two plants produce a panel sold under one catalogue description, but their drawing revisions and acceptance samples differ. Replacement orders cannot be allocated confidently between locations. Put the controlled product definition and both line trials into the expansion package before promising common supply.
