## The Fundamental Difference

Telescopic boom lifts extend in a straight line — like a telescope. They reach maximum horizontal distance (outreach) but can only go where the boom points.

Articulating boom lifts have joints (knuckles) along the boom arm. They can bend around obstacles, reach over structures, and access confined spaces that a straight boom can't reach.

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on what you're trying to reach and what's in the way.

## Telescopic Boom Lifts: Maximum Reach

**How they work:** A straight telescoping arm extends outward from the base. The platform follows the boom's path in a straight line.

**Strengths:**
- Maximum horizontal outreach for a given platform height
- Simpler mechanics, generally lower maintenance cost
- Faster setup — no articulation to manage
- Better for open, unobstructed spaces

**Typical specifications:**
- Working height: 20m–60m
- Horizontal outreach: 10m–25m
- Platform capacity: 230–500kg

**Best applications:**
- Open construction sites with clear access
- Bridge and highway maintenance
- Ship loading and unloading
- Large warehouse ceiling work (where column spacing allows straight-line access)
- Exterior building maintenance on flat facades

## Articulating Boom Lifts: Maximum Versatility

**How they work:** Multiple boom sections connected by joints allow the arm to bend and fold. The platform can reach up, over, and around obstacles.

**Strengths:**
- Can reach over obstacles (walls, equipment, structures)
- Access confined spaces with complex geometry
- Up-and-over reach for elevated platforms
- More positioning options in tight areas

**Typical specifications:**
- Working height: 15m–50m
- Horizontal outreach: 7m–20m (less than telescopic for same height)
- Platform capacity: 230–500kg
- Up-and-over clearance: varies by model

**Best applications:**
- Industrial plant maintenance with pipes, ducts, and structural elements in the way
- Construction sites with multiple levels and obstacles
- Stadium and arena work where roof structures block straight-line access
- Airport terminal maintenance with signage, lighting, and HVAC in the path
- Any situation where you need to reach "around" something

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Factor | Telescopic | Articulating |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Horizontal outreach | Longer | Shorter |
| Obstacle clearance | Poor | Excellent |
| Setup speed | Faster | Slower |
| Maintenance cost | Lower | Higher |
| Turning radius | Larger | Smaller (some models) |
| Price | Lower | Higher |
| Versatility | Limited | High |

## What About Pick & Carry?

Neither telescopic nor articulating boom lifts address the fundamental limitation of ceiling work: the need for a large, mobile work surface directly under the ceiling plane.

Boom lifts position a single worker (or two) at a specific point. For ceiling work that covers thousands of square meters — installing panels, running conduit, mounting lighting — you need a platform that moves along the ceiling line, not just reaches a single point.

This is where crawler under-ceiling platforms like the ARCLIFT FDDPT series fill a different niche entirely. The FDDPT isn't a boom lift — it's a mobile work platform that travels while elevated. The 12m×8m platform covers ceiling sections that would require dozens of boom lift repositions.

## Decision Framework

**Choose telescopic when:**
- You need maximum horizontal reach
- The access path is clear and unobstructed
- You're working on exterior facades or open structures
- Budget is the primary constraint

**Choose articulating when:**
- Obstacles block straight-line access
- You need up-and-over reach
- The work area has complex geometry
- You're in an industrial or congested environment

**Choose pick & carry (FDDPT) when:**
- You're working on ceilings (stadiums, airports, warehouses)
- You need a large work surface, not just a single point
- The project covers hundreds or thousands of square meters
- You need to move while elevated with materials
- Speed and productivity matter more than reach distance

## The Bottom Line

Telescopic and articulating boom lifts serve different access challenges. For ceiling-level work covering large areas, neither is ideal — that's a pick-and-carry job. Match the platform type to the work type, not the other way around.
