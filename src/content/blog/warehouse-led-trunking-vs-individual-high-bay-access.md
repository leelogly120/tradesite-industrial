**Choose LED trunking when the approved lighting layout follows repeatable aisles and the complete rail, feeds and service package costs less. Choose individual high-bays where suitable attachment and connection points are scattered or roof interruptions make the rail route expensive. Use an ARC-F25 shortlist only where the reviewed equipment offer covers consecutive personnel positions from a released floor zone. Ask both lighting bidders for itemised quotes for one typical aisle and one interrupted aisle before committing either equipment package.**

A lighting schedule gives you the number and identity of the fixtures. To buy the installation, you also need the length and delivery format of the rails, the position of each feed or fixture connection, and the locations where installers will stand. A continuous row on a lighting plan can cross several different work conditions.

The photograph looks along several bays from floor level. Mark the row and floor release together. Their overlap gives the access bidder the stretch of aisle it can assess for consecutive personnel positions.

## Price a complete lit aisle

Start with the same issued lighting brief for both bidders. A lighting designer accepts the layout, distribution of light and any emergency lighting or controls requirements. The buyer compares two ways of delivering that result. Include changes needed at the supports and electrical interfaces, plus attendance through the lighting contractor's acceptance work.

For trunking, the schedule should identify each rail run, its supports, feeds, joints and end points, plus the compatible luminaire modules. [Signify's Philips Maxos fusion](https://www.signify.com/global/prof/indoor-luminaires/trunking/philips-maxos-fusion/LP_CF_LL500X_EU/family) is an example of a common wired track that accepts compatible luminaire modules. Obtain a compatible component schedule for the offered rail and list any included sensors or emergency modules explicitly. Ask how the priced run crosses an aisle break or starts again after a structural obstruction. The start and finish of a rail section are work positions in their own right.

For standalone fittings, the [Philips CoreLine Highbay family](https://www.signify.com/global/prof/indoor-luminaires/high-bay-and-low-bay/high-bay/philips-coreline-highbay-gen5/LP_CF_9243999_EU/family) illustrates an individually suspended luminaire with a local connector and an optional mounting bracket. Obtain the proposed support and electrical connection for each location. A feed serving a group of fittings can still leave local work at each fixture. Price those visits from the actual arrangement.

## The aisle break belongs in the estimate

Ask the estimator to mark up an uninterrupted row and a row interrupted by a crane runway, ventilation branch or fire separation. Identify which ceiling positions remain near each other and which need a different approach. If rail is offered in multiple runs, compare the real support and connection quantities. If standalone fittings cluster in one cleared bay, count the crew positions from the accepted access proposal and use that figure in the labour estimate.

Buy trunking where the repeated aisle layout is accepted and the rail, installation and priced service visits together cost less. Retain standalone fixtures in isolated areas where the separate hardware better fits the accepted layout. Price mixed zones explicitly if the designer elects to combine systems. Shared rail spares and independent fixture replacements then have identifiable locations and owners.

## Quote a service call before making the purchase

Give each bidder the same service scenario. Ask what the trade needs to do when a fitting over an active aisle fails, what parts it orders, which zone it needs the operator to release and what personnel position it needs. For trunking, ask where a replacement compatible with the offered rail comes from and whether the case involves the lighting module or part of the common backbone. For individual fittings, check the accepted replacement mounting and connection arrangement. Include return access in both examples. Ask whether the replacement uses the same mounting position and what changes if the original product is discontinued.

A wide deck offers working area for adjacent tasks. Draw the crew's work positions, tool space and hatch or edge restrictions on the quoted equipment drawing. Keep delivered rail sections and any loads requiring specialist presentation on the contractor's separate handling schedule.

## Two estimating mistakes that make an aisle look cheaper

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A bidder totals fixtures and metres of uninterrupted trunking. A crane runway divides the real layout into separate sections and the price leaves feeds, ends and extra possessions unfunded. Obtain a run-by-run offer against the exception bay and carry its added work into the comparison.

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A warehouse buys a large-deck platform during shell works and uses the empty shell route to justify all future lighting maintenance. Racks and guarded loading areas later remove the base positions below several fittings. Plot one actual maintenance visit in the final layout before counting its utilisation value.

## What to include in the aisle access RFQ

1. Named aisles and the designer's issued lighting layout and acceptance brief.
2. Rail segment, feed and joint schedule or separate fixture support and connection schedule.
3. A normal bay and an interrupted bay, with roof-grid references.
4. Installer's personnel work positions and hands-on heights from the local floor.
5. Crew, tools and permitted task items plus separate handling needs.
6. Surveyed openings and floor routes, proposed support positions and linked floor records.
7. Fit-out release windows, acceptance visit dates and a sample future service task.
8. Delivery destination, equipment entry and withdrawal dates, and required support scope.
