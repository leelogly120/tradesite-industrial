**Buy factory-finished module roofing where the accepted building system delivers its final weathering roof through the modules and specified site joints. Buy a separate site overroof where joined modules require a further continuous roof assembly.** Invest in metal forming only for the sheets actually retained by your factory or site contract. A roof-shaped module top can be a structural deck, an intermediate layer or the final weathering surface; those are different production orders.

The useful purchasing question is where the completed building obtains its permanent roof. Settle that point before ordering repeated short panels, a site former or a roof-forming lift. Otherwise, a module production forecast can be mistaken for a metal-roof sales forecast.

## Follow the building section, not the module count

Start with the module arrangement and the accepted roof build-up. Identify top-level modules, intermediate modules, separate roof structures and the connections between units. Assign each physical layer to its function and supplier.

Steel roofing exists within modular building products: [Portakabin identifies a steel roof among its building components](https://www.portakabin.com/gb-en/faqs/standards-and-structural-design/what-are-your-buildings-made-from/). That product fact is a starting point for a specific offer. The buyer still needs the selected building system's completion boundary and joint details.

Where the modules contain the final metal roof, the factory's repeat sheet schedule can support a defined production cell. Include the actual special pieces, end conditions and finishing operations. Where the final roof spans joined units, the site-roof schedule determines the production case instead. Intermediate module tops belong in their own bill of materials and should not be counted again as permanent weathering panels.

## Factory work can remain an intermediate roof stage

A useful example is [NibbiPrefab's account of its temporary-to-permanent roofing approach](https://www.nibbi.com/news/nibbiprefabs-innovative-roofing-solution-featured-in-modular-advantage-magazine/). In that specific system, an air and vapour barrier is installed during module manufacture, its interfaces are completed on site, and additional insulation, cover board and a final membrane complete the roof.

The commercial lesson is to identify each delivered layer accurately. Record factory-installed components and the remaining finished-roof layers separately. Equally, a retained factory layer can form part of the final assembly rather than being discarded. Use the selected system's actual specification to allocate the work.

For a proposed metal overroof, obtain the accepted structural and weathering assembly from the responsible designers. Price the supports, sheets, closures, installation and access included in that offer. An optional overroof added to a drawing is not yet an accepted metal production order.

## Put the order into three separate quantities

Record factory roofing components, site joint-completion components and the final site-roof components separately. A component appearing in more than one schedule needs a defined handover, rather than a second purchase.

For factory metal roofing, identify each recurring module family and the accepted sheet lengths. Carry special end units and changes in the building design into the tooling and cutting proposal. A regular module footprint does not make every roof part identical.

For site joint completion, identify the issued connection and closure package. The module provider and roofing contractor should agree what arrives with the unit and what the site crew supplies. The required access follows those real positions after modules are placed.

For the final site roof, use the building-wide panel layout. That layout can create lengths and receiving needs different from individual module dimensions. The selected former and handling route must serve those objects in the actual assembled-building stage.

## Decide where a former earns its place

A factory line is justified by a stable eligible component schedule across actual orders. Compare making those parts with buying accepted sheets delivered into the module assembly process. Include coil handling, receiving, inspection and storage in the owned-cell offer.

A site forming package needs a separate case: the final site roof's lengths, quantities, production positions and release sequence. Owning a busy module factory does not itself justify owning an elevated former. If final roofs are bought as complete specialist packages, that work belongs to the specialist's equipment case.

Where the business retains both duties, show two production allocations and their dates. Determine whether one supported equipment arrangement can serve them without conflicting commitments. The [roof cassette comparison](/blog/prefabricated-roof-cassettes-vs-site-built-roof-package/) concerns individual prefabricated roof assemblies; a volumetric building purchase also needs the unit hierarchy and mate-line completion resolved.

## Two hypothetical module-roof mistakes

**Hypothetical failure case: every module becomes a roof-panel order.** A buyer sizes a line from the total module count, including intermediate units and roofs already supplied within another package. Separate the permanent weathering schedule from internal module components, then calculate the retained metal workload.

**Hypothetical failure case: an early layer is purchased as the finished roof.** Another buyer removes the site roofing allowance because modules arrive with a factory-installed weather-protection layer. The accepted design still requires subsequent layers and final completion. Price the actual delivered state against the finished-building endpoint before selecting equipment or closing the roof contract.
