**Buy seat removal and reinstatement when the complete released-floor method is accepted and its total cost is lower than a complete retained-seat access package. Retain seating only with a separately accepted arrangement serving every required ceiling position, including protection, material handling and final reopening.** Removing seats releases objects from the room; it does not flatten a stepped floor or establish machine support.

The commercial decision belongs to the auditorium refit, not just the access booking. Seats have an identity, location, condition and reinstatement obligation. A floor route has geometry and structural requirements. Keeping both sets of information visible prevents an apparent platform saving from becoming a larger seating or reopening cost.

## Define what removing the seats actually buys

Have the venue and seating specialist identify the rows, components and associated services included in removal. Record what remains: floor tiers, fixed pedestals, rails, barriers, power or data interfaces and any retained furnishings. A drawing showing an empty seating layout is inadequate if permanent geometry still interrupts the proposed machine route.

Obtain the seating provider's scope for identification, removal, storage, condition management, reinstallation and acceptance. Include the facility's actual constraints on storage and reopening. Avoid assuming the refit contractor can move those duties into an unpriced general protection allowance.

The building team should then review the resulting route and supporting positions using the equipment supplier's actual configuration information. If seat removal leaves no usable ground arrangement, it has not created the proposed access benefit. Compare another method before committing the seating work solely for the platform.

## Retained seating is a different service purchase

For a retained-seat route, obtain a complete specialist access proposal for the actual ceiling tasks. Its boundary needs to identify installation and removal, structural interfaces, seat protection, material delivery and the task positions served. Do not treat a cover over seats as an engineering design or a working surface.

[HSE's MEWP guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm) makes equipment selection depend on the job and site, including ground and overhead conditions. That Great Britain guidance supports examining the actual auditorium arrangement; it supplies no permission for a platform to cross seating or stand on tiers in your project.

## Reopening is the comparable endpoint

Ask both bidders to price the same completed ceiling scope and venue handback. Seat removal can add early work and later reinstatement. Retained-seat access can add specialist installation, protection and constraints on material routes. Neither route wins from platform rental days alone.

Give the table to the venue operator as well as procurement. A cheaper access method that consumes a critical rehearsal, testing or reopening period needs its full programme cost shown. Use the operator's actual commitments; do not invent revenue loss or apply a generic ticket-sales calculation.

## Make the seating and ceiling quantities agree

Divide the room into zones linked to actual ceiling tasks. Removing every seat is unnecessary if the accepted ground arrangement only needs specified released areas. Equally, a small removal allowance is inadequate if the proposed delivery or support arrangement occupies additional rows.

The provider should state which zones its equipment or service addresses. Mark isolated perimeter, balcony or service positions separately. A combined purchase can use a broad deck in accepted open-floor zones and another documented arrangement for exceptions. Its price must include the interfaces rather than pretending one partial solution serves the whole auditorium.

The low platform below helps frame the intervals between those zones. If equipment remains inside while seating or other refit work proceeds, reserve its actual waiting location and exit route in the programme.

Include final ceiling inspections and any authorized returns before completing seat reinstatement where the chosen sequence requires that order. Where seats must return earlier, price the later access method explicitly. The objective is a credible complete programme, not an absolute rule that seating always comes last.

## Two hypothetical refits with an incomplete saving

**Failure case: the empty-seat plan is mistaken for a level floor.** A buyer orders removal and a crawler platform without retaining the floor tiers on the access section. The proposed route remains interrupted. Review the permanent geometry and offered equipment before paying to remove seats for an access benefit the project cannot obtain.

**Failure case: the comparison stops at the last ceiling panel.** Another bid includes inexpensive floor access but omits seat storage, reinstatement and service checks. A retained-seat proposal includes its full removal and handback. Compare both at the same accepted venue reopening, with every seating duty priced.
