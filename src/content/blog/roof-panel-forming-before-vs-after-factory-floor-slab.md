Choose pre-slab roof-panel production when an accepted temporary or external setup completes the supply route and the programme includes its later removal. Choose post-slab production only when the responsible engineer accepts the actual equipment cases on the completed floor and the main contractor reserves that floor for the work. If neither stage provides a complete accepted route, buy another panel-supply arrangement before ordering the lift.

The permanent slab is a construction milestone and an engineered asset. Its presence changes levels, services, finishes and trade occupation; it does not automatically create a suitable equipment platform. Compare the complete cost of working before that milestone with the complete cost and availability of working afterward.

## The floor stage changes more than the surface

For a pre-slab proposal, identify the temporary support and travel areas, the construction work needed to provide them and the point at which they must be removed. Include coils, the former, delivery or transport equipment and panel receiving in the layout. An accepted machine position alone leaves the material route incomplete.

For a post-slab proposal, obtain the floor engineer's assessment against the actual proposed configurations and support or travel cases. The floor owner also has to release the area for the proposed duration. Strength acceptance, access permission and programme reservation are distinct decisions.

## Compare three supply arrangements at the same roof endpoint

The first arrangement produces before the permanent floor, using the project's accepted temporary or perimeter support. Include enabling works, agreed maintenance or inspection, material supply and reinstatement so the slab contractor receives the required work area afterward.

The second produces after floor release. Include the actual accepted floor-use scope, protection required by its owner, occupied aisles, material storage and restoration of the area when the package leaves. Do not substitute a generic waiting period for the responsible engineer's release.

The third uses an external production position or delivered panels while floor work proceeds. It still needs an accepted panel receiver and handling resources. Compare it when either internal option conflicts with the slab programme, rather than assuming the only alternatives are delaying the slab or delaying the roof.

| Buyer input | Pre-slab entry | Post-slab entry | Owner and decision use |

| Material route | Coil receipt, storage and feed positions ___ | Coil receipt, storage and floor-use boundaries ___ | Logistics lead tests the complete supply path |
| Work remaining | Removal/reinstatement before slab activity ___ | Protection/restoration before floor handback ___ | Site owner defines the accepted final condition |

Keep the roof receiver tied to the project datum in both columns. A new finished-floor level changes the relationship to equipment supported there. The supplier should receive the actual supporting surface and required outlet point, rather than a roof height measured from a different construction-stage reference.

## Separate acceptance from the promised production start

A floor can meet an engineer-defined release condition while being unavailable because another trade owns the area. Conversely, an apparently empty finished slab can lack the required equipment-use acceptance. The equipment schedule needs both the technical release and the programme reservation.

List the actual deliverables and their owners: the equipment configuration inputs, the support assessment, the work-area release and the panel-receiving readiness. Use their agreed dates in the procurement programme. Do not promise setup, production or floor return durations from a catalogue model name.

If the post-slab option misses the roofing requirement, compare a supported pre-slab or external route. If temporary support would duplicate expensive works without a useful programme benefit, compare the accepted post-slab package instead. This is a full-project decision, not a rule that roofing must always precede or follow the permanent floor.

## Include everything that has to leave before the next trade

A long former can have a separate transport or handling arrangement from its lift. Coils, auxiliaries, supports and remaining panels also occupy space. Identify their actual removal scope and the surface condition the slab or floor owner expects at handback.

## Two hypothetical floor-milestone failures

**Failure case: a finished slab is treated as a universal machine approval.** Procurement books production from the floor completion date without the configuration-specific floor-use assessment. Obtain the responsible engineer's acceptance for the proposed cases and the main contractor's area release before selecting the post-slab option.

**Failure case: temporary equipment remains in the slab contractor's work area.** The pre-slab quote ends at final panel production, while the former and material still require removal. Price the complete removal and reinstatement scope and use its actual handback event in the construction programme.
