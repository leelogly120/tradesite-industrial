Choose recovery-oriented disassembly when the owner requires an accepted panel-reuse output and its contract delivers that output by the removal deadline. Choose the complete scrap route when recyclable material is the required output. When either output is acceptable, choose the lower complete net removal cost after both proposals meet the same handback deadline. Obtain the recipient's acceptance and value terms, then request two complete net contract prices before assigning a recovery credit.

The decision to remove the donor roof is already made, and the replacement roof is fixed. The procurement question is what output the removal contractor must deliver and where its responsibility ends. Recovering panels for further use and delivering metal to a scrap outlet create different contracts.

<!-- audit-section: buyer-intent -->
## Recipient acceptance comes before a recovery premium

Name the intended recipient and accepted use before paying for preservation. Specify which recovered output the recipient will consider, who inspects it and what evidence is required at transfer. Use the existing-roof survey to connect those criteria to the proposed removal package.

Map the surveyed donor panels to the removal zones before assigning an output category. Record the actual material and condition entering the contract, then use those zone references to keep accepted and rejected outputs traceable after removal.

An expression of interest in “used metal” is weaker than a defined acceptance route for the intended panels. Put the recipient's output criteria and the release authority into the recovery offer. If acceptance occurs only after inspection, allocate the rejected output and its disposition cost in advance.

## Opening and cutting tools support different output briefs

[DRACO's K9-1 ERB page](https://www.dracotools.com/en/products/roofing-system/k9-1-erb/) identifies a fold-opening roll for dismantling, restoration, repair or opening a seam. The listed opening accessory belongs to the maker's own equipment range. The contractor must match the proposed equipment to the surveyed seam and the required recovered output.

[Buschmann's Falz Cutter page](https://buschmanntools.com/en/products/cutting-tools/falz-cutter) describes a cutting tool for dismantling double-standing-seam roofs by dissecting the seam. That supplies a real cutting category for the specified seam type, with a different output brief from preservation-oriented opening. The buyer should evaluate the contractor's complete scope and accepted output, with the roof-specific method owned by the responsible specialist.

[RHEINZINK's circular-economy account](https://www.rheinzink.com/sustainability/sustainable-planning-and-building/circular-economy/) distinguishes scrap collection for remelting from a named Kiasma Museum example in which intact dismantled titanium-zinc panels were assigned to further use. The manufacturer's example demonstrates that the two output routes exist. The present donor roof requires its own condition assessment, recipient and acceptance criteria.

These sources explain why a seam-opening tool price and a seam-cutting tool price are poor substitutes for complete removal quotations. The purchased outcomes include handling, protection, inspection and final disposition, with the necessary equipment selected within that contract.

## Give both contractors the same end point

Set the same removal zones, retained items, handback condition and completion date. Ask the recovery bidder to price accepted panels at the named transfer point and the handling of rejected material. Ask the scrap bidder to price identified material at its stated outlet, including deductions and transport.

| Contract input | Owner | Datum to enter | Decision controlled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common removal boundary | Owner and designer | Zone plan, retained items and revision: ___ | Comparable physical scope |
| Existing roof condition | Roof specialist | Material, seam and survey record: ___ | Credible removal proposal |
| Intended recipient | Recipient and product authority | Accepted use and output criteria: ___ | Basis for recovery value |
| Output classification | Contractor and inspector | Accepted/rejected categories and release owner: ___ | Payable recovered output |
| Scrap disposition | Disposition provider | Outlet, deductions and net-value terms: ___ | Comparable scrap outcome |
| Complete removal resources | Removal contractor | Method responsibility, handling and protection: ___ | Complete purchased package |
| Custody and transfer | Owner and logistics lead | Storage, responsibility and delivery point: ___ | Exposure before recipient handover |
| Net total and handback | Procurement lead | Accepted complete totals and common date: ___ | Award between deliverable routes |

The comparison should end at an agreed transfer event, rather than at “panels removed.” Identify who owns recovered panels while they wait for inspection, who provides storage, and who carries them to the recipient. For scrap, identify who owns the material and receives the stated credit. A credit retained by the contractor should appear clearly in the net price.

## Evaluate the recovery premium against accepted output

Calculate complete net removal cost as the quoted amount payable, plus owner-paid inspection, protection, storage, transport and rejected-output disposition, less credits actually received by the owner. A credit already deducted in a net contract quotation stays inside that figure and is subtracted only once. Compare both routes at the same handback deadline, using the recipient's actual value terms for accepted output.

Where output acceptance is uncertain until inspection, request clearly priced consequences for the accepted and rejected categories. This lets the owner compare the payable contract under the actual proposed terms. A general assumption that every removed panel earns the highest recovery value can make a preservation premium appear justified when the offered contract says otherwise.

The programme is equally concrete. Reserve the agreed time for inspection and transfer inside the removal commitment. An owner facing a fixed replacement start date must know which party handles recovered panels that remain at the site when the next contractor arrives. Price the fallback disposition and custody responsibility before award.

The replacement-panel production plan is a separate procurement stream. Keep its equipment, access zones and handover requirements coordinated with the removal programme while maintaining distinct output acceptance records. This prevents the new roof schedule from depending on an unassigned pile of old panels.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two contract mistakes to avoid

**Mistake — hypothetical:** An owner pays a recovery premium because the contractor offers a seam opener, while the proposed recipient has accepted neither the existing panel condition nor an intended use. The tool has been mistaken for a saleable output. Correct the award basis with named recipient criteria, inspection responsibility and priced rejected-output disposition.

**Mistake — hypothetical:** The scrap bidder prices cutting and removal from the roof, while the recovery bidder includes protected storage and delivery to a recipient. The owner selects the scrap figure as though both end at the same place. Correct both bids to the same handback date and defined transfer points, with all handling and disposition included.

If the recipient accepts only scrap material, procure that complete output route. Panel-preserving removal earns a premium when its separately defined accepted output provides the required value and delivery outcome.

## Attach eight items to the replacement-equipment RFQ

Use the contract worksheet above with the removal provider. Once that handback is defined, give the new-panel equipment supplier these eight production inputs:

1. Approved replacement profile drawing, seam details and product revision.
2. Actual new-panel material grade, gauge, coating and coil data.
3. New-panel quantities, length mix and required production-release dates.
4. Awarded removal handback zones, dates and retained material-storage areas.
5. Coil-to-former layout, receiving datum and planned panel handover.
6. Former identity, mass and mounting interface when a lift is included.
7. Ground or support information, utilities and equipment-access routes.
8. Destination, transport boundary and included forming-line or lift scope.
