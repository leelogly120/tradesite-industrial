Buy a construction hoist when the required result is delivering people or materials between designated building landings. Buy a ceiling or wall work platform when the crew needs an elevated working surface beside the installation task. Specify both packages when the job needs both results; one large deck does not combine them by default.

A request for a “construction lift” leaves this purchasing decision unresolved. The important question is what happens at the top of the journey. Does the load leave at a defined landing, or does the crew remain on the machine to complete work? Put that answer in the equipment schedule before comparing height classes.

## A landing is a destination; a workface is a place to work

A transport package connects identified receiving points. Its design must address the people or materials being transferred, the landing interfaces and the receiving operation. A working platform instead presents the crew, tools and task materials at the installation position. The two duties need different acceptance descriptions.

[Alimak's product-family explanation](https://alimak.com/news/introducing-the-alimak-350-range-one-platform-three-solutions/) separates construction hoists for vertical personnel and material transport from mast-climbing working platforms. That distinction is useful buying language. Its products and their interfaces belong to Alimak; it is not evidence that an ARCLIFT ceiling platform performs hoist duties.

Write the workface as an actual object: the underside fixing strip, a wall panel edge or an installation assembly. Then describe the space the crew occupies while completing it. The yellow upright arrangement in the photograph below illustrates why a wall task needs its own envelope rather than a floor number alone.

![A yellow upright work frame stands inside an unfinished hall beside another frame lying on the ground.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261030-wall-site.webp)
*Draw the working surface and the installation face together before requesting the platform arrangement.*

Do not turn the choice into a contest between two nominal heights. A machine selected for a broad working area is not automatically a means of delivering people through an upper-floor doorway. Likewise, delivery to a floor does not provide the local ceiling work surface once the crew arrives.

## Write two purchase lines when the work needs two outcomes

Use this worksheet at tender stage. Enter actual levels and drawing revisions in the blanks. Keep transport and working-position outputs separate even when one specialist offers a coordinated package.

| Buyer input | Project entry and datum | Responsible owner | Decision made from the entry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transport result | People/materials to be delivered ___; origin and destination landing IDs ___ | Logistics manager and trade supervisor | Identifies the required hoist or material-transfer service |
| Landing interface | Opening, receiving area and floor level relative to project datum ___ | Building designer and hoist specialist | Defines the complete delivered-to-landing package |
| Elevated task | Workface/grid ___; task height measured from its actual supporting floor ___ | Surveyor and installation lead | Defines the working-platform purchase separately |
| Working arrangement | Crew positions, tools and material distribution ___ | Installer supplies duty; equipment provider supplies arrangement | Tests the offered working surface against the actual task |
| Building support | Proposed support locations, reactions and structural references ___ | Equipment provider and structural engineer | Establishes the accepted equipment/structure combination |
| Material handoff | Delivered component dimensions and receiving-to-workface stages ___ | Material supplier and handling contractor | Exposes handling left between transport and installation |
| Mobilisation | Equipment delivery, assembly, removal and site occupation dates ___ | Logistics planner and respective providers | Completes both purchase scopes |
| Completion | Accepted transport output ___; accepted access positions ___; receiving owner ___ | Procurement lead and project manager | Prevents one quotation from being treated as two completed duties |

The logistics manager owns landing demand. The installer owns the working task. Equipment providers describe their actual configurations, and the building's engineer assesses their loads against the structure. A purchaser should not substitute one person's building-height estimate for those different inputs.

For a hall with no intermediate floor, a working platform can serve a continuous overhead area from the accepted supporting level. For floors separated by slabs, specify the local access package and its delivery route on each level. The [floor-by-floor access guide](/blog/multi-storey-building-ceiling-platform-access-packages/) develops that building geometry; the decision here is which transport and working duties to purchase.

## Follow the material beyond the hoist door

A ceiling panel delivered to a floor still has to reach its installation position. List the remaining movement, orientation and presentation stages. Assign each stage to the material-handling package or the installer's defined resources. Do not place a component-lifting allowance inside the personnel-platform price merely because both operate nearby.

The photograph below shows the underside of a broad working surface close to roof steel. It makes the final task relationship visible. The components brought to that task, and the means of presenting them, still need their own identified scope.

![The underside of a red mesh work platform sits below white roof panels and structural beams.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261101-deck-underside.webp)
*Locate the final working surface against the fixing line, then identify how task materials reach it.*

Use the same completed outcome when comparing offers. Include transport equipment, landing works, working access, component presentation, equipment mobilisation and removal where the project retains them. If an existing hoist already covers vertical delivery, price only the missing tasks. Do not buy a second transport resource to solve an installation-space problem.

A combined commercial offer is useful when it names both outputs and their handoff. A single line reading “lift included” is insufficient. Split that line into the delivered location, the served working positions and the resources between them before ranking the price.

## Two category mistakes to remove from a tender

**Mistake: a large work deck is ordered as a floor-delivery lift.** A hypothetical buyer chooses a ceiling-platform quotation because its catalog class exceeds a floor's elevation. The scope contains elevated work positions but no landing transfer arrangement. Restore the transport requirement as a distinct specialist package and compare its receiving interfaces before ordering.

**Mistake: a hoist delivery price is treated as complete ceiling access.** Another hypothetical tender covers people and materials reaching an upper level but omits the local installation surface. The crew reaches the floor without a priced method of working at the ceiling. Add the appropriate floor-based access and material-presentation scope to the comparison.

Neither mistake is solved by a higher model number. The procurement correction is to buy every retained duty once, with an identified provider and completion boundary.
