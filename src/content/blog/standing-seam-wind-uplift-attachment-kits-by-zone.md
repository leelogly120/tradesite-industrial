Buy one standing-seam wind-attachment package when the designer accepts the same uplift components and pattern for all zones and that option has the lower total installed cost. Buy zone-specific kits when the released uplift pattern or components vary by position. Obtain the engineer's roof-zone map and attachment drawing before pricing either route, and preserve the separate details for panel movement.

## Put the zone plan next to the bill of materials

The roof field is the interior area. Perimeters and corners are separately marked when the project's wind design assigns them different requirements. Ask the engineer for the applicable plan, pressure basis and accepted attachment details. Use the relevant grid lines, roof steps and plan boundaries to tie component quantities to the same coordinates the crew will have on site. Where one panel crosses two zones, count the placements assigned to each part of its run.

A Leon County, Texas roof tender required the standing-seam submittal to show uplift calculations and attachment patterns for the field, perimeter and corners. It also called for showing where panels are fixed for thermal movement. Use that example to scope submissions and obtain the actual release from your own engineer and system provider. A uniform bid needs one accepted uplift arrangement that covers every zone, including the locations with the greatest stated demand. One approved arrangement can simplify the stock issue and reduce part-selection errors. If it uses the same higher attachment count in the interior as at the corners, add that extra material and installation time. Weigh those costs and any specified support changes against the labor and logistics of issuing zone kits.

For a zoned bid, count actual attachment placements from the released plan and multiply by the component quantities given in each detail. Add labor and inspections for those placements. Where zones share a part number, issue the appropriate count for each bay from the same stock. Where the details name different parts, require packs labeled with the grid, zone and revision. Those packs can travel in one shipment.

## Keep the detail with its supporting evidence

Identify the panel and its material, clip and fastening arrangement, and support under the specific roof design. Where the contract uses a test or listing, ask the system provider to identify the represented assembly and have the responsible designer accept the project application. Attach that decision and its drawing revision to the order. If pricing includes extra rails or purlins, have the structural supply contractor price the change on its own scope.

FM's panel roof guidance discusses several uplift failure modes, including fasteners pulling out and seams releasing from clips. Its discussion shows that failure mode affects the relationship between clip spacing and uplift resistance. Price the released assembly for each zone. Refer changes in fastener, clip, panel or support to the project engineer and system provider before treating them as equivalent. Use the FM guidance as an example and obtain the insurer's requirements where they apply. [FM Property Loss Prevention Data Sheet 1-31](https://www.fm.com/FMAApi/data/ApprovalStandardsDownload?itemId=%7BAEF0413F-4019-4305-AF58-AB9D2911C525%7D)

Confirm the roof system's fixed and movement provisions with the designer as part of the documented tender return. The Metal Construction Association describes clip geometry as specific to its panel interface and links clip selection with thermal movement. Procurement should carry that approved allocation into the packing and replenishment schedule. [Metal Construction Association clip guide](https://www.metalconstruction.org/index.php/online-education/standing-seam-best-practices-clips)

## Two mistakes that turn into substitutions on site

**Mistake one.** In a hypothetical bid, the buyer prices the interior clip count across the whole roof and allocates the shortfall to site stock. At a corner, the crew receives the wrong quantity and part type. Require the schedule at award, then check each receiving pack against the released location and revision.

**Mistake two.** In another example, a contractor buys kits for a uniform arrangement proposed before a perimeter support revision. The offered clip and fastener combination was evaluated for the earlier support. Compare the affected zones against the revised accepted detail and release replacement packs before using the tender quantities there.

## RFQ attachments for a complete roof bid

1. Issued zone map with field, perimeter, corners and pressure reference.
2. Roof drawings showing support material and layout by zone.
3. Released panel sections, materials, lengths and piece marks.
4. Assembly evidence references and accepted project application.
5. Clip, fastener and fixed-point details by zone.
6. Attachment counts, installation labor and inspection stages by bay.
7. Pack labels, revision cutoff, return terms and replacement delivery dates.
8. Forming plan, trial sample and delivery schedule for retained panel work.

If the accepted package includes contractor-formed panels, use the [ARC-RF8 roll-forming line](/products/arc-rf8-roll-forming-machine/) as a catalog starting point. Request tooling and trial pricing with the controlled drawing and representative material. A zone schedule that changes attachments only can use the same released panel definition where the designer accepts it. Check the complete panel takeoff before buying separate tooling.
