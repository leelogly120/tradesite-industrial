Choose an overcladding equipment package only after the roof designer accepts retaining the existing roof within a defined new assembly. Choose the tear-off package when the accepted refurbishment scope requires removal before replacement. Then select the forming lift against that design's new panel receiver and material route. Keeping the old roof or removing it changes much more than the panel supply price.

For either route, compare delivered panels with on-site forming against the same finished roof product. An attractive lift position is not a reason to select a roof system, and a forming line buys only the specified sheet-production task. Retrofit framing, insulation, flashings, dismantling and installation access need their own priced owners.

## The roof-system decision comes before the equipment height

Overcladding adds an accepted new roof assembly above a retained existing roof. Tear-off removes the parts identified in the refurbishment scope and replaces them with the specified assembly. The designer decides which route suits the existing building and its performance requirements, using the actual survey and proposed system.

Roof Hugger's published specification treats retrofit sub-framing as an identified roof-system component and calls for an existing-roof survey covering equipment curbs, penetrations and flashing requirements. This supports a purchasing distinction: the new roof plane and interfaces need their own design package. Its details are specific to that system, rather than generic instructions for placing new panels on an old roof. [Roof Hugger structural retrofit specification](https://roofhugger.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Roof-Hugger-Specifications.pdf)

For the forming lift, obtain a section showing the proposed support level, former outlet and first panel receiver. On an overclad project, the old eave remains a survey reference while the new assembly defines the required receiving relationship. On a tear-off project, the receiver must be coordinated with the actual replacement stage and its available support.

## Two packages built around different site outputs

**The overcladding package** follows the accepted retained-roof design. Price the retrofit components, their delivery and installation, the new panels, receiving arrangements and defined access work. Identify rooftop items that require separate treatment at the new plane. A retained covering is not an automatically available stockyard or working platform; the project team must define its permitted role.

**The tear-off package** includes the specified removal work, waste movement, temporary protection and replacement sequence. Those tasks compete with incoming coil or panel movements. The forming package must serve the replacement areas that the roofing team can actually accept, rather than produce against the total roof area at once.

The distinction affects procurement even if both routes use the same former. One buys new-roof assembly work around retained material. The other also buys a removal and protection operation. Neither should be represented by a line-and-lift price alone.

## Keep production and the rest of the roof separated in the order

A repeat long-panel requirement supports an elevated-forming enquiry when the defined outlet and receiver make a useful production route. Small or isolated replacement areas with a practical delivery route should start with a finished-panel quotation. The refurbishment label by itself establishes neither the need for a line nor the need for a lift.

If the designer specifies an insulated panel product, buy or manufacture that actual product through a separately defined process. A single-sheet former is not a complete insulated-panel production system. Equally, the proposed former must address the released outer-sheet profile; a generic metal-roof description is insufficient.

## Illustrative purchasing mistakes with different remedies

**Mistake: the lift is quoted to the old roof plane.** Procurement supplies the existing eave dimension while the accepted overcladding design introduces another receiver relationship. The equipment offer therefore answers the wrong section. Submit the new assembly section and its outlet datum before accepting the configuration.

**Mistake: tear-off logistics disappear from the comparison.** One quotation includes replacement panels and production equipment, while the project budget omits removal, waste transfer and temporary protection. The apparent equipment saving leaves essential work unpurchased. Price those functions and their occupation of the site beside both production alternatives.

These examples concern purchasing scope, not a determination that an existing roof is fit to retain or remove. That decision remains with the appointed building and roof specialists.
