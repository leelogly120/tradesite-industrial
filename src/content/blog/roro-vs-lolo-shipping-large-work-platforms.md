**Book roll-on/roll-off (RoRo) when the carrier accepts the machine or its loaded rolling trailer, and both terminals can move that unit through the booked service. Book lift-on/lift-off (LoLo) when the carrier and terminals accept a documented crane-handling proposal for the packed unit and its transfer ashore.** Compare charges up to the same inland handover. A machine on tracks qualifies for neither route solely because it can move on a construction site.

Start with the configuration the equipment manufacturer releases for shipment. List the main unit, removed attachments and loose components. Record packed dimensions, weight, centre-of-gravity information where required and machine-specific handling restrictions. The carrier will need those facts before it can accept the booking. The importer needs them again when appointing a terminal or inland haulier.

The open container photograph shows folded red equipment and separate red frame pieces on a trailer. A photograph from a previous shipment tells the buyer what to inventory, but the current package needs its own list and transport drawing. Loose items continue to need a receiving and onward carriage plan whichever vessel handles the main unit.

## Rolling can include equipment that does not drive aboard

[Wallenius Wilhelmsen describes its RoRo handling equipment](https://www.walleniuswilhelmsen.com/what-we-do/ocean-transportation/handling-equipment), including equipment for moving cargo aboard on carrier-supplied units. Determine whether the carrier will accept self-propelled movement, require a roll trailer or propose a different handling method. Ask for the acceptance of the specific packed machine, port and sailing. A platform delivered on a roll trailer cannot be assumed to stay on that equipment for its inland road journey.

For a self-propelled proposal, the carrier specifies its requirements for operability, access, clearances, equipment condition and who will handle it. For a loaded trailer proposal, obtain bedding, securing and transfer responsibilities from the appointed specialists. The handling provider needs the machine manufacturer's transport information for the exact configuration. The commercial buyer should request the accepted plan and its cost, leaving movements and securing methods to the responsible professionals.

On LoLo, the booking involves a lift over the vessel side or hatch using the accepted lifting arrangement. Distinguish ship's gear from a shore crane and check both load and discharge terminals. [Spliethoff describes its sealift fleet and cargo-handling scope](https://www.spliethoff.com/markets/sealift/). A vessel having cranes does not settle whether a carrier can lift this machine, at this berth, in its proposed configuration. Obtain the carrier's booking acceptance and the manufacturer's handling data before comparing freight.

## Where apparently cheaper freight becomes dearer

Compare each offer from the named pickup to the same named delivery or handover point. Include terminal handling, specialist equipment, carrier-owned transport equipment, transfer to the road haulier, destination storage assumptions and separate pieces. Show exclusions as priced lines. Keep customs and tax treatment consistent between the alternatives. A freight-only difference is of limited value if one bid stops on the ship and the other includes delivery to a collecting trailer.

Ask the carrier to describe what happens when the accepted condition at delivery is different from the booking data. A non-running unit booked as a runner can require a different handling proposal, space or date. A machine booked for crane handling can arrive with additional packed sections or inaccessible nominated points. Keep the commercial consequences visible while obtaining revised technical acceptance from the responsible parties.

In the yard photograph, the green deck sits low while support feet and a side boom remain extended. A low deck alone gives no packed length or shipping configuration. Send the actual stowed drawing and the agreed packing list alongside photographs of the actual consignment.

## Two mistakes at discharge

**Mistake one — assuming the roll trailer travels to the customer's yard.** A hypothetical importer obtains a RoRo price and books an ordinary inland collection. The carrier requires its equipment to remain at the terminal. Add a named transfer provider and an accepted road vehicle before calling the route cheaper.

**Mistake two — obtaining crane acceptance at only one end.** The origin operator can handle the unit, but the destination quotation excludes discharge or the inland transfer. Secure both written acceptances against the same unit data, or change port and reprice the complete trip.
