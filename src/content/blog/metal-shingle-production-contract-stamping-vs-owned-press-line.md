Buy custom dies and contract the pressing when you have an accepted metal shingle design and saleable orders, but too few repeat runs to keep a complete press line occupied. Buy the line when specific shingle families justify the investment and your shop can deliver a finished, inspected carton through every stage. Count a separate order for long roof panels against the roll former that produces those panels.

A roofing brand can own a design, pay for tooling and sell the finished shingles while another business operates the press. The question is where the presses, people, storage and interruptions belong. Settle that before treating the price of a die as the cost of entering production.

## Start at the carton a roofer will accept

Select one released shingle and the adjacent pieces that complete its roof detail. Identify the visible face, engagement with neighbouring units, exposed course, acceptable finish and pack quantity. Give both bidders the same reference assembly and packaging requirement. Require them to say which pieces come from the quoted process and which accessories come from another source. A box of field shingles has limited value to an installer waiting for a matching starter or edge detail.

## What the contract shop has to return

Ask a contract stamper to quote a first production lot and its repeat order. Show material supply, setup, pressing, secondary operations, inspection, packing and carriage separately. Define who owns material rejected during start-up and who pays if agreed finish or engagement criteria fail. For a colour change, enter the minimum batch and how unused blanks and partly packed cartons will be returned or carried forward.

Tooling is its own purchase. Record who owns the physical dies, the drawings and the permitted manufacturing rights. Include maintenance records, identification, storage and the conditions for collecting the tools. Transfer rights have practical value only if another suitable shop can receive the die and use the controlled product information. Get the proposed receiving press and die interfaces assessed by the toolmaker before assigning a resale or transfer value.

Offer the contractor a realistic order pattern. Several short releases spread across the building season occupy more setup and booking windows than the same annual quantity in a single run. Price the actual number of releases and the time between material approval and finished cartons at the shipper. If the price assumes the entire campaign runs together, add the storage and cash required to carry the finished goods.

## Build the owned-line bid around the missing operations

Walk the product backwards from a labelled, protected carton to the incoming metal. For each step, mark the machine, supporting equipment, people, floor area and acceptance responsibility. A press and a die can be the largest entries on the quotation while leaving the shop to supply blank preparation, parts transfer, secondary forming or packing. Ask the equipment bidder to describe the proposed route for the exact product, including any stage it expects the buyer to subcontract.

Request a witnessed sample lot made from the released material and finish. Agree who evaluates the parts as an assembly, who records finish damage and who accepts packaged output after ordinary handling. For the operating budget, use accepted cartons per staffed shift from the proposed operating case, including changeovers, inspection and pack-out. A press-cycle figure alone gives no measure of complete cartons ready for collection.

## When a press earns its place

Compare the delivered cost of the same accepted cartons over the same purchasing period. For contract pressing, include tooling, booked setups, production, freight and the inventory created by minimum lots. For ownership, enter the installed and commissioned line, staff, supporting work, tools, maintenance and the cost of any operation still bought out. Give each option the same credit for usable finished stock at the end of the period. Count die expenditure once on the route where it is paid.

Run the comparison again with the largest unawarded family removed. If the machine still earns its place on released work and can meet the delivery sequence, proceed to a line quotation. If the economics depend on winning a different product family, keep the current stamping contract and make the future machine order conditional on that award.

## Two hypothetical purchasing mistakes

**Mistake one.** A brand buys its dies from a contractor and later assumes it can move production freely. The tool fits the current press, but the buyer has neither transfer drawings nor an accepted replacement shop. Negotiate the release terms and price a specific destination before treating the tooling as portable.

**Mistake two.** A bidder counts an awarded long-panel order toward the utilisation of a shingle press. The two products leave different processes, and the shingle line stays idle between releases. Put each order against the equipment that actually makes it before approving either investment.

## RFQ for a shingle production decision

- Attach controlled shingle drawings and the mating assembly reference.
- Identify approved stock, finish and visible-face acceptance criteria.
- List field pieces, starters, edges and the supplier responsible for each.
- Enter awarded cartons by delivery date and the requested release sizes.
- State die ownership, maintenance access and required transfer records.
- Request first-lot inspection, pack-out checks and rejection responsibilities.
- Identify site utilities, staffing and space for the complete proposed line.
- Separate any long-panel drawings and orders from the shingle schedule.
