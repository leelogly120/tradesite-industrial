**Choose a released-floor package when its entire equipment route and supporting positions are accepted and its complete closure cost beats the accepted specialist alternative. Buy specialist access when the retained pool condition or building geometry requires that arrangement.** Price both routes through reopening. Draining a pool leaves its basin geometry and support requirements for engineering review; a crawler platform is not a pool-spanning system.

The owner is buying a reopened facility with completed ceiling work. Equipment hire or purchase is only one part of that result. Water-area closure, building protection, specialist investigations and reinstatement can outweigh an apparent difference in access-day rates. Put those commitments into the comparison before ordering a platform.

## Give both proposals the same pool condition

State whether each pool is filled, drained, isolated or otherwise controlled during each work stage, using the facility's actual plan. The operator and appointed specialists own those decisions. A quotation describing “access above the pool” leaves its fundamental supporting arrangement unanswered.

For a released-floor proposal, show the delivery route through the building and the actual positions from which equipment is proposed to work. Distinguish surrounding walkways from the basin. Include steps, slopes, service covers, finishes and any other features affecting the connected route. Each proposed supporting position needs its own review against the offered machine information.

For specialist access, obtain a complete proposed method and commercial boundary from the responsible provider. Identify the task positions served, structural interfaces, construction and removal scope, protection and reopening duties. A supplier's word “bridge”, “gantry” or “suspended” is a category description, not a project design or permission to install it.

## Ceiling renewal and pool reopening share a programme

The ceiling specialist must identify the actual retained and replacement assembly, its condition survey and its required environmental performance. Water-area exposure belongs in that system decision. Selecting an access machine cannot resolve a damaged ceiling support or substitute for the specified ceiling product.

[Ecophon's swimming-hall renovation report](https://www.ecophon.com/en/articles/inspiration/swimming-hall/) describes a project where investigation revealed additional defects and the chosen ceiling arrangement affected access occupation. It is Ecophon's project, not an ARCLIFT reference. Its useful purchasing lesson is to connect investigation, the released ceiling scope and the facility closure before fixing the equipment period.

## Compare the whole closure with the whole access service

Use the operator's reopening milestone as the common endpoint. Include every activity needed to reach it, with responsibility and price assigned. A specialist-access offer that excludes protection and final removal should not be compared with a closure package including those duties.

| Purchase input | Fillable project record | Responsible owner and reference | Decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pool condition | Condition by basin and stage ___ | Facility operator; closure plan revision | Defines the actual work environment |
| Ceiling scope | Retained/replaced zones ___; system ___ | Ceiling specialist; condition survey and issued layout | Fixes the work to be served |
| Ground route | Route segments ___; basin/walkway boundaries ___ | Surveyor; levels from named building datum | Establishes possible machine approaches |
| Supporting positions | Position references ___; building records ___ | Building engineer; configuration-specific machine reactions | Decides whether floor access is feasible |
| Specialist method | Provider ___; served positions ___; exclusions ___ | Access specialist; project-specific proposal | Defines the alternative complete purchase |
| Closure activities | Water, plant, protection and reinstatement scope ___ | Operator and relevant specialists; facility plan | Prices duties outside personnel access |
| Task resources | Crew ___; materials ___; separate handling ___ | Ceiling installer; actual work stages | Defines working-space and load inputs |
| Reopening total | Complete cost ___; accepted reopening milestone ___ | Buyer and programme lead; same facility endpoint | Supports the commercial choice |

Keep the release decision separate from a promise about business continuity. If one pool remains open, define its actual operational boundary and the resources needed to maintain it. Do not treat retained water as proof that users can remain beneath or beside the work.

## Price the edges of the work, not just the centre

Include ceiling positions above perimeter walkways, changes in level, services and any areas outside the main basin. A specialist arrangement serving the centre can leave perimeter tasks to another package. Review walkway and water-area task coverage separately on each provider's proposed arrangement.

Ask every provider to identify those exceptions on the same drawing. Price a combined arrangement where the task genuinely needs one. The useful comparison is complete task coverage with accepted interfaces, rather than a contest to name one machine for the entire hall.

## Two hypothetical closures with an unpriced boundary

**Failure case: draining is treated as machine-floor acceptance.** A buyer orders a crawler deck because the pool will be empty, while route and supporting-position assessments are omitted from the purchase. Complete those project reviews before purchasing ground access, or compare an independently designed specialist alternative.

**Failure case: the cheaper access quote ends before reopening.** Another buyer selects a specialist service whose price excludes protection removal and the facility's reinstatement duties. Those activities later extend closure. Compare both routes at the same accepted reopening endpoint and include the named providers' full obligations.

Consider the [ARC-F25 crawler large-deck ceiling platform catalog model](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/) only for the ground-based work zones whose proposed arrangement and route can be accepted. Attach eight items to the RFQ:

1. Ceiling survey and released repair/replacement scope by zone.
2. Basin, walkway and building sections with named level datums.
3. Operator's pool-condition, closure and reopening plan.
4. Delivery route and proposed machine supporting positions.
5. Floor/structure records and the responsible engineering contacts.
6. Crew, tools, materials and separately appointed handling scope.
7. Protection, environmental conditions and reinstatement responsibilities.
8. Equipment dates, destination, removal and alternative-access boundaries.
