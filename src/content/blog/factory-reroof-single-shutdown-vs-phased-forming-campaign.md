Choose one concentrated re-roofing campaign when the owner can release the required areas and the contractor has the complete resources to finish the accepted roof scope within that shutdown. Choose phased forming only when each phase has a defined roof-completion boundary, a supported supply route and fully priced recurring mobilisation. Keep delivered panels available as a separately quoted option where a phase cannot support its own site-production package.

This decision concerns the exterior roof replacement campaign. It includes removal, the specified roof assembly, temporary protection where designed, panel production and receiving, associated details and accepted handback. A former's available operating hours are only one input to that complete programme.

## Define the roof condition handed back after each phase

Have the responsible roof designer and contractor mark the actual completion boundaries. Identify the retained roof, replacement panels, ends and junctions, associated components and any temporary arrangement that belongs to the selected work method. The owner defines which building areas return to use and the required handover evidence.

Dividing total roof area into equal pieces is not enough. A phase ends at an accepted roof detail, with its specified completion work and responsible party. If an independent phase cannot be defined for a proposed short window, compare a larger continuous release or another accepted supply/work arrangement instead.

This is different from counting repeated indoor access cycles. Roof phases must connect panel delivery with accepted roof-completion and building-protection boundaries. The [occupied-factory equipment guide](/blog/occupied-factory-reroofing-roll-forming-lift-selection/) screens a forming arrangement for the live site; this purchase chooses the campaign pattern and recurring resources.

## Give production four named release events

For each phase, identify the event releasing the work area, the event releasing the panel cut list, the first receiver readiness and the event releasing the forming package from that phase. Their dates come from the actual project teams, not a generic machine productivity allowance.

The elevated apparatus below shows production at the building edge with ground-side material. Include both the roof-side receiver and this material area in each phase reservation. A factory can release roof work while keeping a necessary ground lane occupied by its own operations.

Do not schedule a permanent production station where later phases require its standing area for another trade. Obtain an accepted retained-equipment arrangement or price complete removal and return. Include the former, feed items, remaining materials and transport resources, rather than treating movement of the lift as removal of the whole package.

## Price recurrence without promising machine rates

A phased campaign can preserve portions of the owner's operation, but it can also repeat transport, setup, material coordination and handback work. Count each actual event from the accepted programme. Include hired services and attendance for the periods when they are genuinely required.

A continuous shutdown can consolidate those events, but it needs the full roof, production and receiving teams ready for the concentrated work. Price the owner's disruption using the owner's own records. Do not infer that continuous forming automatically shortens the whole shutdown when removal or roof completion controls the sequence.

Where phase timing remains subject to a defined owner decision, identify the decision event and the priced alternative. For example, obtain an actual delivered-panel offer for a specified later zone if the site-production reservation cannot be retained. A programme fallback must have a product and supplier scope, not an assumed immediate capacity elsewhere.

## Keep unfinished roof work distinct from idle equipment

The project parties determine how interrupted roof work and materials are addressed. The equipment providers supply information for the actual production, idle and storage configurations in their scope. Those two responsibilities need coordination, but equipment availability cannot release the roof work by itself.

The [weather-scope guide](/blog/roof-level-roll-forming-weather-hold-points/) separates those equipment conditions. In the campaign purchase, obtain the applicable documents and include whatever project resources the accepted plan requires. This article supplies no weather threshold, temporary-roof design or restart procedure.

## Two hypothetical campaign failures

**Failure case: repeated short releases end before a complete roof boundary.** Procurement divides the roof by area and books a forming visit for each slice, but the specified junction and protection work cannot reach an accepted handback within those releases. Obtain finishable phase boundaries or change the campaign before ordering repeated production visits.

**Failure case: the first phase releases production but retains the machine.** Main panel forming ends while a later detail still needs the same package, making the next promised visit impossible. Identify the remaining panel supply and the full equipment-release condition; price a defined return or purchased-panel route for the exception.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the actual campaign

1. Roof replacement drawings with retained areas and final completion scope.
2. Accepted phase boundaries and owner handback requirements.
3. Controlled panel profiles, materials and phase-specific cut lists.
4. Work-area, production-list and receiver release events with responsible owners.
5. Equipment and coil positions, support datums and available access routes.
6. Complete mobilisation, retention, return and final removal scopes.
7. Associated roof components, specialist protection work and alternative panel supply.
8. Actual shutdown or phased programme, destination, utilities and quotation boundaries.
