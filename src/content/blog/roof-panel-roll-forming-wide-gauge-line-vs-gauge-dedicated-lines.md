Buy one gauge-range line when its accepted complete configuration produces every ordered thickness and its real changes leave enough capacity for the dated programme. Buy separately qualified gauge-dedicated lines when endpoint requirements or recurring change work prevents that supported single-line route from meeting the same orders, and the full parallel operation is funded. When both alternatives qualify, choose the lower complete cost over the same buyer-approved period. Begin with a dated order schedule by thickness before requesting either machinery package.

The panel programme and selected material family are already accepted. Keep their required grade and coating specifications in the common brief while separating the ordered thicknesses. This decision does not replace a thicker roof sheet with thinner high-strength steel or add aluminium to a steel business. The buyer must deliver the ordered thicknesses. The investment question is how to allocate their manufacturing capability, rather than which new material or profile to sell.

## Keep the product fixed while separating its gauges

Record the controlled section and the drawing's inspection references for each ordered thickness. Keep any thickness-dependent dimensions explicit. A nominally common panel name can conceal different reference surfaces, edge requirements or supplied conditions. Have the product owner release the actual programme that both machine offers must serve.

Then place its orders on the production calendar. List material identity, thickness basis, coating, lengths, quantity and due date. Separate an occasional thickness from sustained repeat work. A yearly tonnage hides the urgent small batch that repeatedly interrupts the main gauge and the period when both order groups must be delivered.

## Maximum thickness answers only one endpoint

[KOLEV Engineering's roll-forming FAQ](https://kolev.com/faq/) explains that equipment is sized for a defined thickness range and relates complete-line sizing to material properties and the profile being formed. That supports a range-specific machinery enquiry. Its illustrative ranges and design recommendations are not universal thresholds for another manufacturer's equipment.

[ASC's Alpha Series panel-line description](https://www.ascmt.com/alpha-series-panel-roll-former) identifies a gauge range with a particular configuration while separately describing the rollformer, tooling and cutting equipment. Carry the same complete-package discipline into your own purchase. A former that shapes a sample does not establish that its feed, cutoff and output arrangement deliver all required products.

Ask for a configuration schedule mapping the actual thicknesses to the supplied equipment and acceptance evidence. Qualify the thin endpoint as deliberately as the heavier endpoint. Include the combinations that matter commercially, using identified material and the released drawing criteria. A maximum rating cannot supply the missing thin-gauge result.

## A calendar that can choose the architecture

Use the same released orders, operating periods and accepted-output endpoint in both columns. Keep the existing decision about drive transmission or tooling-change architecture fixed unless the bidder explicitly offers a different complete package.

| Input to complete | Datum or record to attach | Responsible owner | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Controlled product | Section/revision, thickness-dependent datums and finish ___ | Product owner | Defines the same ordered output |
| Actual material range | Substrate/overall thickness basis, endpoints/intermediates, grade and coating ___ | Material buyer | Names the inputs requiring qualification |
| Dated demand | Lengths, quantities and deadlines by gauge ___ | Planner | Sets the common delivery target |
| Complete configuration | Feed/form/cut schedule and qualified combinations ___ | Machine provider | Exposes unsupported endpoints |
| Change and return | Stated start/finish, crew, material and accepted restart ___ | Production and quality leads | Measures recurring occupation |
| Output acceptance | Dimensions, face, seam/cut criteria and specimen IDs ___ | Quality and product owners | Counts accepted production |
| Parallel resources | Both footprints, people, coil service and receiving calendar ___ | Plant manager | Prices the full dedicated-line operation |
| Allocation total | Same period; equipment, changes, rejects and support costs ___ | Buyer and finance | Ranks qualifying complete packages |

Enter observed or provider-supported results against the actual programme. The purpose is a purchase calculation, not instructions for changing roll gaps, drive connections or cutting settings. Those tasks remain within the supplied machine's documented configuration and competent work.

## Test a return to the main gauge

A shared line's useful availability includes getting back to an accepted product. Choose a representative transition from the main gauge to another ordered thickness and back again. Record the agreed preparation, line occupation, trial material and first accepted output for the offered arrangement. Include work completed away from the stopped line as a separately resourced activity.

Keep the specimen and inspection references consistent. If the section drawing uses different thickness-dependent datums, have quality apply the correct released criteria rather than compare two unrelated measurements. Record material consumed and rejected output as well as the accepted batch. A quick change that resumes with unusable panels does not create delivery capacity.

![A wide ribbed metal sheet rests across shaped rolls in a blue machine beside its side enclosure.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261101-profiled-sheet.webp)
*Connect each inspected product to its material identity, configuration and released dimensional references.*

This trial differs from [choosing cassette tooling or conventional roll changes](/blog/roof-panel-roll-former-cassette-tooling-vs-conventional-roll-changes/). That comparison selects the exchange hardware for a shared multi-product machine. Here the buyer uses its accepted change results to decide whether one range-qualified production route can serve the whole thickness programme.

## Two lines need two deliverable routes

For dedicated lines, list the actual gauge duties assigned to each. Obtain each complete feed, forming, cutting and receiving proposal, then draw the installed footprints and service space. Price operators, quality release, material handling and maintenance for the proposed production periods.

Identify shared constraints explicitly. Two machines can still compete for one crane, one coil-service team or the same completed-panel collection aisle. Put those requests on the calendar. If overlapping resources are unavailable, calculate output with that restriction or fund the missing capability before crediting parallel deliveries.

For the single-line route, include its actual changes, range-specific equipment and acceptance resources. Compare both totals over the same order period. Choose the single route when it qualifies and serves the programme at lower complete cost. Choose dedicated routes when their accepted capability and funded resources deliver work the shared route cannot, or their complete eligible total is lower.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical gauge-allocation errors

**Mistake (hypothetical): a heavy-end trial becomes full-range acceptance.** A buyer approves the thickest ordered coil and assumes the thin product follows from the maximum rating. The thin-gauge order has no accepted output record. Qualify both endpoints and the important intermediate combinations before allocating the full programme to that machine.

**Mistake (hypothetical): parallel formers share an unavailable material route.** A heavy-gauge repeat run and an urgent thin-gauge batch are booked on separate lines in the same period, but both need the single coil-handling resource already committed to the heavy run. Rebuild that common-resource calendar and price the additions needed for the promised deliveries before purchasing the second route.

## Eight attachments for the line-allocation RFQ

1. **Panel programme:** released sections, thickness-dependent datums, seam and visible-face criteria.
2. **Coil identities:** thickness basis, actual gauge range, grade, coating and representative lots.
3. **Dated orders:** lengths, quantities, deadlines and fixed operating calendar by gauge.
4. **Configuration response:** complete feed/form/cut coverage for shared and dedicated alternatives.
5. **Change trial:** important transitions, return events, retained crew and accepted-output boundary.
6. **Factory layout:** footprints, coil service, inspection, outfeed and stack-removal resources.
7. **Site interfaces:** destination power, measured line space, floor/support records and any separately required lift/outlet duty.
8. **Complete quote:** installation, tooling/configuration work, support, comparison period and exclusions.
