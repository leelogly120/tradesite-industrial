Relocate the existing roof-panel line when it passes the product and condition assessment, fits the receiving premises, and the priced move, reinstatement and order cover cost less than a new cell serving the same orders. Buy new when the retained equipment fails those product/site conditions or moving the only line cannot provide the required production overlap. Keep the old factory operating until the agreed transition point when that is the selected order-cover route.

A premises move is different from sharing a portable former between projects. The purchase covers an existing fixed production operation, its surrounding equipment, a new site and the interruption between accepted output at the two locations.

## Check what deserves to move

Start with the product plan for the next workshop. List the controlled sections, material families, order lengths and acceptance requirements. Compare those with actual accepted output from the retained line. A machine that served the previous catalogue is not automatically the correct centrepiece for an expanded product range.

Commission a competent condition assessment of the equipment proposed for retention. Separate the former, tooling, input equipment, receiving equipment, controls and documentation. Obtain a defined renewal proposal for defects or unsupported components. Do not count a repair allowance as a diagnosis or assume relocation itself restores machine condition.

The [controls-retrofit versus replacement guide](/blog/roof-panel-roll-former-controls-retrofit-vs-new-line/) addresses one possible renewal issue. Use its result inside the move comparison. The relocation decision also includes premises interfaces, installation, acceptance and continuity of orders.

## Survey the receiving workshop against the complete operation

Transfer the actual cell layout into a measured receiving-site plan. Show material receipt, coil storage, input handling, forming, cutting, panel runout, inspection, packing and loading. Mark door openings, columns, floor references, available utilities and the routes shared with other work.

The receiving premises must serve the intended product lengths and handling arrangements. A building that accommodates the former's footprint can still fail the finished-panel route. Obtain the engineer's review of the actual equipment support and building interfaces rather than carrying over an approval for the previous premises.

If the move changes an equipment interface, identify the changed design and acceptance scope. A longer runout, different input arrangement or revised utility supply is project work with its own responsible provider. Keep it visible in the comparison with a new line proposed for the same receiving site.

Use the same order book and comparison period for both routes. Do not compare the transport invoice for relocation with the full installed cost of a new cell. Equally, a new line needs its own installation, trial, staffing and handover allowance.

## Work backwards from the first accepted delivery

Set the date on which the receiving workshop must provide accepted panels for a defined customer order. The project team then places its actual assessment, design, transport, installation, trial and handover milestones into the transition schedule. Use provider commitments and named owners rather than a generic assumed move duration.

Price the order-cover route before releasing the old line. Stock must match the product and actual lengths; outside production must have an identified supplier and accepted product; continued old-site operation must have premises and resources available for the required period. The finance and legal advisers assess the commercial arrangements where a lease or transaction is involved.

The two lines illustrate the equipment side of an overlap. Staffing, material supply and accepted output still need their own plan. Choose replacement for continuity only when the complete new operation can actually serve the transition orders.

## Two hypothetical relocation failures

**Failure case: the quote ends at unloading.** The buyer compares a low move price with a new installed line, then discovers that reinstatement and acceptance were excluded. Reissue both offers against accepted production at the receiving workshop, with every intervening scope assigned.

**Failure case: the only line leaves before order cover exists.** The old factory stops while the new site still needs work. A vague promise to buy panels elsewhere cannot satisfy the controlled customer sections. Price and release the actual cover route before the transition point.

## Send the receiving-site and retained-line pack

Attach these eight inputs to the RFQ:

1. Future panel drawings, materials and representative customer orders.
2. Existing equipment identities, drawings and condition findings.
3. The measured origin and receiving-site layouts.
4. Defined retained, renewed and replaced equipment scope.
5. Transport and receiving handover requirements.
6. Site utilities, floor and handling information from responsible specialists.
7. Transition programme and actual order-cover arrangement.
8. Restart acceptance method, destination and supplied-equipment boundary.
