Keep central dispatch when jobs are scattered or regional demand cannot support a complete local readiness service. Establish a regional depot when repeat contracted work justifies its premises, people, stock and maintenance scope after counting the full movements it removes. For a temporary cluster of jobs, compare a defined project staging base before committing to a permanent second depot.

This decision fixes where the fleet is kept ready for work. It is separate from whether the company owns a transporter or hires haulage. Either transport model can serve either depot strategy. A shorter map distance is valuable only when the right lift, compatible former and supporting resources are ready at the regional end.

## Define what leaves the depot ready to work

Make an inventory for each production package: lift, forming line, mounting and connection items, tools, documents and task-specific auxiliaries. Mark which items are assigned permanently and which are shared. Include the people needed to release the package after inspection, organise its movement and support its next job.

A parking yard stores an asset. A service-ready depot prepares and releases an operating package. If the regional proposal still depends on a technician, critical tooling or the former travelling from the central base for every job, include those journeys in its cost and calendar. It is not yet an independent dispatch point.

The transport photo below shows a long forming line carried on a road vehicle. It makes a common omission visible: the former can be a separate transported asset from the lift. The depot plan must place both where the next contract needs them.

## Compare a complete set of awarded jobs

Plot actual project locations and work windows over a defined period. For each strategy, record loaded dispatch, collection, repositioning, empty carrier legs where charged or internally incurred, and visits for people or parts. Treat a delayed site release as an availability event; the closest depot cannot dispatch equipment that remains committed elsewhere.

Keep a second view for prospective work, but do not merge it into the committed utilisation total. The regional route must show which contracted jobs pay for the additional base. If the advantage disappears when unawarded work is removed, use central dispatch or a time-limited staging arrangement with an explicit exit plan.

## A temporary staging base is a separate option

When several awarded projects cluster in one region for a limited campaign, price temporary secure storage and specifically contracted support. State the opening date, equipment list, allowed activities and closure date. Include preparation for return to the central fleet. Avoid turning a project laydown area into an assumed workshop or long-term equipment base without an agreed scope.

Choose a permanent regional depot for continuing work when the base can release complete packages without routine dependency journeys that erase the transport saving. Retain central functions where they genuinely remain shared, and allocate their cost visibly. A regional depot does not need to duplicate every central resource, but the remaining dependencies must fit the actual dispatch promise.

## Two regional-fleet proposals that fail

**The lift moves regionally, but the tooling and crew stay central.** In an illustrative plan, local storage removes a long equipment journey from the estimate. Each project still requires central staff and a separately carried former to travel. Recalculate the complete package movements and readiness cost before approving the regional base.

**A busy first project is treated as permanent utilisation.** A second proposal budgets a depot from one long awarded roof and several sales prospects. When the roof ends, the base has no contracted next assignment. Compare a time-limited staging arrangement with central return, and fund permanent capacity only against the continuing workload and support plan the business accepts.
