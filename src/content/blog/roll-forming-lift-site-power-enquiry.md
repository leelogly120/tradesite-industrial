## Write the site source before the equipment line

### Name the person who owns the source record

Ask who can issue the current record for the place where the lift will stand. A buyer who forwards a catalog page is not that person. Write the role, the building, and the date on the record. If nobody will sign the source, the cell stays unknown. ARC-T18 is an 18.5m truck screen. ARC-C25 is a 25m crawler screen. ARC-RF8 is a former with a catalog sheet class of 0.3–1.0mm. None of those names is a supply.

### Split recorded fact from a hoped-for connection

Use three marks: record in hand, buyer expectation, unanswered. An existing panel on a drawing is a record. A future roof zone with no service yet is an expectation. A blank frequency cell is unanswered. Do not fill the blank from another building. Confirm on RFQ is the honest mark until the source owner writes the words.

### Keep the machines identifiable

List the lift, the former, and any feed equipment as separate names next to the source brief. Do not merge their requirements into the source-owner column. If the former is buyer-supplied, say so and ask its provider for its own requirement sheet. The source owner should see which combination is being discussed. The equipment provider should see which paper is a site record.

### Mark the place, not a cable route

Show the intended outlet and the source location on one sketch. That is a location note, not a route design and not a protection study. A source at the yard does not answer a proposal at the far end of the roof. Write which end the offer is supposed to address.

## Compare two source briefs, not two labels

### Put the yard and the expansion on one table

| What you can point at | Yard position | Expansion position | If you do not know yet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who issues the source record | Named site role | Project services role, or unknown | Confirm on RFQ |
| Where it sits | Shown on the existing layout | Shown only on a proposal | Measure on site |
| Words on the record | Copy them; do not translate | Future provision, not confirmed | Leave blank |
| Which offer uses it | Ask each bidder | Ask each bidder | Do not assume |

### Read the replies for a changed assumption

One bidder may price against the yard record. Another may assume the expansion source will exist before arrival. Their offers then rest on different sites. The corrected enquiry names the yard record, marks the expansion as proposed, and asks which source each equipment reply addresses. No calculation is required to see that the commercial comparison is not ready.

### Keep availability in its own row

Ask whether the record describes the site today, a planned installation, or a temporary provision. Write the phase and who can confirm it. Do not turn a construction milestone into confirmed power. If readiness depends on another contract, say so.

### Leave protection and cable size out

Report known restrictions: an occupied aisle, a floor limit, an exposed yard. Do not prescribe devices or cable sizes to make the form look finished. The electrical reviewer needs the conditions. The buyer needs the two columns.

<!-- audit-section: evidence-tradeoffs -->
The trade-off is a short file against a wrong site. The limitation is that a height class contains no supply. The constraint is the record the source owner will actually issue. The interface is the quotation line that says which source the equipment reply used. Without that line, stop comparing machines and compare assumptions.

An anonymous warehouse had a documented source near the service yard and a second mark beside a roof expansion. The buyer sent both bidders the sentence "site power available." One priced the yard. The other priced the expansion that did not yet exist. The note that replaced the sentence named the yard record, marked the expansion as proposed, and asked each bidder which source its sheet addressed. The 25m screen stayed a height question. It did not become a supply.

A second anonymous file pasted a voltage from a lift brochure into the site column. The source owner had not written that number, and the former was still unnamed. The paste made both columns look closed. They were not. The useful file put the brochure words back on the equipment side, left the site cell as unknown, and screened ARC-T18 or ARC-C25 only as height classes. Confirm on RFQ who owns the source record before any bidder treats the paste as a fact.

A third anonymous job shows why the folders have to travel together. The buyer screened an 18.5m truck class and a 25m crawler class for one outlet. The site record lived in a folder that named the yard board and its date. The equipment sheets lived in another folder that named neither the yard nor the expansion. Mailed as one zip, the seller answered the equipment folder and ignored the site folder. The reply repeated the height class and a single power adjective. It did not say which board it had used. The buyer then asked the site owner to confirm the reply. The owner could not confirm a text that never cited the record. The file that worked put the record's date and the board's name in the first lines, repeated the height screens underneath, and asked one question: which source does this reply use.

Count the open cells before you ask for a revised offer. A useful revision names the source, names the machines, and leaves unknown where the paper is silent. A revision that only changes 18.5m to 25m has not touched the supply. If the expansion board is still a proposal, say proposed in the same sentence as the height screen so a later reader cannot promote it by accident. Measure on site if the sketch and the yard disagree about which end of the building the board serves. The catalog class will not settle that disagreement.

## A mixed power story may not fit

### A brochure number in the site column

This enquiry may not fit when the only "site" figure was copied from equipment paper. That figure is not a record of this building. Move it back, or delete it. A taller class does not repair the column.

### Two bidders, two buildings

It may not fit when the offers cannot be lined up because each one assumed a different source. Ask the question before you rank the prices you do not have on this page. Ranking comes after the source is the same source.

### A future board treated as present

It may not fit when the drawing shows a board that another contract has not installed. Write proposed. Do not write available. The lift screen can still go out. The supply claim cannot.

### One sentence for lift and former

It may not fit when a single power line is asked to cover the lift, the former, and the feed. Split the names. If a name has no requirement sheet, that name stays unknown.

## Source lines to attach

- Work height at the outlet, and the catalog class to screen
- Roof geometry and which end of the building is in the brief
- Profile, material, and the coil the former must run
- Wind limit the panel crew already uses, or unknown
- Floor or ground under the lift and under the source
- Access route from the gate to the set-down
- Outrigger or track footprint if the class needs it
- Destination country and who reviews the supply there
- Transport to the site, separate from the power rows
- Power words copied from the site record, or unknown
- Documents attached, and the date on the source record

## FAQ

#### Should an unknown source value be guessed?
No. Mark it unknown and ask the source owner. A guess makes the reply look more definite than the paper.

#### Can one brief cover two sites?
Only if each source owner, location, and record stays separate. A shared machine does not make two supplies the same.

#### Does a full brief prove the supply is enough?
No. It gathers inputs. It does not size protection or approve a connection.

#### What changes when the outlet moves?
Update the location, the source assumption, and which earlier reply still applies. Do not carry a yard answer onto a different end of the roof.
