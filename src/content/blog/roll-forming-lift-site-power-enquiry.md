A roll-forming lift power enquiry starts with two different sets of information: what the site can document about its source, and what the actual equipment providers require. Keep those sets separate until the responsible electrical reviewer assesses the proposed combination. Copying a value from a lift brochure into a site form does not answer either question reliably.

This guide produces a site-source brief that a buyer can send with a [crawler roll-forming lift enquiry](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts). The system raises a compatible roof-panel former toward the receiving level, so the source brief must describe the actual place where that equipment is proposed. The broader [electrical interface guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-electrical-control-interfaces/) covers interactions between machines; here the useful output is the information arriving from the site owner.

The public <a href="https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/71256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IEC 60204-1 scope record</a> places the machine-side boundary at the supply connection. Use that distinction when requesting site-source records and equipment requirements; the abstract does not assess this site's supply or establish equipment conformity.

## Establish what the site actually knows

### Identify the source owner

Name the person or organisation responsible for providing the site's electrical information. A purchasing contact may coordinate the response without owning that information. Ask the source owner to identify the records they can supply and the location those records describe. This avoids the common circular enquiry in which the equipment seller asks about the site, the buyer returns a catalogue page, and both parties believe the other has confirmed compatibility.

The owner field also gives later questions a destination. It should identify a responsible contact or role, rather than a general statement that power will be available.

### Separate recorded facts from project expectations

Use three columns in the brief: supplied record, buyer expectation, and unanswered question. A site drawing describing an existing connection belongs in the first column. A proposed connection near a future roof zone belongs in the second until its owner confirms it. An unknown supply characteristic belongs in the third. Do not fill empty cells using assumptions from another building or an earlier project.

This distinction makes an incomplete brief useful. A seller can identify the questions that prevent an equipment-side response instead of treating every entry as a settled site fact.

### Keep the equipment request identifiable

Attach the quoted lift and former identities alongside the site brief, but do not merge their requirements into the source-owner fields. If the forming machine is buyer supplied, say so and request its current equipment information from its own provider. Where feed equipment or another powered component is proposed, list its identity separately.

The source owner should be able to see which equipment combination is under consideration. The equipment provider should be able to distinguish an actual supply record from a desired arrangement that the buyer has not yet discussed with the site.

## Locate the supply in the building plan

### Describe the proposed work zones

Mark the intended forming location and roof receiving zone on a layout. Use a consistent reference for the former's outlet height rather than labelling every vertical dimension simply height. Include the available source location as a separate object. This is a location brief, not a cable route drawing or an installation design.

A source described for one end of a building may not answer a proposal at the other end. The distinction remains relevant even when the buyer expects to use the same machine package throughout the project.

### Record availability by project phase

Ask whether the source information describes the present site, a planned installation, or a temporary project provision. Record the relevant phase and who can confirm its readiness. Equipment discussions may begin before the building's final services are available, making a final-building drawing an incomplete description of the purchase conditions.

Do not translate an expected construction milestone into confirmed power availability. The brief should show what remains dependent on another contract so that the equipment and site reviewers can discuss the actual planned arrangement.

### Describe restrictions without designing around them

Report known access restrictions, occupied areas, floor constraints and exposed locations that affect where equipment or site services may be considered. Attribute restrictions to the site owner or project records. The electrical reviewer needs the conditions; the buyer should not prescribe protective devices, cable sizes or connection methods in order to make the form look complete.

For example, identifying a proposed source behind an area unavailable during part of the project reveals a question. It does not establish that an alternative route, extension or relocation will be acceptable.

## Compare two source briefs rather than two voltage labels

### Work through a warehouse example

Consider a hypothetical warehouse with a documented source near the service yard and a second proposed source beside a roof expansion. The buyer initially sends both bidders the sentence “site power available.” Bidder A prices its equipment connection requirements against the yard information. Bidder B assumes that the expansion source will exist before arrival. Their prices therefore rest on different site assumptions.

The corrected enquiry names the yard record, marks the expansion source as proposed, and asks each bidder which source its equipment-side response addresses. No electrical calculation is needed to reveal the commercial difference.

### Use the source-information worksheet

| Brief entry | Yard position | Expansion position | Question that changes the enquiry |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Source record owner | Named site representative | Project services coordinator | Who can issue current source information? |
| Physical location | Shown on existing layout | Shown on proposed layout | Which position does the equipment offer address? |
| Record status | Existing record supplied | Future provision not confirmed | What remains an assumption? |
| Project availability | Owner response requested | Dependent on another package | Which date or phase needs reconfirmation? |
| Equipment scope | Lift and named former | Same proposed equipment set | Has either offer changed the equipment assumption? |
| Review response | Site and equipment replies pending | Missing-source question pending | What can be assessed now? |

These are illustrative statuses, not a statement about a real warehouse. The worksheet deliberately contains no supply rating or recommended electrical arrangement. Its value is making unlike inputs visible before a quotation comparison.

### Read the replies for a changed assumption

Suppose A returns a configuration-specific requirements sheet and asks for the site's current record. B replies only that its standard arrangement is suitable. A has not proved suitability, but it has identified a clear interface for review. B's reply needs clarification about which source and equipment identities it covers. A short assurance cannot resolve the missing expansion-source information.

The trade-off is between progressing a bounded enquiry with known omissions and comparing apparently complete offers that assume different sites. Neither reply authorises connection. Keep the responsible electrical review distinct from commercial completeness.

## Complete the site-source handover

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise a brief that cannot support the decision

This approach may not fit a final equipment decision when the source owner cannot identify the intended supply, the equipment combination remains unknown, or the proposal depends on an undocumented future provision. Record the specific missing item rather than calling the whole project ready or unsuitable. A more limited enquiry may still be possible, provided its assumptions are explicit.

A photograph of raised forming equipment cannot establish its incoming power arrangement. Nor can an old machine label answer a new site's design question. The final assessment belongs to the parties responsible for the actual equipment and installation.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Send a brief with useful missing fields

- Identify the site source owner and attach the current records they actually provide, keeping unsupported entries marked unknown.
- Mark the former outlet height and roof receiving zone so the supply location is connected to the intended work area.
- Name the forming machine, feed equipment and any buyer-supplied powered items rather than treating the lift as the whole line.
- Describe ground or floor restrictions and access limitations that affect the proposed equipment and source positions.
- State destination, transport arrival phase and whether source availability depends on separate building works.
- Ask for the offered equipment power requirements and the local review needed to reconcile them with the source records.

Keep the response next to the source brief it answers. If the site changes the proposed location, identify that change directly instead of silently replacing an attachment while retaining the earlier supplier assurance. This keeps the next enquiry focused on the changed input.

## FAQ

#### Should an unknown source value be estimated?

Leave it identified as unknown and ask the source owner for the relevant record. An estimate presented as fact can make the supplier's response appear more definite than its inputs justify. The enquiry can still explain which equipment and location the missing information concerns.

#### Can one source brief cover several sites?

Only identify common information as common when it actually is. Keep each site's source owner, location and record separately attributable. A shared equipment package does not make different site supplies interchangeable, and this worksheet does not decide their electrical suitability.

#### Does a complete brief establish sufficient supply capacity?

No. It gathers inputs for the actual equipment and installation review. It contains no demand calculation, protection selection or connection design. A complete commercial information package and an accepted electrical arrangement are different outcomes with different responsible parties.

#### What should change when the proposed forming location moves?

Update the location relationship, source assumption and affected restrictions. Ask the equipment and site reviewers which earlier responses remain applicable. Do not automatically discard every record, but do not carry a location-specific conclusion forward without identifying the changed basis.
