Buy automated coil storage and retrieval when the qualified coil population, peak retrieval demand and available footprint justify an integrated store with a workable recovery route. Keep conventional storage when accessible planned locations, an accepted handling method and disciplined part-coil identification can serve the actual roof-panel orders on time.

## Test one ordinary shift and one busy changeover period

Use time-stamped coil requests rather than total annual steel usage. A store serving long repeat runs performs a different duty from one serving many colours, slit widths and short project orders. Record the next required coil, the time it must reach the entry station and whether a partially consumed coil returns to storage. Include receipt of incoming material, inspection holds and stock corrections. A system that presents new coils rapidly can still become the constraint if it cannot accept returns while lines are waiting.

## Build an inventory the equipment can actually address

Separate full incoming coils, qualified returns, quarantined material and unusual stock. Record a part coil's remaining dimensions, restraint, packaging and identification on return, then assess its storage eligibility against those recorded conditions. Ask the storage provider and material specialist to define acceptance for these returned units. Show rejected and exceptional stock on a separate area of the layout. Crediting an automatic store with the entire inventory while budgeting a manual exception area later exaggerates both space recovery and labour saving.

Give both bidders the same order replay. Start with the recorded inventory, including one returned part coil and one held lot. Let each bidder explain the sequence of requests, when identification is checked, where conflicting transfers wait and how the correct coil reaches the correct forming line. Ask for the limiting condition when two changes coincide with an inbound delivery. Place the cost of any standby buffer and the space it occupies in the proposal that relies on it.

## Recognize what automation leaves outside the store

List work that remains at the dock and at line entry. Packaging removal, inspection, preparation, production release and return preparation can fall outside the automated store. Write down who performs each task, how much space it needs and what happens when the next station is occupied. Compare total installed cost, supporting building works, controls integration, maintenance, spare parts and support response with the conventional layout's equipment and labour. Count released space as a financial benefit only where the business has a specific use for it.

**Mistake: One stock identity is used for several physical units.** A planner records all coils of one colour under the same item. A returned coil has a different remaining quantity and needs a different handling condition. The automatic schedule calls a coil that cannot complete the released order. Assign each physical unit its own traceable identity, quantity and eligibility before costing repeat retrieval.

**Mistake: A second line is credited with instant supply.** A proposed store has a fast internal retrieval mechanism, but both lines share one constrained outgoing station. The investment calculation treats their changeovers as independent. Replay the two requests together, test a buffer or second handover as a priced option and retain the conventional alternative if it already serves the commitments.

Approve the automated route where the replay meets the orders, the project can fund the eligible layout and downtime recovery is acceptable. Otherwise improve conventional location control, release discipline and accessible part-coil storage first. Recheck the case when additional lines or product families create measured concurrent requests. More tonnes purchased can mean larger coils and fewer requests, so purchased weight alone is a poor trigger for expansion.

## Eight RFQ inputs for a coil-store proposal

1. Send physical coil inventories, dimensions and condition groups.
2. Provide a dated request history for receipts, issues and returns.
3. List the identities and dispositions of returned part coils.
4. Map accepted handling routes, transfer stations and line entries.
5. Give order sequencing, release rules and required inventory records.
6. State floor, building and control-system responsibilities.
7. Request an exceptional-coil and interrupted-service proposal.
8. Price both installed layouts against the same order replay.
