**Buy suitable cargo cover when the business cannot accept the remaining shipment loss after the actual carrier-liability terms and any existing policy are assessed. Rely on existing arrangements only when the appointed insurance adviser and commercial owner establish the applicable protection and explicitly accept the retained exposure.** A freight provider having insurance does not automatically insure the buyer's machine for its complete purchase value.

Make this decision before the equipment enters the buyer's transport-risk period. Identify the supplied machine, package values, route, handling stages and the sale's delivery boundary. Obtain the relevant transport terms and current insurance documents. Ask the adviser to reconcile those records rather than treating “insured shipment” in an email as a complete answer.

## Liability and cargo protection answer different questions

[Chubb's marine-cargo information](https://www.chubb.com/uk-en/chubb-global-markets/our-products/marine/marine-cargo-insurance.html) addresses physical loss or damage to goods in transit. The carrier's contractual liability concerns its own responsibility under the applicable carriage agreement and law. Compare those actual documents with professional advice; the freight rate and a carrier's general liability certificate cannot establish the buyer's recovery for a particular event.

Avoid universal recovery figures. Limits, valuation, exclusions, deductibles, obligations and claim conditions follow the actual transport and policy documents. Ask the adviser to identify the uninsured amount and scenarios for the declared shipment, including any existing policy. The business then decides what risk to retain and what additional cover to buy.

## Reconcile the sale boundary with the whole route

Write down who bears the relevant transport risk at each agreed delivery point. Obtain the buyer's commercial advisers' interpretation of the sale terms and the broker's response for those periods. Include inland collection, terminals, ocean transport, authorised warehouse storage and destination delivery where they fall within the required protection.

Separate transport protection from operational equipment insurance. A policy covering shipment does not automatically cover later assembly, testing or everyday use. The receiving date and start of installation are therefore important events to disclose. If storage or commissioning needs separate cover or an endorsement, obtain that actual offer before relying on it.

## Declare the equipment as it will actually travel

A forming system can include several high-value assemblies. List the former, lift modules, controls, tooling and any supplied vehicle/carrier separately in the declared shipment schedule. If equipment travels in separate lots, preserve the value and identity of each lot. Ask how the proposed policy treats those shipments and any concentration at a warehouse.

Inform the adviser when the packing breakdown changes. A more assembled machine on a special-cargo route and disassembled modules in containers create different handling facts. Obtain the written response to the actual configuration before dispatch rather than assuming the earlier quotation remains applicable.

Use the [shipment-receipt guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-destination-receipt-plan/) to organise package identity and condition records. Assign the person receiving the cargo and the contact responsible for reporting an actual loss. Follow the actual transport and policy notice requirements. Preserve packaging and evidence according to the responsible professionals' instructions when a claim arises.

The folded assembly and separate frame sections explain why a declared total should be reconciled with a package schedule. A small package can hold an essential control or tooling item. The buyer needs both its financial value and the operating consequence of losing it when assessing retained business exposure.

## Two hypothetical cargo-protection mistakes

**The forwarder's policy is treated as the buyer's equipment cover.** A buyer sees a liability certificate and assumes every transit loss will be reimbursed at the machine invoice amount. The actual carriage and policy basis is different. Have the adviser assess the shipment and obtain the required buyer-interest cover before the risk period begins.

**The cargo cover ends before an unplanned storage interval.** The site postpones installation and the machine remains at another facility under conditions not disclosed in the original proposal. Notify the insurance owner and obtain the provider's written response to the changed route and period. Fund the accepted protection and preservation together.
