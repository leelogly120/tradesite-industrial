A former illustrated on a lifting structure can look correct while **its material inlet and cutter face the opposite way from the intended roof route**. A roof-forming lift positions a compatible roof-panel roll former; it does not make every machine orientation interchangeable. Before comparing offers, the buyer needs one agreed drawing convention that names the inlet, cutter, roof receiver and viewing direction.

This page develops that common orientation drawing and tests two apparently similar layouts. The [mounting-interface guide](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) deals with the physical machine connection. Here the useful outcome is exposing a reversed assumption before it becomes part of the purchase scope, rather than designing an adaptor or prescribing a feeding method.

## Establish a common orientation language

### Name the ends by function

Use inlet or feed end for the side receiving material into the former, and cutter or outlet end for the identified discharge reference. Ask the equipment party to confirm the terms it uses on the particular arrangement. Left and right depend on where the viewer stands, while front can mean the vehicle front, machine front or building facade. Functional names give the parties a stable reference even when drawings have been rotated on a page or translated between teams using different conventions.

### State the direction from which the drawing is read

Put a recognizable building grid, north reference or agreed site direction on the plan. Label any elevation by its viewing direction. If an offer shows an underside or opposite-side view, request the corresponding relationship to the common plan instead of mentally mirroring it. This is particularly useful where a cabinet or accessory appears at one end and the image alone does not identify the material flow. A photograph may show visible components, but it does not establish the intended direction of a new supply proposal.

### Identify the actual former behind the outline

For an existing line, use its attributable arrangement and current configuration. For a new former, ask which orientation information is established and which is provisional. A generic former rectangle can serve an early discussion only if its inlet and outlet assumptions are named. The [forming-line specification guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-specification-long-span-roof-panels/) owns profile and material requirements. Do not let a matching profile description imply that the proposed line has the same physical orientation as the machine used in another supplier's sketch.

## Place both material ends on the site plan

### Connect the outlet to the named roof receiver

Mark the first roof receiving location and the intended panel direction using the same references as the equipment drawing. This makes it possible to see whether the outlet faces the task it is expected to serve. A machine can be placed near the correct roof elevation yet point toward a different bay. Do not close that mismatch by assuming the panel route can bend or change sides. Ask for the arrangement that the equipment and receiving parties actually propose for the identified relationship.

### Keep the feed side in the same comparison

Locate the proposed material-supply interface as far as it is defined, and show what remains unknown. Turning the equipment illustration to align its outlet also changes where the inlet appears on the site plan. That can place the material-side question beside another lane, a building face or occupied space. The [coil-feeding guide](/blog/coil-handling-roll-forming-line-feeding-plan/) addresses the separate supply arrangement. Here the key is to avoid solving the roof-facing arrow while silently moving the other end into a different physical task.

### Preserve side access and equipment features

If the actual former's cabinets, service openings or other identified features matter to the proposed arrangement, retain their side references. Mirroring a picture is not confirmation that those features can move with the drawing convention. The equipment party must identify the configuration being offered. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/are-you.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE guidance for equipment purchasers and installers</a> discusses the importance of the manufacturer's installation information. That is general jurisdiction-specific context, not permission to reverse, remount or alter the actual former.

## Compare a mirrored offer with the required task

### Keep one site view for both proposals

In a **hypothetical comparison**, the project requires output toward the eastern roof receiver, with a proposed material interface on the opposite side. Offer A identifies inlet and cutter on a common site plan. Offer B shows a similar-looking line, but its illustration is viewed from the opposite side and uses only front and rear labels. Both name the same panel task. The immediate difference is not performance or price; it is whether the reader can establish that the same physical orientation has been proposed.

### Test whether the difference is notation or equipment

Ask B's supplier to add the functional ends and connect its view to the site plan. If the apparent reversal disappears, the problem was a viewing convention. If the labelled cutter now points away from the required receiver, a real arrangement question remains. The buyer should not decide that reversing the feed, moving the machine or changing the receiver is an available remedy. This is an interface difference rather than a drawing preference. Those responses would each alter the task or configuration and need an actual proposal. Identifying the difference early keeps a drawing clarification separate from a physical redesign.

### Follow an orientation correction through the scope

Suppose the supplier proposes a different actual arrangement in response. Ask which machine, support, services and material interfaces the revised offer now includes. A corrected arrow alone is not a changed equipment scope. Compare the drawing and commercial description together so the same arrangement is being priced. The table below is an interpretation aid for this hypothetical review. It does not suggest that any particular line is reversible or that the lifting structure accepts arbitrary orientations.

| Comparison observation | What it might mean | Useful clarification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opposite left/right labels | The parties may be using different viewing directions | Add the common site reference and functional inlet/cutter labels |
| Cutter faces away from the named receiver | A real proposed-layout difference may remain | Ask the supplier which actual configuration addresses the required output direction |
| Outlet arrow is corrected but accessories stay unexplained | The picture may not identify a complete revised arrangement | Reconcile the actual line, cabinet and interface drawing with the revised offer |
| Material interface moves to another site boundary | The inlet-side task has changed with the layout | Obtain a corresponding material-side response rather than retaining the earlier assumption |
| Offer relies on an owned former | The actual machine's configuration controls the question | Use its attributable current arrangement instead of a generic mirrored symbol |

## Carry the orientation into the purchase discussion

### Ask bidders to answer on the same convention

Attach the common plan to the enquiry and ask each bidder to identify the functional ends on its own actual arrangement. State whether the former is already owned or forms part of the proposed supply. Refer to the [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) or [truck-mounted family](/products/#truck-mounted-roll-forming-lifts) as relevant, while keeping the line orientation as an explicit interface question. A consistent convention lets the buyer compare physical proposals without treating the direction in which a brochure picture faces as technical information.

- Label the inlet, cutter or outlet and the intended material direction and panel requirement on the actual line reference.
- Show the roof receiver, requested outlet height and proposed ground position on the same plan.
- Identify each elevation's viewing direction so opposite-side views can be reconciled.
- Preserve the actual machine's cabinet, accessory and service-side references where relevant.
- Keep the material-supply boundary and its access route visible whenever the outlet arrangement changes.
- Ask for a revised drawing and scope together where the response proposes a different physical configuration.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize what a drawing convention cannot settle

A proposed orientation may not fit the intended receiver even when the machine outline appears to fit. Common labels cannot establish mechanical compatibility, support loads, available motion or suitability for the panel task. An unresolved reversal can be a reason to pause the equipment comparison, rather than select a line whose image merely looks convenient. This page does not authorize reverse feeding, machine rotation, relocated cabinets or changed mounting. The responsible equipment and integration parties need to evaluate any actual revised arrangement, including the consequences beyond the outlet direction that first prompted the question.

## FAQ

#### Are front and rear labels enough?

They can be used where their reference is defined, but they are ambiguous across a vehicle, forming machine and building. Add inlet and cutter labels and identify the viewing direction. This allows the same physical relationship to be recognized by the different parties without relying on assumptions about which end of a brochure image is the front.

#### Can the drawing simply be mirrored?

Mirroring can change how a view is presented; it cannot establish that the equipment itself has an interchangeable arrangement. Ask whether the discrepancy concerns the view or the actual machine configuration. Any proposed physical change needs its own equipment response, including mounting, services and material interfaces. The buyer should not treat a graphic correction as an approved modification.

#### Does the outlet direction settle coil orientation?

No. This article identifies the functional ends and their relationship to the site. Coil winding, material face and the actual feeding arrangement involve additional machine and material information. Keep those inputs with their responsible suppliers and use the separate feeding discussion. A common direction arrow is useful, but it does not establish every property of the material entering the former.

#### What if two offers use different formers?

Compare each actual line against the same site receiver and panel requirement, with its own inlet and cutter references. Do not transfer one machine's physical layout to another because the intended panel family is similar. The buyer's result should identify which arrangement each offer describes and which orientation or interface question still prevents a fair scope comparison.
