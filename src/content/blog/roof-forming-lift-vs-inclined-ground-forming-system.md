Choose an engineered ground-to-eaves production package when its supplier accepts your panel, the complete inclined sheet path fits the site, and the roof crew can receive the output at the agreed eave. Choose a roof-forming lift when a reviewed raised-former arrangement reaches the receiving position but that ground-to-eaves path cannot be accommodated. Compare the two complete layouts before buying either machine.

The difference is what crosses the height between the yard and the roof: the developing sheet along a designed feed route, or the forming equipment on a dedicated lifting structure. This is a production-architecture decision. Tilting an ordinary floor-mounted roll former or improvising a ramp is not a third option.

## Two ways to connect coil supply with the roof

Ground-to-eaves forming is an established specialist category. Kalzip's manufacturer-authored [systems brochure, page 66](https://www.specifiedby.com/kalzip/kalzip-acoustic-liner-roof-system-u-value-0-18/Kalzip-Systems.pdf) distinguishes ground, ground-to-eaves and eaves-level production. That historical source establishes the category; use the selected provider's current engineered arrangement for the actual sheet path and operating conditions.

For a proposed inclined feed route, the supplier must design the transition from the line to the roof for the specified profile and material. The roof designer and installation contractor must accept the receiving arrangement. Include sheet support, surface protection, output direction and the space occupied throughout production in that package. Buying a ground former alone leaves this connecting system outside the order.

A dedicated roof-forming lift instead raises a compatible former toward the receiving elevation. It still needs an approved ground position, coil supply arrangement and roof-side receiving system. The photographed red crawler structure shows the separation between the ground base and the enclosed forming module above. Plot both levels on the procurement drawing.

*Read the base position and upper module position together when drawing the raised-former alternative.*

## The available corridor decides the shortlist

An eave height by itself cannot select the route. Measure the receiving point from the proposed machine-support level, then draw the horizontal space between them. Include fences, traffic lanes, stored material, wall projections and the receiving crew's work area. The actual proposal must fit this space while keeping required site access open.

For example, a broad clear apron with a fixed eave receiving point is a useful starting condition for a specialist ground-to-eaves proposal. A boundary immediately outside the wall removes that long approach. In the second layout, shortlist a raised former only if its complete support footprint and elevated envelope fit the available position. If neither fits, procure a different panel supply and handling route; do not force one of these two layouts into the drawing.

## Panel support is part of the purchase

Keep the chosen profile, material specification and length schedule attached to both offers. A provider's solution for one sheet family is not permission to feed a different panel through the same geometry. Have the line provider identify the accepted material and tooling, and have the route designer identify the support and transition details used with it. Changes to the roof receiving point must go back to both parties.

The straight panel on separate rollers below illustrates why the discharge route deserves its own scope line. A formed panel continues beyond the machine. In the ground-to-eaves alternative that continuation includes the designed inclined path; in the raised alternative it includes the roof-side receiver. Neither purchase is complete at the shear outlet.

## Two mistakes that turn a cheap machine into an expensive package

**Mistake 1 — pricing a former and leaving the inclined route unpriced.** In this illustrative bid, the buyer compares a ground machine with a complete lift-and-line offer. After award, the site team must procure the sheet support system, engineering and roof receiver. Correct the comparison before award: require a delivered ground-to-eaves package with those items assigned and priced, or remove it from the comparison.

**Mistake 2 — choosing the raised former from height alone.** In a second illustrative layout, its outlet reaches the eave on the section drawing, but the required support position blocks the only coil delivery route. The cure is a combined plan and section with delivery movements scheduled. Move to an accepted alternative layout or a different panel supply method if the conflict remains.

## RFQ attachments for two comparable offers

1. A surveyed plan and section connecting the proposed support surface to the actual roof receiver.
2. The controlled panel profile, material specification and finished length schedule issued by the roof designer.
3. A marked approach corridor with occupied areas, delivery routes, boundary restrictions and work dates.
4. Coil dimensions, masses and packaging from the material supplier, with the proposed unloading and feed arrangement.
5. Ground records and the named engineer responsible for assessing the offered travel and production load cases.
6. The roofing contractor's receiving layout, support requirements, crew positions and handover sequence.
7. Destination, available electrical supply and a scope split for transport, assembly, operation and removal.
8. A commercial comparison sheet covering identical panel quantities, accepted output and all included enabling works.
