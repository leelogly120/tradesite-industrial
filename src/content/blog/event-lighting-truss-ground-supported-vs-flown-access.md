**Buy a flown lighting truss when the venue accepts the proposed overhead points, the appointed rigging service can deliver the required show geometry, and retaining the floor space is worth the full rigging cost. Buy ground support when the approved floor footprint and reactions fit the exhibition layout and the venue accepts the complete temporary structure.** If neither arrangement preserves egress and access for the show crew, revise the exhibit before placing the rigging order.

Read the venue's occupied-floor plan beside the empty-hall plan. A tower base in an empty corner can become a lost display bay or an obstruction once an exhibitor builds its stand. A flown installation frees that position only if the venue has approved points in the right places. The cost comparison starts with two eligible show layouts, not two prices per length of truss.

The mesh deck in the photograph has rails, overhead booms and equipment on the working surface. This is a personnel-work context. An event rigging supplier separately designs and supports truss, luminaires, cables and other show loads. Agree which high-level tasks people must carry out after the hardware reaches its final position.

## Obtain the event rules for this particular hall

Ask the organiser for the current venue rigging plan, permitted suppliers and deadlines. The venue and its rigging authority determine accepted points and loads, who supplies lifting equipment and who can carry out different operations. [NAMM's truss and lighting guidelines](https://www.namm.org/thenammshow/exhibitor/guidelines/truss-lighting) distinguish aerial and ground-supported arrangements and assign work at the installed aerial truss to a rigging crew in its show context. Use the actual event rules for your bid. Labour privileges and access permissions do not automatically carry from one convention hall to another.

For a flown design, obtain a plot submitted by the rigging provider. Include actual fixtures and positions, equipment and cable loads, point availability, venue review and the time needed to install and remove the system. An approval for a previous booth is historical information. It cannot release the changed fixture schedule or a different booth grid. The event manager determines the final allowable configuration.

For ground support, ask the supplier for a complete structure and the footprint of bases, towers and any stabilising elements in the approved design. Include floor protection, doors and aisle interfaces. Draw the complete supporting area on the lease plan, not just the vertical tower centreline. The venue's floor authority needs the reactions from the actual proposal. The exhibitor needs to see which area remains available to visitors and product displays.

## Compare the show that can actually open

Build both estimates up to the same opening time and continue through the completed strike. For the flown option include venue review, authorised rigging calls, equipment, installation, adjustment and removal. Credit saved exhibition area only when it supports a genuine change, such as an additional booked exhibit or an aisle the organiser requires. Unused floor space does not repay the rigging bill by itself.

For ground support include floor occupation, design and review, delivery, installation, floor protection and restoration. Count personnel access for final connection or focusing wherever the proposed method requires it. An assembly starting on the floor still has a final elevated position. Likewise, accepting a flown layout does not give an exhibitor unrestricted use of a platform during public hours.

The lowered red deck in the workshop occupies floor space between the supports and the surrounding shop equipment. In an exhibition, mark the approach after the stand is built and the withdrawal path before visitors enter. If no route survives the build, arrange the required work before closure or buy a venue-approved alternative.

## Two mistakes on an otherwise convincing show quotation

**Mistake one — reserving riggers without reserving suitable points.** A hypothetical organiser accepts a lighting design by appearance. The venue plot cannot support that layout at the allocated booth. Obtain the rigging authority's accepted arrangement or revise the ground-supported design before promising the display.

**Mistake two — omitting strike access.** A ground-support quote includes installation in an empty hall, but neighbouring stands occupy the planned withdrawal route during dismantling. Agree the sequence, authorised crew and transport slot with the event logistics manager. Include extra calls where the programme genuinely requires them.
