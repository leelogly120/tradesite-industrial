**Buy a roll-forming lift that fits your committed regular work, and obtain a specific outside solution for rare higher projects. Choose the higher candidate when those projects justify the additional purchase and its complete configuration also fits everyday sites.** If a high roof is already a contractual obligation, an unspecified future rental or subcontractor is too weak a basis for choosing the regular-work machine.

The choice is how much equipment to own for the work you can reasonably expect. Buying the highest catalog candidate can leave everyday constraints unresolved. Buying only for familiar roofs can leave an awarded exception without a production route. Compare both purchases with a third option: regular-work ownership plus a defined service for the exception.

## Start with the work that pays for ownership

Divide the order book into repeat committed work, awarded exceptions and possible bids. Give each category its own decision weight. A high project that has been awarded deserves a technical and commercial solution now. A speculative high project deserves an allowance in planning, rather than automatically controlling the whole machine purchase.

For regular work, use the sites your crew actually visits. Identify the normal access, available setup area, panel profile, production arrangement and receiving point. Then test whether the proposed higher candidate would bring any additional transport, setup or support requirements to those jobs. Obtain those requirements from the supplier's configuration response; do not infer them from apparent size in a photograph.

An exceptional project also needs more than extra reach. If the equipment position prevents the proposed outlet from connecting to the roof receiver, price a different panel path or support arrangement. Owning a higher candidate is useful only if its proposed configuration solves that whole task.

## Put three investment routes on the same sheet

Compare the same regular jobs and the same exceptional job under all three routes. Exclude an outside-service route from the shortlist until a named provider accepts the product, site and required dates. Price another feasible route or decline the exceptional work if none is available.

| Investment route | Strong reason to choose it | Purchase risk to resolve | Fill from actual offers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Own for regular work | Repeated committed jobs fit the proposed package | Exceptional work still needs a separate solution | Buyer: enters regular-work configuration, price and transport/support cost for the committed order book |
| Own the higher candidate | Credible high-roof work needs repeated access to that package | Everyday sites must also accept its configuration | Estimator: enters incremental purchase and recurring costs; site team maps everyday jobs served |
| Own for regular work and buy exceptional service | High work is infrequent and an actual provider can cover it | Service scope and required dates must be confirmed | Planner: names actual external package, attendance, mobilisation, dates and accepted-panel boundary |

For each route, add the cost of work left uncovered. This is not a hypothetical penalty figure. It means obtaining a quotation for the remaining equipment or production service, or explicitly accepting that the business will decline that work.

A contractor can decide to serve a defined part of the market well. Declining an occasional project can be better than owning a package whose additional obligations have little connection to the regular order book. That choice should be deliberate before the contractor bids, not discovered after accepting the roof contract.

## What the extra purchase actually buys

Ask the higher-candidate proposal to show the specific awarded work it makes possible. The extra purchase is justified by useful project coverage, not by a more impressive catalog number.

Compare the difference in complete purchase scope with the actual cost and availability of outside coverage over your planning period. Include transport arrangements, storage, maintenance support and any different crew requirements. Use your accountant's normal method for financing and capital costs; the equipment comparison should supply the real inputs to it.

Avoid turning possible jobs into certain revenue. A practical worksheet can show a base case containing awarded work and a separate case containing named bids. If the higher purchase makes sense only when every bid succeeds, the business is taking a commercial risk that belongs in the buying decision.

Similarly, outside coverage needs a real quotation. A provider should identify the forming package, operating attendance if required, dates and scope. A generic personnel-lift hire listing is not a substitute for a system that supports the agreed roof-panel forming operation. ## A higher candidate must remain usable on ordinary jobs

Check everyday transport and setup before paying for the exception. The supplier should answer against the proposed former, support arrangement and actual access route. A former outlet, an equipment platform and a roof receiving point are different measurement references; carry the relevant one consistently through the proposal.

The [height-datum guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-height-specification-datum/) can help prepare those measurements. For this investment choice, the important result is a clear answer for each purchase candidate: which committed jobs it addresses and which jobs still need another solution.

## Two ways the exception can distort the purchase

**Failure case: a possible high roof controls a certain order book.** A contractor selects a higher candidate for an unawarded bid, but the proposal leaves access on its regular sites unresolved. The purchase review has prioritised an opportunity over committed work. Restore the regular-site checks, then compare an outside solution for the bid before deciding which machine to own.

**Failure case: the high project is real but its backup is imaginary.** A buyer selects a regular-work package and assumes a suitable operated forming service will be available later. No provider has reviewed the profile, site or required dates. Obtain that service proposal before relying on it. If the required service cannot be secured, reconsider the equipment purchase or the project commitment.
