**Choose toll forming when customers supply specified coil and committed processing orders. Choose material-inclusive panel sales when your business can purchase coil, fund inventory and customer credit, and manage the complete product supply. Buy the forming line for the contracted profile and output; customer ownership of the coil does not remove your responsibility for the processing work.**

The first business sells a conversion service. The second sells a finished material product. Both need a suitable line and competent production organisation, but they earn revenue against different obligations. A machine quotation alone cannot choose between them.

## What does the customer actually buy?

In a toll arrangement, the customer retains ownership of the supplied material under the agreed commercial terms. The processor receives an identified lot, forms the agreed profile, cuts the ordered lengths and returns accepted output and the agreed balance of material. Define the treatment of offcuts, rejected output and unused coil before production.

In material-inclusive sales, the panel seller purchases material and sells finished panels. The selling price must cover more than processing time: material purchasing, transport, storage, credit exposure, rejects and the promised delivery scope all belong in the calculation. Buying a line does not create an order book or a material purchasing organisation.

The wrapped forming line below shows the production asset common to both businesses. Its ownership does not decide who owns the coil or pays for a change in the customer's cut list.

![Blue and yellow roll-forming line with a wrapped cutting end on a workshop floor.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-wrapped-line.webp)

*The same equipment category needs different commercial controls when the sale changes from processing a customer's material to supplying panels.*

Use toll work as the initial business only when identifiable customers have both suitable material and scheduled work. Expressions of interest without profile drawings, material specifications or order quantities do not support the purchase. Choose own-material sales only when the buying and credit arrangements are funded alongside the machine.

## Fill the order boundary before comparing margins

Complete one copy of this table for each proposed business route. Enter quantities against a named order revision; avoid mixing a customer's estimate with a supplier's accepted production specification.

| Input to complete | Toll-forming entry | Own-material sales entry | Responsible party and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sale unit | Enter processing unit and included operations | Enter panel unit and included delivery scope | Commercial manager makes quotations comparable |
| Material lot | Enter customer lot ID, mass basis and delivery date | Enter purchase lot, purchase basis and payment date | Material owner identifies exposure before production |
| Profile and cut list | Enter drawing revision, quantities and length datum | Enter the same controlled product definition | Customer and production lead define accepted output |
| Incoming acceptance | Enter documents, condition record and rejection route | Enter purchasing inspection and supplier return route | Quality lead prevents unsuitable material entering the order |
| Setup material | Enter agreed allowance and who owns it | Enter allowance included in selling cost | Production and sales allocate trial and setup consumption |
| Rejects and offcuts | Enter weighing method, ownership and disposition | Enter recovery value and disposal responsibility | Commercial and quality leads separate material balance from liability |
| Payment exposure | Enter processing invoice milestone | Enter coil payment and customer receipt milestones | Finance lead calculates cash required between payments |
| Release for dispatch | Enter output acceptance and collection responsibility | Enter acceptance, packaging and transport scope | Dispatch lead establishes when the order is complete |

Build the cash comparison from dated payments, not just a profit percentage. Toll work still incurs wages, power, handling and storage before the processing invoice is collected. Own-material sales add the timing and value of coil purchases. Compare the largest cumulative cash outflow over the actual order calendar with available funding.

For margin, subtract only the costs relevant to each route and show them explicitly. A toll fee is not comparable with a panel selling price until material and delivery are placed on the same basis. Keep the machine purchase separate from each order's operating result so that an attractive individual order does not hide an underused asset.

## Material acceptance and output acceptance are separate decisions

Record incoming material against its specification, lot identification, packaging condition and agreed measurement method. The forming proposal needs the profile drawing, material grade, coating, thickness and tolerance information. Ownership alone says nothing about whether the line is suitable for the customer's coil.

Define output acceptance around the ordered profile and lengths, surface requirements and inspection method. The finished profile on the roller supports below illustrates the product that is inspected and released. A complete order includes a plan for supporting, identifying and collecting the cut panels, not only feeding strip into rollers.

![Long profiled metal panel supported on roller frames inside a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-long-panel.webp)

*Link finished-panel inspection and identification to the customer's drawing and cut-list revision.*

A material reconciliation should distinguish accepted panels, agreed trial material, offcuts, rejected pieces and remaining coil using one stated measurement basis. This record explains where material went. It does not, by itself, decide who pays for every rejected piece; allocate causes and remedies in the commercial scope before taking the order.

## Two purchasing mistakes to avoid

**Mistake: treating customer coil as a free production input.** In a hypothetical toll order, a contractor delivers material with a different coating and grade from the material used for the line proposal. The processor starts because the customer owns the coil. The resulting output fails the agreed surface requirement. Prevent this by making acceptance of each material specification a prerequisite to scheduling that order, with a named person authorised to reject a mismatched delivery.

**Mistake: comparing revenue while ignoring the funding gap.** A hypothetical panel seller accepts a large material-inclusive order on customer credit and places a coil order requiring earlier payment. The apparent gross margin is positive, but available cash cannot fund the material. Plot the actual purchase and collection dates before committing. Use toll processing for that order only if the customer agrees to supply suitable material and the changed commercial scope is explicit.
