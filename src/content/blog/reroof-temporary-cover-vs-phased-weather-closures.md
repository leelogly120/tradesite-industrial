Buy an engineered temporary roof when the replacement work must stay open across shifts and the building operator requires continuous protection below it. Choose phased weather closures when the roofing contractor can finish each released section or make it temporarily weathertight within its agreed window. If the operator rejects the exposure under both proposals, change the occupation or work sequence before booking a forming lift.

Take the last hour of the working day as the test. The roofer has removed some old covering. Part of the new roof is in place. A curb still needs attention and the tenant wants the space below back in use. Which contract delivers the protection at that moment? The answer changes how much roof the contractor can open, where new panels can enter and how much of the forming equipment booking will produce usable work.

## An overhead cover buys time across an open boundary

A temporary roof has value when the work below it has longer, less predictable durations than the permitted exposure. The roofing crew might need the deck inspected after opening it, followed by another trade's repairs before new covering goes down. A single protected work zone can carry that sequence across several handovers. The tender should identify the area actually protected, the surfaces that receive runoff, access openings and who takes care of the protection during interruptions.

Layher describes temporary roofs for refurbishment and a mobile configuration that can move as a project advances. A movable cover is worth comparing where repeated zones have suitable support and a practical transfer sequence. Price the move and the handover of each new zone. A bidder proposing fixed protection over the whole released area has a different occupation period and different access requirements. The cover design also changes the receiving route. A long sheet delivered at eaves level needs room to pass into the protected zone and be taken away. An overhead opening intended for pallet deliveries serves a different shape. Put the actual panel length and direction on the protection supplier's plan. Obtain a separate offer for shorter supplied lengths only where the roof designer accepts the resulting panel layout.

## Phased closure buys repeated, completed sections

Sectional working can be the economical purchase where the roofer can take a complete strip from removal through closure while the area below is released. Include all the time-sensitive interfaces in the strip. A perimeter, penetration, junction with old roofing or temporary drainage path can govern the section size even when the main sheets go down quickly.

Carlisle describes daily seals and drainage for its membrane roofing work. That illustrates the scope of a day-end boundary. On a metal roof, the roofing system designer and contractor provide the details appropriate to the existing and new assemblies. The owner's representative should name the condition required for return to use and the person who accepts it. [Carlisle's daily-seal guidance](https://www.carlislesyntec.com/en/Resources/Media/Blog-Landing-Page/SpecTopics/2020/06/05/A-Daily-Seal-for-Roofing-Projects)

Work backwards from the closure crew's last completed boundary. Assign a strip to the panel supplier only when all of its details, accessories and receiving space will be ready. Under this purchase, a faster forming line has little value during time reserved for flashing or inspecting the deck. Include the waiting or remobilisation that follows a late handover in the equipment offer.

## Two purchasing mistakes on a live building

**Mistake 1 — Buying cover without buying panel entry.** Imagine a tender that covers every opened bay but shows an uninterrupted support line across the only receiving edge. The contractor has already booked long sheets from an exterior lift. Price a designed delivery opening, an alternative receiving edge or a revised approved panel supply before awarding the cover. The protected area alone cannot settle that choice.

**Mistake 2 — Counting sheets laid as zones closed.** Suppose the day programme ends after laying the final sheet while a specialist is due the next morning to finish a junction. The owner needs the room overnight. Include the accepted interim closure and its removal, move the specialist's attendance, or carry that zone under temporary protection. Compare the cost of the resulting complete section.
