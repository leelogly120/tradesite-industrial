Choose prepainted long-run metal-sheet production when committed orders require project-length sheets, the accepted finish survives the specified forming route and the complete factory layout accommodates their receiving and dispatch. Buy a stone-coated tile plant only when its own accepted tile orders support forming, coating, granule control, curing and finished-product release. If the specialist process and its operating team are unfunded, buy finished tiles while investing in the supported sheet business. Start by separating the two order books and obtaining a complete process layout for each.

Both products use metal. A stone-coated tile brand sells defined finished units and their matching details; a project-length sheet supplier sells its specified lengths and roof-component basket. The tile investment includes a coating operation after shaping. The sheet investment starts with the specified incoming coil finish and preserves that finish through forming, cutting and delivery. Compare accepted roofing orders, with their actual component baskets, over the same financial period.

## Follow a tile beyond its metal shape

[DECRA Europe's material description](https://www.decra.eu/en/benefits/material-structure) identifies the basecoat bonding its stone chips and the acrylic overglaze. For a specific stone-coating process example, [DECRA's 2021 homeowner manual, page 5](https://decra.com/hubfs/DECRA%20Homeowners%20Manual%202021.pdf) describes removing loose granules before heat curing the basecoat. These manufacturer examples identify a coating and cure stage beyond metal shaping. Use the selected product specialist's process and acceptance requirements for the actual plant being purchased.

The [DECRA granule explanation](https://www.decra.com/pro/quality-roofing-granules-features-and-benefits) also identifies controlled granule size distribution, colour and fixation testing, and blending and application equipment. This explains why ordinary decorative aggregate and a matching colour sample are insufficient purchasing definitions. Buy the nominated coating-material set and its required quality controls from the providers responsible for the finished tile.

List the proposed stage outputs: shaped metal piece, coated piece, cured piece, inspected tile and packed saleable unit. Assign the transfer between each pair to a named equipment or production provider. Include the required accessory pieces in this route, or identify an accepted external source and its delivery commitment.

The coating specialist defines application and curing requirements. The plant provider supplies the corresponding equipment, handling and services. The product owner accepts the completed roofing item. Price these responsibilities together instead of letting the forming supplier's sample become approval of the whole product.

## Use the factory's actual batch pattern

A tile forecast needs quantities by profile, colour blend, release date and pack format. Two forecasts with the same annual quantity create different work when one runs a stable product and the other repeatedly changes finish. Obtain quoted changeover and cleaning resources for the selected equipment and materials. Ask bidders to cost one representative release sequence through accepted packs, including changeover labour, rejected output and bought-in operations. Use the resulting accepted output for that pattern in the operating model.

Include stock between shaping, coating, curing and release. A count leaving the metal-forming stage is work in progress until the promised finished tile passes its release criteria. Ask the process provider for its proposed stage capacities and inventory locations, then have finance fund that stock through dispatch and customer payment.

For long sheets, budget coil procurement, inspection, protective handling, the real length mix and the space to support and pack the longest sold pieces. Keep the qualifying sheet workload separate from the tile workload. Cost the customer's actual accepted roof package: tile units, gross sheet length and installed coverage are different quantity bases. Use the released layout's coverage datum when comparing eligible alternatives for one roof. Shared sales staff or premises should receive one cost allocation rather than becoming a reason to credit both factories with the same orders.

## Where the cheaper offer becomes unfinished roofing

The shaped cutting station below shows the sheet line's geometry-specific output interface. Include that metal-forming endpoint in its own offer; show the tile plant's forming-to-coating transfer on the separate specialist layout.

![A shiny ribbed sheet sits beneath a profile-shaped metal cutting edge in a blue and orange frame.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261102-profile-tooling-closeup.webp)
*Match the cut section to its output drawing and include the receiving and packing work needed for that sold product.*

**Mistake (hypothetical): bare pieces count as finished sales.** An investor compares a tile-forming offer with a complete sheet cell and omits the coating equipment, curing facilities and release team. The apparent lower capital cost purchases unfinished inventory. Rebuild the tile offer through inspected, packed output before comparing either business's return.

**Mistake (hypothetical): one colour sample qualifies the coating supply.** A buyer substitutes a locally available granule and binder combination because a sample looks similar. The chosen finished-product process has never accepted that combination, leaving the purchased coating stock outside the production-release plan. Obtain the responsible coating and product specialists' material qualification before buying the substituted stock or sizing plant around it.

Choose the business whose qualifying accepted sales pay for its delivered production costs, installed plant, staff and process stock over the chosen investment period. Remove speculative orders and repeat the calculation before awarding the factory. Supply the other roofing family through an identified finished-product source until its own orders support ownership. This investment differs from [contracting metal-shingle pressing](/blog/metal-shingle-production-contract-stamping-vs-owned-press-line/), where the same released product is supplied by an external or owned press route.
