Keep suitable uncoated tool steel when it meets the required roof-panel finish and service duty. Buy a specified working-surface treatment when the specialist has identified a material-contact or wear problem and the treated parts deliver the required panel in a representative trial. Price selected surfaces, a complete treated set and their future rework separately. Start with a marked contact problem or a defined new-product requirement, rather than ordering a premium finish for every roll.

## A shiny roll is not a surface specification

Separate three entries on the tooling proposal: the base material, its heat treatment and the working-surface treatment. Give the toolmaker the actual coil material, coating, section and appearance requirement. A familiar tool-steel name leaves the other entries open.

[Formtek's tooling-engineering discussion](https://blog.formtekgroup.com/top-5-real-world-considerations-for-roll-tooling-die-design-maintenance-and-operation) treats base materials, heat treatment and application-specific coatings as distinct choices. It also describes a problem roll as a possible candidate for a targeted coating application. That supports evaluating a selected surface, rather than assuming that every station needs the same premium.

The shaped cutting tool and sheet below illustrate a second distinction. Cutting hardware and forming rolls have different physical duties. List each proposed treated component and its working surfaces in the price schedule so a general “coated tooling” description does not absorb unrelated parts.

## Establish what the treatment is meant to change

For existing production, preserve accepted and rejected panels with their coil lots and run records. Mark the affected visible face, bend or edge. Have the tooling specialist assess the location and cause of the problem alongside the machine's condition.

For a new toolset, define the released surface target and representative material before either bidder selects a treatment. The comparison then starts with a product requirement, rather than inheriting a fault diagnosis from another machine.

[Roll-Kraft's materials guidance](https://www.roll-kraft.com/roll-tooling/roll-form-products-roll-forming-tube-roll-forming-pipe/roll-form-tooling-materials/) warns that incorrect setup or alignment can cause marking, even where the tooling material is suitable. It also emphasizes the importance of correct heat treatment. Buying a more expensive surface does not remove those underlying responsibilities.

Ask the specialist to distinguish the proposed contact improvement from geometric correction. If the base contour is wrong or worn beyond its accepted condition, obtain that corrective work as a separate item. The surface-treatment bid should then identify the defined geometry it receives and the finished condition it returns.

Keep the uncoated offer when it meets the product and service duty after the identified machine or setup issue is resolved. Choose the treated alternative when the demonstrated contact result and complete ownership case justify its additional scope.

## Put the premium on named parts

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required surface | Face ___; accepted sample ___ | Product quality lead; marked panel section | Defines the result the buyer is paying for |
| Input material | Coil product ___; trial lot ___ | Material buyer; actual supplied surface | Keeps the comparison representative |
| Underlying geometry | Tool drawing ___; assessed condition ___ | Toolmaker; working contour datums | Separates correction from treatment |
| Base tooling | Material ___; heat-treatment scope ___ | Toolmaker; identified part specification | Prevents a coating label replacing the base requirement |
| Selected treatment | Part IDs ___; working surfaces ___; treatment specification ___ | Toolmaker and treatment provider; marked component drawing revisions | Identifies the actual premium |
| Returned dimensions | Finished measurements ___; acceptance ___ | Toolmaker and inspection lead; final drawing datums | Establishes a usable delivered component |
| Future work | Receiving provider ___; condition assessment ___; retained/repeated stages ___ | Maintenance buyer; actual return-to-drawing service quotation | Prices later ownership |
| Production release | Trial order ___; accepted panel record ___ | Quality lead; specified output and inspection point | Ends the purchase at the sold product |

Require a reason for treating each selected part. Request the specialist's connection between the observed contact duty and the included surfaces. An inexpensive treatment on the wrong component has little value. Conversely, a premium covering the entire set needs a complete-set justification rather than the result from one improved roll.

Compare the returned component against its final drawing, rather than only the untreated blank. Have the toolmaker identify responsibility for the finished contour and any specified work after treatment. Carry those operations into the accepted-part quotation.

Compare actual installed and service costs. Include the trial, transport, competent fitting, inspection and production interruption required by each proposal. Include every retained operation needed to return the line to production before ranking treatment invoices.

## Buy the next service route with the first treatment

The owner must support the treated tooling through its next service event. Ask the actual tooling and treatment providers what their next correction or renewal service includes. Record who receives the used part, evaluates its condition, performs any specified surface work and returns it to the agreed dimensions.

These are quotations for defined future work, not predicted universal life. Use the producer's real service history where available. For a new product, retain clearly stated planning assumptions and compare the consequences if the expected service event occurs earlier. Do not turn the vendor's best observed result into the life of every roll in the set.

The loose components below make custody important. Identify the treated part, its assembly position and drawing revision in the owner's records. Keep the treatment and inspection records attached to that identity, so a stored component can be connected to its intended working set.

*Keep each treated component's identity, assembly position and inspection record together through storage and service.*

A segmented roll and a coated roll answer different questions. One describes construction; the other describes a working surface. Likewise, restoring worn geometry and choosing a treatment premium are separate decisions. State which condition the priced work starts from, then compare its finished deliverable.

## Two coating purchases that miss their target

**Mistake: every roll is coated before the cause is assessed.** A hypothetical producer buys a premium across its whole set after seeing marks on one panel face. The specialist later identifies an unresolved alignment fault. Correct the underlying condition and assess the treatment against the remaining contact requirement before purchasing the premium.

**Mistake: the invoice ends before the component is usable.** Another buyer accepts a treatment-only price but has not assigned the required dimensional inspection and competent refitting. The returned part has no agreed release point. Obtain a complete deliverable from the responsible providers and include the accepted panel trial in the comparison.
