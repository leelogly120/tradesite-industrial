## Separate the chassis function from the ground's capacity

[JLG's engineering explanation of its self-levelling chassis](https://www.jlg.com/en/directaccess/a-look-into-the-development-of-jlgs-self-leveling-chassis-technology) describes independently acting wheel arms and integrated sensing, distinguishing this architecture from a conventional oscillating/fixed-axle arrangement. Its procurement significance is the different hardware offered to accommodate terrain, rather than preparing each required standing surface to suit a conventional machine. That is a named boom-lift technology, not a characteristic of every tracked platform.

Obtain the exact candidate's documented scope for each relevant state: transport, relocation and the proposed elevated work. Transport gradeability does not approve operation at height on the same surface. A self-levelling label also does not establish that a candidate handles the project's local transitions, cross-profile or required task position. Have the provider mark each surveyed location as served in its proposed state or assigned to a retained station/other resource. A conventional oscillating axle is not the independently adjusting chassis described by JLG.

[IPAF's ground-assessment guidance](https://www.ipaf.org/en/operator-feeds/do-you-know-what-look-when-assessing-ground-conditions) includes surface angle, evenness, visible or hidden weaknesses and the ground travelled over as well as the final work area. Keep these separate inputs in the purchase. Removing a levelling operation does not remove the need to assess support capacity.

## Buy a finite station scheme, not an allowance called groundworks

For the conventional route, require a located station schedule tied to the real installation sequence. Each station needs an identifier, the tasks served, accepted standing footprint, required release date, occupation period and restoration owner. The responsible engineer defines and releases the station scheme; the access buyer prices its delivered resources and interfaces.

Identify which stations remain available throughout the work and which are removed, reconstructed or relocated as other trades advance. Price establishment, access to establish the next station, protection during occupation, required maintenance and removal/restoration. Separate those items from a travel route that merely gets the stowed machine to the area.

For the adaptive route, ask the provider to identify which listed station works disappear because the actual machine serves those positions on the surveyed profile. Retain common support improvements, restricted-area measures and preparation that the candidate still needs. The comparison becomes specific: hardware premium against identified station work removed, not against every item in the site's groundworks budget.

## Follow the machine through successive working positions

A project with a few durable stations buys a different site package from a project where every relocation requires another released standing area. Count real station changes over the programme. Include the equipment, labour and access that establish the next position, plus the time and space needed for that operation. For a phased wall campaign, link each station's release to the task it serves. If a delivery corridor must reopen before the next face is worked, price the station's removal and subsequent re-establishment, not one continuous occupation. If an accepted station remains available for all returns, credit that real reuse instead. These are different site-work purchases even when the machine and wall height stay unchanged.

The adaptive route needs an equally located account. Establish which task positions the offered machine actually serves and which remain dependent on other equipment. If its basket serves only part of a shared installation front, include additional people-access visits, material presentation and coordination in its complete offer. Do not price one small basket as an automatic substitute for a broad working deck.

If both complete routes meet the work and dates, choose the lower total. If only one serves the accepted workface and programme, purchase that complete route.

## Two errors that inflate the apparent saving

**Mistake (hypothetical): driving capability is treated as working approval.** A buyer selects a machine from its transport gradeability figure and deletes all level stations. The supplier's actual elevated-work scope does not admit the proposed positions. Correct the tender with state-specific documentation matched to the surveyed locations and reinstate every station or alternative resource still required.

**Mistake (hypothetical): every groundworks cost is credited to the adaptive machine.** The comparison removes the entire site-preparation allowance, although weak support and buried services still require separate measures. The resulting saving is fictitious. Split geometry-related station work from support-capacity work, identify the actual released stations eliminated and compare only the complete retained packages.

## Eight RFQ attachments for actual personnel access

1. Workface and task plan showing supporting datums, required heights, offsets, obstacles and completed-work endpoint.
2. Crew, tools and simultaneous-material schedule with required continuous or separate personnel working positions.
3. Current surface survey with located longitudinal/cross profiles and local transitions on both routes and standing areas.
4. Ground/slab, buried-service and edge records, plus the engineer receiving actual offered support reactions.
5. Entry dimensions, turns, relocation corridors and all proposed equipment standing/support positions.
6. Alternative provider's exact adaptive configuration and documented work-state response for the common task plan.
7. Conventional station scheme, IDs/count, footprints and phase releases, with establishment/reconstruction resources and maintenance/removal/restoration ownership.
8. Destination, site power and transport envelope, requesting offered deck geometry, simultaneous task/load limits, reactions and configuration/acceptance records.
