Choose dry roof-panel forming when the coil producer specifies processing the supplied surface without added lubricant and the proposed line delivers the required panel. Buy an added lubrication package when the actual material and forming duty require a compatible fluid process. Include application, collection and the panel's clean, dry condition at packing in that purchase. Obtain the material-specific process response before awarding the line, rather than adding a standard spray system after the price is agreed.

## The coil product decides whether more lubricant belongs on it

Start with the complete supplied surface designation. Metallic coating, paint, additional surface treatment, protective film and any existing oil belong in that description where present. A colour name or the broad description galvanized sheet leaves too much of the material undefined for this decision.

The coils beside processing machinery below make this an incoming-material question. Keep the material producer's product identity and current forming instructions attached to the line specification. Different surface products should occupy separate rows, even where their finished panels share a shape.

![Metal coils on separate stands sit beside blue processing machinery, motors and sheet offcuts.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-coil-processing-workshop.webp)
*Identify the supplied surface of each coil product before allocating it to a dry or lubricated forming route.*

U. S. Steel's [Roll-Forming Guide for Building Panels](https://www.ussteel.com/documents/40705/43746/Roll-Forming%2BGuide%2Bfor%2BBuilding%2BPanels.pdf/a0328c19-8924-d74c-e5c9-b2c905419be9?t=1604458480698) discusses forming lubricants but specifies no added lubricant for its named ACRYLUME and ACRYZINC acrylic-coated products. That example shows why a generic “oil helps forming” rule is inadequate. Obtain the current producer's instruction for the exact product being bought; do not extend one branded product's treatment to every coated coil.

BlueScope's [lubrication bulletin, section 7.2](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/52185825-1d41-49b7-9261-0be3212385fc/TB-F1-Lubrication-of-steel-sheet-and-strip-for-Forming_BSL.pdf) relates roll-forming lubrication need to the surface, coating, tooling and forming conditions. The equipment decision therefore follows a defined material and section. It is separate from choosing bearing grease or the lift's hydraulic fluid.

## Dry forming is a specified production route

The dry-forming bid should name its covered input products and the line configuration used to make accepted panels. Include the surface condition arriving from normal supply, rather than a specially prepared demonstration strip. Have the equipment and material providers reconcile any proposed departure before releasing the production order.

The attraction is avoiding an unnecessary added-fluid process and its associated handling. It still takes a suitable machine, the specified input and an accepted product. Dry forming is the chosen process condition; it is not a reason to omit normal equipment maintenance or cleaning required by the machine's instructions.

## A lubrication package ends at the packing station

When additional forming fluid is required, price its complete working boundary. Identify the exact fluid product and the material producer's compatibility response, together with application equipment, collection arrangements, consumables and competent maintenance. Include the current fluid safety information in the responsible site's resource assessment. The responsible providers define operating requirements. The buyer purchases the equipment and resources needed to fulfil them.

Follow one finished panel from the outlet to packing. Establish who checks the required surface condition, whether an additional drying or cleaning stage is needed and where the panel becomes ready for packing. U. S. Steel's guide specifically calls for panels to be dry before shipment in its lubrication discussion. Carry the purchased material's drying and dispatch requirements into the equipment offer, ending at the packing handover.

The sheet over the roller bed below illustrates that outlet boundary. A finished shape still has a surface to protect during receiving, packing and delivery. Put the relevant handling contacts and any downstream film or sealant process into the acceptance comparison.

## One plant can need two material routes

For a mixed product range, do not assume that one process serves every surface. The buyer can retain a dry route for one identified coil and price another process for a different product. Ask the provider to define the transition between them, including any retained cleaning and acceptance work. Count those transitions using the actual production schedule.

## Separate a process need from a machine fault

A surface mark deserves a cause assessment before an equipment addition. Compare incoming material, the position where the mark first appears, the machine condition and the actual tooling arrangement with the responsible specialists. Additional lubrication should answer an established process need.

Hold the agreed line condition and trial material constant when evaluating the proposed fluid route. If a supplier changes the coil product or repairs the tooling between trials, retain those changes in the record. Attribute the improved output to the complete changed condition instead of purchasing a fluid package for a benefit it has not demonstrated.

## Two mistakes at opposite ends of the line

**Mistake: the spray system precedes the material decision.** A hypothetical buyer orders a standard lubrication option before choosing the supplied surface treatment. The selected coil producer subsequently specifies a route without added lubricant. Freeze the material identity and its applicable process instruction before buying the extra system.

**Mistake: accepted output is still waiting to become packable.** Another buyer compares panels directly at the machine outlet. The selected route leaves drying or cleaning work between that point and the dispatch condition. Include the necessary stage, resources and inspection in the installed package and its order-time comparison.
