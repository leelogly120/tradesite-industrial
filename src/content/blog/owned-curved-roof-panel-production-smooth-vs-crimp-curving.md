Choose smooth curving when the accepted roof product requires a smooth finished appearance and the proposed cell supports its actual material and longitudinal geometry. Choose crimp curving when the product authority accepts the crimped appearance and the machine supports the specified product and curve. If both cells pass the specified panel trial and dispatch requirements for the same accepted dated workload, choose the lower complete cost over the owner's fixed comparison period. Where their eligible order books differ, allocate demand by accepted process before funding capacity.

Mechanical pre-curving and owning the curving equipment are already decided here. The remaining investment is the mechanism for producing the specified curved panels. Architectural approval, product acceptance and the production schedule must describe the same finished output.

## Start with the approved surface, then name the process

A smooth surface requirement is an output condition for a particular order. It belongs beside the material, profile, curve direction and approved longitudinal shape. A crimped product has a different visible result that must be accepted in its own right. Put an actual approved appearance specimen or reference beside the geometry drawing before pricing either cell.

Section forming supplies the profile that enters the curving stage. For the roller-forming equipment pictured below, the upstream purchase needs a section drawing and output-acceptance record that match the separately specified curving input.

![Rows of shaped silver rollers and a side chain extend along a blue forming-machine bed.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-roller-train-detail.webp)

*Define the former's section output before matching it to the purchased longitudinal-curving cell.*

A supplier can demonstrate a curved panel while still demonstrating the wrong product for the order. Comparing the completed specimen with the approved appearance reference helps the buyer identify that mismatch before machinery is purchased.

## Real product families show both the choice and its limits

[Kingspan Architectural's KingZip page](https://www.kingspanarchitectural.com.au/products/kingzip) lists mechanically smooth-rolled and factory-crimp convex routes within the same named product family, with material-specific limits. It lists smooth concave curving separately. This establishes a real mechanism choice within a defined system; the product's exact material and shape remain part of that choice.

[Schlebach's RBM page](https://schlebach.de/produkt/rundbogenmaschine-rbm-25-rbm-32-rbm-38/) describes convex bending of already profiled double-standing-seam panels matched to the maker's profile family. The example reinforces the input boundary: a curver is purchased for an accepted section and product combination.

[Dimond's curved-roofing guidance](https://www.dimond.co.nz/products/curved-roofing?tab=downloads) describes machine crimps across the profile pans and publishes separate product and material groupings for its curved-sheet processes. Its description makes the crimp pattern part of the finished panel geometry. For procurement, specify that visible result and the eligible product alongside the curve drawing, rather than compare machines through one advertised radius.

Those manufacturer examples establish process distinctions. The offered machinery must demonstrate its own accepted capability for the buyer's specified roof product. Transfer the requirement drawing and output criteria into the machinery specification; leave each manufacturer's published capability attached to its own named product.

## Separate orders before calculating utilization

Consider a hypothetical fabricator with a signed smooth-surface architectural order and a second order explicitly accepting a crimped product. Combining their panel quantities into one curver-utilization figure overstates the workload for either cell unless that particular cell is accepted for both.

Build two eligible-work lists. Include the delivery date and actual material with each order, then assign it to the supported process. Leave a commercially attractive but unaccepted order outside the machine's investment case. This reveals whether one mechanism carries the confirmed workload, whether separate process capacity is required, or whether the machinery purchase needs a different scope.

| Order or cell input | Owner | Datum to enter | Decision controlled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Approved roof product | Product authority | Profile, material and revision: ___ | Eligible machine input |
| Longitudinal geometry | Responsible designer | Shape drawing, direction and datum: ___ | Required curving output |
| Finished appearance | Architect or product authority | Accepted specimen/reference: ___ | Smooth or crimp route admission |
| Eligible order book | Commercial manager | Orders, accepted process and dates: ___ | Real workload for each cell |
| Former-to-curver handover | Equipment integrator | Defined section and interface schedule: ___ | Upstream compatibility |
| Complete working layout | Provider and production planner | Handling, supports and storage plan: ___ | Installed cell scope |
| Representative panel trial | Quality lead | Full-panel acceptance record: ___ | Release of the purchased process |
| Ownership and dispatch plan | Procurement lead | Common period, totals and delivery schedule: ___ | Complete investment choice |

Record one accepted process against each order, including any expressly accepted alternatives. A drawing revision that changes the visible roof finish should trigger a fresh allocation before the order is counted toward the machine's workload.

## Buy a finished-panel trial and its handling envelope

A short profile sample is useful for identifying the section. The machinery acceptance task also needs a representative full curved panel assessed against the specified longitudinal geometry and appearance. Agree who supplies the input material, who accepts the result and what records accompany that acceptance.

![Short white profiled pieces lie beside two blue and orange forming-machine ends in a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261030-panel-samples.webp)

*Use section samples to identify the input, then assess the specified full curved-panel result separately.*

Include the practical route through the cell: incoming profiled panels, support arrangements, the curving machine, outgoing panels, storage and dispatch preparation. A bare curver quotation can leave those responsibilities distributed across several suppliers. The buyer needs one complete installed-cell total, with named owners for each handover.

Use the same representative order programme when comparing eligible proposals. Fund equipment, accepted tooling, handling and support scope, trial material, training included in the offer, and the owner's production resources. Ask each provider to price the scope it actually supplies. Place separately purchased items alongside it rather than silently assuming they are included.

Where eligible orders occur in one concentrated run, include the following idle period in the ownership comparison and retain the actual delivery dates in the workload plan. A broader sales forecast earns investment weight only after the additional product and process combination is accepted. This protects the investment case from a catalogue of curves that the business has yet to sell or qualify.

## Two ways a curver purchase misses the order book

**Mistake — hypothetical:** A fabricator combines a smooth architectural-panel order with an accepted crimp-panel order, then purchases a crimp cell using the combined annual quantity. The smooth order cannot support that investment case under its existing appearance approval. Correct the calculation by allocating accepted orders to their actual eligible processes before pricing capacity.

**Mistake — hypothetical:** A buyer copies a tight curve value from one manufacturer's crimped product and uses it to evaluate a smooth-curving offer for a different profile and material. The two quotations answer different output specifications. Correct the comparison with the actual product's accepted geometry, material and appearance, then require the representative finished-panel trial.

Where the responsible product authority accepts only one process, compare complete machinery proposals within that process. Price any proposed change of finished appearance only after the revised roof product has its own acceptance.

## Send eight attachments for the section-forming RFQ

The curver brief above establishes the required handover. For the upstream forming-line quotation, consolidate it into these equipment inputs:

1. Accepted profile drawing, seam details and current product revision.
2. Actual material grade, gauge, coating and specified coil characteristics.
3. Accepted input width, finished section and section-trial acceptance criteria.
4. Order quantities, panel length mix and required production dates.
5. Accepted smooth or crimp route and curver-input interface requirements.
6. Coil-to-former layout, panel supports and defined handover point.
7. Site utilities, destination and transport or packing constraints.
8. Included forming-line equipment, trial responsibilities and complete-price boundary.
