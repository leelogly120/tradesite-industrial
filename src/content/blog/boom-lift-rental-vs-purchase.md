## The Real Question Isn't "Which Is Cheaper"

The real question is: how many hours per year will you use the machine? The answer determines whether renting or buying makes financial sense.

Rental companies make money because most contractors don't use their equipment enough to justify ownership. But if you're running ceiling projects regularly — stadiums, airports, warehouses — ownership can pay for itself quickly.

## Rental Costs: What You Actually Pay

Aerial work platform rental rates vary by type, size, and region. Here are typical monthly rates (US market, 2026):

- **Boom lift (40–60ft):** $2,500–$4,500/month
- **Scissor lift (20–40ft):** $800–$2,000/month
- **Spider lift (60–80ft):** $3,500–$6,000/month
- **Crawler under-ceiling (FDDPT):** Contact for quote

But the monthly rate is just the starting point. Add:
- **Delivery and pickup:** $500–$2,000 per move
- **Fuel:** $200–$600/month depending on usage
- **Insurance:** 2–4% of machine value annually
- **Operator training:** $500–$1,500 per operator
- **Downtime:** waiting for availability during peak season

A spider lift that costs $4,000/month to rent actually costs $5,500–$6,500/month when you include delivery, fuel, and insurance.

## Purchase Costs: The Full Picture

The purchase price is the obvious cost. But ownership brings additional expenses:

**Capital cost:**
- FDDPT-20M: $130K–$180K
- FDDPT-25M: $150K–$200K
- FDDPT-31M: $180K–$250K
- FDDPT-35M: $250K–$350K

**Annual operating costs:**
- Maintenance and parts: 3–5% of purchase price
- Insurance: 2–4% of machine value
- Storage: $200–$500/month (if no yard space)
- Depreciation: varies by accounting method

**Total annual cost of ownership:** roughly 8–12% of the purchase price.

## Break-Even Analysis

Here's the math that matters. If a machine costs $200K to buy and $6K/month to rent, the break-even point is:

**$200,000 ÷ ($6,000 × 12) = 2.8 years**

If you'll use the machine more than 34 months over its lifetime, buying is cheaper. If less, renting wins.

But this calculation ignores several factors that favor ownership:

**Rental price increases:** Rental rates typically increase 3–5% annually. Your purchase price is fixed.

**Availability:** During peak construction season (spring through fall), rental companies run out of equipment. If you own your machine, it's always available.

**Resale value:** Aerial work platforms retain 40–60% of their value after 5 years. The $200K machine might sell for $80K–$120K after its useful life.

**Customization:** Rental machines are stock configurations. Owned machines can be modified — additional safety systems, custom paint, specialized attachments.

## When to Rent

Renting makes sense when:
- You need a machine for a single project or short campaign
- Your utilization rate is below 500 hours/year
- You're testing a new application before committing
- Cash flow doesn't support a large capital purchase
- You need different machine types for different projects

## When to Buy

Buying makes sense when:
- You'll use the machine more than 500 hours/year
- You have recurring ceiling projects (stadiums, airports, warehouses)
- Rental availability is unreliable in your market
- You want to customize the machine for your specific applications
- You plan to operate the machine for 5+ years
- You have maintenance capabilities in-house

## The FDDPT Calculation

For the FDDPT series, the ownership equation is particularly favorable because:

1. **No comparable rental alternative.** You can't rent a crawler under-ceiling platform from most rental companies. The FDDPT is a specialty machine — buying is often the only option.

2. **High utilization on ceiling projects.** If you're doing stadium, airport, or warehouse ceiling work, the machine runs 8+ hours/day for weeks at a time. That's exactly the usage pattern where ownership beats rental.

3. **Long service life.** Crawler under-ceiling platforms are built for heavy-duty industrial use. With proper maintenance, 15–20 years of service life is realistic.

4. **Resale demand.** The FDDPT series serves a growing market. Used units hold value well.

## Decision Framework

**Calculate your utilization:**
- Estimated annual usage hours: ___
- Estimated project count per year: ___
- Estimated years of ownership: ___
- Total lifetime hours: (annual hours × years)

**Compare total costs:**
- Rental total: (monthly rate × months used) + delivery + fuel + insurance
- Purchase total: (purchase price × (1 + annual cost % × years)) − resale value

**If purchase total < rental total:** Buy.
**If rental total < purchase total:** Rent.

For most contractors doing regular ceiling work with the FDDPT, the answer is clear: buy.
