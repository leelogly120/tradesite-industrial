Choose a corrugated roof-panel line when repeat customers order a defined corrugated section and its complete roofing package is available for those jobs. Choose a trapezoidal line when the awarded work specifies that rib geometry and the matching details. If neither profile has a documented order base, buy finished panels for the first orders and use the sales records to select the first tooling investment.

The decision is which product to manufacture first. It is not a contest between attractive sample sheets. A panel business earns its return from accepted, delivered orders with the correct profile, material and accessories. A cheap machine for the wrong local section creates stock and quoting activity without serving the intended customers.

## Replace the profile nickname with a controlled section

Buyers use “corrugated” for several visibly different products. Separate the smooth repeating wave from a defined trapezoidal rib section, then attach the actual drawing. Record rib spacing, depth, side-lap geometry, cover width, feed width, material and surface requirements. Use drawing revision identifiers so the sample, quotation and acceptance record refer to the same product.

[MBCI's exposed-fastener catalogue](https://www.mbci.com/products/roof/exposed-fastening-roof-panels/) lists multiple separate profiles, including corrugated and PBR products. This illustrates why a broad family name is insufficient to order tooling. MBCI's product details describe its own range; they are not ARCLIFT tooling specifications.

![Rows of shaped silver rollers sit in a blue machine frame.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261109-roller-bed-perspective.webp)
*The formed section starts with a defined tooling geometry, not the machine's paint colour or general appearance.*

The rollers in the photograph make the tooling decision visible. Specify the output drawing and representative input material before evaluating a proposed line. Do not select tooling by finding a workshop photograph that resembles a supplier's sample.

## Compare the same customer orders through each product route

Build a short order register from actual customer requests. For each order, identify the specified section, permitted alternatives, lengths, quantity, destination and required supporting documents. Mark an alternative as acceptable only when the person controlling the project specification has issued that decision. A buyer's interest in a lower price does not amend the drawing.

For each candidate profile, separate orders it directly serves from orders requiring a redesigned roof package or another supplier. Price the second group through purchased panels. A first line does not need to produce the entire market, but its business case must show which orders it earns and which orders remain outside its scope.

Use the same planning period for both routes. Compare accepted material cost, start-up scrap, order change losses, handling, inspection, delivery and bought-in accessories. Keep quoted speed separate from accepted output over the actual mix of short and long orders. A fast demonstration of one long sheet is not the operating record for a mixed order book.

## A worksheet for the first tooling commitment

Complete one column for each exact candidate section. Use the blanks for drawings and business records, rather than generic market estimates.

| Purchase input | Fillable buyer record and datum | Responsible owner | How it changes the choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repeat demand | Customer/order IDs ___; section drawing revision ___; monthly release dates ___ | Sales lead | Identifies work the first line directly serves |
| Allowed substitution | Project document ___; approving party ___; accepted alternative revision ___ | Customer's designer or specifier | Separates an accepted alternative from an unapproved sales assumption |
| Product geometry | Section dimensions ___; side-lap detail ___; effective cover datum ___ | Product engineer | Defines the tooling and inspection target |
| Material input | Grade ___; thickness range ___; coating ___; strip width ___ | Material buyer | Defines the representative coil for the line trial |
| Material yield | Feed width ___; accepted cover width ___; actual trim/start-up loss ___ | Production and finance leads | Compares material used per accepted covered area |
| Complete roof package | Matching trims, fixings, closures and installation detail references ___ | Roofing system coordinator | Identifies products needed to sell a usable package |
| Operating resources | Premises ___; handling route ___; staff ___; inspection tools ___ | Operations lead | Establishes the complete cost of starting either line |
| Acceptance evidence | Trial order ___; measuring method ___; accepted samples ___; rejects ___ | Quality lead and buyer | Tests the proposed tooling against the nominated product |

The cover-width datum matters when comparing material use. Coil width and roof coverage are different quantities. Keep the supplier's section drawing and the buyer's costing basis together so the calculation does not compare a gross sheet width for one profile with effective installed coverage for the other.

## Buy the missing supply route before promising the full range

A contractor needs the panels and the associated components that complete its ordered roof. Decide which items the new business produces and which it purchases. List the outside suppliers, product references, minimum order arrangements and delivery responsibility for the bought-in scope. Keep those quotations beside the machine quotation.

![Long metal ribs extend across a silver roof towards distant workers.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261109-long-roof-panels.webp)
*Cost a delivered roof-panel order with its actual lengths and matching details, rather than selling a profile name alone.*

The long ribs in the photograph are a reminder that manufactured sheets become part of a roof layout. The machine selection follows that layout's controlled product requirement. The appearance of an installed or partly installed sheet does not determine its allowable span or structural performance; those decisions belong to the project's design documents.

If a second profile is requested only occasionally, price purchased finished panels before buying another line or changeable tooling. If two sections have independently supported order books, compare dedicated lines with a documented changeover proposal using actual lost production time. The [multi-profile versus dedicated-line guide](/blog/multi-profile-roof-panel-roll-former-vs-dedicated-lines/) covers that next investment decision.

## Two failures that turn the first line into the wrong investment

**Hypothetical failure case: a local nickname replaces the order drawing.** A buyer selects a corrugated line from a sample photograph. Customers actually use a different repeating section and the supplied trims fit their existing product. The new output cannot fulfil the quoted orders. Attach the approved section and accessory references to the line purchase before taking panel orders.

**Hypothetical failure case: sheet width becomes assumed roof coverage.** A quotation compares equal coil widths and calls one panel cheaper. The costing excludes overlap and uses inconsistent coverage datums. Rework the comparison per accepted covered area using the controlled sections, then include the same delivery and accessory scope for both options.

## Send a product-backed line brief

For the first-line RFQ, attach these eight items:

1. The controlled drawing and revision for each candidate section.
2. Actual customer orders or a clearly separated repeat-demand register.
3. Material grades, thicknesses, coatings and nominated coil widths.
4. The length and quantity mix of a representative production order.
5. Matching accessory drawings and the proposed bought-in supply list.
6. Premises, handling route, utilities and finished-panel storage plan.
7. Trial acceptance method, critical dimensions and sample identification.
8. Destination, delivery scope and equipment items already owned.
