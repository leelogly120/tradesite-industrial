Choose recognized complete-equipment certification when it genuinely covers the offered forming line and its actual configuration. For an eligible low-volume or one-off line, engage a recognized agency for an accepted field-evaluation scope early enough to complete required alterations before use or sale in Ontario. A component mark or PANEL ONLY label does not approve the connected machine. Before ordering, give the agency the full equipment boundary and drawing revisions, then allocate review, alterations and final release to named parties. Without a workable complete-equipment route, the purchase is not electrically ready.

## Start with the whole electrical product

A roof-panel line can include the former, powered feed equipment, cutting equipment and several controls. State which connected units the purchase includes and which the buyer adds. The approval comparison concerns that defined electrical equipment. Keep site installation and the machinery's other conformity requirements in their separate reviews. A supplier's cabinet, component or separate-machine record answers only its stated scope.

Ontario's [Electrical Safety Authority recognized-mark guidance](https://esasafe.com/electrical-products/recognized-certification-marks/) identifies certification and field-evaluation routes. It specifically distinguishes component recognition and PANEL ONLY labels from approval of the connected end-use equipment. Read the exact mark, its scope and the relevant unit identity before assigning approval value to an offer.

## When complete-equipment certification is the better order

Start with a genuinely certified offered configuration when its recognized approval covers the actual equipment you will receive. Match the manufacturer, model, electrical construction and applicable record to the order. Ask the issuing certification body to resolve a scope ambiguity before treating the marked assembly as the complete line.

This route is useful when the approved configuration matches the required product and avoids purchasing a separate evaluation programme for that same equipment. Compare the certification scope with the final bill of equipment. An attractive marked cabinet inside an otherwise undocumented line is a different offer.

Put changes under control. A substituted drive, altered control assembly or added powered module needs review against the actual approval basis by the responsible parties. Keep the accepted physical configuration and its current documents together; a copied mark image in a general quotation cannot settle a changed line's scope.

## Where an eligible field evaluation fits

[eSAFE's field-evaluation guidance](https://www.esafe.org/en/field-evaluation/) describes low-volume and one-off electrical equipment, including assembly-line machinery. It recommends early involvement and offers development-stage and pre-shipment review. Equipment that fails inspection receives an alteration report; successful inspection and the applicable label follow the provider's process.

Use that route only after the chosen recognized agency accepts the actual equipment and evaluation scope. Give it the line layout, electrical records, connected modules and intended Ontario installation. Obtain the required review stages and documentation before committing to a build whose alterations would otherwise become a late destination problem.

Price the complete route: agency work, supplier engineering and alterations, relevant inspections, documentation updates and any destination work that remains. Assign the party paying for a failed inspection, corrective work and reinspection. An evaluation booking is a purchased activity, not an approval result.

Field evaluation also needs the actual units in scope. eSAFE's published process requires each product to be inspected rather than using a prototype result to issue labels for uninspected copies. If the order contains several lines or modules, have the agency identify the inspection population explicitly.

## Complete the approval-boundary schedule

Keep the required production scope fixed between offers. Use the equipment's electrical drawings and connection boundary as the comparison datum. The responsible electrical designer and agency determine the wiring, protection and tests for the actual line.

| Boundary or release input | Buyer entry and record datum | Responsible owner | Purchase decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Actual Ontario use | Facility ___; intended use/sale event ___; release requirement ___ | Importer and facility owner | Establishes the destination approval task |
| Whole-line population | Former/feed/cut/control IDs ___; connected-unit drawing ___ | Supplier and buyer technical lead | Fixes the equipment each route must cover |
| Offered certification | Issuer/recognized mark ___; model/scope record ___; revision ___ | Supplier and certification body | Accepts genuine complete-equipment cover |
| Component or panel labels | Label type ___; covered item ___; excluded connected equipment ___ | Supplier and reviewing agency | Rejects partial marks as whole-line evidence |
| Field-evaluation eligibility | Recognized agency ___; accepted scope/drawing revision ___; units/stages ___ | Evaluation agency and importer | Establishes a usable alternative route |
| Electrical record baseline | Drawings/BOM/revision ___; equipment supply datum ___ | Equipment electrical designer | Supports review of the actual construction |
| Alterations and total cost | Work owner ___; agency/alteration/reinspection costs ___ | Supplier, agency and finance lead | Compares routes through the same release event |
| Final release and change control | Final unit/label IDs ___; closed findings/release record ___; change reviewer ___ | Importer and responsible agency | Prevents use of an unresolved or changed configuration |

Reject either offer when its claimed approval stops short of essential connected equipment and no accepted completion route covers the gap. Where both routes cover the same line and meet the required release date, choose the lower complete quoted cost through that release. If only one route meets those conditions, price that eligible route against the purchase budget. A low initial machine price does not compensate for an unassigned approval or alteration scope.

If destination assembly changes the reviewed arrangement, refer that change to the appropriate agency and designer before treating the earlier result as covering the installed configuration. Retain the final approval records with the equipment identity and current drawings. Electrical product approval remains distinct from site electrical installation and the machine's separate mechanical and operating requirements.

## Two hypothetical approval mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): a panel label becomes a whole-line promise.** A buyer accepts a PANEL ONLY record on the control cabinet as approval of the connected former and powered feed unit. The purchase contains no accepted review for those items. Reopen the equipment boundary and obtain complete-equipment certification or an eligible agency-accepted evaluation route before the required Ontario release.

**Mistake (hypothetical): destination additions miss the review.** An importer purchases an evaluated forming assembly, then adds a separately sourced powered feeder outside the agency's original drawing. The team treats the original result as approval of the expanded combination. Send the revised boundary to the responsible agency and designer, allocate any required work and obtain the applicable release records for the actual arrangement.
