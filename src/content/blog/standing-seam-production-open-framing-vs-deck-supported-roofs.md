Choose tooling for the standing-seam product accepted on the customer's actual support assembly. Count open-framing and deck-supported orders together only when the responsible product and design parties support the same manufactured panel for both. Otherwise buy the distinct product package each market requires, or purchase the exceptional panels externally while investing in the supported repeat range.

The purchase is for weathering roof panels, not for manufacturing structural deck. The support beneath those panels changes the applicable roof assembly and product evidence. A vertical seam, an architectural label or a similar profile photograph cannot establish that relationship.

## Support is a product-selection input

Open framing presents the specified roof product with discrete supporting members. A deck-supported arrangement has an identified substrate and associated roof layers. The designer supplies the actual arrangement, including relevant support references, attachment details and other project requirements. Procurement should not choose support spacing or substitute a substrate to suit an available former.

Avoid absolute category shortcuts. [MBCI's LokSeam product page](https://www.mbci.com/products/lokseam/) expressly identifies its named panel for open framing or a solid substructure. Its [substrate guidance](https://www.mbci.com/blog/metal-roofing-substrates/) distinguishes support arrangements and product needs. These examples show why the actual product definition matters; they are not an approval for another panel or an ARCLIFT tooling configuration.

Ask the roof-product authority to identify the panel and complete assembly covered by its decision. Where the same section serves different arrangements, retain the applicable assembly records separately. A common panel outline does not make every clip, supporting layer or attachment condition common.

## Separate the customer order book before specifying the line

Sort confirmed demand by actual roof product and support arrangement. Record which orders the proposed tooling/material combination is authorised and accepted to serve. Keep an exceptional deck-supported requirement outside an open-framing production forecast unless its product route is also established.

This can lead to one common manufactured panel with separately controlled assembly scopes, two distinct tooling/product families, or one owned range plus purchased panels for the remainder. Choose among those outcomes using the accepted products and complete cost, rather than assuming two support descriptions always need two machines.

If a proposed common product changes the customer's selected section or assembly, obtain the responsible technical decision before counting those orders as demand. A cheaper standard tool set is useful only for orders that can actually receive its output.

## Distinguish panel manufacture from roof acceptance

A forming trial checks the output within its agreed equipment, material, sample and inspection scope. It can establish whether the required manufactured features are achieved under those conditions. Acceptance of the roof on its stated support is a separate product/project decision.

Keep the two records connected rather than substituting one for the other. For example, retain the panel sample's section revision and material beside the product authority's assembly reference. When the product changes, identify which manufacturing and assembly records must be revisited.

## Buy the range your organisation can support

For a first line, a controlled repeat product with an accepted market can be a better defined purchase than an unsupported promise to serve every support arrangement. Obtain actual offers for exceptional finished panels and compare that mixed supply route with adding another product family.

Expanding the range needs product records, tooling, material stock, component supply and competent technical support. Add these costs to the machinery comparison. Do not count the entire cost of product development against one option while describing the other as ready simply because its seam looks familiar.

## Two hypothetical support-category failures

**Failure case: a visual seam match substitutes for the required support application.** A buyer counts an open-framing order in the line forecast using a similar-looking panel accepted only for a different defined assembly. Obtain the actual product decision or buy the specified panels instead of treating appearance as evidence.

**Failure case: a shared section hides changed components.** The producer accepts two support categories under one sales description but supplies the same unspecified clips and detail package for both. Keep the designer's assembly references and component schedules distinct, even where one manufacturing section is supported for both orders.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the supported product range

1. Customer roof sections identifying actual supports and substrate arrangements.
2. Controlled panel sections with product identities and measurement datums.
3. Material, finish and required manufacturing-input specifications.
4. Applicable attachment details and component schedules for each assembly.
5. Product-authority decisions and references to the exact supporting evidence.
6. Confirmed orders separated by accepted product and support application.
7. Manufacturing trial, sample and inspection requirements with accepting parties.
8. Workshop layout, utilities, destination and full tooling/component supply boundary.
