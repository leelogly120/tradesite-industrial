An inlet arrow does not identify which material face a coil supply presents to the proposed former. The coil supplier's winding description, the profile owner's face requirement and the equipment provider's input convention need to describe the same intended material condition. The buyer's useful output is a material-to-inlet correspondence record: three attributable descriptions with any mismatch marked, without assuming that the material or machine can be reversed.

## Identify the supplied coil condition at the inlet

### Ask what the material supplier's description refers to

Request the actual material supplier's explanatory record for the proposed coil. Ask it to identify the object condition, face labels and winding convention it describes. A sales photograph of a coil may show wrapping or an outer surface without identifying the face that matters to the panel requirement. Keep the supplier's own description instead of replacing it with procurement's interpretation of clockwise or left-hand. The buyer should obtain a record that the equipment provider can read against its inlet requirement, not invent a universal winding vocabulary from an unrelated illustration.

### Identify the former provider's proposed input condition

Ask the actual former provider which material-face and feed-side information its proposal assumes at the identified inlet. Retain the machine arrangement reference and the provider's explanation. An inlet location alone does not answer that material-state question. The provider may need further coil information before it can respond; ask which record is missing. Do not copy an assumption from one machine into another offer because both serve the same panel profile. The correspondence record needs the actual intended input and actual proposed equipment basis, with unanswered fields left visibly open.

### Carry the product face requirement into the correspondence

Ask the profile owner to mark the required face on the controlled panel section. Keep that product requirement beside the material supplier's face description and the equipment provider's proposed input condition. These three records serve different purposes. A completed inlet arrow can coexist with an unresolved face correspondence, and a documented material face can coexist with an unconfirmed machine proposal. The buyer should show which link is missing rather than declaring the entire feed arrangement accepted. The wider site plan remains a referenced input from the output-direction discussion, not the artifact being recreated here.

## Connect material-face evidence to machine references

### Request the material supplier's winding convention

Ask the actual material supplier to identify how its winding and face information should be read. Preserve its explanatory drawing or document reference. If a commercial description uses terms such as inner or outer face, ask what object and condition those words describe. Do not translate an unclear label into a machine instruction. Procurement needs an attributable description of the intended input so the forming provider can review it. It does not need an improvised rule about which way to move, rotate or unpack a delivered coil.

### Tie the intended panel face to the profile section

The profile owner should identify the required face on the controlled section. Connect that requirement to the material supplier's actual face labels and the former provider's proposed entry convention. Where the three descriptions cannot be reconciled, send the marked discrepancy to the relevant parties. The buyer should not decide that the material can be reversed or that a different coating face is acceptable. The [profile-data guide](/blog/roof-panel-profile-material-tooling-data/) provides the broader product definition; this record carries its face requirement into the orientation discussion.

### Retain uncertainty rather than drawing a guessed solution

A useful preliminary plan may show a confirmed machine outlet and an unresolved material face. Mark that specific gap. Do not redraw the whole arrangement merely to make the arrows appear continuous. Ask the provider which missing record prevents a response and whether its offer assumes a particular material condition. The difference between an established fact and a proposal assumption matters commercially: a bidder may have priced an arrangement that is conditional on material information the buyer has not yet secured. Clarifying it early avoids comparing differently conditioned offers as though they were identical.

## Compare a material-face match with an unresolved inlet assumption

### Work through a hypothetical changed coil supply

Imagine a project keeps the same former and site outlet direction but proposes a different coil supply. The original supplier's face and winding explanation was linked to the equipment provider's response. The alternative is labelled only as the same coated material, without the corresponding face description. These are hypothetical circumstances. The unchanged machine layout does not answer the missing coil-specific question. Ask the new material supplier for its record and the former provider whether its input assumption addresses that condition. Do not conclude that the coil can be turned, rewound or fed from another side to make the documents appear consistent.

### Use a three-party material-to-inlet sheet

| Correspondence field | Documented original input | Proposed alternative input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material identity | Actual supplier's record | Alternative's own record, not the original label |
| Winding description | Issuer's stated convention and object condition | Obtain the new supplier's explanation |
| Face labels | Link to the required panel face | Ask the product owner to resolve ambiguous labels |
| Former inlet basis | Provider's response to the original input | Request response to the alternative's actual condition |
| Unresolved dependency | Retain any original limitation | Identify the precise missing correspondence |

The interface trade-off concerns evidence about the intended input, even when the site plan stays unchanged. A clear machine arrow does not compensate for an undocumented face convention in the material offer.

### Separate an alternative supply from a proposed remedy

If the new supplier's record reveals a different stated condition, ask the product and equipment parties what their actual proposals address. Do not transform a purchasing alternative into a handling remedy. A provider might need additional information or might propose another scope for review, but this article establishes neither response as available. Keep the material alternative, its document basis and the corresponding equipment reply together. A revised site-output drawing only becomes relevant if an actual provider proposes a physical change; the earlier output-direction guide owns that separate comparison. The coil-specific result here is a known correspondence or a precisely identified remaining gap.

## Return an orientation question the supplier can answer

### Send the linked six-part orientation package

- Issue a top view with an identified building reference, machine inlet, cutter end and arrangement revision.
- Attach the material supplier's winding and face explanation for the intended coil condition.
- Mark the required panel face on the controlled profile drawing and identify its decision owner.
- State the outlet height and roof receiving zone without equating them to equipment-platform height.
- Show relevant site access, ground and support-space constraints for the separate lift-layout review.
- Identify destination documentation, transport state and power/control interface questions affected by the proposed arrangement.

Ask the respondent to mark the exact reference it cannot reconcile. This produces a more answerable enquiry than asking whether the machine can feed either way without an identified input or assembly.

### Request the response to the actual material condition

For a missing winding or face explanation, ask the material supplier to provide its attributable record. For a known difference from the former provider's assumed input, request a configuration-specific reply rather than suggesting a handling action. Keep the material and machine questions separate until the providers connect them. A proposed alternative may remain conditional while that information is obtained. The [line-interface guide](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) takes any actually proposed assembly change into the mounting review; the material-to-inlet sheet does not decide that a machine modification is required or that another supply is equivalent.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Identify a direction brief that may not fit

A proposal may not fit the project when its identified inlet and outlet cannot be reconciled with the required product and site arrangement. An enquiry may also remain too uncertain when winding or face information is unknown. This guide authorises no coil reversal, threading, machine rotation, electrical change or handling method. A reference photograph cannot settle handedness for an unseen configuration. Keep the unresolved relationship explicit and ask the actual suppliers and project reviewers for the configuration-specific response needed before committing to the layout.

## FAQ

#### Is an inlet arrow enough to identify the coil condition?

No. It identifies a machine direction, while the material supplier's winding and face information describe the intended input. Connect those records to the required panel face and obtain the actual former provider's response.

#### Can the buyer decide to reverse the coil?

This article provides no such instruction. Send the actual winding, face and machine information to the responsible providers. They must address the identified mismatch within the approved project and equipment scope.

#### Does an unchanged material name preserve the earlier response?

Do not assume it. A proposed supply needs its own identifiable face and winding description. Ask the actual equipment provider whether the earlier assessment basis addresses the new input, rather than transferring it through a trade name.

#### Which document should control the panel face?

Ask the responsible product owner to identify the controlled section and its face requirement. The material and equipment documents then need to address that same requirement; a sales photograph cannot choose it for the project.
