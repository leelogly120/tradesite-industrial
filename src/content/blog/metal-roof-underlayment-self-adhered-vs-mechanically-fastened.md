Choose a mechanically fastened synthetic underlayment when the approved deck-supported roof permits it in the main field. Include the specified self-adhered zones in that bid. Buy full-deck self-adhered coverage where the roof specification requires it and the installation conditions meet the product instructions. If both build-ups qualify, award the lower installed cost that meets the planned panel start. Ask the roof designer to issue the marked coverage plan and roof section first.

## The bid starts at the top of the deck

This comparison applies to roofs designed with continuous decking below the metal sheets. The deck surface, existing materials if retained, panel system and project requirements determine which layers can be ordered. Check the selected metal, surface finish and roof build-up together. The system detail and written product instructions establish whether the proposed membrane contacts the metal, lies below another layer or needs an approved separation.

The open strip beside the sheeting below illustrates an installation boundary. For your deck-supported roof, mark the boundary on the bay plan before estimating how much deck and underlayment the roofer can open at once. Use it to schedule inspections and temporary protection.

McElroy Metal distinguishes mechanically fastened and self-adhered synthetic underlayments. The first uses the fasteners specified for the product and substrate. The second bonds to the deck with an adhesive backing. McElroy describes self-adhered use in particular areas such as eaves and valleys as well as full coverage. Copy the approved boundaries into the takeoff and measure overlaps separately. ## Compare the work each route brings to the roof

For a mechanically fastened bid, obtain its fastener schedule, names of compatible accessories and treatment at overlaps. Price the fastener delivery, placement and inspection with the rolls. If the design uses self-adhered strips elsewhere, identify whose scope includes the interface between products. Require the bidder to show those quantities as distinct line items, including the overlap consumed in each.

For a self-adhered bid, price deck cleaning, repairs or drying, primer where specified, and the approved treatment where the membrane meets adjoining materials. Obtain the application temperature conditions and in-service temperature suitability as separate product checks. A product chosen for hot service still needs an installation window that matches its instructions. GCP's U.S. Grace Ultra data sheet specifies a clean, dry, continuous structural deck and calls its product a vapor barrier. If an alternative changes the membrane's vapor resistance or coverage, send the complete build-up to the envelope designer for acceptance. Ask the membrane and panel suppliers for written compatibility at each direct contact in that build-up. [GCP Grace Ultra U.S. data sheet](https://gcpat.com/sites/default/files/pdf/current/resource/GCPAT_grace_ultra_us_10726.pdf)

Compare installed costs for the same released roof area and panel-start date. For each product, take off its sloped area from the marked plan, account for specified laps and offcuts, and convert usable coverage into ordered rolls. Separate the named fasteners, primer and preparation from that material subtotal. Then add crew time, inspections and temporary protection so each total carries the same responsibilities.

## Use the exposure window to schedule panel release

Get the manufacturer's maximum exposure period for the exact product supplied in the destination market. Show an intended cover date for each bay in the roof program. The exposure period is a limit on leaving that product uncovered. Ask the roof contractor to price temporary weather protection for the project's planned sequence and site conditions. GCP publishes product-specific exposure limits and calls for checking laps before covering after prolonged exposure. Use the chosen manufacturer's requirements for inspection and correction. [GCP Technical Letter 3 on exposure](https://gcpat.com/en/solutions/products/grace-ice-water-shield-roofing-underlayment/tl-0003-exposure-guidelines-roofing)

Give the panel crew the latest released bay and its installation date. If deck preparation runs late, switch to another released bay with compatible panels, reschedule forming or use a protected holding area included in the site plan. Attach that response to the bid so the site team can price the next production run.

## Two mistakes that increase installed cost

**Mistake one.** A hypothetical buyer accepts a self-adhered alternate using a high service-temperature rating as proof that the planned morning application is suitable. The deck conditions fall outside the product instructions and the installer cannot start as booked. Obtain the separate application limits and establish a workable installation period before committing the forming crew.

**Mistake two.** In a second example, an estimator quotes fastened synthetic by gross roof area and leaves self-adhered transition zones out of the job total. Each of two installers expects the other to include compatible products and lap work at those boundaries. Issue the grid coverage and transition scope to both before choosing the bid.

## RFQ for the underlayment and panel schedule

1. Released roof sections and top-of-deck materials or repairs by bay.
2. Roof system, exact membrane and approved materials touching each face.
3. Coverage drawing for field material and each self-adhered area.
4. Lap, attachment, primer and accessory scope by bidder.
5. Application conditions, service suitability and permitted exposure interval.
6. Deck-release dates, inspection records and temporary protection responsibilities.
7. Panel section, material, lengths and quantities by workfront.
8. Forming and receiving plan, measured outlet height datum and first usable panel date.
