Keep segregated loose scrap collection when its contracted service, occupied space and full handling cost fit the factory's waste stream. Buy a suitable metal baling system only when measured eligible volumes, the recycler's acceptance and the complete cell cost justify ownership. Select the collection route for actual offcuts and rejected panels, not for an assumed waste volume derived from the former's maximum speed. Obtain loose and baled receiving offers for the measured waste stream before valuing the investment.

Reduce recurring production loss, then evaluate collection equipment for the remaining measured scrap. Investigate repeated rejects and excessive start-up loss through the [accepted-panel yield decision](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-material-yield-cost/). A baler is a waste-handling investment; its business case should not reward the factory for making defective product.

## Measure waste by material and shape

Record actual scrap over representative orders. Separate steel, aluminium and any specialist metals according to the recycler's accepted classifications. Identify long edge trim, short offcuts, rejected full panels and other process residues. Keep packaging and unrelated waste as separate streams.

For each stream, record mass, physical envelope, collection frequency, internal transfer and storage occupation. A bulky lightweight shape and a dense small offcut can create different handling costs at the same mass. The machinery specialist needs the actual dimensions and material condition to propose a suitable metal-processing system.

[ENERPAT's sheet-metal offcut baler description](https://www.enerpatrecycling.com/Sheet-Metal-Off-Cuts-Baler-pd540852858.html) identifies a metal-specific compression equipment category. Use the measured metal stream to request an offered input schedule and a complete metal-baling cell from the specialist. Obtain a configuration-specific suitability and safety proposal for the actual factory waste; a cardboard or film baler is not a generic substitute.

## Ask the recycler what it will actually take

Obtain written offers for loose and proposed baled material using the same metal classification. Identify accepted bale conditions, contamination limits, packaging, weighing point, collection equipment and payment basis. Have the recycler identify any sorting or rejection cost outside the offer. Use actual current quotations for value; do not assign an invented premium to compressed scrap.

Where the recycler already provides collection containers or a compaction service, include that offered arrangement in the comparison. The factory can buy a service without owning the processing asset. Price transport frequency, minimum collections and the cost of keeping the production area clear.

## Build a complete cell, then compare totals

For ownership, include suitable machine, installation, utilities, material presentation, collection of bales, guarding and access provisions defined by the provider, maintenance, competent staff and factory space. Have the provider and site team define the supported handling arrangement. Procurement should not invent loading methods or operating instructions.

For loose service, include containers, internal transfer, floor occupation, collections, material sorting and any contract minimums. Use the same measured waste stream and evaluation period. Credit scrap receipts once, using the actual recycler offer for each route. Do not count a higher assumed price and a lower assumed transport frequency without evidence for both.

Consider variability. A busy project can generate a temporary peak of long offcuts while regular work produces a smaller stream. Ask how each route serves the peak and the quiet periods. A fixed asset has ownership costs between jobs; a collection contract has its actual minimum obligations. The comparison should show both.

## Keep product traceability outside the scrap shortcut

Assign who decides a panel is rejected and whether a usable remnant returns to stock. Retain the required records for the customer and quality system before waste disposition. Where different metals share a factory, the recycler's classification and the product's material identity both need clear segregation.

If loose collection already serves the measured stream at the lower complete cost, retain it and improve layout or collection frequency through the actual contract. Buy baling when a suitable cell and accepted outgoing material produce a funded advantage. For a small factory, a specialist collection service remains a complete decision rather than an incomplete step toward equipment ownership.

## Two hypothetical scrap investments that fail

**The quotation is for the wrong waste.** A producer buys a convenient general packaging press without a metal-specific input proposal. Its actual long, formed offcuts fall outside the documented duty. Supply representative waste dimensions and obtain the specialist's explicit supported-input schedule before buying.

**Compressed scrap has no buyer.** Finance credits an assumed bale premium, but the intended recycler quotes another class or rejects the proposed form. Obtain both receiving offers before assigning recovery value to the ownership case.
