A coated-coil enquiry needs to explain what the finished panel should look like, where that appearance matters and which material the buyer actually intends to supply. A colour name and a photograph of a good-looking panel leave important questions open. The practical output is a face-and-sample package that lets the tooling provider respond to the same cosmetic requirement the project intends to judge.

A roll-forming lift positions a compatible roof-panel former near the intended receiver. The lift does not establish coating compatibility. Use the [profile and material guide](/blog/roof-panel-profile-material-tooling-data/) for the complete product definition and the [forming-equipment reference](/products/#roll-forming-machines) for the related machine discussion. Here the narrower question is how to communicate finish expectations before comparing tooling replies.

## Identify the surface the buyer intends to accept

### Mark the exposed face on the controlled section

Ask the roof-product owner to mark the intended exposed face on the actual profile drawing. Continue that marking onto the sample identification rather than relying on which way a photograph happens to be held. Identify any face that is concealed in the intended assembly but still has a stated finish requirement. A reviewer can then distinguish an appearance question about the visible surface from a different concern about another part of the product. The drawing owner should resolve disagreement over those requirements before a machine proposal is treated as comparable.

### Name the areas with different cosmetic expectations

The buyer may care differently about a broad visible area, a return or an edge that interfaces with another component. Show those areas on an annotated section or photograph and say who supplied the expectation. Do not write a new acceptance rule merely because a mark looks noticeable in a close-up. Ask the project party to define what it will assess and where. The useful distinction is between a requested observable feature and a judgement already made under agreed conditions; a tender sketch supplies the former, not the latter.

### Keep colour identification separate from material evidence

An approved colour reference can communicate the intended appearance without identifying the coil substrate, ordered finish or condition supplied for a tooling review. Keep the actual material order and coating description beside that visual reference. If the project colour is fixed but the material supplier remains undecided, say so explicitly. The provider can then explain which part of its response remains conditional. A familiar trade description should not silently become proof that a particular machine has previously processed the same material in the same condition.

## Build a sample package with a clear purpose

### Distinguish a colour sample from a forming sample

Label a sample according to the question it can answer. A flat colour sample may explain the desired appearance. A formed sample may illustrate a particular surface area or edge, but its material and production history still matter. A photograph can identify the location of a concern while leaving physical observations unresolved. Rather than asking the provider to approve an unidentified object, write a short purpose statement for each sample. Keep missing history visible so a present cosmetic requirement is not confused with evidence of earlier machine performance.

### Connect the sample to the intended supply

Record the material supplier's identification and the project order reference where available. Ask which sample actually represents the intended coil and which is a visual comparison only. If the intended supply changes after a sample discussion, return the affected material and finish information to the tooling provider. This does not predict whether tooling changes will be needed. It prevents the buyer from treating a response about one input as an unconditional answer about another. Retain the earlier sample identity so the difference between the two requests remains understandable.

### Agree what a proposed observation would establish

Ask the parties to identify the sample condition, viewing area, relevant record and person deciding the requested cosmetic observation. Leave the actual assessment method to those responsible for agreeing it. A video or photograph supplied after a trial should identify what it shows and what it leaves outside its scope. The [acceptance guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-fat-sat-acceptance-checklist/) owns the wider trial agreement. This package should carry the specific finish question into that discussion rather than inserting an invented tolerance, lighting arrangement or acceptance result into the equipment enquiry.

## Compare two replies against the same finish request

### Work through a clearly hypothetical finish purchase

Imagine a roof contractor requiring a named visible face and a separate appearance expectation at a return. These are hypothetical requirements, not an ARCLIFT case. Provider A replies with a formed sample whose material identity is documented. Provider B supplies a colour photograph and a general statement about coated sheet. A's sample may be more relevant to the stated forming observation, while B's photograph may address only colour. Neither establishes the current offered machine's complete capability. The buyer should request the missing connection rather than treating both attachments as equivalent finish evidence.

### Expose the actual comparison in one worksheet

| Finish question | Reply with identified formed sample | Reply with visual reference only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which face is shown? | Link sample face to the project section | Ask for an unambiguous face reference |
| Which material is represented? | Retain the accompanying material record | Request the actual intended material basis |
| Which appearance area is addressed? | Identify the observed region | Separate visible colour from unshown features |
| Which equipment does evidence describe? | Request the machine and trial connection | Do not infer a machine from the photograph |
| What remains outside the reply? | Identify unobserved conditions and areas | Request the missing forming-evidence proposal |

The trade-off is the relevance and completeness of evidence, not an automatic preference for a larger document pack. An identified sample that addresses the wrong face can be less useful than a concise, accurately scoped reply. Compare the requested observable feature with what the record actually covers.

### Separate finish approval from the rest of the roof purchase

A project party may accept a colour while leaving formed-surface expectations unresolved. Conversely, a provider may address a specific forming observation while the roof-product owner has not accepted the complete product. Keep those decisions named separately. The official <a href="https://store.astm.org/a0792_a0792m-25a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ASTM A792/A792M catalog record</a> concerns a particular aluminum-zinc-coated steel-sheet specification; it is a material-standard context, not a certificate for the buyer's coil or a cosmetic approval. Use the actual project specification rather than borrowing a standard merely because it mentions coated sheet.

## Send a finish-specific purchase brief

### Include the six decisions that change the reply

- Attach the controlled profile and mark each face or region with a distinct finish requirement.
- Identify the actual coil material order, coating description and any unresolved supplier selection.
- Give every sample a purpose, identity and known relationship to the proposed supply.
- State which cosmetic observation the project wants the provider to address and who will agree its basis.
- Show the former outlet height, roof geometry and site access context for the separate lift-interface discussion.
- Identify destination documentation, transport assumptions, ground conditions and power information without treating them as finish evidence.

Keep the attachment order practical: marked requirement, material record, sample references, then the requested response. That sequence makes the question understandable even when the reader has not attended earlier discussions.

### Ask for a qualified answer about the actual proposal

Request a reply that names the proposed tooling or machine, the material and sample it addresses, the evidence being offered and any outstanding finish question. If a provider proposes another material condition, ask it to identify the departure rather than assuming it is interchangeable. Separate a price for an evidence activity from a promise of a result. The buyer can then decide whether to clarify the requirement, obtain another sample or seek a revised offer. Those are purchasing choices, rather than instructions for treating or processing coated surfaces.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise an enquiry that may not fit the available evidence

A finish-specific offer may not fit when the visible face is disputed, the intended material cannot be identified or the sample exists only as an unexplained photograph. More lift height cannot resolve those gaps. This article supplies no process settings, coating treatment, machine compatibility or roof-performance conclusion. Protective film is a separate material-state question, while the core material designation remains part of the broader profile brief. Keep the finish package focused on what the buyer wishes to observe and which provider is actually being asked to address it.

## FAQ

#### Does a colour sample prove that a former can process the coil?

No. It may communicate appearance, while machine suitability needs the actual material, proposed equipment and relevant review. Explain the sample's purpose so it is not treated as evidence of a forming result it never addressed.

#### Should both panel faces appear in the brief?

Identify the face relevant to each stated requirement. Where another face also has a project requirement, mark it explicitly. Do not assume the visible face in a sales photograph matches the project's intended exposed face.

#### Can a previous sample support a new material order?

Its relevance needs review against the changed input. Retain the previous identity and describe the new material rather than silently relabelling the sample. Ask which questions it still addresses and which require another response.

#### Is surface acceptance the same as roof-system approval?

No. An agreed surface observation addresses its named scope. The panel geometry, connections, roof assembly and installation decisions remain with the responsible project parties. A satisfactory photograph cannot establish those separate conclusions.
