Choose distributed rooflight units when the daylight design demonstrates the required distribution at the actual work planes. Choose continuous strips when they serve the accepted aisle or building layout and the selected rooflight system permits that arrangement. Release metal-panel marks after the daylight layout and roof interfaces are coordinated. Equal translucent area is not proof of equal useful light. For work spread throughout the hall, evaluate distribution over that whole working layout; an aisle-oriented daylight band needs its own accepted result outside the aisle.

The building buyer should first choose where daylight is needed, then select compatible specialist rooflights and the surrounding metal product. The metal-panel former makes its agreed sheet profile. Translucent units, their performance and their installation remain a separate specialist supply package.

## Put the working layout under both roof options

Give the daylight designer the occupied zones, working planes, internal heights, racks, partitions and major equipment. Include planned solar panels, plant and roof obstructions. Obtain comparisons for the same operation and stated environmental assumptions rather than comparing two drawings from different stages of the project.

The [Eurolux rooflighting guideline](https://www.eurolux-eu.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/2018_06_11_eurolux_guideline_daylighting._final-002.pdf) discusses distributed individual rooflights and continuous arrangements, including their different applications for illumination and orientation. Use those distinctions to request a project-specific daylight layout. The appointed daylight and roof designers select the opening sizes and spacing for the actual building and chosen rooflight system.

The roof edge photograph below shows a metal field adjoining exposed framing. Mark the proposed daylight zones on that roof plan and identify the supporting construction below. Each opening affects both the specialist rooflight order and the surrounding metal panels.

## Distributed units: buy the light pattern and repeated interfaces

Distributed units suit a design that needs daylight spread across selected occupied areas. Obtain the actual analysis and rooflight product schedule. Price the repeated supports, joints, weathering, installation positions and inspection work specified for that arrangement.

Coordinate the units with the metal panel seams, supporting lines and service zones. For in-plane rooflights, use the actual matched product sections and joint details. For kerbed units, obtain the opening, kerb and surrounding roof construction from the responsible designers. Distribution is a spatial choice; it does not define one universal rooflight assembly.

Include future access to inspect, clean or replace the nominated products. Keep the building owner's care requirements and the access designer's arrangement in the retained package. A layout that meets the initial daylight brief still needs a deliverable maintenance route.

## Continuous strips: qualify the actual product arrangement

A strip layout can follow an accepted aisle, ridge or other designed zone. Price the complete continuous arrangement with its supporting construction and product-specific joints. Check the selected provider's layout requirements before drawing an unbroken translucent band.

[Kingspan's Day-Lite trapezoidal rooflight publication](https://www.kingspan.com/content/dam/kingspan/kip-west/rooflight-systems/kingspan-day-lite-trapezoidal-rooflight-eu122-en-ie.pdf) includes specific layout restrictions for that named system, including joint relationships. This is why a proposed continuous strip needs a real product arrangement. Do not transfer those exact restrictions to a different rooflight; obtain its provider's accepted layout instead.

Where the accepted strip includes opaque interruptions or other special details, show them on both the rooflight and panel schedules. Compare the actual supplied pattern with the daylight analysis; revise any idealised glazed rectangle absent from the provider's accepted product offer.

Use a common roof grid for every drawing. State the working-plane datum in the daylight brief and finished-piece datums in the panel schedule. They serve different decisions and should not be replaced by an overall building-height figure.

## Turn the selected layout into a coordinated kit

Create a zone schedule listing metal pieces, specialist rooflight units, supports and separate details. Preserve the identity of each product and revision. If a metal profile changes, send the changed section back through the rooflight interface review before releasing replacement tooling or units.

For elevated forming, coordinate the sequence with the roof receiver and rooflight installation. Retain separate handling and personnel-access scopes. A forming lift raises a compatible former; it is not a translucent-sheet production line or the complete crew-access package.

## Two hypothetical layout errors

**Mistake: equal area is accepted without the working layout.** A buyer selects a single rooflight strip over the main aisle by total translucent area, while the occupied work benches lie behind tall racks outside that aisle. Re-run the specialist comparison using the actual occupied layout before ordering the rooflight and metal-panel packages.

**Mistake: the strip ignores product joint rules.** A drawing shows continuous adjoining units, but the selected product requires a different arrangement. Obtain the provider's accepted layout and update supports, opaque interruptions and metal piece marks before release.

## Eight attachments for the metal-line RFQ

1. Accepted daylight brief, work planes and analysis assumptions.
2. Selected distributed or strip layout on the roof grid.
3. Internal and rooftop obstruction plans.
4. Rooflight identity and provider's accepted joint arrangement.
5. Metal-panel profile, material and matched interface details.
6. Panel marks, lengths, quantities and separate components.
7. Delivery groups, production location and installation releases.
8. Destination, utilities and named design/supply owners.
