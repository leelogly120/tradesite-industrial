**Choose a commission-agent arrangement when an identified supplier contracts with the equipment customer and your business is funded to perform its agreed sales role. Choose resale distribution when you can fund equipment purchases and deliver your own written customer obligations.** Obtain actual partner terms before treating either route as available. A local contact, a catalogue and an attractive sales forecast do not establish an agency or distribution agreement.

For roof-forming equipment, the choice affects more than margin. The customer needs a compatible former, lifting arrangement, transport boundary and attributable technical proposal. Someone must contract for that equipment, collect the required inputs, approve the commercial offer and resource the commitments that continue after delivery. Put those responsibilities beside the proposed income before choosing a business model.

## Follow the customer contract and the equipment invoice

An agent introduces or develops sales under an agreed relationship. In the arrangement compared here, the equipment transaction remains between the named supplier and the end customer. The local business earns the remuneration stated in its agreement and performs the sales or support activities that agreement actually assigns.

A distributor buys equipment and resells it under its actual customer contract. The business therefore needs a funded purchasing route and a complete onward offer. Stocking adds a further commitment: a specific configuration occupies capital, space and care resources before its customer is known.

The UK government's [agent and distributor guidance](https://www.business.gov.uk/export-from-uk/learn/categories/prepare-sell-new-country/routes-to-market/when-use-agent-or-distributor/) explains this general distinction between selling on a supplier's behalf and buying products for resale. Use the signed agreement and relevant professional advice for the actual transaction. Commercial labels alone do not allocate every destination, support or customer obligation.

## Stock belongs to a product decision

A roof-forming lift is a configuration purchase. A truck-based proposal has a vehicle and integration boundary; a crawler proposal has its own carrier and site interfaces. The former's profile, material and output direction also need controlled records. Stock only after qualified enquiries show an identifiable recurring package and the business can carry that exact asset through storage, demonstration and eventual handover.

Agency avoids buying resale stock in the arrangement described above, but the local sales work still needs resources. Budget site-information collection, translation, travel, proposal coordination and customer communication where those tasks belong to the agent. Agree how that work is paid and what happens when a prospect changes the requirement or never places an order.

## Build the transaction before forecasting the margin

Use one representative equipment order to compare the models. Keep customer identities private in public marketing; use the actual proposed documents internally.

| Transaction field | Commission-agent entry | Distributor entry | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer contract | Named contracting supplier and offer reference ___ | Your contracting entity and onward offer reference ___ | Commercial lead; identifies whose equipment sale is proposed |
| Purchase funding | Agent's agreed working-cost budget ___ | Equipment payment dates and funding source ___ | Finance lead; tests the capital required before customer receipts |
| Remuneration | Actual fee/commission basis, trigger and payment terms ___ | Actual purchase/resale amounts and retained cost classes ___ | Commercial adviser; compares supported income rather than headline percentages |
| Equipment identity | Supplier's offered configuration and revision ___ | Purchased unit, stock identity and resale configuration ___ | Technical lead; prevents an unapproved substitution |
| Customer information | Assigned collection and transmission tasks ___ | Your required input and proposal-control process ___ | Sales engineer; establishes the cost of a complete technical sale |
| Delivery | Supplier/customer handovers and assigned local tasks ___ | Your onward handover and purchased delivery resources ___ | Logistics lead; exposes destination work outside upstream supply |
| Continuing commitments | Exact assigned activities and payment basis ___ | Customer promises, providers and funded resources ___ | Service lead; tests the offer after equipment delivery |
| End of relationship | Agreed treatment of open orders and customer records ___ | Treatment of owned stock and continuing customer commitments ___ | Management and advisers; prices the route out of the arrangement |

The practical output is a complete order, rather than a preferred label. Record payment events by reference to the proposed transaction documents. Keep an assumed future commission, resale payment or credit facility separate from an actual agreed term. Use the same order and period when comparing the two models.

## Technical sales authority needs its own boundary

Agree which party issues quotations, approves equipment descriptions and accepts changed requirements. An agent coordinating information should pass the attributable supplier response through the agreed channel. A reseller needs the upstream evidence required to support its own onward description. Neither role should turn a demonstration photograph into a load, compatibility or destination-approval claim.

If local completion is part of the customer offer, identify its provider and commercial scope. Chassis sourcing, integration, transport and site services can sit with different parties. The [importer supply-package guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-importer-distributor-supply-package/) develops those equipment boundaries. This decision establishes which business is actually purchasing and selling that package.

## Two hypothetical market-entry failures

**Hypothetical failure case: an introducer budgets reseller income.** A business forecasts the difference between an equipment purchase and a selling price, although its proposed agreement pays only for introductions and the supplier contracts directly. Rebuild the plan around the actual remuneration, payment trigger and sales work before investing in the market.

**Hypothetical failure case: a reseller prices the machine but omits its own completion promise.** The customer offer includes local integration and handover, while the upstream purchase ends with delivered modules. The expected margin contains no provider for the missing work. Price the onward obligations and assign real resources before accepting that equipment order.

Attach eight items to the RFQ and commercial discussion:

1. Proposed agent or reseller role and the intended contracting parties.
2. Destination market and supported customer/product families.
3. Representative panel drawings, materials and former requirements.
4. Intended lift architecture, vehicle/carrier and delivery boundaries.
5. Qualified demand supporting project orders or identified stock configurations.
6. Named technical-response, customer-information and quotation owners.
7. Local completion and continuing-support commitments with real providers.
8. Proposed transaction sequence, funding assumptions and required written partner terms.
