**Price directly mounted acoustic panels when the accepted product and assessed existing wall support that route. Buy a complete framed lining when the released design requires its support geometry, cavity or finished wall arrangement.** Compare substrate work, new construction and room handback together. An access platform is purchased for the retained installation tasks, not as a substitute for the wall-system decision.

This is a useful distinction for a contractor entering acoustic refurbishment. A quotation for absorbers on an accepted wall and a quotation for a new complete lining contain different work. Equal treated area does not make their preparation, service interfaces or access occupation equivalent.

## Decide what stays behind the new acoustic surface

Have the responsible specialists assess the existing wall and define the accepted acoustic treatment. Record which finishes, supports, services and openings remain. Identify defects requiring their own repair scope before the new product is installed. Do not turn an absorber quotation into an implied assessment of an unknown substrate.

For directly mounted panels, obtain the actual product and its nominated mounting arrangement. “Direct” should not be read as “no supporting components” or as permission to choose an arbitrary adhesive or fixing. The selected product's documents and project design establish the actual assembly.

For a framed lining, obtain the complete support, cavity, panel and junction details. The appointed designer accepts the complete wall outcome and identifies the service or construction changes required to deliver it. Keep that accepted assembly beside the acoustic surface specification in the purchase.

## Compare the same accepted wall result

Complete the worksheet for the project, using the acoustic designer's required outcome and the selected system proposals. Do not rank unrelated product area rates as if they deliver the same room.

| Scope decision | Fillable buyer entry | Owner and document/measurement basis | Purchase consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required treatment | Accepted system and room objective ___ | Acoustic/project designer; released specification | Defines the actual result being bought |
| Existing wall | Retained substrate ___; repair scope ___ | Building specialist; located condition survey | Decides whether direct installation is available |
| New support | Mounting/frame details ___; cavity ___ | System designer; wall sections | Establishes construction beyond panel supply |
| Finished geometry | Wall lines ___; returns/openings ___ | Surveyor; building grid and finished-room datums | Fixes quantities and usable room space |
| Services | Retained/altered items ___; responsibility ___ | MEP coordinator; coordinated elevation | Prevents hidden relocation and making-good costs |
| Installation objects | Panel/package dimensions ___; masses ___ | Product supplier; delivered component schedule | Defines separate handling requirements |
| Access positions | Work faces ___; levels ___; exceptions ___ | Installer; local supporting-floor datum | Tests the proposed personnel arrangement |
| Complete cost | Preparation ___; construction ___; access ___; handback ___ | Buyer; same accepted room boundary | Compares whole installation packages |

The result can be a mixed installation: accepted direct treatment in some areas and a specified lining in others. Give each zone its own product references and quantities. A blended rate is useful only when the underlying scope remains visible to installation and procurement.

## The new wall changes the space below it

Include final wall lines, projecting elements, retained furniture and other trades on the access layout. The platform must serve the task in the building condition expected during installation. The available space can change between support construction, panel placement and final inspection.

Request the supplier's actual working and delivery envelopes, task-load limits and support reactions. The responsible building parties assess those inputs against floor records and route geometry. Keep the material equipment and staging area on the same coordinated plan so the wall crew does not book two resources into one occupied space.

The low deck in the photograph remains a substantial machine even when its working surface is lowered. It helps explain why entry, waiting and departure belong in a room refurbishment quotation.

For a business considering buying a broad deck, use the awarded work pattern. Sustained adjacent tasks support a comparison with shared-deck access. Isolated panels in separated occupied rooms deserve a localized-access price as well. Base utilization on committed task periods and review suitability against their actual work positions.

## Two hypothetical retrofits with an incomplete wall order

**Failure case: the panel price omits substrate renewal.** A contractor selects direct treatment from a product quotation, then learns the retained wall needs specified repair work before the system can proceed. Complete the condition assessment and allocate that work before comparing installation totals or equipment periods.

**Failure case: a framed-lining budget ends at the panel face.** Another buyer excludes service relocation, perimeter details and final room acceptance. Its lower price hides work still required by the released design. Compare the same complete wall and update the access layout to the actual finished geometry.
