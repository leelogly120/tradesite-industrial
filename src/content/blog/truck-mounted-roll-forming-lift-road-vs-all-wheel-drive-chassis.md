Choose a road-oriented chassis when the accepted completed forming-lift vehicle serves the committed roads and site approaches at the lower complete ownership cost. Choose an actually offered all-wheel-drive build when the road build fails a committed approach and the accepted AWD package provides the lowest-cost qualifying supply route, or when AWD meets the same jobs at a lower complete total. Compare exact integrated vehicles over the same job calendar and ownership period before ordering.

The truck-mounted architecture and vehicle sourcing route are already chosen. This decision is between physical road-oriented and AWD builds for the same forming-lift task. Extra driven wheels concern traction. Ground bearing, stationary support, lift stability and permission to use the road remain separate requirements for either candidate.

## Let the recurring route earn the driveline

Separate public-road mileage from the final site approach and the stationary roof-forming position. Name the jobs actually committed to the fleet, the frequency of visits and the seasonal conditions involved. A photograph of one difficult site is a poor basis for a vehicle that spends most of its ownership period on different routes.

The current [Mercedes-Benz Trucks Arocs description](https://www.mercedes-benz-trucks.com/int/en/trucks/arocs.html) distinguishes road and construction chassis variants and identifies all-wheel-drive configurations. It demonstrates that complete vehicle architectures differ. The actual dealer and equipment integrator must nominate the destination-market builds being compared for this project; an outside OEM example is separate from ARCLIFT's supply schedule.

Price the resources needed to make each eligible vehicle serve the same jobs. Where the road build requires an accepted improved approach or an alternative supply arrangement, add that defined scope. Where AWD still requires route or ground works, retain them. The price of those resources belongs beside the vehicle that uses them.

*Match the vehicle approach and building-side position to issued dimensions for the complete proposed equipment.*

## A changed chassis is a changed integration

Use the exact market version, driveline, wheelbase and issued vehicle specification. A road chassis and an AWD proposal from the same model family are separate integration cases. The accepted lift/former layout must follow the actual frame, mounting, utility and envelope information for each build.

Compile the installed equipment and carried-item schedule without overlap. The lift, former, connecting structure, fluids, permanent accessories and journey-specific items belong to their documented states. Send the complete identities, masses and positions to the vehicle specialist. A nominal truck class or the number of visible wheels cannot replace that assessment.

The [payload-allocation review](/blog/truck-mounted-roll-forming-payload-allocation-review/) helps organize those inputs. Here they are prerequisites to choosing the physical build, rather than the purchase decision itself. If a candidate fails the required completed-vehicle assessment, exclude it before comparing the remaining commercial offers.

## Build a two-vehicle purchase record

Use one column of answers for each exact offer. Keep the evidence revisions attached so a later chassis substitution is visible.

| Input to fill for each build | Datum or evidence required | Responsible party | Decision affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Committed jobs ___ | Actual road/site routes, visit frequency and seasonal conditions | Fleet planner | Establishes recurring traction need |
| Exact vehicle ___ | Market version, driveline, wheelbase and equipment specification | Chassis dealer | Defines a real candidate build |
| Installed/carried scope ___ | Complete equipment identities, masses, positions from the vehicle specialist's datum and named states | Equipment providers/vehicle specialist | Qualifies the completed vehicle |
| Physical integration ___ | Issued frame, mounting, utility and transport/working envelopes | Chassis/body/lift integrators | Retains an accepted assembly |
| Site approach ___ | Measured route, clearances and ground/surface records | Site engineer/transport parties | Prices an eligible approach |
| Stationary work ___ | Pad datum, supporting footprint and actual roof outlet position | Site and lift specialists | Separates support from traction |
| Local ownership resources ___ | Actual service, tyres, driveline parts and production cover | Fleet maintenance lead | Funds supported availability |
| Same-period total ___ | Vehicle, integration, site resources, upkeep and alternatives | Buyer | Ranks qualifying complete builds |

An AWD label does not resolve an approach that the complete vehicle's dimensions cannot pass. Nor does reaching the work area settle the support conditions at the roof outlet. Keep those findings separate so a failed gate or support case receives the right remedy instead of a more expensive driveline.

## Compare cost where the vehicle actually spends its time

Road-oriented and AWD proposals need the same ownership period, committed work and accepted handover endpoint. Include the exact chassis, equipment integration, required site resources, maintenance, local parts support and defined replacement-production provision. Use the offers for those particular vehicles rather than an assumed percentage premium for AWD.

Daimler Truck's [September 2020 Arocs discussion](https://www.daimlertruck.com/en/newsroom/pressrelease/innovating-the-construction-industry-with-the-new-mercedes-benz-arocs-47528431) used occasional additional traction and permanent all-wheel drive to explain different duty choices and cost, mass and consumption trade-offs. That historical example is a reason to compare the whole offered build. It is not a current-market specification for a forming-lift truck. If a dealer proposes an intermediate traction system, evaluate that exact accepted build as another complete offer.

Obtain local support scope for the actual driveline and installed equipment. Match the quoted axle and transfer-system identities to the named workshop and parts offer, separately from engine servicing and lift/body support. Price those competent resources and the production alternative used during an interruption. A broad dealer-network statement is weaker purchasing evidence than coverage for the exact integrated build.

![A red elevated lifting structure stands beside an industrial building above a truck, coils and a wet site lane.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261102-elevated-former-coils.webp)

*Keep the supporting work area and material supply footprint in the same job plan as the vehicle approach.*

For repeated work at completed hard-surfaced sites, select the qualifying road build when it has the lower complete total for those jobs. For sites where the road proposal fails the accepted approach, compare the qualifying AWD build with an accepted alternative supply route through the same work. Select the lower complete qualifying total. Where neither vehicle meets the route and stationary requirements, change the access or production arrangement before ordering.

## Mistakes that survive an AWD badge

**Mistake (hypothetical): a late substitution inherits old conclusions.** A buyer changes to an AWD chassis whose issued integrated transport height differs from the reviewed road build. The buyer keeps the old gate-clearance decision and vehicle assessment. Reopen the complete integration and route case for the actual substituted build. Price any accepted route change and obtain the revised destination acceptance before ranking its delivered total.

**Mistake (hypothetical): site work disappears from the budget.** A contractor removes approach and support-area preparation because the vehicle is advertised for difficult terrain. Restore the competent route and ground assessments and their required resources. Compare the AWD proposal with the accepted road build plus its real site-access package through the same jobs.

## Eight RFQ attachments for comparable truck builds

1. **Forming task:** profile/material/length programme, roof geometry and named panel outlet heights measured from each supporting surface.
2. **Job calendar:** committed road transfers, visits, seasonal conditions and representative site records.
3. **Candidate chassis:** exact road/AWD builds, market version, destination and sourcing responsibility.
4. **Equipment account:** lift/former/accessory identities, actual masses, positions and road/working states.
5. **Integration package:** frame/mounting/power interfaces, envelopes and responsible acceptance parties.
6. **Site eligibility:** approach dimensions, supporting-ground records, outlet positions and required site works.
7. **Ownership provision:** actual driveline/equipment service, parts access and alternative production resources.
8. **Complete supply endpoint:** vehicle inclusion, destination power, transport, acceptance and same-period total.
