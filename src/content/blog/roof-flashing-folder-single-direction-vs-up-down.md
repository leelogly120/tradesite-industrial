Choose a single-direction folder when your actual roof-flashing range has an accepted bending and turning sequence that the available crew can complete economically. Buy an offered up-and-down folder when repeated opposite bends and long-blank turns constrain the order book, and a complete-batch trial shows that removing those actions justifies the additional machine cost. Start with released flashing drawings and measure loading through accepted stacking.

This decision comes after choosing to make flashings and selecting folding as the process. The same trim package can contain simple angles, returns and profiles with successive bends in opposite directions. A machine that makes one sample quickly can still occupy the workshop for too long when every blank requires several handling actions.

<!-- audit-section: buyer-intent -->
## Follow the part through its reversals

In the single-direction offer, identify the folds that require turning the workpiece to present the next bend. Record the support, clearance and people needed for those actions. In the up-and-down offer, identify which of those turns the actual machine removes and which rotations or repositioning remain. Keep the complete geometry in view: bending direction is one capability among the clamping, tooling and clearance conditions needed to finish a part.

[CIDAN's PRO Z description](https://us.cidanmachinery.com/products/cidan-up-down-folder-pro-z/) explains bidirectional bending and reduced material turning on that named machine. Its separate positioning description still refers to rotating sheets. [RAS's XXL-Center guidance](https://www.ras-online.de/en/know-how/) describes bending upward and downward without turning the sheet for those bends. Use those OEM examples to specify the bending-direction trial. Obtain the offered tooling, support and handling scope from the actual folder maker, then demonstrate your complete drawings on that configuration.

![A white shaped piece is held against the profiled edge of a blue roof sheet.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261109-panel-edge-sample.webp)
*Use the panel-edge relationship to set the adjoining-profile datum in the flashing drawing and trial.*

A flashing has to meet its released interface, rather than merely resemble a bent sample. Mark the reference edge, visible face and section orientation on the drawing. Where a handed version is needed, provide that drawing too. Otherwise, two demonstrations can produce opposite-handed parts and appear equally successful until the pieces reach the roof package.

<!-- audit-section: conditions -->
## Trial the order that causes the problem

Select representative committed orders, including the longest part, the frequent reverse-bend part and a finish-sensitive item. Use the actual material specification and blank preparation. Agree the dimensional datums, finish acceptance and permissible disposition of rejected pieces before either trial. The quality owner should apply the same criteria to both offers.

Record preparation, loading, bending, necessary repositioning, removal and stacking. Have the supplier define the permitted trial method and staffing. Include the supports and handling equipment that the purchased arrangement actually supplies. A demonstration with extra assistance or different supports answers a different purchase.

| Order input | Enter the actual datum or observation | Responsible owner and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Part identity | Drawing/revision ___; section orientation ___ | Design owner fixes the part compared |
| Material and face | Specification ___; protected visible face ___ | Quality owner sets finish acceptance |
| Blank | Length/width ___; preparation source ___ | Planner holds input stock constant |

Photograph or record the observations through the agreed project channel, retaining the drawing revision and trial conditions. Keep a setup problem separate from a geometry that the offered configuration cannot complete. The resulting comparison should identify the supported part family and the real production resources behind it.

<!-- audit-section: evidence-tradeoffs -->
## Value the time the business can use

Multiply observed complete-order occupancy by the real order mix. Include setup changes between profiles and the time spent clearing finished parts for the next batch. An up-and-down folder's advantage is commercially useful when it releases capacity for identified orders, reduces paid handling or avoids documented rework. Put the saved time on the production calendar before valuing additional sales. A shorter operation has little current value if another process still limits every delivery and the saved crew time has no useful destination.

Compare the whole installed purchase: machine, applicable tooling, supports, controls, training and actual destination completion. Add the continuing resources each offer requires over the same ownership period. Use the suppliers' quotations and your operating costs; avoid assigning a generic labor saving to every bend reversal.

![Metal coils on stands sit beside a blue processing frame and loose sheet pieces.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-coil-processing-workshop.webp)
*Keep stock handling and blank preparation on the same basis before valuing a faster folding sequence.*

Test the smaller purchase against a realistic peak period. Choose it when it completes the committed trim orders with the available crew and accepted handling arrangement at the better complete ownership cost. If a few unusual profiles drive the entire upgrade, price an actual outside supply route for those parts as well. Conversely, recurring difficult orders belong in the base comparison rather than being dismissed as exceptions.

A bidirectional folder can be justified by more than nominal bends per hour. Repeated long-part handling can occupy scarce people, require floor area or create finish rework. Enter the observed consequences separately so the same benefit is counted once. A single avoided reject should not become both a full replacement-material saving and an additional sale unless the actual order supports both entries.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical purchasing mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): timing only the bending beam.** A buyer ranks machines from a short programmed cycle while three people still turn and stack each long blank. The production promise fails during a full mixed order. Repeat the comparison from blank collection to accepted stack, with the actual crew and purchased supports.

**Mistake (hypothetical): assuming two directions finish every profile.** A buyer sees an opposite bend made without flipping and orders without demonstrating a deep return on its own drawing. The selected tooling and clearance do not complete that part. Require the full drawing sequence and accepted sample before crediting the order to either machine.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Eight attachments for the folder RFQ

Attach these items to the RFQ for each actual folder candidate:

1. **Part drawings:** released sections, handed versions and opposite-bend directions.
2. **Material brief:** specified stock, thickness range and visible-face requirements.
3. **Blank schedule:** actual lengths, widths and order frequencies.
4. **Production sequence:** loading, bending, turning, removal and stacking requirements.
5. **Current observations:** complete accepted-batch time, crew and rework records.
6. **Cell layout:** available space, support equipment and material transfer points.
7. **Offer boundary:** proposed machine, tooling, services and separately required resources.
8. **Acceptance sheet:** drawing datums, finish criteria and named trial signatories.
