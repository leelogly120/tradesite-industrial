Existing pipes and services can occupy the access space beside a ceiling installation strip even when they are not the objects being worked on. The buyer needs **a located service-corridor record tied to the actual task**, with uncertain geometry and service status preserved. A line labelled pipe on a drawing does not establish its diameter, supports, surrounding fittings or whether the project has reliable current information about it.

The [overhead-clearance guide](/blog/ceiling-platform-overhead-clearance-survey/) covers the broad survey process. This page narrows the output to services obstructing proposed access, rather than duct installation or maintenance as the task itself. It does not propose removal, isolation, relocation or work on any service.

## Define the service corridor beside the task

### Name the actual installation strip

Locate the ceiling work on the building plan and matching section. Identify the component or fixing face involved and the project stage when access is required. The service record should answer what is beside that task, rather than catalogue every pipe in the building. If the work itself concerns a duct or service face, describe that as a different task. This distinction prevents an equipment enquiry for ceiling installation from silently becoming a service-maintenance method or an instruction to reach through an existing installation.

### Record the extent relevant to the access question

Identify each service or associated feature by a project reference and show its relevant position in plan and section. A nominal centreline cannot represent every bracket, valve, hanger or surrounding object. State what the available drawing or observation actually describes. If fittings have not been located, mark that part of the corridor unknown. Do not add an assumed free zone around the line. The responsible project and equipment parties need the actual relevant geometry before considering their proposed arrangement.

### Separate the pipe from its status

Appearance does not establish whether a service is active, isolated, abandoned, unidentified or subject to another project restriction. Record who owns the service information and what status is actually known. This buyer record does not determine the status or authorize disturbance. Where information is absent, the next action is to obtain an attributable response through the project's responsible process. A smaller platform outline cannot close an unknown service question by making the drawing look less crowded.

## Keep service information attributable

### Connect drawings with current observations

Ask the project to identify the drawing revision, observation date and relevant limitations. A record labelled as-built may still differ from the current visible condition. Preserve a discrepancy as a specific question: for example, a branch or hanger present at the task location but absent from the supplied view. Do not resolve it by choosing whichever record leaves more space. The service owner or responsible project party must establish the information used for the assessment, while the buyer makes the missing relationship easy to identify.

### Relate the corridor to the local floor and task

Use the same project reference for service surfaces, task face and local floor. Ask the equipment provider to identify its actual proposed deck and task arrangement separately. Platform height and task-face elevation cannot be substituted for service geometry. The [crawler ceiling-platform family](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms) is one architecture for discussion, not proof of clearance near a particular pipe. Include task materials and tools in the project question rather than assuming that a machine-only outline represents everything occupying the proposed work area.

### Keep electrical and other specialist questions with their owners

An unidentified line must not become an ordinary geometric obstruction simply because its function is unclear. Ask the project to route status and applicable separation questions to the appropriate responsible owner. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE personnel-platform guidance</a> discusses overhead cables and other nearby hazards. That supplies general selection context, not an electrical clearance, isolation decision or classification of the candidate platform. This article provides no approach distance and does not instruct work near any live or unidentified service.

## Compare two obstruction records

### Keep the hypothetical task strip fixed

Consider a **hypothetical ceiling strip S** beside two recorded service features. Record A shows a pipe centreline P1 above the strip and reports one minimum ceiling height. Record B locates P1's relevant observed surfaces and identifies a separate fitting P2 beside the task, while marking P2's status information as unresolved. Neither record establishes platform fit. B makes the actual access question clearer: the pipe and fitting occupy different parts of the corridor, and the unknown service information remains a real project dependency.

### Separate missing geometry from unknown status

If A lacks the pipe's relevant extent, ask the project for that information. If B has useful geometry but unknown status, ask the responsible service owner for the applicable response. These are different actions. The trade-off is between a compact summary and a record that preserves the distinct questions affecting the proposed access relationship. A single low-point value cannot replace either answer. Do not infer that moving a drawing outline away from P2 is an approved alternative, or that a measured object is safe to approach because its dimensions are known.

### Ask what the actual platform response has considered

Give the equipment provider the same located service record and task information used by the project reviewers. Ask which actual configuration its response represents and whether it addresses the complete task objects rather than the platform alone. A reply can identify missing information or leave a task region outside its scope. The table below keeps P1, P2 and the task separate, without proposing to remove, isolate or reroute either service. Any such project change would require a new actual proposal and responsible authority.

| Record element | What the buyer can establish from attributable information | What still requires a response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task strip S | Its location, face and component requirement | Actual proposed access arrangement for that task |
| Pipe P1 | The specific surfaces and extent observed or shown by the relevant record | Any missing geometry and the applicable service information |
| Fitting P2 | Its separate location beside the intended task | Unknown status or ownership routed through the responsible project process |
| Minimum-height summary | One measurement at its named point | The full located corridor and task relationship that the summary omits |
| Candidate equipment view | The actual configuration identified by the provider | Whether the response considered people, tools and materials as well as the machine |

## Send the corridor question for configuration review

### Ask about the service beside the named task

Send a located plan and section with the record's actual limits. Keep sensitive service information in the agreed project channel, using anonymized references for a preliminary enquiry where appropriate. The equipment provider should be able to identify which relationship its proposal addresses and what site information is still needed. The record supports that discussion without claiming that geometry alone establishes clearance. The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) can then relate the proposed task to access, material areas and neighbouring activities beyond this particular service corridor.

- Locate the ceiling strip, task height and roof or building section for the intended work.
- Identify the relevant service surfaces, fittings and extent using attributable drawing or observation references.
- Connect local floor and service elevations to a common datum, with unobserved regions marked.
- State the task-material requirement and the proposed objects that need space beside the services.
- Identify access route, destination and transport context for the actual candidate configuration.
- Separate missing geometry, unknown service status and equipment-response gaps so each reaches its responsible owner.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize an enquiry that assumes services disappear

A proposal may not fit the actual task where it omits fittings, relies on unverified service status or assumes a pipe can be removed or relocated. A smaller outline does not make those assumptions acceptable. This guide provides no service-isolation instruction, removal method, clearance allowance or permissible task position. The responsible project parties must establish applicable conditions for the actual services, and the equipment response must address the resulting task relationship. Preserve an unresolved region instead of shading it as available space.

## FAQ

#### Is a service centreline enough for platform selection?

It can locate one reference but may omit relevant surfaces, fittings and support features. State exactly what the record describes and identify missing geometry. The actual task and candidate arrangement still need assessment against the complete relevant information. Do not extend a centreline into an assumed clear corridor or infer a permissible approach distance from it.

#### Does measured geometry establish service safety?

No. Geometry and service status answer different questions. Keep the responsible owner and applicable project response visible, especially where the function or status is unknown. This article does not establish isolation or authorize disturbance. A useful record allows both the geometry and specialist status questions to reach the appropriate parties without treating one answer as a substitute for the other.

#### Should the enquiry propose moving the pipe?

Not as an assumed remedy. If the project later develops an authorized service-change proposal, its actual scope and revised geometry require their own responsible review. The initial equipment question should describe the existing corridor and its unknowns. Do not make a preferred platform appear suitable by silently removing an object from the drawing or assuming enabling work is available.

#### How is this different from a duct-work access page?

Here the service is an obstruction beside the ceiling task, rather than the face being installed or maintained. The useful output locates that corridor and its information ownership. A task involving the duct itself needs its own face, material and service requirements. Keeping the distinction clear prevents a ceiling-access enquiry from implying a method for work on building services.
