**Pay cash when the complete acquisition leaves the business able to fund its committed material, installation and operating needs, and that funded route compares favourably with the actual finance offer. Choose hire purchase when its complete payments and equipment-use terms fit the business and preserving working funds justifies the additional commitment.** Defer the purchase when neither route funds accepted production readiness. Compare the same equipment and start-up scope before choosing how to pay.

This decision starts after the business has justified long-term acquisition. A contractor choosing temporary project hire should use the separate [buy-versus-rent comparison](/blog/roll-forming-lift-buy-vs-rent-project-decision/). Here the question is how an identified forming investment reaches the business while coil purchases, transport, people and other committed work remain funded.

## The machine amount is only one demand on cash

Build a dated acquisition budget from the actual equipment offer. Include the former, lifting assembly and any vehicle or carrier in the chosen scope. Add purchased integration, freight, destination work, premises preparation, installation and agreed acceptance. Then list material stock and operating resources needed before the first customer receipts.

Separate those entries by payment date and payer. A cash balance after paying for the former tells the finance lead little if the same balance must also fund a chassis, incoming coils and committed wages. Equally, a financed equipment offer is incomplete when it leaves essential installation or material purchases outside the available funds.

Use the business's actual funding policy and downside assumptions. Record which receipts are contracted, which depend on accepted deliveries, and which are sales prospects. Compare the routes across the same period through funded production and customer collection. Keep forecast sales distinct from available cash.

## Hire purchase needs an actual equipment and contract match

The [British Business Bank's asset-finance guidance](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/business-guidance/guidance-articles/finance/what-is-asset-finance) describes finance for machinery and plant and distinguishes hire purchase as a route toward ownership after agreed repayments. It also explains that ownership remains with the finance provider during repayment. This is general UK context; the actual funder's documents and applicable jurisdiction govern the proposed transaction.

Obtain a written offer identifying the equipment covered, deposit, payment schedule, fees, total payable and ownership endpoint. Establish which party buys from the equipment supplier and what documents the funder requires before releasing money. Have the business's qualified advisers assess the actual terms, affordability, accounting and tax treatment.

## Compare funding routes across the whole launch

Fill this worksheet with actual proposed amounts and dates. The equipment supplier establishes its scope; the funder establishes its offer; the finance lead reconciles both with the business's complete commitments.

| Funding question | Cash-purchase record | Hire-purchase record | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acquired package | Supplier equipment and completion schedule ___ | Same package, with funded/excluded items ___ | Buyer; keeps the compared asset constant |
| Initial payment | Actual supplier payment dates and amounts ___ | Deposit, fees and unfunded initial items ___ | Finance lead; tests the first funding requirement |
| Later payments | Remaining acquisition payments ___ | Dated repayment schedule and total payable ___ | Finance lead; compares complete committed outflow |
| Working resources | Coil, people, premises and transport funds after purchase ___ | Same resources after deposit and repayments ___ | Operations and finance leads; tests funded production |
| Equipment identity | Former, lift, vehicle and tooling references ___ | Asset identities accepted in the funding offer ___ | Technical buyer; prevents gaps between contracts |
| Use conditions | Relevant owned-asset commitments ___ | Actual location, alteration, transfer and other use terms ___ | Management and advisers; checks the intended business duty |
| Acceptance/payment | Supplier milestone records and payment owners ___ | Funder release requirements and matching evidence ___ | Buyer and funder; aligns equipment delivery with funding |
| Ownership/end event | Purchase completion and future disposal plan ___ | Agreed ownership endpoint, settlement or other end terms ___ | Management and advisers; compares the intended asset outcome |

The worksheet should reveal the binding constraint. Cash purchase can be feasible for the equipment yet leave the operating plan unsupported. Hire purchase can preserve initial funds yet create payments the contracted workload cannot support. A smaller technically accepted investment or a later launch is a valid result when the complete route lacks funding.

## Mobile equipment brings more than a factory address

A forming line retained in one workshop and a compatible forming package moving between jobs present different business plans. Give the funder the actual intended duty and obtain its written response to relevant location, transport, alteration and onward-use questions. Read the resulting terms rather than assuming a general asset-finance label permits every business activity.

Keep independently supplied modules visible. A former, lift and locally completed vehicle can involve different suppliers and payment events. The equipment transaction needs a defined acceptance route, while the funding transaction needs the records its parties actually require. Align those events before treating the acquisition timetable as funded.

The [price-scope guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-price-quotation-cost-scope/) helps reconcile the completed purchase. Use that completed boundary in the funding comparison. Financing an incomplete package does not close the omitted integration or transport work.

## Two hypothetical funding failures at acquisition

**Hypothetical failure case: paying for the line consumes the coil budget.** A buyer treats cash purchase as the cheapest route because it compares only the machine amount with finance payments. The purchase leaves no funded input material for the first contracted orders. Compare complete acquisition and operating commitments before choosing the payment route or launch date.

**Hypothetical failure case: the monthly amount hides an excluded module.** Another buyer accepts an appealing repayment figure for a former, while the production plan also requires a separately supplied lift and local vehicle completion. Those items remain unfunded. Reconcile the financed asset schedule with the accepted production package and price every exclusion before signing.
