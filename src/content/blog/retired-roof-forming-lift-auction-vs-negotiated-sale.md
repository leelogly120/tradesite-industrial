Choose an actual auction proposal when its sale timetable, bidding terms, fees and collection arrangements meet your release deadline and accepted downside. Choose negotiated sale when retaining price-acceptance control and finding a buyer for the particular configuration justify the extra holding time and selling work. Before appointing either route, use the same review deadline to compare net cash received, physical removal and the funded outcome if the asset remains unsold. Select the route whose accepted downside still leaves the retirement plan workable.

Retirement is already decided. The purchase question is how to dispose of one identified owned asset, with the same included equipment and disclosed condition under both routes. A roof-forming lift positions a compatible former near the roof receiving level. Its sale inventory can include a carrier, forming line, tooling and loose equipment with different records and removal requirements.

## Give both routes the same asset to sell

Create one inventory with the machine identifiers and photographs, ownership records, known modifications, condition information and included documents. Identify which forming line, controls, mounting items, tooling sets and loose accessories transfer with it. Mark retained items on that inventory before either selling proposal is priced.

The workshop scene below contains stacked forming-machine beds, loose cylindrical tooling and boxed parts. A seller needs an itemized allocation for such separate objects. A buyer seeing them together in a photograph will otherwise form a different view of the lot from the owner's intended sale.

![Several grey forming-machine beds stand stacked beside loose tooling and wooden boxes in a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261030-stored-line-frames.webp)
*Allocate installed equipment, loose tooling and retained workshop items individually before the listing is issued.*

Give prospective purchasers a factual condition and records package. Have competent parties define any permissible demonstration and its preparation. The selling channel should work with that boundary. Expanding a demonstration to attract bids introduces a separate technical decision and cost.

## Read the auction format before valuing its deadline

An auction concentrates a sale process around a defined event or bidding period. Establish the actual submission date, listing preparation, inspection arrangements, bidding conditions, settlement and collection endpoint. The event date alone does not say when the owner receives usable funds or regains the occupied space.

[Ritchie Bros. describes several selling formats](https://www.rbauction.eu/selling/about-our-auctions), including reserved auctions and routes selling equipment from the owner's location. Those are different commercial arrangements. An auction proposal needs its own reserve or acceptance terms, treatment of an unsold item, fees and location duties. Do not budget every auction as an unconditional sale on auction day.

Owner-location selling can avoid one initial transfer while leaving the owner with storage, visitor access and collection coordination. An auction-yard route can shift those activities but add transport and preparation. Price the route offered for your asset, including the work left at your premises.

Negotiated sale gives the authorized seller a point at which to accept a particular offer. The [Ritchie Bros. Buy Now/Make Offer page](https://www.rbauction.com/buying/how-to-buy/buy-now-make-offer) distinguishes listed-price purchase and private offers from bidding, followed by payment and release steps. Use that as an example of separate sale events, rather than transferring its terms to another marketplace or direct transaction.

For negotiated sale, target buyers whose required profile, former interface and carrier arrangement match the identified asset. Give them the same inventory and condition package used for the auction proposal. Treat the search period and actual review work as selling costs, with holding and demonstration costs continuing until the agreed release endpoint.

## Compare receipts after all seller costs

Use the same asset boundary and time horizon in both columns. Start with the actual sale-price scenario, deduct seller-paid fees and preparation, then include storage, transport, demonstrations and removal work assigned to the owner. Keep any tax treatment with the responsible adviser using the actual transaction.

| Disposal input | Auction entry | Negotiated-sale entry | Datum, owner and decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asset boundary | Lot inventory/revision ___ | Listing inventory/revision ___ | Equipment manager matches every included identifier |
| Price control | Reserve or acceptance terms ___ | Authorized acceptance amount/conditions ___ | Owner's signatory sets the acceptable downside |
| Seller deductions | Sold/unsold/withdrawal fee bases ___ | Listing, agent and completion charges ___ | Commercial lead applies each charge to its actual event |
| Holding period | Preparation through release dates ___ | Marketing and completion scenario ___ | Fleet planner prices occupied days |
| Demonstration | Permitted review and owner costs ___ | Same condition-review boundary ___ | Technical owner controls the allowed activity |
| Collection | Location, provider, duties and date ___ | Location, provider, duties and date ___ | Logistics lead prices seller-retained work |
| Unsold outcome | Reserve miss, relisting or withdrawal ___ | No acceptable offer by review date ___ | Asset owner selects the funded fallback |
| Cash and space release | Net-settlement date and removal deadline ___ | Net-settlement date and removal deadline ___ | Finance and operations approve separate cash and space endpoints |

Keep the unsold calculation visible. Record the further holding period and costs under the actual terms. If a route needs a reduced acceptance amount after a failed attempt, identify who can authorize that decision. A high headline estimate is weak protection against an unbudgeted second selling cycle.

## Put collection beside the sale decision

The truck scene below shows why physical release deserves its own price and owner. The sold asset and its loose items need an agreed collection boundary, equipment information and a qualified handling provider. Price that preparation and give its provider a defined collection boundary alongside the sale terms.

![A blue and orange forming assembly rests on a green-sided flatbed beneath workshop crane hooks.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-former-truck-workshop.webp)
*Assign preparation, collection and loading duties with the actual sale inventory and removal date.*

**Mistake (hypothetical): the auction date becomes the cash-release date.** A fleet owner commits a replacement-former payment for the day after bidding closes and books the vacated bay for the new line. The selling contract has later settlement and item-release conditions. Rebuild the cash plan around those conditions before choosing the auction proposal.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the negotiated price includes the wrong tooling.** The buyer's offer includes the loose roller sets visible behind the former, while the owner has allocated those sets to a retained production line. Reissue the included-equipment inventory and resolve the price and collection consequences before accepting the transaction.

## RFQ attachments for sale-route proposals

Send the auction provider or sale representative eight attachments:

1. Asset identifiers, ownership records and authority to release any recorded interests.
2. Included former, carrier, mounting equipment and loose-tooling inventory.
3. Known condition, alteration history and available technical records.
4. Permitted inspection or demonstration scope and responsible technical party.
5. Auction reserve, fees, unsold, withdrawal and settlement terms.
6. Negotiated listing, offer acceptance, payment and release terms.
7. Collection location, loading responsibilities and required removal date.
8. Net-receipt scenarios, holding costs and named acceptance signatory.
