**Buy access for the actual modular supports and connection positions when the project releases a busbar-trunking design. For a released cable-and-tray design, price its support, cable-installation and termination duties as a complete separate package.** Compare personnel access, material handling and specialist acceptance together. A common overhead route does not mean both systems consume the same equipment resources.

The electrical designer decides the accepted distribution system. The installation business must then decide which resources it owns, hires or buys as specialist services. This guide addresses that second purchase. It provides no energized-work, connection or isolation procedure, and the platform order remains separate from the authorized electrical assignment.

## Convert the design into actual construction objects

Obtain the released route, support details, installed product references and work boundaries. For busway, identify the actual supplied modules, fittings and other components. For cable-and-tray, retain the actual supporting system and cable-installation scope rather than treating a tray supply price as the whole distribution installation.

[Schneider Electric's Canalis information](https://www.se.com/uk/en/download/document/SE12287/) identifies prefabricated busbar trunking as a product family. Its [Canalis installation-document page](https://www.se.com/au/en/download/document/QGH3492101/) also identifies a specific system manual and revision. These examples show why the nominated product and documents belong in the project. No Schneider dimensions, ratings, installation claims or permitted functions are transferred to ARCLIFT equipment.

Ask the actual installer to issue a task schedule against those objects and drawings. Separate support work, component or cable services, electrical work and accepted completion. Each line needs a real provider, even if procurement appoints one coordinator for the combined package.

## A personnel deck and a handling package buy different functions

The equipment proposal must identify what people do from the working surface, the required faces and the objects proposed there at the same time. A broad deck is a candidate for adjacent positions along a released work area. Localized equipment or a specialist access service deserves comparison for separated or obstructed positions.

The appointed handling specialist defines material presentation for the actual modules, cable products and other assemblies. The personnel deck should not inherit a component-support, pulling or material-lifting duty from a general description of overhead installation. Allocate those resources expressly and coordinate their space with access.

Complete the table from actual specialist proposals. It is a procurement comparison, not a method for choosing electrical ratings or designing supports.

Where an installer offers a complete package, require those boundaries to remain visible. Where the buyer integrates separate contracts, appoint the project coordinator who keeps drawing revisions, provider dates and interface requirements aligned. A lower equipment count is useful only when every retained function is supplied.

## The final connections can need a different visit

The installation programme should identify which tasks proceed in released areas and which depend on other trades, equipment delivery or specialist acceptance. Price the actual waiting intervals and returns. Do not assume a platform booked for support construction will remain available for every later system stage.

For a factory upgrade, the operator supplies the actual area possessions and continuity requirements through the responsible project process. Procurement includes those duties and dates without inventing a service-state decision. The selected access arrangement must fit the expected building occupation, not an obsolete empty-hall drawing.

The yard view below separates the broad deck from the support footprint at ground level. Put the whole proposed arrangement on the coordinated layout with material staging and the other installation resources.

Give the supplier the actual floor records and route sections. Obtain configuration-specific support reactions and dimensions for the responsible building review. A cable-route drawing locates an electrical system; it does not also describe the platform's entry, setup and exit arrangement.

## Two hypothetical distribution budgets with unbought resources

**Failure case: prefabricated modules are treated as a complete installation service.** A buyer compares module prices with a full cable-and-tray contract while excluding support work, specialist handling and accepted completion. Reconcile both totals to the same installed zone before allocating personnel access or claiming a saving.

**Failure case: the platform allowance silently includes material functions.** Another proposal supplies access for workers but the installation budget assigns component support and cable services to that same machine without an applicable arrangement. Purchase the specialist handling functions and coordinate them with the actual personnel proposal before ordering.

Attach eight items to the RFQ:

2. Route, support and interface drawings with current revisions.
3. Actual modules, cables and components with dimensions and masses.
4. Installer's personnel task and required-face schedule.
5. Work levels referenced to local supporting-floor datums.
6. Separate handling and electrical-specialist responsibilities.
7. Building route, floor records, setup plan and operational possessions.
