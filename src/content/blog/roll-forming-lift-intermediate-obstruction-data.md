A roof-forming layout can show an unobstructed outlet near the roof while leaving a conflict **between the ground position and that outlet**. A canopy, projecting structure or nearby fixed equipment may occupy space relevant to the proposed lifting apparatus. The buyer needs to locate that object in the same plan and section as the actual equipment proposal, rather than recording only the final outlet height.

This guide develops an intermediate-obstacle log through one worked spatial comparison. The [roof-zone survey](/blog/roof-zone-survey-roll-forming-lift-selection/) covers the general project brief. Here the question concerns apparatus space between base and outlet, distinct from the emerging-panel path across a parapet or the external roof receiving arrangement.

## Locate the obstacle within the equipment relationship

### Name the actual object

Identify the canopy, beam, projecting facade item or other fixed object by a stable project reference. Describe its observed extent and information source without assuming that its name explains its geometry. A canopy can have supporting members below its visible roof, and nearby equipment can have attachments not apparent in a distant photograph. Where the object or its status is uncertain, say which part remains unknown. The useful enquiry locates a specific obstacle; it does not declare the surrounding space clear merely because one main surface was recorded.

### Connect the object to the candidate base

Mark the proposed equipment position on the same plan and include the ground or support reference used for its section. A measured obstacle elevation from the building datum must be related to that location before it can inform the equipment comparison. Do not treat the machine's track rectangle as the whole apparatus. Ask for the actual proposed arrangement and identify which supplier-provided geometry remains missing. The buyer supplies the site relationship, while the equipment party supplies the configuration information needed to consider it.

### Keep the final outlet as a separate reference

Show the cutter or outlet and the first roof receiver as distinct points. An intermediate obstruction can matter to the proposed apparatus even when the line between outlet and receiver appears clear. Conversely, a roof-edge obstruction can affect the panel corridor without occupying the same apparatus-space relationship. Separating those questions stops a supplier reply about outlet height from being treated as an answer to a canopy below it. The [height-datum guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-height-specification-datum/) explains the measurement-point distinction behind that drawing.

## Record extent rather than one convenient height

### Put the whole relevant projection on the plan

Record the object's location and boundaries across the requested equipment space. A single offset from the wall cannot represent a projection extending only across part of the bay. Name the section that passes through the actual proposed relationship. If the object's extent has not been observed, mark the missing region rather than continuing a measured edge by assumption. The next survey request can then identify what needs checking without repeating unrelated measurements elsewhere on the facade.

### Identify the surfaces represented in section

A top elevation, underside elevation and supporting member can be different parts of an object. State which surface each dimension refers to, together with the relevant local ground reference. The difference between two labels is not automatically usable clearance. The equipment party must relate its actual apparatus information to the site data and stated configuration. Do not create a generic allowance around the projection or infer the apparatus's changing space from a single final-position illustration.

### Keep changing work separate from fixed geometry

Temporary scaffold, deliveries or another contractor's equipment may alter the same area even where the permanent canopy is unchanged. Record the intended construction stage and the owner of changes to that space. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/workplacetransport/information/safeworkplace.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE workplace-route guidance</a> discusses obstructions and temporary routes. That provides planning context, without establishing a forming-lift clearance or an apparatus path. A field observation remains an input for review rather than permission to position or move equipment near the object.

## Compare a canopy conflict and a panel-path conflict

### Separate the two hypothetical locations

In a **hypothetical project**, Position A places the proposed base beside a canopy projecting into the region between base and outlet. Position B avoids that projection but uses a receiver behind a roof-edge feature. The panel requirement is common to both. A has an apparatus-space question; B has a panel-corridor question. The example does not establish that either position is viable. Its purpose is to show why one general obstruction label can send both issues to the wrong review or conceal one beneath an apparently sufficient height statement.

### Ask the equipment party about A's actual arrangement

For A, provide the canopy's located plan extent and section surfaces, then ask which proposed apparatus arrangement the supplier has considered against them. If only the final outlet point is identified, the intermediate relationship remains unanswered. The trade-off is between a convenient base position and the information needed to assess its surrounding volume. Do not infer a movement path, changed support configuration or component removal that makes the sketch appear to fit. Those would each require a separate actual proposal.

### Route B's different question to the panel boundary

For B, identify the outlet-to-receiver corridor and the relevant edge detail, retaining the external support and receiving scope. The [handover-zone guide](/blog/elevated-roll-forming-roof-panel-handover-zones/) develops that question. A reply addressing B's panel route does not close A's canopy relationship, and a clear apparatus position at B does not prove a suitable panel path. The following log structure preserves the difference so procurement can request the missing response from the responsible parties without designing either arrangement.

| Located feature | Relationship requiring assessment | Information the reply must address |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canopy at Position A | Object between the proposed base and outlet region | Actual apparatus arrangement compared with the canopy's located extent |
| Canopy support below its roof | Another relevant surface within that same spatial question | Whether the supplied site detail identifies the surface used in the comparison |
| Roof-edge projection at B | Emerging-panel corridor beyond the outlet | Actual external support and receiving proposal, separately from apparatus space |
| Temporary occupation near A | Availability of the proposed position at the stated stage | Which site assumption applies when the arrangement is intended to be used |
| Unobserved side extent | Missing geometry rather than an established collision | The particular measurement or equipment information needed before assessment |

## Request a response to the located apparatus question

### Give each obstacle a useful next action

Attach the located plan and section to the [crawler forming-lift enquiry](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts), identifying the actual former and proposed lift arrangement. Ask whether the missing answer concerns the site object's extent or the supplier's apparatus information. That distinction makes the follow-up concrete. A general request for all clearance documents is less useful than pointing to the unnamed surface or undefined equipment relationship that prevents comparison. Keep any revised base position as another proposal rather than changing the original drawing silently.

- Name each obstacle and locate its relevant extent on the roof-zone plan.
- Record the measured surface, height datum and local ground reference in the matching section.
- Identify the proposed apparatus, former and outlet separately from the panel material corridor.
- Show the site access route and construction stage relevant to the candidate position.
- State destination and transport context so the requested equipment arrangement remains identifiable.
- Separate missing site geometry, missing configuration information and a reviewed conflict in the requested response.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize when an unexamined volume remains

A proposal may not fit a selection decision if an important projection has no located extent or the actual apparatus arrangement cannot be related to it. An unobstructed outlet point does not answer the intermediate-space question. This guide provides no clearance margin, swept path, movement method or structural assessment. Another position may be worth considering, but it needs its own equipment and site relationships. Procurement should preserve the unanswered volume rather than reduce it to a guessed distance that makes the preferred drawing appear acceptable.

## FAQ

#### Does a clear outlet position mean the apparatus fits below it?

No. The outlet is one equipment reference, while the apparatus occupies space between its base and that point. A projection can affect the latter relationship without appearing in the final panel corridor. Ask for the actual configuration information and located site geometry needed to assess it. Do not infer the intervening arrangement from a height label alone.

#### Is one canopy height enough for the enquiry?

It may identify one measured surface, but the relevant extent, supporting features and plan location still need description. Name the point or surface represented by each measurement and mark unobserved areas. The buyer should request the missing geometry that affects the actual proposed relationship, rather than extending one measurement across the whole object or calling it a clearance value.

#### Can a different ground position close the issue?

It can create another proposal to assess, but it may change the outlet, support, feed-side or receiver relationship. Preserve those changes explicitly. This guide does not select a position or authorize a movement to reach it. The responsible equipment and project parties must assess the actual alternative against its own site geometry and task.

#### Is a panel-path obstruction the same question?

It is related but different. A panel-path feature affects the requested relationship beyond the former's outlet, while the intermediate apparatus question concerns equipment space between base and outlet. Keeping the two distinct directs each to the relevant configuration, external support or receiving response and prevents a partial answer from appearing to resolve the complete layout.
