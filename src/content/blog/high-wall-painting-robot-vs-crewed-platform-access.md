## A wall has field area, edges and a finish

Begin with a painting-only award whose substrate preparation, coating system and appearance acceptance are fixed. The investment buys a way of delivering that finish. It does not choose whether the building needs a new lining or redefine the coating to suit an attractive machine demonstration.

Count continuous fields separately from openings, reveals, corners, returns and areas interrupted by services. A large gross elevation can contain short disconnected paintable strips. Another wall of similar gross size can provide a long clear field. The first consumes more edge and exception work even before either bidder applies material.

[Okibo's painting application page](https://okibo.com/applications/wall-painting-robot/) describes a robot serving wall and ceiling surfaces while painters retain details, touch-ups and quality control. It also states that actual output depends on the surface, coating and layout. Those qualifications make the measured field schedule more useful than importing an advertised area rate into an investment forecast.

## Preparation is a purchased handover

Write down the condition presented to the painting equipment. Identify repairs, preparation, masking or other protection that the specified finish requires and the contractor responsible for each. Use the coating specialist's actual requirements. A robot starting on a released surface and a crew starting with damaged walls have different commercial starting points.

Keep preparation quantities on the same zone plan as application. If one route includes work from the shared deck and another uses a separate preparation crew, price both. Avoid dividing all preparation cost by total wall area when only particular zones need the work; that average hides the crew positions the access purchase must serve.

Release representative substrate and finish samples through the agreed quality process. Carry the accepted appearance reference into both bids. Compare the finished field and its transitions to hand-completed edges under that same requirement, rather than approving a small attractive sample and assuming the rest of the room is equivalent.

## Follow material and work through the whole wall

Obtain the offered painting method and coating acceptance for the named machine. Define where material arrives, where application equipment stands and which items reach a human work position. Include actual supply, replenishment, cleaning and end-of-zone work in each provider's quotation. These activities consume people and space even while no paint reaches the wall.

Use the current offered application rather than a general robot capability list. [Okibo's application roadmap](https://okibo.com/applications/) distinguishes ongoing applications from taping, roller painting and other developments marked as tests or future work. Specify the offered spray process, its supported coating and its demonstrated finish for this order. Keep applications marked as tests outside the base equipment bid.

Next, follow the specified coating and inspection sequence through completion. Count actual returns to each zone, including detail completion and corrections. If the provider's application equipment remains between stages, price its occupied space. If it leaves, include the real mobilisation and preparation required for the next visit. Put the coating specialist's actual release intervals into that programme.

The purchase can split fields and details, but their handover must be usable. Record which provider leaves an accepted field, who completes adjoining details and who releases the whole elevation. Assign a discrepancy at their junction to a defined correction process, rather than leaving two crews to debate whose area the mark occupies.

## Buy human workspace from the detail map

A shared large deck earns comparison where repeated adjacent preparation, application or finishing tasks need several people and their permitted tools or materials together. Isolated reveals or scattered ceiling corners support a different access case. Keep those points separate rather than retaining a campaign-sized deck merely because the original painting estimate included one.

Use sections as well as elevations. Mark the hand position beside each face from the supporting-floor datum, with obstructions and approach direction. List simultaneous crew, tools, material objects and external hose or cable interfaces. The access provider assesses that inventory and environment for its actual configuration; the painting specialist retains the application method.

## Two hypothetical painting-budget mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): ready-to-paint pricing beats a complete wall quote.** One robotic offer begins on released masonry, while the crewed offer includes the specified repairs and preparation. Finance calls the difference an application saving. Add the same preparation handover and accepted finish to both totals before comparing machinery.

**Mistake (hypothetical): one spray visit becomes the whole booking.** The robot and detail-access budget covers one shift, but the coating and inspection plan returns after another trade occupies the floor. Price the actual visits and reserved positions, or release suitable alternate zones in the programme. Spray-application time is not the equipment occupation period.
