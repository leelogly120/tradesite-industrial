Use qualified hired haulage as the starting point when crawler roll-forming lift movements are occasional, routes vary, or your business has no established transport operation. Compare an owned transporter when repeat movements and dependable staff can support the full transport duty. Count round trips, waiting and unavailable days before deciding. Buying a crawler machine and buying its carrier are two linked investments, not one automatic equipment package.

The crawler's useful working position and its road-transfer arrangement answer different questions. The site photograph below shows a machine near a building; its tracks do not settle the route, loading or carrier choice between projects. Start with the confirmed transport configuration for the equipment you intend to order.

![A red lifting structure stands on a tracked base with extended supports inside a steel-framed building.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-crawler-base.webp)
*A suitable site machine still needs a separately supported plan for each transfer between projects.*

## Count transport jobs, not just project addresses

One project can create several movements: delivery, a return to storage, a transfer to a different work zone requiring road transport, or collection after a delayed finish. Conversely, a machine assigned to one long-duration project generates only the movements in that project schedule.

List actual loaded journeys and empty return legs. Add the periods when the carrier waits, is reserved for uncertain collection, or is unavailable for another job. A spreadsheet that records only distance with a machine on board can make ownership look easier than it is.

List other equipment proposed to use the same driver or vehicle. Include that shared workload only where schedules and vehicle suitability are compatible. A busy-looking fleet calendar is not useful if every machine requires the carrier on the same day.

## Put both routes into the same worksheet

| Transport input | Hired haulage comparison | Owned carrier comparison | Buyer record / confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed machine configuration | Carrier quotes for the issued equipment data | Proposed vehicle must match the same data | Equipment supplier: issues transport dimensions, mass and drawing revision for carrier matching |
| Movement frequency | Price actual bookings and uncertain extras separately | Use supported repeat movements in the ownership case | Fleet planner: counts loaded and empty legs over the chosen period to test utilisation |
| Waiting and delayed release | Agree how booking changes and waiting are handled | Count occupied vehicle and driver time | Project manager: enters release windows and waiting causes to price occupation |
| Loading and unloading | Identify who provides each service and suitable arrangements | Add equipment, personnel and site coordination | Site logistics lead: names loading/unloading provider, equipment and quoted scope |
| Route and access | Obtain route-specific suitability confirmation | Check the owned combination for each proposed route | Carrier: assesses measured gate/turning constraints for the identified machine/vehicle combination |
| Staff and availability | Confirm booked carrier and backup arrangements | Include staffing, maintenance and unavailable days | Transport manager: names staff cover, maintenance downtime and booked backup |
| Full period cost | Include all quoted services and exclusions | Include ownership, operation and support over the same period | Estimator: enters ownership/operation and haulage totals over the same period and service boundary |

Keep the cost boundary consistent. If hired haulage includes a service that the owned option excludes, add the equivalent resource before comparing totals. Avoid applying one favourable route quote to every project when their access and loading conditions differ.

Also list any forming equipment that travels separately from the lift. The photograph below shows a long production unit on a truck outside a workshop. A transport budget needs the complete dispatched equipment list, including separate components and their own receiving arrangements.

![A truck carries a long blue production machine along the outside of a factory building.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-truck-dispatch.webp)
*Include separately transported production equipment in the logistics scope instead of budgeting only for the lifting structure.*

## Ownership works only with an operating plan

An owned carrier can be worth comparing where the business has repeat routes, a reliable scheduling process, suitable staff and a support arrangement. It can also serve other compatible machinery. Those advantages depend on actual utilisation and coordination; they are not automatic savings from removing a haulier's invoice.

The opposite case is equally concrete. If a specialist machine spends most of its time at one project, exclude the stationary period from the carrier utilisation forecast. For geographically scattered jobs, choose a local haulier when its accepted route and dates meet the job and its complete quote is lower than the owned vehicle transfer cost. Obtain current route-specific quotations before making that judgement.

Use a conservative workload as well as the expected workload. Identify what happens if the first project holds the crawler longer than planned, or another site becomes ready early. Compare a second carrier booking, rescheduling the move and holding the work zone. Do not assume a newly purchased transport vehicle removes all scheduling conflicts.

## Would a truck-mounted forming lift change the equation?

A truck-mounted architecture is another equipment option when the actual jobs and transfer routes support it. The visible truck in the photograph makes the architecture difference clear. However, the proposed vehicle still needs destination-specific road, chassis and site review, and its complete transported configuration must be defined.

## Two fleet decisions that fail

**Failure 1 — the vehicle is economical only on loaded journeys.** A buyer counts regular outward trips but omits empty travel, collection delays and the driver's other commitments. The carrier is then unavailable when a second project releases its machine. Build the calendar around the entire transport job and check genuine backup availability.

**Failure 2 — a generic carrier is ordered before the machine is final.** The buyer commits to transport equipment using an early machine sketch. The final configuration changes the transport envelope or loading arrangement. Freeze the relevant machine data and obtain a matching transport review before placing the carrier order.
