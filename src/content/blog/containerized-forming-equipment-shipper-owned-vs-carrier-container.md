**Use a carrier-owned container for a one-off machinery delivery when the receiver can unload it and complete the agreed empty-return cycle. Price a shipper-owned container when retaining the box or a committed reuse programme has concrete value and the carrier accepts that specific equipment and route.** Before purchasing a box, obtain the carrier's response to its identity and the full destination retention plan. Compare the machine delivery together with the container's eventual disposition. When both arrangements meet the real retention need, choose the lower complete cost through that agreed endpoint.

Here container fit and the machine's accepted packing arrangement are already established. Keep the same cargo, destination and packing condition in both quotations. Choosing who provides the box is a separate purchase from changing the machine breakdown, selecting special cargo or deciding how a truck waits at the receiver.

A shipper-owned container, or SOC, is equipment the shipper provides to the carriage arrangement. A carrier-owned container, or COC, comes through the carrier's equipment cycle. The importer still needs a feasible loading, carriage and receiving chain under either arrangement.

The wrapped machine below explains why that cargo basis must remain fixed. Its actual packed length, gross mass, support and securing requirements belong to the issued package records. Changing container ownership does not authorise a different machine-loading arrangement.

## The carrier box has a complete return cycle

For a COC offer, obtain the actual equipment-use period and the events defining it. Record pickup, delivery, empty notification, collection and return responsibilities as specified by the providers. The practical question is whether the receiver's accepted unloading and release plan fits that agreement.

Include the quoted consequences of longer retention, extra collection or changed return arrangements where they apply. Do not copy a free-time allowance or charge from another port, carrier or shipment. An importer with a ready receiving workshop and a one-off machine arrival often has a straightforward reason to use the carrier's box: the equipment leaves the importer's responsibilities through a defined return process.

A late assembly programme is not automatically a reason to retain a container. The machinery can have an accepted unloading and storage arrangement outside the box. Compare that complete receiving service with buying or leasing container retention. Preserve the required machine protection and handling obligations in both proposals.

The separate [live-unload versus drop-and-collect guide](/blog/roll-forming-machine-container-live-unload-vs-drop-delivery/) addresses last-mile service. Either ownership route still needs an actual receiving appointment and an accepted unloading method. A box held at the site and a box owned by the importer are different commercial conditions.

## An owned box needs its own accepted business use

SOC earns a serious comparison when the buyer has a defined reason to keep the container: an accepted long-retention requirement, repeated eligible shipments or another funded use after machinery receipt. Identify the location, duration, responsible custodian and cost of that use. Speculative future value should not cancel a real purchase and repositioning bill.

Separate buying the container from obtaining carriage for it. [Maersk's SOC guidance](https://www.maersk.com/support/faqs/2023/08/12/shippers-own-containers) requires its SOC application form, photographs of the boxes and photographs of their CSC plates at booking. It states that the carrier validates the details before processing the booking request. Follow the actual selected carrier's response for the particular box and route.

Those are Maersk’s stated booking inputs. The selected carrier supplies any additional box, route or inspection conditions for its actual offer. A seller's description of a container as suitable for shipping is not the carrier's booking acceptance. Complete this step before buying a container whose planned use depends on the chosen voyage.

Account for the box after the machine has left it. Price the actual holding, inspection, repair, empty repositioning, next use or final sale/disposal arrangement that your programme requires. An owner avoids the particular obligation to return a carrier's box only by accepting a different asset responsibility; it does not make all other logistics costs disappear.

Use the same machine-ready milestone in both columns, then retain the different box-related obligations beyond it. Show the container's residual value separately from certain costs, using the buyer's supported estimate rather than an assumed resale amount. A plan that relies on a second use needs that actual movement and its acceptance cost in the calculation.

The port photograph connects ownership with a wider handling chain. Terminal, lifting and inland services remain real work even when the importer supplies its own container. Keep them in both quotations wherever the same tasks are required.

## Two hypothetical container-ownership mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): a carrier box becomes the workshop store.** An importer budgets a carrier-provided box as a permanent workshop store after machine receipt, although the quote provides only the agreed equipment cycle. The comparison omits the actual cost and terms of securing a retained box. Obtain the actual extended-use offer and compare it with accepted unloading/storage or an SOC route before selecting the container provision.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the SOC is bought before its booking is accepted.** A buyer purchases a used box because a seller says it is ready for sea transport. The booked carrier still needs its own documentation and validation. Obtain the named carrier's acceptance response and price any required inspection or remedial work before relying on that box for the machinery dispatch.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the same import

1. Equipment identities and the already accepted packed configuration.
2. Package dimensions, gross masses and issued handling records.
3. Origin, destination and proposed carriage route.
4. COC equipment-cycle offer and applicable actual terms.
5. Named SOC purchase/lease proposal and identifying records.
6. Selected carrier's SOC document requirements and acceptance response.
7. Receiving, retention, custody and empty-movement programme.
8. Complete cost comparison through return or planned disposition.
