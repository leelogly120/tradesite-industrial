Include a rainwater-reuse package when the owner has a defined non-potable demand, an accepted water-system design and a funded operating responsibility. Retain a discharge-only roof when those conditions are absent. Before ordering metal sheets, identify the selected catchment areas and obtain the water specialist's material and collection requirements for the intended use. Where reuse has no named connected demand, award the complete discharge roof and price any designed future provision as a separate owner decision.

A tank aspiration is not a complete building brief. The real choice links roof materials, collection, treatment where specified, storage, distribution, overflow and ongoing care. A metal roof can form part of that route, while its manufacture remains a distinct equipment duty.

## Start at the demand end

List the intended non-potable uses and their operating pattern. Obtain the water specialist's assessment of demand, available collection and the proposed system. Let that specialist establish storage and treatment requirements for the accepted use. Do not size a tank from roof area alone or promise that stored water can serve every process.

The [NSW Planning Portal's rainwater-system overview](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/basix/water/design-principles/rainwater-harvesting-systems) distinguishes catchment, storage, allocated uses and overflow. Those categories form a useful purchasing boundary even where a different jurisdiction's design requirements govern the project. Use the appointed specialist's accepted local design for the actual industrial building.

## Reuse route: purchase water all the way to its use

Ask for a schematic from roof zones through the system to the nominated endpoints. Identify every supplied item and every connection supplied by another trade. Include the design's filtration or treatment equipment, controls, pumps, tank supports and distribution where specified. The roof contractor needs the collection interfaces; the water provider needs the accepted roof and gutter materials.

Record the ordinary and overflow destinations separately. Coordinate tank overflow and any bypass arrangement with the drainage designer. A storage tank does not remove the roof's need for a complete discharge strategy under the accepted design.

Include commissioning, identification and the owner handover required by the specialist's package. Name who operates and maintains each part. Choose this route where the actual use and operating arrangement justify the complete project, rather than a tank with no connected demand.

For phased projects, identify the date reuse begins and the interim discharge arrangement. If the roof is completed well before the water plant, buy the accepted interim and final interfaces together so one trade does not leave another an unpriced conversion task.

## Discharge only: complete the roof without invented future credit

A discharge-only package still includes the designed collection, outlets, discharge and maintenance provisions. Select it where no accepted funded reuse brief exists or where the owner's evaluated alternative is preferable. Compare its actual installed and operating costs with the full reuse proposal over the same owner-selected period.

If future reuse is a genuine client requirement, commission a defined provision now. Have the water-system designer state the retained catchment zones, accepted materials, reserved equipment space and future connection scope on an issued provision drawing. Price that provision explicitly. An empty note saying “tank later” has no reliable value in the procurement comparison.

Keep future provisions separate from current equipment utilisation. Buying a roll former for roof panels requires its own accepted product and workload. The possibility of a future water system does not increase the amount of sheet the machine will produce for this roof.

Write the measurement basis for each catchment quantity. A sloping sheet area used for material ordering and a horizontal catchment area used by a water designer are different quantities. Retain each owner's stated datum in its respective schedule.

## Control materials beyond the main sheet

[BlueScope's rainwater-harvesting publication](https://www.bluescopesteel.com.au/files/11733_rainwater_brochure4.pdf) draws attention to materials within the catchment and ongoing care. The procurement consequence is a complete material review. Give the responsible specialist the actual roof, accessories and any planned coating or later rooftop installation, rather than asserting suitability from the main sheet alone.

Keep traceability through panel production. Receive the nominated coil, record its identity and link accepted panels to the released roof zones. The workshop coils below illustrate where that chain begins. Later substitutions require the same roof and water-system review as the original material.

![Metal coils stand beside blue processing machinery and metal offcuts in a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-coil-processing-workshop.webp)
*Preserve the accepted material identity from incoming coil to the roof zones assigned to collection.*

**Mistake (hypothetical): the tank is priced without the use connection.** A warehouse buyer credits reuse for toilet flushing while the tank offer excludes the pump, connection to the toilets and the treatment specified by the water designer. Obtain the complete designed route and operating cost before comparing it with discharge-only roofing.

**Mistake (hypothetical): a later accessory bypasses the catchment review.** A roof alteration introduces a different material or finish into a selected collection zone without the specialist's acceptance. Update the material and collection records before buying that alteration, including any required separation of the affected zone.
