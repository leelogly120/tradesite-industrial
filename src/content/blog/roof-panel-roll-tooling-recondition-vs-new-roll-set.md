Choose recutting when the toolmaker can restore the existing roll set to the released roof section, the mill is fit to carry it, and orders can be covered while the rolls are away. Buy a new matched set when the original geometry cannot be recovered or when keeping the old set available through the change has greater value than the rework saving.

The purchasing question starts with a panel that no longer consistently passes the customer's inspection. Before inviting tooling prices, establish what changed. A new bank of rolls on a machine with an unresolved fault is an expensive way to repeat a rejection.

## Follow a rejected panel back to its production run

Put the rejected and last accepted examples next to the controlled profile drawing. Identify which edge mates to the adjacent sheet, the cover width when assembled and the actual defect. A flatness complaint, a loss of rib definition and an edge that fails to engage are different observations. Give the tooling specialist the coil identity, material specification, line identity and job record for each example. Preserve the finish and show which face was exposed.

Have the appropriate specialist assess the machine and its existing tool set together. Bradbury describes calibration, recutting, selective tooling replacement and complete replacement as distinct responses on its agricultural-panel lines. It also identifies the condition of the underlying machine as relevant to the result. Use that distinction to obtain a diagnosis with explicit limits. *Mark the specific machine and its matched rolls on the enquiry. Similar lines in the same shop can have different job histories.*

Ask for three procurement outcomes from that examination. An identified mill or material issue belongs in its own corrective work order. A recoverable roll set needs a quotation specifying the original components retained and the finished profile they will produce. A new set needs a proposal covering its interfaces with the existing line and the disposition of the old tooling. If the supplier offers a few replacement rolls, require the same finished-section acceptance as for a complete bank.

## What “the same profile” has to mean on delivery

A customer ordering the present roof section expects the next production run to mate with other material supplied under that designation. Mark the drawing revision that governs new orders. Identify any older revision still required for open contracts. Preserve a known accepted mating sheet, edge detail and accessories relevant to the product. The toolmaker should specify how those references will be used and returned.

A finished profile arises from a progression across stations. Samco describes material behavior, geometry and station sequence as related tooling-design inputs. Ask each bidder to state which stations, spacers, guides or other interfaces their price covers and who resolves fit at an omitted interface. A quotation per roll is useful for accounting after those boundaries are agreed. [Samco's roll tooling description](https://samco-machinery.com/equipment/roll-tooling/)

## Compare the cost of the interruption

For a reworked set, plot the period from its removal until accepted production restarts. Include packing, collection, specialist examination, rework, return, installation, trial coil and inspection. Ask what happens to the price and the programme if inspection reveals that the set cannot be restored. A quoted machining duration begins well after the last saleable sheet has left the mill.

A new set can sometimes be made while the original remains on the machine. Count this benefit only for orders the existing set can still supply to specification. New tooling still needs fit checks, a change window and a production trial. Compare both bids through the first accepted order, including outside panel purchases or the margin lost on work that cannot be covered. Test that arithmetic against the supplier's proposed delivery dates.

## Two purchasing mistakes

**Mistake: accepting one attractive sample.** A hypothetical buyer approves a short piece that looks straight. The trial uses a different feed material and never puts the side lap against the customer's stock. The first replacement batch fails the receiving check. Put the agreed material and the mating test into the trial order before either bidder prices acceptance.

**Mistake: paying to keep an unusable set available.** Another buyer chooses new tooling to avoid downtime, although the existing bank already produces rejected parts on every open contract. The parallel build gives the business no saleable output. Obtain an outside supply price for that interval and compare specialist rework and new manufacture on their actual completion dates.
