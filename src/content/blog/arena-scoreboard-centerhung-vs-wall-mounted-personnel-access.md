**Choose a centerhung scoreboard when the accepted audience brief calls for central viewing and the roof support, event envelope and maintenance route fit. Choose wall-mounted boards when they satisfy the same viewing brief and their supported, serviceable installation has the lower complete cost through the same venue handback. Shortlist ARC-F25 for sustained adjacent ceiling or wall work; use separate local access where seating or obstructions prevent its approach. First overlay the display, service faces and access footprints on the actual event layouts.**

This purchase fixes where the scoring system lives. It decides which building structure receives the display, where components are presented and which part of the arena must be released for technicians. Display price and screen dimensions alone cannot answer that installation decision.

A crew position in front of the visible board does not cover a rear-service task behind it.

## Compare the views the arena will actually use

Ask the venue designer to identify the audiences and activities that the system must serve. Include court or rink layouts, seating arrangements, staging and other uses already in the operating brief. Evaluate each proposed display face from those required viewing positions. A central assembly and several wall boards can be eligible alternatives only where both deliver the accepted result.

[Daktronics' hockey-scoreboard overview](https://www.daktronics.com/en-us/support/products/hockey-scoreboards) identifies wall-mounted and four-sided centerhung models, with alternative module arrangements for limited ceiling space. Select the roof-hung or wall-supported configuration against the required audience views and the actual building support locations.

## Center court buys a roof interface and a floor possession

Obtain the selected centerhung assembly's complete dimensions, mass, attachment interfaces and service requirements. The building engineer accepts the support and any roof work; the rigging provider supplies the actual handling and suspension package. Personnel access is a separate resource for people working at the defined connection and service locations.

Static suspension and a motorized lowering system create different service purchases within the centerhung route. [Daktronics' suspension comparison](https://www.daktronics.com/web-documents/HSPR-Documents/DD1627665.pdf) describes fixed cable suspension requiring an elevating platform to service the scoreboard. Its hoisted alternative lowers the display for service or event clearance and adds structural, electrical and hoist-related work. Treat that distinction as a design choice inside the centerhung package.

For fixed suspension, preserve the technician route to the installed board in the completed arena. For lowering, preserve the actual movement envelope and lower service position. Locate the overhead mechanism's own care positions separately. The fact that the display comes down does not move every roof-level task with it. Include the board itself in the access section: its housing can stand between the proposed personnel position and a roof connection. The installer and access provider must identify a documented approach to each retained task face rather than counting the arena's clear height as reachable work.

Use the real event calendar to price possession. A central platform or lowering zone competes with floor staging and event preparation. Identify the latest removal or reinstatement time required by the next booking. Put the released floor and the resource using it in the purchase programme.

## Wall boards buy repeated perimeter interfaces

For each proposed board, obtain the accepted wall support and its complete installed envelope. Mark the service face, component removal path, power and control interfaces. Name the providers responsible for building work and completion. A lower display location remains an access task when seating, barriers or fixed equipment occupy the approach.

Map the number of separate work stations from the actual design. Several wall boards create several support and service positions, while one centerhung structure can retain multiple elevated faces. Compare those located duties rather than awarding a presumed access saving to either arrangement by its name.

Where retractable or removable seating provides the accepted approach, include the venue's actual clearing and reinstatement resources. Where fixed seating blocks a floor-supported deck, price another suitable access arrangement. Extra platform height cannot create missing approach space behind the seats.

## Two hypothetical scoreboard buying mistakes

**The hoist is bought without a lower service zone.** A venue selects a movable centerhung assembly, then fixes a permanent stage within its required lowering envelope. The intended floor-level service benefit disappears. Coordinate the complete movement and service space with every adopted event arrangement before awarding the suspended package.

**The wall-board bid assumes an empty perimeter.** Another buyer prices board installation from a bare-shell drawing. Fixed seating later occupies the only proposed platform position, and rear service access is also absent. Review wall sections and the completed seating plan, then price the actual alternative access before selecting the wall route.

## Eight attachments for ceiling and wall personnel access

1. Accepted sport, audience and event-layout brief.
2. Selected scoreboard products, faces and service-side drawings.
3. Roof or wall support and interface design references.
4. Suspension/hoist layout and movement or fixed service envelopes.
5. Technician positions with elevations and offsets from the supporting floor.
6. Component dimensions/masses, separate handling and rigging scope, crew count and tool/item task-load schedule.
7. Entry, floor, seating, setup and possession records.
8. Installation, integration, return and handback dates with destination delivery scope.
