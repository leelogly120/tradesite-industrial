Choose a roof-sheet roll former when your accepted orders are for single-skin metal profiles. Choose a complete sandwich-panel production project when customers require bonded insulated panels and committed demand supports the factory, materials, quality system and distribution scope. For occasional insulated-panel orders, buy finished panels from a qualified producer instead of treating a sheet former as a complete composite-panel line.

The decision starts with the product your customer buys. Both businesses use metal facing material, but their manufacturing boundaries differ. A contractor buying roof sheets for a site-assembled insulated roof is also making a different purchase from a factory investing in bonded composite-panel production. Keep those three businesses separate in the investment brief.

## Define the invoice item before comparing machines

A single-skin sheet business supplies an accepted profile, material, finish and length. Its forming package needs appropriate coil handling, tooling, cutting, inspection and output handling. Those resources produce the specified sheet; they do not create an insulating core or establish the performance of a bonded assembly.

For a category example, [Hennecke's sandwich-panel production portfolio](https://www.hennecke.com/en/solutions/machines-and-production-systems/sandwich-panel-lines) describes complete systems spanning material handling, profiling, metering and finished-panel handling. It distinguishes continuous production from discontinuous equipment for different production needs. That manufacturer's portfolio illustrates the wider factory scope. It is not an ARCLIFT offer or a reason to prescribe one process to every buyer.

Specify your composite product first: facing types, core, joint details, dimensions, performance requirements and intended market. Then obtain a process proposal that delivers that complete product. A chemical-core process and a mineral-wool-core process require their own named material and production arrangements. Select the process against the actual product; do not transfer equipment lists from a different core system.

Avoid using a headline square-metre selling price as the entire demand case. Separate product types and customers that accept them. A factory producing one joint and core combination does not automatically serve every insulated-panel enquiry received by the sales team.

## Start with the scope the business can operate

For repeated sheet orders, a defined forming line is the direct investment. Establish incoming-coil control, output inspection, packing and a reliable delivery process. Add new product families only when their specific tooling and accepted production requirements justify them.

For repeated composite-panel orders, commission a full factory proposal. Include raw-material management, the selected core process, facing preparation, bonding or foaming as applicable, finishing, handling and product release. Assign people and costs to each stage. The plant purchase is incomplete if it ends at a machine list without the operating organisation that makes accepted panels.

For mixed demand, retain a sheet business and buy in specified insulated panels where that combination meets actual contracts. This creates a clear sales boundary: your own formed sheets and separately procured composite products. Keep product records and supplier responsibilities traceable rather than selling both under one undocumented performance statement.

## Two scope mistakes that distort the purchase

**Pricing a sheet line against an insulated-panel sales forecast.** In this illustrative proposal, the buyer forecasts revenue from finished insulated panels but budgets only a former and coil stock. Core materials, processing, product release and finished-panel handling are absent. Rebuild the proposal around the saleable composite product, or narrow the business plan to sheet orders.

**Buying capacity before defining the accepted panel family.** Another buyer groups every insulated-roof enquiry into one volume total. Customers actually request different cores, joints and product evidence. Separate the accepted product families and obtain a complete process offer for each supported family before calculating utilisation. Buy the exceptions externally when a separate investment lacks repeat demand.
