A large deck beneath a beam grid can look capable of covering an entire ceiling area, while the required task is fragmented by structure. The buyer needs to know **which named task strips the actual proposal addresses and which gaps remain between them**. Deck area alone does not establish continuous coverage, and a drawing of one open bay cannot represent every interrupted bay around it.

This guide builds a bay-by-bay coverage plan for comparing offers. The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) covers the complete shared area. Here the narrower output is a plan distinguishing required work, specifically addressed regions and unanswered regions, without calculating reach or prescribing a sequence of platform positions.

## Divide the required ceiling work by bay

### Use the actual structural grid

Identify bays and relevant beam lines on a shared building plan. Mark the actual strips or points where fixing, inspection or installation is required. A gross ceiling rectangle contains both task areas and structural interruptions; it should not be used as though every part were one open workface. Name the grid convention so the supplier's response can identify a specific task region. If a bay is not part of the purchase task, distinguish that exclusion rather than letting it appear as missing equipment coverage.

### Locate interruptions along the work strip

Show where beams, braces, projections or other actual objects divide the required work. Record their relevant extent and associate section references where the vertical relationship matters. Do not assume that a beam line is only a drawing symbol with no effect on the task. Equally, a structural interruption does not itself prove that another arrangement is required. It identifies a location for the actual equipment and task reviewers to consider, using their own configuration and project information.

### Keep the task object with each region

State the installation component and relevant tools or materials that the task owner proposes for the region. A continuous ceiling panel, a short trim piece and an isolated inspection point can involve different task-space questions. This does not authorize handling them on the platform. The [task-load guide](/blog/aerial-platform-worker-tool-material-load-planning/) develops the quantities and positions needed for load review. The coverage plan should identify which actual work the supplier is responding to, rather than treating all shaded ceiling area as an identical task.

## Keep task coverage separate from deck area

### Ask for actual candidate geometry

The [crawler ceiling-platform family](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms) describes a broad-deck architecture. Ask for the actual proposed configuration and its relation to the task regions. A photograph or a maximum deck dimension cannot establish which positions it addresses. Keep the base, supports and relevant lifting structure identifiable as well as the deck. The plan should allow the provider to say which configuration information is still needed, rather than forcing a coverage conclusion from an approximate rectangle.

### Use sections where plan shading conceals depth

A plan can show that a task lies beside a beam while concealing whether its face is above, below or recessed relative to that member. Associate the required section with that region. The [overhead-clearance guide](/blog/ceiling-platform-overhead-clearance-survey/) supplies the broader survey framework. Keep the vertical-face question distinct from coverage across the grid. A response to one section does not establish the full horizontal task extent, and a broad plan region does not establish access around a deep beam.

### Preserve building functions in the same plan

Identify access, doors, material areas and adjacent work that the project says must remain available. A proposed position can address a task region while depending on floor space that the project cannot release. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE personnel-platform guidance</a> frames selection around the actual job and site. It provides context for considering more than deck size, without determining the classification, coverage or permissible use of this particular configuration. No plan shading is a work authorization.

## Compare a continuous-area claim with a bay response

### Describe the hypothetical common workface

Imagine a **hypothetical three-bay ceiling assignment** in which a continuous installation strip is interrupted by projecting beam regions. Offer A describes a broad platform and marks one large coverage rectangle across the area. Offer B identifies the same required strip but responds to the central bay and names the two interrupted regions as unanswered. Neither drawing establishes final suitability. B exposes its partial scope; A's larger shaded area needs a configuration-linked explanation before the buyer can treat it as broader task coverage.

### Ask about the gaps rather than only the outer boundary

Request A's response for the named interrupted regions and the actual arrangement represented by its shading. Request B's missing task responses without assuming that another position or extension will provide them. The trade-off is between a simple area total and a more detailed statement of the task actually addressed. More claimed area is useful only when the configuration and task relationship support the claim. A remaining gap should stay visible in the purchase comparison rather than disappear into a general allowance for repositioning.

### Read partial coverage as a concrete procurement outcome

A clarified offer may cover the main strip while leaving an exception requiring another actual proposal. That is not the same purchase as complete coverage, and it need not be disguised as failure of the entire enquiry. Identify the unresolved task boundary and ask the relevant parties what concept should be investigated. The table below is a coverage-response structure for the hypothetical plan. It does not determine a number of positions, permitted travel or a route through the beam grid.

| Plan region or claim | Buyer must identify | Supplier response should clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central bay task strip | Actual work extent, face and component requirement | Which identified arrangement has been considered against it? |
| Interrupted beam region | Located projection and matching section | Is this task addressed, conditional on more data or outside the proposal? |
| Adjacent bay | Its own task and available floor relationship | Does the response specifically cover it rather than infer it from the central bay? |
| Large coverage rectangle | The regions the bidder intends it to represent | Which actual configuration and assumptions support that scope? |
| Remaining gap | Named task still required by the project | What separate equipment or project response is needed before comparison is complete? |

## Request a coverage statement tied to the plan

### Use a task map rather than a deck-area total

Ask the provider to respond by named region, with the actual proposed arrangement and unresolved information kept together. A short explanation for each different task relationship is more useful than a colour covering the whole ceiling. The buyer can then compare actual scope while the project retains responsibility for its task methods and site decisions. Keep candidate-specific statements separate; do not combine one offer's coverage of the middle bay with another offer's treatment of an exception and call that a single completed equipment proposal.

- Locate each required ceiling strip or point on the roof and structural grid.
- Mark interruptions and link the relevant task-height sections to their plan regions.
- State material requirements and task objects for regions that are genuinely different.
- Identify floor, support and access-route information for the actual candidate positions.
- Record destination and transport context so the equipment configuration being compared is clear.
- Ask which regions are addressed, which need more information and which remain outside the offer.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize coverage that relies on an unstated reach

A proposal may not fit the full assignment where a shaded area crosses an unresolved structural interruption, relies on an assumed personnel reach or omits a required bay. A larger platform or nominal height does not establish the missing coverage. This guide supplies no reach allowance, extension permission, operating sequence or number of positions. Any alternative access concept needs assessment against the same named task regions and site conditions, so an exception remains a real procurement question rather than a blank part of the plan.

## FAQ

#### Does a deck wider than a bay establish coverage?

No. The actual task face, structural interruptions and candidate arrangement still need review. An overall width cannot identify where people, materials or the lifting structure would be relative to the work. Ask for coverage of the named task regions using attributable equipment information. Do not convert a geometric comparison into a reach claim or a permitted task arrangement.

#### Should every bay have a different equipment proposal?

Not necessarily. Several genuinely common task regions can share an identified response, provided the relationship and assumptions are clear. Keep different or interrupted regions recognizable so common wording does not hide an exception. The goal is comparable task coverage, not a prescribed drawing count or an assumption that the equipment needs a separate position in every bay.

#### Can the beam-depth section replace the plan map?

No. It answers the vertical relationship at a particular location, while the plan identifies the extent and interruptions across the required task. Use both where necessary. A response to a recessed face in one section should not silently become coverage of the entire grid, and a broad shaded plan should not imply access around unseen vertical structure.

#### What if an offer leaves a small gap?

Identify the actual required task in that gap and ask what separate response is needed. Its physical size does not establish whether it is trivial or whether another concept is suitable. Keep the exception in the commercial and technical comparison until an actual arrangement addresses it. Do not rely on an unspecified reach, extension or repositioning allowance to close it.
