Choose a roof-supported roll former when the construction-stage structure is accepted for the complete operating station and the contractor can bring in coil, receive panels and remove the machine on schedule. Choose a ground-supported forming lift when a usable exterior position can deliver the specified panel to the roof. If neither route has a workable support and material path, price finished-panel supply or production at another location before buying equipment.

Both offers can put the panel outlet near roof level. What you are buying is the right to occupy a particular place for the whole production run. On one job the scarce resource is space beside a busy building. On another it is a roof bay that must remain available to the steel or roofing crew. Give each resource a named owner and a date for handing it back.

## What stays on the building?

A portable machine can be stationed on a roof for panel production. In a first-person interview, the operator of Retro Custom Metals describes bringing his own machine onto a roof to run panels to schedule. That establishes a purchasing option for a suitably designed job. The project structural engineer still needs the proposed station arrangement, support points, relevant reactions and the state of the building on each working date. [Operator interview in Rollforming Magazine](https://rollformingmagazine.com/rolling-in-salt-lake-city/)

Draw the machine, coil feed and receiving line on one plan. Mark where replacement coil arrives and where the crew takes possession of the longest sheet. A location can suit the first panel and still be a poor production station. For example, the next group of sheets may run toward the opposite edge, while the chosen station occupies the only place available for their final installation.

## What stays on the ground?

An exterior lift keeps the machine support off the building structure. The job still needs an accepted ground position, an interface at the receiving edge and a material supply arrangement. Put the outer edge of the actual supports on the civil plan. Then add delivery traffic, adjacent work and the route between positions. A route that is available during steel erection can be occupied by ducts, paving works or stored panels before roof production starts.

Ask the lift supplier for a section through the proposed machine outlet and the receiving point on a common survey datum. Give the roofing contractor the section to mark the space needed to take each panel away. If the lift can serve only part of a roof from released ground, compare a second position or split the panel order. Counting the whole building as eligible production overstates the benefit of the lift.

## Put the two working stations on the same tender drawing

| Enter this project information | Who supplies it | How it changes the award |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mark roof bays, construction stage, available dates and engineer-accepted support locations | Structural engineer and principal contractor | Determines which roof stations can enter the comparison |
| Enter the machine arrangement, operating reactions and coil locations for each offered configuration | Forming-system supplier | Gives the engineer the actual package to assess |
| Record exterior positions, support footprints, ground restrictions and handback dates | Site manager and civil engineer | Establishes which ground-supported runs fit the programme |
| Enter the outlet and receiving elevations from the same survey datum, offset and panel direction | Surveyor and roofing foreman | Identifies usable panel runs from each position |
| List incoming coil lots, replacement-coil handoffs and the party supplying each transfer | Materials coordinator | Exposes repeated handling that one quote leaves to the buyer |
| Mark the last panel zone and the plant removal route available on that date | Erection planner | Prevents the station from being enclosed by completed work |

The useful price comparison is the cost of delivering the same accepted panel schedule to the same receiving crew. For the roof station, include its initial transfer, project-specific supports, subsequent replenishment and removal. For the exterior lift, include site occupation, ground preparation, moves between positions and any panels supplied by another route. A crane used to deliver a machine onto a support is a separate purchase from a specialist crane-supported production system booked throughout forming. Follow the [crane-supported forming comparison](/blog/roof-forming-lift-vs-crane-supported-roll-former/) when the crane carries the production equipment during the run.

## Two purchasing mistakes that change the result

**Mistake 1 — Buying a roof station for the final structural condition.** Suppose a bidder quotes on the finished building drawings, but the intended production bay lacks the construction-stage support specified by the engineer on the booked date. The low machine price now buys an unusable date. Compare rescheduling, an engineered temporary arrangement and an exterior lift against the same handover milestone.

**Mistake 2 — Pricing only the first exterior position.** Imagine a clear service road beside the first bay. In the following phase the road becomes the tenant's delivery entrance and the second planned position disappears. Move those panel runs into a separately priced supply or receiving package. Credit the lift with only the runs it can serve on the agreed programme.

## RFQ attachments for both offers

- Roof plans with panel runs, direction, longest pieces and release sequence.
- Sections showing roof receiving points, exterior ground levels and offsets on one datum.
- Construction-stage engineer's brief for temporary roof loads and accepted support locations.
- Offered former, feed equipment and coil arrangement with installation and operating data.
- Ground layout with delivery roads, site restrictions and occupation dates for each position.
- Material schedule identifying coil arrival, replacement-coil handling and finished-sheet reception.
- Initial plant placement and final removal responsibilities with the associated work dates.
- A price schedule separating production equipment, temporary works, transfers and site attendance.
