Buy an equipment roof hatch when the largest planned replacement unit can pass through its clear opening and the complete transfer route remains available with the lid open. Choose a purpose-designed removable roof section when a whole machine needs a larger clear bay and the owner can provide the handling space, temporary weather protection and reinstatement that each removal requires.

Start inside the building. A pump that passes through the existing service door gives the owner little reason to pay for roof access. A larger process assembly that cannot pass through the wall, floor or internal maintenance route gives the roof opening a clear purpose. Ask the plant operator which components actually leave as a unit, how often and during which shutdown. The biggest item on the asset register is not necessarily the item that governs replacement.

## Test the journey in both directions

Trace the outgoing assembly from its normal position to a place where the service contractor can receive it. Repeat the exercise with the incoming replacement in the delivery condition the supplier requires. A replacement can arrive on a base with protruding connections; the outgoing item can require containment on its return. Use the actual transport arrangements supplied by the responsible specialists for each movement.

On a section through the building, record the governing envelope at the plant position, below the opening, through the open roof and at the receiving location. Include obstructions from permanent steelwork, services and the open hatch leaves. Give the lifting specialist responsibility for assessing the equipment movement and clearances. A roof contractor bidding the weathering detail needs the resulting clear envelope, the roof layout and a defined opening period.

## What the hatch quote should include

Give the hatch bidder the controlling clear envelope and the location of the opening on the as-designed roof. Request a drawing showing the usable opening with the leaves in their operating position, the supporting curb, weathering interfaces and access needed to operate and maintain it. Ask the enclosure designer to return the detail at the field sheets, flashings and any adjacent drainage feature.

A permanent hatch can simplify a repeated exchange if the site can always reserve the path beneath and above it. A later services bridge or permanent pipe run can erase that advantage. Put the keep-clear space on the building owner's drawings and assign responsibility for reviewing later alterations. Ask the owner who will inspect the closure after routine service and who owns repairs to the surrounding roof.

Price the work the next exchange will actually require. Include the plant handling contract, access for the people operating and checking the opening, any isolations identified by the site operator, and the owner’s weather contingency. Compare opening and closing the hatch during a permitted outage with the alternative's complete outage. Record the hours the plant remains unavailable under each accepted exchange sequence.

## What the removable roof section adds

A removable section moves a portion of the enclosure as a separate item before the process equipment passes. Get a drawing defining its boundary, structural supports, drainage and joints with the fixed roof. Ask who supplies the section, its project engineering and the method-specific information the specialist handling contractor needs. Separate the price to move and store the section from the price to exchange the plant itself.

Trace the section to its temporary location and back. A bidder who prices only a clear bay overhead leaves the buyer to find a place for the removed unit. Record the site area, temporary support responsibility and protection against damage while the plant work proceeds. At reinstatement, allocate reclosure of weathering details, any replacement consumables, acceptance of the roof and release of the building to its operator.

## Purchasing mistakes at opposite ends of the route

**Mistake one.** A buyer chooses a hatch from the largest casing dimension and an advertised nominal opening. The opened leaf occupies part of the required transfer path, and a fixed service below blocks the turn. Request a coordinated, dimensioned route through the installed opening before accepting the hatch.

**Mistake two.** A roof section is large enough for an incoming replacement machine, but the site plans to park the removed section on the same space reserved for the delivery vehicle. Ask the logistics and handling leads to price the sequential movements, temporary support and reinstatement against one site plan before authorising the section.

## RFQ for the roof opening and surrounding sheets

- Identify plant items, exchange frequency and the approved service window.
- Supply both outgoing and incoming envelopes in their handling condition.
- Mark intervening structure, services and protected space along the route.
- Request a hatch drawing showing clear passage with leaves open.
- Request the alternative removable section boundary and site storage position.
- Identify structural, handling, temporary covering and reinstatement responsibilities.
- Attach the fixed-sheet schedule, controlled section and perimeter details.
- Define roof acceptance, operating handback and responsibility for future alterations.
