Buy installation access for the fire-separation solution selected in the project's approved fire strategy. Where the responsible fire designer accepts both a retractable fire curtain and a fixed compartment wall for the specific boundary, compare their complete installed and handed-over packages, including upper interfaces, specialist attendance and future service access. Choose the compliant package that meets the required room use and completion date at the lower complete cost. A cheaper platform or a clear floor layout is never a basis for substituting one fire-separation solution for another.

For the access buyer, the immediate job is to price all the work at that boundary. A curtain quotation ending at the moving fabric and a wall quotation ending below the structural interface do not describe comparable completed work. Put the same accepted separation and room handover at the end of both options.

## A curtain system and the boundary above it are separate scopes

[Coopers Fire's product catalogue](https://www.coopersfire.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Full_Product_Catalogue_v23.pdf) identifies applications in which its fire curtains replace non-loadbearing walls. Its current [FireStopping overview](https://www.coopersfire.com/products/firestopping/) also explains the importance of the barrier above an active curtain and its interfaces. This distinction matters: the static work above a curtain complements it; it is not the fixed-wall alternative being compared here.

Use the fire designer's issued section to locate the complete separation. Mark the headbox and its support, side interfaces, the boundary to the construction above and every service crossing shown in the design. For a fixed wall, mark its supporting construction, junctions, openings and service crossings on the corresponding section. The responsible specialists provide their system details and acceptance evidence.

Do not convert these drawings into improvised installation instructions. Use them to assign attendance, work locations, material handling and access for each specialist. The fire-system designer and installer retain responsibility for the proposed separation and its acceptance.

## Put the same end point on the comparison

The comparison ends when the selected boundary has been installed, its required inspections and commissioning have been completed, and the owner has the specified operating and service handover. It does not end when personnel finish attaching the visible components.

If the room brief excludes a permanent wall, remove that option rather than assigning it an artificial cost advantage. If the accepted design requires a wall, buy the wall installation package. Commercial comparison begins only between options that satisfy the same project brief and accepted fire strategy.

## Buy the work left around the headline component

A retractable curtain changes the room arrangement during normal use, but its installation still includes fixed high-level components and interfaces. Price personnel access for those actual locations. Keep the curtain specialist's handling method separate from the platform proposal; a personnel deck is not automatically the appliance for raising a headbox or other heavy assembly.

For the wall route, describe the complete build-up and the work stages the installer actually proposes. A broad workface suits repeated personnel activity only when the offered deck configuration, route, floor and work envelope match the task brief. Price the trade's separate material handling and resupply as well as the personnel platform.

Include the additional access visits in the project schedule. A specialist who returns for inspection or commissioning needs a usable route and work position at that later date. If fit-out blocks that position, the access plan has lost part of its completion scope. Agree the retained zones and timing with the main contractor before pricing one continuous equipment occupation.

## Two gaps that hide inside a low headline price

**Mistake: comparing a curtain component with a completed wall.** In a hypothetical tender, the curtain price excludes the upper boundary, controls attendance and specialist handover while the wall price includes its full separation. Rebuild both quotations to the same designer-issued completion boundary before choosing the installation package.

**Mistake: releasing the access equipment before specialist completion.** A hypothetical contractor finishes visible installation and returns the platform, then the specialist needs a high-level visit after other trades occupy the route. Include the actual completion visits and retained work positions in the programme and access quotation from the outset.

## Eight attachments for the access RFQ

1. The fire designer's issued solution reference and the options accepted for this boundary.
2. A coordinated section locating the complete separation and upper construction.
3. The specialist's task list and required work positions, measured from building datums.
4. A service-crossing schedule with the responsible trade for each interface.
5. The separate material-handling plan for major components.
6. A construction-stage route and floor plan showing proposed base and support areas.
7. The installation, inspection, commissioning and handover attendance schedule.
8. The owner's retained inspection or service-access requirements and affected fit-out zones.
