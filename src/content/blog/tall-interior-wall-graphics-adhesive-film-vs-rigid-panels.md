**Buy adhesive wall film for a tall mural when the selected film passes the substrate trial and the owner accepts its seams and future removal scope. Buy rigid printed panels when the protected printed surface is required and the selected system's supports and visible joints meet the brief. If both qualify, buy the lower complete installation and service offer for the same defined refresh or removal visit. Shortlist ARC-F25 where the offered deck can serve several adjacent personnel positions along the wall. Send both bidders the same scaled artwork and wall survey before paying to print the full image.**

The decision starts in the occupied building. A transit concourse buying a permanent image and a retailer planning its next campaign will judge the same painted wall differently. Give the supplier the required artwork life, the next expected change and the wall condition the contractor is expected to return at removal.

## Buy the sample before the print run

Give the printer current artwork, the dimensions of the graphic field and the viewing positions where seams and colour matter. Include corners, openings, columns, access panels and anything that will cut through the image. Ask for one representative junction and one sample on the actual receiving finish. Agree who signs off the physical sample and records the accepted site conditions.

[3M's wall graphics guidance](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/graphics-signage-us/windows-walls-floors-campaign/transforming-windows-walls-floors/) identifies adhesion testing as a selection step and notes that large graphics can require multiple pieces. Give the installer the actual wall paint or coating records and the locations of repairs and repainting. A sample from a different wall does not resolve whether the selected film suits the high wall's finish. Price local substrate work, protection of occupied areas and any programme needed between wall acceptance and application.

[Inpro's Aspex printed wall protection](https://www.inprocorp.com/products/aspex-printed-wall-protection-aspw-drih-printed-wall-protection) is a commercial example of a clear sheet back-printed with custom imagery. Specify the actual sheet, attachment and accepted joints in the purchase. Price structural or surface work only where the selected assembly requires it. Ask which selected sheets can be replaced locally and who restores their edges and the wall finish. An image divided into sheets offers no automatic promise of quick changes.

## Two graphic buying mistakes that add a return visit

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A tenant signs off a print proof at a desk and buys the film before the installer trials the wall. Repairs have introduced a second finish in the highest band. The selected film has no accepted trial there, so the crew loses its booked access window. Tie print release to the located wall sample and reserve time in the schedule for the chosen finish.

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A venue orders numbered rigid sheets based on a promise of quick section replacement. Its offer omits a replacement arrangement for a sheet finishing against a service hatch. Before award, ask the panel provider to identify how that exact section is restored and quote the corresponding access and disturbance.

## Price the second visit while both bids are open

Ask each supplier for a price against the same future event, such as removing the whole mural at the end of a tenancy or replacing a single damaged section. Record which pieces need printing again, which surrounding areas need access, and whose contract covers the finish underneath. Accept the proposals against the agreed wall life and actual appearance, then compare preparation, artwork, installation and the defined future visit.

For film, agree the required wall condition after removal and who provides making good. Set that condition before counting the next graphic change as a simple repeat booking. For rigid sheets, ask the printer what it retains to reproduce a numbered piece and the panel supplier what it needs to release and replace that piece. Budget longer possessions if a future visit requires removing adjacent artwork or relocating floor displays.

## Attach the graphic and access drawings to the RFQ

Include these eight RFQ attachments.

1. Scaled artwork and the signed colour and seam brief.
2. Dimensioned elevation with the artwork origin and all interruptions.
3. Wall finish history, located sample results and required repairs.
4. Accepted film or panel proposal and work required at the outer edges.
5. Piece dimensions, masses and specialist presentation needs.
6. Personnel tasks, tool inventory, workface heights above local floor and measured offsets.
7. Building entry, turning space, floor records and occupation windows.
8. Required next refresh or removal task, delivery destination and handback scope.
