A crawler roll-forming lift raises a compatible roof-panel forming machine towards a planned production and receiving level. Its tracks are easy to see, but they do not answer the whole ground-support question. The proposed configuration may use different supporting arrangements during travel, positioning and elevated work. A statement about crawler contact pressure cannot automatically answer what the ground must support at an outrigger or another work-state support point.

Begin by identifying how the proposed equipment meets the ground in each relevant state. Then connect those locations to the actual soil, pavement, slab or underground construction. This guide helps buyers ask a precise support question before selecting a position. It does not provide a ground-bearing calculation or support design. The [crawler roll-forming lift product reference](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/) introduces the equipment family; the configuration-specific technical information defines the cases that the project must review.

## Understand which equipment state loads the ground

### Separate track contact from work-state support

Tracks describe a mobility arrangement. They do not, by themselves, establish whether the proposed machine works on its tracks, uses outriggers, or relies on another configuration-specific support arrangement. Obtain that distinction from the equipment documents before asking a site engineer to assess the position.

The distinction changes the question. A travel case may involve a particular contact representation along a route, while a stationary work case may supply reactions at defined support points. Neither input can be substituted for the other. Even where the same visible components remain near the surface, the approved equipment information must establish the applicable load path and conditions.

### Identify what the lift will carry

The supported equipment includes the agreed roll-forming line and whatever attachments or materials the relevant state document includes. A discussion based on the lifting structure alone may omit a significant part of the intended arrangement. Conversely, adding every material expected anywhere on the site can create a case that does not describe the machine at all.

Confirm the boundary with the equipment party: which forming modules, auxiliaries and materials belong to the stated configuration, and what changes between arrival and work? The [forming-line mounting interface review](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) addresses how the line is matched to its support. This page follows the resulting equipment case down to the site.

### Keep the proposed geometry attached to the case

An equipment support input is useful only with its stated assumptions. Where the issuing party identifies a particular height, arrangement or operating condition, retain it alongside the supplied reactions or contact information. Do not detach a convenient number from the configuration and apply it to every proposed location.

Request clarification if the information refers only to a product family or does not say which state it represents. Model labels, maximum-height descriptions and photographs cannot supply the missing assumptions. The buyer's task is to obtain the compatible inputs; the competent project reviewer determines how they apply to the site's supporting construction.

## Read the site below the visible surface

### Distinguish the surface from the supporting construction

Concrete, asphalt, paving and compacted-looking ground describe what can be seen. They do not fully describe the layers, structure or condition carrying the equipment. Record the actual location and obtain the available construction drawings, investigation records and relevant current observations. Let the appointed reviewer identify what further evidence is necessary.

A paved yard can include repairs, changes in build-up and buried features. An indoor floor can have a supporting structure that requires a different review from ground-bearing construction. Use the [indoor floor and ground-pressure guide](/blog/indoor-aerial-platform-ground-pressure-guide/) where the proposed location involves a building floor; do not treat all hard surfaces as equivalent.

### Locate local features within the support area

Plot covers, drainage channels, trenches, service routes, slab edges and known changes in construction against the proposed equipment support locations. An overall site plan may identify a service corridor without showing whether a particular support point falls within its influence. The relationship must be resolved at the scale needed by the project reviewer.

The <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/excavations.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE guidance on excavations</a> explains why underground structures, services and loads from nearby plant matter to excavation planning in Great Britain. It helps frame questions about adjacent work. It does not supply a setback, support capacity or design for a roll-forming lift.

### Notice construction changes that records may miss

An outdoor forming position can remain in the same place while the construction around it changes. Record nearby excavation or backfilling, temporary material stacks, other equipment positions and temporary works against the proposed support locations. Include when those activities are expected to be present. Separate plans for the forming lift and the neighbouring work may describe different moments in the construction programme.

Consider a hypothetical roof-forming proposal beside a yard used for deliveries. The equipment support case describes elevated forming; a separate site record describes temporary material storage near a drainage repair. The buyer needs to establish whether that storage is expected to remain during the proposed forming period and whether the ground specialist's response covers that combined site condition. Separate reports for each activity do not answer that question unless their assessed conditions connect. Equally, proximity alone does not establish that the activities affect the same supporting construction.

Give the specialist the locations, construction-stage information and anticipated overlap. Do not combine equipment reactions with a guessed stack load or infer an influence boundary from a photograph. Water, excavation and repair changes also remain part of the review context: a note saying “dry when visited” is a dated observation, not an enduring ground property.

### Check which support locations the records actually cover

A report for the site can contain different zones, exclusions or investigation locations. Ask the ground specialist to relate that coverage to the proposed equipment supports. The useful overlay shows the equipment arrangement and the relevant site-record boundaries together. Having a report in the project folder does not establish that every support location falls within its assessed area or assumptions.

Consider a hypothetical yard where the proposed support arrangement crosses an original paved area and a later drainage repair. The available report describes the original construction; the repair record identifies a trench but leaves its final extent unclear. The purchasing question is whether the equipment case can be assessed at the proposed locations with that evidence. Neither the continuous-looking surface nor the absence of visible damage answers it. The specialist may need information about the repaired area and its relationship to the other support locations before returning a bounded conclusion.

Record a boundary that is uncertain as uncertain. Do not draw an invented dividing line or allocate part of a support load to each side using a simple area ratio. Likewise, shifting the machine on the sketch to place a support inside a documented zone changes the proposed geometry and needs review. The buyer's comparison should show which candidate positions have relevant evidence, which cross different construction zones, and which still need investigation. It should leave capacity and support-design decisions with the appointed specialists.

### Keep the approach route distinct from the final position

The machine may cross several surfaces before reaching the proposed work area. A reviewed work position does not establish the suitability of those route segments, and a reviewed travel route does not establish the later work-state support condition. Link the two reviews while retaining their separate cases.

Use the [initial access-route survey](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-access-route-survey/) to identify where the surface or geometry changes on the journey. This article concentrates on the support inputs that those locations require. It does not define a movement method, allowable slope or permissible state of travel.

## Match equipment support cases to actual locations

### Use a case map that exposes missing information

Give each proposed location a reference on the site plan. Alongside it, identify the equipment state and the document supplying the support input. The following table shows why several records may be needed even for one machine. Its entries are planning prompts, not assumed operating permissions.

| Proposed location or state | Equipment information to request | Site information to connect |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Arrival or unloading interface | Actual transport and proposed transfer arrangements | Defined area, supporting construction and responsible transport party |
| Initial crawler route | Permitted travel state and relevant support input | Surveyed route segments, transitions and local features |
| Positioning area | Configuration and cases applicable to the proposed positioning | Geometry and current condition of that specific area |
| Elevated forming position | Work-state support arrangement and issued case assumptions | Exact support locations and competent ground or structural review |
| Later revised position | Changed configuration or location information | Evidence for the new area, with changed conditions identified |

### Send the specialist the inputs that belong together

- Identify the crawler lift and forming-line configuration, with current technical-document revisions and inclusions.
- State the proposed height and geometry where they define the equipment support case; retain the source datum.
- Describe the coil, forming module or other material state only where it belongs to the supplied load basis.
- Map travel access, slope and surface transitions separately from the final stationary work position.
- Locate outriggers or other supports according to the configuration documents, with their applicable input references.
- Provide ground, floor, slab and underground records for the actual locations, with nearby construction, temporary storage and other equipment identified for the same proposed period.
- Identify transport or unloading interfaces that use the same ground, without assuming those cases are already covered.
- Record the site's weather and wind change procedure where it affects the proposed state or review assumptions.
- Confirm destination documentation requirements and who can return the final ground or structural decision.

### Ask for a bounded answer

The useful return from the appointed specialist identifies the equipment information, state, site zone and surrounding conditions assessed. For the hypothetical yard, it should make clear whether the response covers the forming position with the nearby storage present, a different construction stage, or neither until more information is available. A conclusion based on the area being clear is not evidence for a proposal that assumes simultaneous storage there.

Where the two activities have separate reviewers, ask which party will resolve the relationship between their assessment boundaries. A suitable answer may identify further information rather than a capacity or permission. Preserve that distinction when comparing candidate positions: one may have evidence for the intended period, while another has evidence only for a site condition the programme does not currently provide.

Keep this conclusion separate from the equipment operating authorization. A completed ground assessment does not establish line compatibility, wind limits, roof receiving readiness or permission to travel while elevated. Each decision uses its own evidence. Connecting them in the project record prevents one approval from quietly expanding into another.

## Compare positions without assuming a ground solution

### Compare the full effect of a changed position

If one proposed support location cannot be assessed, another position may be worth investigating. However, changing the position can change the forming outlet relationship, access route and material supply. Compare those effects with the [roof-level production planning guide](/blog/roof-level-roll-forming-long-panels/) before treating relocation on the drawing as a solution.

A position with clearer site records may simplify the evidence exchange while making the panel receiving geometry harder to achieve. A position close to the roof may have the opposite trade-off. The appropriate choice depends on the actual equipment arrangement and project review, rather than which rectangle looks most convenient on the site plan.

### Recognize a mismatch between two plausible documents

Consider a hypothetical proposal with a surface drawing for a paved yard and an equipment sheet describing crawler travel contact. The intended activity is elevated forming using a separately defined support arrangement. Both documents may be authentic, yet together they do not answer the work-position question. Request the applicable work-state input and the site information needed for its actual locations.

Similarly, a general floor-use description may not address a particular local support case. The solution is to clarify the two bases with their issuing parties. Do not create an editorial bearing value, average reactions equally between supports or choose a plate size to make the documents appear compatible. Those are engineering decisions outside this selection guide.

### Know when the proposed ground arrangement may not fit

The proposed arrangement may not fit where the required support case cannot be matched to a defensible ground or structural assessment, or where the resulting project constraints cannot be accommodated by the equipment and site plan. Missing underground records, unresolved local features or unavailable configuration inputs should remain explicit selection constraints.

Possible changes to position, construction sequence or support provision require assessment by the responsible parties. This article cannot prescribe mats, plate dimensions, foundation works or minimum distances. A photograph of another machine on a similar-looking surface does not resolve any of those choices.

## FAQ

#### Does a crawler chassis remove the need for support review?

No. The review must address the actual travel and work-state arrangements. Tracks do not establish the capacity of the ground, buried structures or individual locations used by other supports.

#### Can machine mass divided by track area settle the question?

No. That simplification does not establish the applicable equipment case or the response of the site's supporting construction. Use the configuration-specific inputs requested by the competent reviewer.

#### Is a paved surface stronger than an unpaved surface?

Appearance alone cannot decide suitability. The actual construction, condition, underlying features and proposed support case matter. The reviewer determines the necessary evidence for each location.

#### Does a change of forming machine require another review?

It can change the configuration or assumptions behind the support information. Compare the new line with the assessed basis and ask the responsible parties which affected equipment and site decisions must be revisited.
