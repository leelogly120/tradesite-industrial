Add roof-panel forming when committed orders match the proposed tooling and material route, the missing production resources are funded, and the complete internal route either costs less than accepted outside supply or fills a documented delivery/product gap that route cannot serve. Keep buying finished panels when those conditions are absent. Use purchased panels for a defined market trial before treating prospects as production demand.

Owning coil stock does not by itself justify a former. The relevant advantage is whether those resources serve the actual panel specifications, quantities and delivery requirements. Existing customers who buy flat steel are not automatically committed buyers of a new roof-panel range.

## Identify the orders the service centre can actually win

Build a separate panel-order register. Record controlled sections, materials, lengths, quantities, customer acceptance requirements and delivery dates. Use awarded orders as the committed base. Show actual repeat-order history separately as a continuity scenario without treating it as booked future work. Keep enquiries and prospects outside the funded base unless they become accepted orders.

For each order, compare a purchased finished-panel route with a complete internal route. The comparison should end with the same accepted, delivered product. Do not compare raw coil cost on one side with a finished delivered sheet price on the other.

The [Formtek discussion of in-sourcing roll forming](https://blog.formtekgroup.com/sourcing-roll-forming-production) illustrates the distinction between an existing business and a newly internalised manufacturing process. Its example belongs to that manufacturer and product context. Your service centre needs its own panel-demand and process case; no outside company's outcome is a forecast for your factory.

## Credit only the resources that fit the new job

List usable existing premises, material storage, handling equipment, people, customer relationships and delivery arrangements. Give each resource a defined role in the proposed cell. The site and equipment specialists assess the actual handling and support arrangement; a resource's presence on the asset register does not establish suitability for every new duty.

Then list the gaps: profile tooling, forming/cutting, receiving, panel inspection, long-product storage, packing, order control, support and the associated component supply. Some gaps are filled by new machines, others by competent people or contracted services. Price them all before calling the expansion a small addition to the existing yard.

The premises row needs actual product lengths and movement paths. A yard handling compact stock can have an entirely different route from one dispatching long finished roof panels. Do not use total building area as a substitute for that measured plan.

## Keep a purchased-panel route during the transition

An existing service centre can test a defined panel offer without immediately owning every process. Use a named finished-product provider, controlled product references and actual delivery responsibilities. Record the orders served and the costs or service gaps that ownership would address.

Choose internal forming when the evidence supports a complete operating model and the equipment proposal serves the nominated products. Continue buying when demand is intermittent, the missing capabilities are substantial, or the accepted outside route remains the better fit. A mixed outcome is valid: manufacture supported repeat sections and buy exceptions under their own identified supply route.

Avoid using a broad “metal roofing” market estimate to fill gaps in the actual order book. Your technical and commercial case needs the products the proposed tooling can make, the customers it can serve and the resources needed to deliver them.

## Two hypothetical diversification mistakes

**Failure case: coil customers become assumed panel orders.** The business treats its steel sales volume as committed demand for one new roof section. Customers actually need several different products and bought-in components. Build the controlled panel-order register and price purchased exceptions before selecting tooling.

**Failure case: existing equipment receives every new duty for free.** The expansion budget assigns material handling, inspection and dispatch to busy existing resources without a suitability or availability plan. Credit only the resources with a defined accepted role, then price the actual gaps.

## Send the service-centre expansion brief

Attach eight items to the RFQ:

1. Supported panel orders with controlled sections and lengths.
2. Materials, available stock families and representative input samples.
3. The proposed manufactured and purchased product split.
4. Existing equipment identities and assigned duties.
5. Measured premises, receiving, storage and dispatch routes.
6. Production, inspection and support roles the business will provide.
7. Complete product trial and accepted-output requirements.
8. Destination, utilities, installation and delivery boundaries.
