Treat 40HQ as a packing question, not as proof a lift fits a box. If the container is the hard limit, open [ARC-T25HQ](/products/arc-t25hq-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift-40hq/) and ask for a packing study. [ARC-T25](/products/arc-t25-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/) at the 25m class and [ARC-T18](/products/arc-t18-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/) at the 18.5m class are working-height screens, not container results. A complete truck often does not belong in a 40HQ at all. A lift module might. Which one you are shipping is confirm on RFQ until the packing plan says otherwise.

## Say which object is supposed to enter the box

Buyers write "40HQ" when they mean three different things: the lift module, the former, or a whole vehicle. Those are different drawings. Name the object. If you do not know yet, write unsure and ask the quote to list what would have to be removed for a container to be even plausible.

### Height class is not a packing length
18.5m and 25m describe catalog working height. They are not the length of a crate. Do not subtract a container size from the catalog class and call the remainder a folded dimension. Folded size is confirm on RFQ.

### Local chassis changes the box
If the chassis is built in the destination country, the container holds a module. If the chassis is included from origin, you may be shipping a vehicle that never sees a 40HQ. Mark chassis included, local, or unsure on the same line as the container question.

### What a packing study has to show
The study should restate the object, the removed parts, the crate count it is willing to own, and the work still required after the box opens. A sentence that only says "40HQ possible" is not a study.

### After the box
The roof still needs the outlet height you marked. Packing does not change the eave. Keep the site sketch in the same RFQ so the container conversation does not replace the machine conversation.

## The trade-off is a smaller box against a longer rebuild

Shipping a module in a container can satisfy a freight limit and leaves chassis, registration, and reassembly in the destination. Shipping a complete vehicle can simplify the first start-up and may miss the container entirely. The constraint is the interface between those choices. ARC-T25HQ exists so that packing route can be discussed as its own catalog model. It is not a certificate that your configuration is inside a 40HQ today.

## Failure modes

The 40HQ plan may not fit when the object in the box is still "the whole machine," when the former and the lift are packed by two people who have not shared a drawing, or when the destination needs a road-ready truck the week the box arrives. It may not fit when someone has already promised a crate count in a tender. Pull that sentence. Ask for the packing study before the tender repeats it.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Freight lines that belong on the RFQ

- Work height you still need at the roof after unpacking
- Roof geometry, so packing does not become the only topic
- Profile and coil data if ARC-RF8 shares the shipment
- Wind is a site issue; note the site anyway
- Ground at the destination yard where the box is opened
- Access from the port to the chassis builder, if the chassis is local
- Outrigger spread the site must still accept after assembly
- Destination country and port
- Chassis included, local, or unsure
- Transport mode you hope to use, stated as a question
- Voltage the destination will provide at start-up
- Documents the packing plan must match later

#### Does ARC-T25HQ fit a 40HQ?
Not as a standing claim. It is the catalog model for that packing conversation. The study decides.

#### Can a complete truck go in a 40HQ?
Treat that as unproven. Say if the chassis is even in the shipment.

#### Should the former be in the same box?
Only if you want one packing plan. Otherwise mark it separate.

#### What number do I need from site?
The outlet height and the door the crate must pass. Measure those. Do not invent the folded length.
