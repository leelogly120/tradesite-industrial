A 40HQ conversation starts with what you need at the destination, not with a freight rate. A truck-mounted roll-forming lift might be quoted as a module, as a module plus a former, or as a conversation that also includes a chassis. None of those sentences means the cargo has been shown to pass a container door. [ARC-T25HQ](/products/arc-t25hq-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift-40hq/) is the catalog model for a packing-route discussion. It is not a statement that a unit goes in a box.

## Decide the arrival state

### Module, former, chassis
Write three yes/no/unsure lines. The lift module. The former, often [ARC-RF8](/products/arc-rf8-roll-forming-machine/) if you are buying it. The chassis. A truck-mounted catalog model such as [ARC-T25](/products/arc-t25-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/) can be discussed without a container. Open T25HQ only when the container is a real constraint you are willing to study.

### Complete at the port or complete at the yard
Some buyers need a vehicle that can leave the port. Others need crates that a local builder finishes. Those are different packing lists. Say which yard does the assembly and who lifts the crate there. Do not use the phrase "ready to work" unless you also list the power, the crew, and the registration, because those are rarely in the box.

### What you are not buying from a photo
The photographs show crawler equipment on carriers and a suspended module. They are reference photographs of other arrangements. They do not show a container, a lashing plan, or your chassis.

## Ask packing questions that have answers

### Pieces and lifting points
How many pieces do you expect, and which of them have known lifting points today? If the answer is "none yet", the container study has not started. A study needs masses and lifting points from the arrangement being quoted, not from a similar photo.

### Door, length, and the thing that surprises people
People fixate on the door and then lose a week on length. Ask the quotation to list the longest piece and whether it is allowed to be split. Splitting a frame is a design change. It is not a note for the forwarder to solve alone.

### Local road after the box
A module that leaves a container still has to reach the site or the chassis shop. Name that road and the truck you expect to use. Container fit does not answer bridge height on the highway after unpacking.

## The trade-off is a smaller shipment against a longer destination job

Breaking a lift into container pieces can make the ocean leg thinkable and the destination assembly longer. Shipping larger pieces on other transport can do the opposite. The constraint is the interface between the piece list and the people who will bolt it together. Until those people and those pieces are named, a 40HQ label is a wish. Compare ARC-T25 and ARC-T25HQ as two enquiry paths, not as a promise that one of them has already passed a packing test.

## A container route may not fit

It may not fit when the longest piece cannot be split, when lifting points are unknown, when the destination has no one to assemble the module, or when you actually need a crawler that will live on a low-bed. It may not fit when the chassis must be part of the same booking and nobody has chosen the chassis. Stop the freight question until those blanks are filled.

## Packing-route inputs

- Work height and the outlet the package must serve
- Roof geometry the module will stand beside
- Profile and former mass if a former travels in the same shipment
- Wind is a site issue, not a container issue, but name the site anyway
- Ground at destination after the box is opened
- Access from the port or yard to the chassis builder
- Outrigger spread you hope the site can take, or unknown
- Destination country and the port of arrival
- Chassis included, local, or unsure
- Container assumption stated as a question, not a result
- Voltage the destination will actually provide
- Documents the packing plan must match later

## FAQ

#### Does ARC-T25HQ fit in a 40HQ?
The page does not say that. It is a packing-route catalog model. Fit is a study of the actual pieces.

#### Should every truck-mounted enquiry mention a container?
No. Mention it only when it is a constraint. Otherwise you will get a packing discussion you did not need.

#### Can the former travel in the same shipment?
It can be asked. Name the former and keep its mass on the RFQ. The answer is not on this page.

#### Who signs the packing plan?
The quotation should say which document will control it. Until that document exists, do not book the box from a website.
