Choose external eaves gutters when the building geometry and accepted discharge layout allow collection at the outside edge with practical maintenance access. Buy internal box gutters when the roof arrangement requires internal collection and the designed support, overflow route and servicing package are funded together. Mark the water collection lines on the roof plan before releasing panel lengths. For a simple roof with an accepted external discharge route, start the comparison at the eaves; internal collection needs a specific geometric or operational reason.

This decision comes before choosing the downstream pipe system. An internal collection point and an external gutter each need a complete drainage design. Procurement selects between accepted building arrangements; it should not move a gutter merely to shorten an available sheet or simplify a forming-machine quotation.

## Follow the roof edge around the building

On a simple pitched roof, the designer can evaluate collection along an external eave. Between adjacent roof slopes, behind a parapet or at another constrained boundary, an internal collection arrangement needs its own detail. Ask the architect and drainage designer which alternatives actually fit the site, including property boundaries, entrances and adjacent structures.

For each layout accepted by the architect and drainage designer, trace the normal discharge path and the specified overflow path to their respective receiving destinations. Record what lies below each route: loading doors, occupied space, pedestrian areas or equipment. The comparison needs the consequence and required management of each flow path, not a general claim that one gutter type cannot overflow.

Where panels are handed over at the eaves, the former outlet, receiving edge and collection detail meet the same construction boundary. Give the equipment planner the released edge section so the metal-sheet package ends at the intended location.

## External collection: buy the accessible edge

An external gutter proposal should identify brackets or other support, the roof-sheet end, fascia or eaves construction, outlets and downpipes. Include access for installation, cleaning and replacement. A gutter visible from the yard is not necessarily reachable when a canopy, loading operation or neighbouring property occupies the approach.

Use the external route where the full arrangement fits the building and its owner can maintain it. Request the accepted overflow detail rather than assuming outward location alone resolves the issue. [Lysaght's gutter-overflow publication](https://lysaght.com/document/dcs-download/4955) illustrates that overflow provision is a defined part of a rainwater-goods arrangement. Its details relate to specified products and conditions; the project's drainage designer selects the applicable solution.

Price roof-side and external access separately where they are different tasks. Include temporary occupation of a loading lane or other working area in the installation programme. The useful external option is the one the contractor can construct and the owner can service.

## Internal collection: buy its support and future route

An internal box gutter is a construction package with supporting work. [Lysaght's flashing guide](https://lysaght.com/document/dcs-download/1691) describes box-gutter support at the sides and below, together with fall and an appropriate drainage outlet. These requirements explain why the price of a folded channel is incomplete.

Request the structural and drainage designers' coordinated section. Identify the supporting construction, waterproofing interfaces, movement provisions, outlets, overflow and access. For a valley between roof fields, establish the sequence for installing the gutter and adjoining roof sheets before either field is manufactured.

Keep the owner involved in maintenance access. Internal gutters behind plant or deep between roof slopes need a planned route and work area. Obtain the maintenance provider's scope for the actual design and include any access installation or repeated mobilisation in the comparison.

Use drawing datums for sheet ends and gutter positions. A nominal building width does not automatically equal the ordered roof-panel length. Resolve those references through the roof detailer before sending a cut list to the producer.

## Two hypothetical errors at the collection line

**Mistake: a cheap internal channel excludes its support.** A bid for the valley between two warehouse roof slopes includes folded gutter metal but omits its supporting assembly and access for completion. Add the accepted structural support, associated roof details and installation resources before comparing it with a completed external-eaves package.

**Mistake: panels are ordered from the old eave.** The architect changes to an internal collection line after the buyer releases full-length sheets. The ordered end position no longer matches the roof detail. Hold the affected sheet marks until the revised collection section is issued; release unchanged roof areas separately where the programme permits.

## Eight attachments for the metal package

1. Accepted external and internal collection-layout alternatives.
2. Roof sections with panel-end and gutter datums.
3. Catchment, discharge and overflow design references.
4. Supporting construction and interface details.
5. Metal weather-sheet profile, material and lengths.
6. Separate gutter, outlet and flashing supply schedules.
7. Construction and maintenance access arrangements.
8. Destination, production location, power and work-release dates.
