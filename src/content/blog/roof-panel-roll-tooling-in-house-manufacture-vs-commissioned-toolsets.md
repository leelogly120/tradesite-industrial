Choose an internal roll-tool department when recurring new profiles and revisions can fund specialist design, machining, inspection and production trials as a complete capability. Commission complete toolsets when releases are occasional, the design capability is unavailable internally or an established toolmaker can bear the development work more economically.

## Decide whether you are buying tools or a new department

Start with the products you expect to release, their drawing status and the customer orders that justify them. Distinguish a new geometry from a replacement of existing rolls, routine regrinding and a changeover between sets already owned. Those activities can use some of the same people but have different economics. Compare the repair team's actual design experience and equipment with the new forming sequence before assigning the development work internally. Treat its existing resources as available only for the specific stages they can actually deliver.

## Follow one new profile from drawing to repeatable production

Pick a real planned roof section. Identify who reconciles the panel drawing with the released roof assembly, chooses the forming progression, designs the tools and defines critical dimensions. Then list material procurement, machining, any specialist finishing, tool identification, assembly, measurement, trial coil and corrective work. Ask the line supplier what information it can provide about its actual stands, drives, alignment and accepted loading. For both internal and external options, name the party authorized to release the finished section for production.

Use the same acceptance milestone in both estimates. A box of machined rolls is different from a fitted, trialled set making accepted parts on the named machine. Include access to the line for correction runs, the coil and scrap from those runs, and any lost production while the development team uses it. Ask the external bidder to identify the commissioning location and any required use of the buyer's machine. On the internal bid, obtain subcontract quotes for hardening, coatings or specialist metrology that the department cannot perform. Include the outside purchase in the option that still needs it.

## Cost the development pipeline at realistic intervals

Build a program calendar rather than dividing annual sales by the price of a roll set. Several profile launches at the same time can exceed the availability of one experienced designer even when the machine shop has spare hours. A quiet period creates payroll and machine ownership costs without a new release. Give value to shorter iterations only when an earlier release serves an identifiable customer order or reduces a real external expense. Retain a quoted specialist backup for operations where internal capacity would otherwise determine the entire launch date.

**Mistake: The machining budget omits profile development.** A producer funds a CNC machine because external tooling invoices are high. Its comparison omits an experienced roll designer, measurement fixtures and repeated trials on production material. Rebuild the estimate up to a customer-accepted profile, then decide which stages genuinely justify being brought inside.

**Mistake: The external offer ends before the final trial.** A buyer accepts the cheapest set of rolls. The agreement does not name the production machine or the party responsible if the formed section misses the released drawing. Request machine interfaces, trial material, acceptance characteristics and correction terms on the complete-set offer.

A phased approach can be a genuine third bid. The producer can retain profile definition and acceptance, commission tool design and manufacture, and bring selected inspection or maintenance operations inside where demand is already proven. Describe the recurring scope and rights for each stage. Reassess a full internal department when the funded portfolio demonstrates several repeatable development cycles and the business can staff those cycles through absence and turnover.

## Eight RFQ details for a toolroom or commissioned set

1. Send the funded profile program and current customer release dates.
2. Attach section drawings, material ranges and manufacturing rights.
3. Identify the production machine, stand interfaces and trial availability.
4. Describe design, machining, finishing and metrology scope.
5. List acceptance dimensions, trial coil and records required.
6. Request responsibilities for design revisions, corrections and spare rolls.
7. Provide internal staff, subcontract processes and competing workloads.
8. Ask for commercial terms to the same accepted production milestone.
