**Share the capital cost of one roll-forming lift only when the participating contractors need a compatible equipment package, accept an executable reservation priority and appoint one accountable equipment manager. Keep sole-company ownership when control of urgent work is essential or the partners cannot settle use, upkeep and exit arrangements before purchase.** Splitting the invoice does not divide one machine into two available production systems.

This decision concerns control of a single asset across independent businesses. The [one-versus-two-package guide](/blog/one-roll-forming-lift-vs-two-concurrent-projects/) addresses the separate question of how much equipment concurrent jobs need. Solve that quantity question first; shared funding does not resolve overlapping production dates.

## The arrangement has to survive a difficult week

Test the proposed ownership arrangement against an ordinary disruption: one roof is delayed, a second contractor needs the machine on its reserved date, and maintenance is due before transfer. If both companies assume priority, the purchase has no workable allocation rule.

A useful commercial plan names the reservation process, the event that fixes a booking, the party able to approve a change and the alternative when a booking cannot be honoured. Keep maintenance periods and transfer time in that calendar. The partners' actual agreement must settle their respective obligations; this guide provides the operational inputs, not a legal ownership structure.

Joint ownership is strongest where each participant brings compatible, staggered demand and accepts common management of the equipment. It is weakest where everyone expects immediate access during the same roofing season but contributes only a fraction of the capital. Use actual order calendars rather than optimistic statements that the firms will cooperate.

## Buy the common configuration, not a compromise that fits nobody

List the sites the shared investment must serve. Record each former, profile, outlet datum, receiving arrangement, access route and support position. If the companies use different formers, the technical package must identify each reviewed pairing and any change equipment. Avoid buying a preferred model first and negotiating compatibility afterwards.

The crawler-supported apparatus below shows a complete site position. In a joint purchase, every participating contractor needs its own site review; a satisfactory arrangement for one owner's building does not settle another owner's roof geometry.

![Red crawler lifting apparatus with a raised forming module beside a building.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-crawler-roof-side.webp)

*Give each owner's required site and former combination a separate row in the common equipment specification.*

Keep optional ceiling-platform work separate. A forming lift raises a compatible roll former; a personnel work platform is a different equipment duty. A joint buyer should not count both activities as interchangeable utilisation without a specifically defined and reviewed supply configuration.

## A decision sheet for the participating contractors

Complete each contractor's entries separately before agreeing the shared decision. Differences reveal whether the purchase works operationally.

| Decision item | Each contractor supplies | Shared decision to record | Owner and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required equipment duty | Named sites, former details and outlet/receiver dimensions from the standing-surface datum | Configurations that the asset must serve | Technical leads decide whether a common asset is practical |
| Reserved use | Arrival, production and full release dates by actual order | Booking priority and change-authorisation rule | Programme managers decide who obtains the asset when dates conflict |
| Capital scope | Equipment, integration and initial supporting resources requested | Exact funded package and excluded local resources | Purchasing leads prevent an apparently shared price from hiding separate equipment |
| Routine running cost | Expected usage and records available from each job | Agreed cost-allocation basis and payment timing | Finance leads keep ongoing use funded |
| Maintenance provision | Required planned work and local service resources | Maintenance manager, reserved periods and funding route | Equipment manager protects availability between users |
| Handover condition | Inspection records, defects and outstanding work at release | Receipt, defect reporting and release procedure | Outgoing and incoming managers establish asset condition |
| Additional work | Requests to change tooling, former or configuration | Named change approver and cost owner | Technical manager prevents unilateral changes to a shared specification |
| Exit scenario | Expected ownership horizon and obligations to current jobs | Route for resolving withdrawal, sale or replacement | Business owners decide whether the arrangement remains workable |

For costing, keep a distinction between costs incurred by keeping the asset available and costs caused by a particular job. Use actual offers and recorded usage for the agreed allocation; there is no universal fair split. A half-capital contribution does not by itself define half of every operating expense or priority date.

## Put one person in charge of the equipment record

Assign an equipment manager with access to the configuration documents, maintenance records, inspection history and approved change register. Each operating contractor still supplies its site planning and competent staff. Central custody of records prevents one business from receiving a machine whose recent changes are known only to another business.

At handover, record the actual configuration, accessories, documented condition, outstanding work and the destination's receiving contact. Do not describe transport completion as readiness for production. The next site's assembly, support, former interface and release work remain part of that site's plan.

The folded equipment on the carrier below represents the transfer boundary. Price that move, its coordination and the next-site readiness work against the contractor receiving the package under the agreed allocation.

![Folded crawler equipment carried on a separate low-bed trailer.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261027-transport.webp)

*Record custody and outstanding work at transfer; the next production period starts after the receiving site's required preparation.*

## Two joint-ownership mistakes

**Equal funding, incompatible priority.** In this hypothetical arrangement, both partners pay the same capital share and each promises a customer exclusive access in the same week. The problem is not the contribution percentage. Amend the committed programme, provide a separately sourced production alternative or buy sufficient complete capacity before accepting both promises.

**Use is paid for, maintenance is not.** Another group allocates fuel and transport to each user but leaves planned maintenance unfunded and unassigned. The asset reaches the next contractor with required work outstanding. Establish the maintenance budget, named manager and protected work periods before choosing joint ownership.

Where sole control is essential, purchase within one company and treat any service to another business as a separately defined commercial arrangement. Do not assume that this alternative includes rental availability or an ARCLIFT-operated service.
