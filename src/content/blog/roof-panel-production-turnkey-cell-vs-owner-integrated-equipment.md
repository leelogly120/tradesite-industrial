Buy a complete production-cell package with one named integration lead when your team cannot engineer and manage the interfaces between separate machines. Buy separate modules when a competent owner team controls the common product definition, interfaces, installation and complete-cell acceptance. Compare both routes against accepted roof-panel output at your premises, rather than comparing one former with a fully equipped workshop.

“Turnkey” is useful only when the written scope defines the handed-over result. The word does not automatically include foundations, cranes, utilities, stock, operators or every machine shown in a sales layout. Equally, owner integration is a real engineering and project-management duty, not an unpriced gap between several purchase orders.

## Draw one cell boundary before asking for prices

Start at the material handover and end at the accepted finished-panel handover. Identify coil receipt and storage, material presentation, feeding, forming, cutting, runout, inspection, packing and dispatch for the nominated product. Some activities use existing equipment or separately purchased services. Record those arrangements rather than placing everything inside an unspecified machinery allowance.

The supplier's proposed layout should show the included equipment and the connections to buyer-supplied facilities. Give each excluded item a named owner and delivery milestone. If the line depends on an existing crane or fork truck, the site team must establish its suitable role from the actual handling plan and equipment records; a workshop photograph does not establish that capability.

## Compare an integration lead with a competent owner team

A single-lead package is the first route to price when the buyer needs one provider to coordinate the machinery interfaces and demonstrate the agreed cell result. Its technical schedule should state the included machines, buyer works, interfaces, installation responsibilities, documentation and acceptance order. A supplier selling the former alone is not the cell integration lead unless the contract expressly assigns that wider role.

Separate module purchasing is appropriate when the owner can review the combined engineering, manage revisions, coordinate providers and accept the completed cell. The budget must include that team and the work it performs. A lower combined equipment invoice loses its apparent advantage when the missing integration work is later purchased as an urgent extra.

## Complete the boundary worksheet

| Decision record | Buyer-filled evidence and datum | Owner | Effect on the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted product | Profile drawing revision ___; material ___; length/order mix ___ | Product lead | Gives all providers the same production target |
| Cell handovers | Input handover location ___; accepted output and packing endpoint ___ | Operations lead | Makes the two quotations cover the same work |
| Equipment split | New machines ___; retained machines and IDs ___; bought-in services ___ | Buyer engineering lead | Defines each provider's actual scope |
| Physical interfaces | Controlled layout ___; material direction ___; connection drawing revisions ___ | Integration lead | Identifies work omitted from standalone module prices |
| Site works | Floor/layout reference ___; utilities ___; handling resources ___ | Site engineer | Prices the buyer works needed for either route |
| Integration resources | Named engineering, project and document-control roles ___ | Buyer project manager | Tests whether owner integration is a deliverable option |
| Trial order | Material lot ___; cut list ___; inspection method ___; accepted output endpoint ___ | Quality lead | Establishes one complete-cell demonstration |
| Handover | Documentation ___; training scope ___; responsibility and support records ___ | Project manager | Defines the production cell left with the buyer |

Use actual drawings and responsibilities in the interface row. “By others” without an identified provider does not complete the scope. An owner-integrated offer becomes comparable only when those activities are assigned, priced and scheduled.

## Accept a production result, not a collection of successful deliveries

Delivery of each module is a milestone, but it does not demonstrate that the combined cell serves the order mix. Specify a representative trial that follows material through the agreed boundary. Record accepted product, rejects, interruptions and the work performed by the trial crew. Use the same defined result for both procurement routes.

The test should also identify what remains outside the cell. If finished panels are handed over before packaging, do not compare that result with another offer demonstrating packed, labelled output. If the buyer supplies material handling or inspection, those resources must be available for the actual trial and continuing production.

## Two hypothetical integration failures

**Failure case: several module quotations become an assumed complete line.** The buyer prices the former, input equipment and runout separately but assigns nobody to coordinate their physical and control interfaces. The project later needs additional engineering and modified components. Assign an integration lead and a common acceptance result before ordering the modules.

**Failure case: a turnkey label hides the buyer works.** The supplier's price excludes handling equipment and site utilities, while the buyer budgets them as included. The delivered machines cannot complete the planned trial. Issue the explicit cell boundary and price every buyer-supplied activity before comparing totals.

7. Named integration, installation and documentation responsibilities.
8. Complete-cell trial and handover requirements, destination and delivery scope.
