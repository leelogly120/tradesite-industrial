# Pick and Carry Crane vs Spider Lift: Which Is Right for Your Project?

Choosing between a pick and carry crane and a spider lift is one of the most common equipment decisions construction managers face when planning height access operations. Both machines serve distinct purposes, yet their capabilities overlap in ways that make selection challenging. According to the International Powered Access Federation (IPAF), the global aerial work platform market reached $15.2 billion in 2025, with tracked platforms growing at 8.3% annually — driven largely by demand for versatile height access solutions in confined urban environments.

This guide breaks down the technical differences, application scenarios, cost considerations, and safety standards for both equipment types. Whether you're managing a warehouse ceiling installation, a building facade renovation, or an industrial maintenance project, understanding these two machine categories will help you make an informed procurement decision.

## What Is a Pick and Carry Crane?

A pick and carry crane is a compact, mobile crane designed to lift loads while in motion. Unlike tower cranes or crawler cranes that must be stationary during lifting operations, pick and carry cranes can transport loads across a job site without requiring setup time for each move. They typically feature a telescopic boom mounted on a wheeled or tracked chassis, with outriggers for stability during heavy lifts.

The defining characteristic of a pick and carry crane is its ability to move while carrying a suspended load. This makes them invaluable in factory environments, warehouses, and construction sites where materials need to be relocated frequently. According to Crane Market Intelligence Report (2025), pick and carry cranes account for approximately 12% of the mobile crane market in Asia-Pacific, with India and China leading adoption rates.

Modern pick and carry cranes like the [ARCLIFT tracked equipment series](/products/tracked-lifts) incorporate advanced hydraulic systems with load moment indicators (LMIs) that continuously monitor capacity based on boom angle, extension, and chassis inclination. The [ARCLIFT-CTL18.5A](/products/arclift-ctl18-5a), for example, offers an 18.5-meter working height with a 24.3-ton self-weight, providing the stability needed for heavy load handling in confined spaces.

Key specifications that define pick and carry cranes include:
- **Load capacity while moving**: Typically 1–25 tons depending on model
- **Working radius**: 5–20 meters from the center of rotation
- **Travel speed**: 2–8 km/h under load
- **Ground pressure**: 0.3–0.8 kg/cm² (tracked variants)

These machines comply with AS 2550.1 (Cranes, Hoists and Winches — Safe Use) and EN 13000 (Cranes — Mobile Cranes) standards, ensuring global safety compliance.

## What Is a Spider Lift?

A spider lift — also known as a tracked aerial work platform or crawler-mounted boom lift — is a compact, self-propelled platform designed specifically for personnel elevation. The name "spider" comes from the four hydraulic outrigger legs that extend outward, giving the machine a distinctive arachnid-like footprint. This design provides exceptional stability on uneven terrain while maintaining a minimal ground pressure footprint.

Spider lifts excel in environments where traditional boom lifts cannot operate: narrow corridors, uneven ground, sensitive flooring, and indoor spaces with height restrictions. The global spider lift market is projected to reach $2.8 billion by 2027, growing at a CAGR of 6.9% (source: Grand View Research). This growth is fueled by increasing demand in facility maintenance, tree care, and construction sectors.

The [ARCLIFT tracked lift platform](/products/tracked-lifts) series represents the evolution of spider lift technology. Models like the [ARCLIFT-CTL18.5B](/products/arclift-ctl18-5b) combine 18.5-meter working height with full remote control operation, allowing operators to position the platform precisely without being on the machine. The 26-ton self-weight and 11-ton load capacity make it suitable for both personnel and light material lifting tasks.

Spider lifts typically feature:
- **Platform capacity**: 200–500 kg (2–5 persons)
- **Working height**: 10–50+ meters
- **Outrigger spread**: 3–8 meters depending on model
- **Ground pressure**: 0.15–0.4 kg/cm² (significantly lower than wheeled alternatives)

These machines are certified under EN 280 (Mobile Elevating Work Platforms) and ANSI A92.20 (Design, Calculations, Safety Requirements, and Test Methods for MEWPs), which mandate specific stability, structural, and safety requirements for personnel-lifting equipment.

## Key Differences: Pick and Carry Crane vs Spider Lift

Understanding the fundamental design philosophy differences between these two equipment types is essential for making the right choice. While both operate on tracked chassis and use hydraulic systems, their engineering priorities diverge significantly.

### Primary Function

The most critical distinction lies in their intended use. Pick and carry cranes are **load-handling machines** — their primary purpose is to lift, move, and place heavy materials. Spider lifts are **personnel-carrying platforms** — their primary purpose is to safely elevate workers to height. This distinction has profound implications for safety standards, design requirements, and regulatory compliance.

According to OSHA regulations (29 CFR 1926.1400), cranes and aerial work platforms are governed by entirely different safety standards. Using a crane to lift personnel requires specific rigging configurations, safety platforms, and additional compliance measures that many contractors overlook.

### Load Capacity and Distribution

Pick and carry cranes prioritize **concentrated load capacity**. The [ARCLIFT-CTL18.5C](/products/arclift-ctl18-5c) offers 20 tons of lifting capacity — enough to handle steel beams, precast concrete panels, and heavy machinery. Spider lifts prioritize **distributed platform loads**, typically 200–500 kg, designed to carry personnel, tools, and lightweight materials.

| Feature | Pick and Carry Crane | Spider Lift |
|---------|---------------------|-------------|
| Primary function | Load handling | Personnel elevation |
| Typical load capacity | 1–25 tons | 200–500 kg |
| Platform size | Hook/attachment point | 1.2–2.4 m platform |
| Travel under load | Yes | Limited/No |
| Outrigger requirement | Optional for light lifts | Mandatory |
| Safety standard | Crane standards (EN 13000) | MEWP standards (EN 280) |

### Mobility and Terrain Adaptation

Both equipment types use tracked chassis for superior terrain adaptation compared to wheeled alternatives. However, their mobility profiles differ. Pick and carry cranes are designed to **move while carrying loads**, requiring robust chassis engineering to handle dynamic forces during travel. Spider lifts are designed to **move to position, then stabilize** — they travel unloaded to the work area, deploy outriggers, and then elevate the platform.

## Application Scenarios: When to Use Each

### Best Applications for Pick and Carry Cranes

Pick and carry cranes are the optimal choice when your project involves:

**Industrial Material Handling**: In manufacturing plants and warehouses, pick and carry cranes move heavy components between production stages. A typical automotive factory might use pick and carry cranes to relocate engine blocks (500–800 kg each) from machining stations to assembly lines — a task that would require multiple forklifts or overhead cranes otherwise.

**Construction Site Logistics**: On construction sites, pick and carry cranes transport structural steel, precast panels, and heavy equipment. The ability to move while carrying loads reduces cycle times by 30–40% compared to stationary crane operations (source: Construction Equipment Association).

**Bridge and Infrastructure Work**: For bridge construction and repair, pick and carry cranes position heavy girders and deck panels. Their compact footprint allows operation in confined right-of-way areas where larger cranes cannot access.

## Cost Analysis: Total Cost of Ownership

Equipment cost decisions must consider total cost of ownership (TCO) rather than purchase price alone. The TCO framework includes acquisition, operation, maintenance, and disposal costs over the equipment's useful life.

### Acquisition Costs

Pick and carry cranes typically range from $50,000 to $300,000 depending on capacity and height specifications. Spider lifts range from $80,000 to $500,000 for equivalent heights, reflecting the additional engineering required for platform stability, outrigger systems, and personnel safety features.

The [ARCLIFT product range](/products) offers competitive pricing as a direct manufacturer, eliminating distributor markups that typically add 15–25% to equipment costs.

### Operating Costs

Operating costs include fuel/energy, operator wages, and insurance. Tracked diesel-powered machines consume 8–15 liters of fuel per hour under typical operating conditions. Electric models reduce energy costs by 60–70% but require charging infrastructure. Insurance premiums for spider lifts are generally 20–30% higher than for cranes of equivalent value, reflecting the higher liability associated with personnel lifting operations.

### Maintenance Costs

Annual maintenance costs typically represent 3–5% of acquisition cost for both equipment types. However, spider lifts require more frequent inspection cycles due to the critical nature of their personnel-lifting function. EN 280 mandates six-monthly thorough examinations, while crane inspection intervals under EN 13000 are typically annual.

## Safety Considerations and Regulatory Compliance

Safety is the most critical factor in equipment selection, particularly when personnel elevation is involved. Both equipment types are subject to rigorous safety standards, but the regulatory frameworks differ significantly.

### Crane Safety Standards

Pick and carry cranes must comply with:
- **EN 13000**: European standard for mobile cranes
- **AS 2550.1**: Australian standard for crane safe use
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1400**: U.S. crane safety regulations
- **GB/T 3811**: Chinese crane design standard

Key safety requirements include load moment indicators, anti-two-block devices, rated capacity indicators, and operator certification. According to OSHA data, crane-related incidents account for approximately 42 fatalities annually in the United States, with overloading and inadequate setup being the leading causes.

### Spider Lift Safety Standards

Spider lifts must comply with:
- **EN 280**: European standard for mobile elevating work platforms
- **ANSI A92.20**: U.S. MEWP design and safety standard
- **AS 1418.10**: Australian standard for MEWPs
- **GB 25849**: Chinese MEWP safety standard

The [ARCLIFT tracked lift series](/products/tracked-lifts) incorporates comprehensive safety systems including height control, amplitude control, violation operation alarms, and LCD level gauges — all designed to meet or exceed these international standards.

### Operator Training Requirements

Both equipment types require trained operators, but certification pathways differ. Crane operators typically require CCO (Certified Crane Operator) certification or equivalent national credentials. MEWP operators require IPAF PAL Card certification or equivalent training under ANSI A92.24. The training investment for spider lift operators is generally lower (2–3 days vs. 5–10 days for crane operators), reducing labor costs for projects requiring multiple operators.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a pick and carry crane to lift personnel?

While technically possible with proper rigging and safety platforms, using a crane to lift personnel is generally discouraged and may violate local regulations. OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1431 specifies strict requirements for personnel platforms on cranes, including proof testing, safety factor requirements, and additional inspection protocols. For personnel elevation tasks, a spider lift certified under EN 280 or ANSI A92.20 is the safer and more compliant choice.

### What is the maximum working height for spider lifts?

Modern spider lifts offer working heights from 10 meters to over 50 meters. The [ARCLIFT vehicle-mounted series](/products/vehicle-mounted-lifts) provides options from 12 meters (CVL12) to 31 meters (CVL31), covering the vast majority of commercial and industrial applications. For specialized requirements, custom configurations can extend working heights further.

### How do tracked platforms perform on uneven ground?

Tracked platforms significantly outperform wheeled alternatives on uneven terrain. The continuous track distribution reduces ground pressure to 0.15–0.4 kg/cm², compared to 2–5 kg/cm² for wheeled equipment. This allows operation on soft ground, gravel, slopes up to 15°, and uneven surfaces that would be unsafe for wheeled machines. The [ARCLIFT tracked series](/products/tracked-lifts) features LCD level gauges that continuously display chassis inclination, helping operators maintain safe operating angles.

### What maintenance is required for tracked lift equipment?

Tracked lift equipment requires daily pre-operation inspections (hydraulic fluid levels, track tension, safety device function), 250-hour intermediate services (filter replacement, lubrication), and annual thorough examinations by qualified inspectors. The ARCLIFT product range is designed for easy maintenance with centralized lubrication points, accessible hydraulic components, and comprehensive maintenance documentation.

### How do I choose between diesel and electric power?

Diesel-powered machines offer greater power output and longer operating cycles (8–12 hours), making them suitable for outdoor construction sites. Electric machines produce zero emissions and lower noise levels (65–75 dB vs. 85–95 dB for diesel), making them essential for indoor applications and noise-sensitive environments. Many contractors maintain both types to cover the full range of project requirements.

## Conclusion: Making the Right Choice

The decision between a pick and carry crane and a spider lift ultimately depends on your primary application:

- **Choose a pick and carry crane** if your primary need is moving heavy materials across a job site, with occasional personnel lifting handled separately.
- **Choose a spider lift** if your primary need is safely elevating workers to height for installation, maintenance, or inspection tasks.
- **Consider both** if your project scope includes both heavy material handling and personnel elevation — many large construction sites and industrial facilities maintain both equipment types.
