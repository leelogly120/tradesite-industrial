**Buy a field repair when a competent service provider identifies a fault it can correct and test at the actual site, with the machine safely isolated and the required working area available. Return it to a workshop when the accepted repair needs facilities, specialist equipment or controlled tests that the site cannot provide.** If the defect is uncertain, purchase a bounded diagnostic visit first. Keep the machine out of service until the responsible party accepts its return to use.

The lowest call-out fee seldom describes the lowest repair cost. A technician can diagnose a fault on site and still require a workshop to finish the job. A workshop can offer a firm repair scope while excluding recovery of a disabled machine from a congested building. Obtain both proposals for the same asset, reported symptoms and required handback.

The green machine in the yard photograph has the deck low, separate side boom extended and feet deployed. A disabled platform can be left in a different position again. Give the actual provider photographs, machine identification and observed condition. Recovery decisions belong to the competent provider working to machine-specific information.

## Put a ceiling on diagnosis, then price the authorised job

Identify who decides that the equipment is withdrawn, who controls the site and who can authorise repairs for that serial number. Send the provider the fault history and operator observations without asking an unqualified operator to reproduce a hazardous condition. The diagnostician should state the scope of a visit, expected deliverable, necessary access and what triggers a workshop transfer. Make further work subject to the owner's approval.

[AWD Australia describes both workshop capability and on-site service](https://www.awdaustralia.com.au/service) for access equipment. That illustrates the two service locations. Who can work on your actual machine and what they can complete there depends on the equipment, competent provider, applicable requirements and site facilities. Request the provider's scope for the identified asset, including parts availability, test facilities and return documentation.

For field work, get an actual method and resource proposal from the service firm. The building owner must release the repair area, access, isolation arrangements and suitable conditions. Price security, environmental controls or site attendance only where they affect the quoted job. Find out whether the provider can complete the required checks before the site permits the machine back on duty. A fitted part awaiting a mandatory test is still an unavailable machine.

## Compare hours unavailable, not just hours invoiced

Construct a timeline for each option from withdrawal to accepted return. Field repair can avoid outward and return freight, but an unavailable part, restricted site hours or a second service visit can consume that advantage. A workshop can consolidate disassembly, specialist work and testing, yet transport booking and the queue at the facility can extend the absence. Ask for project-specific commitments and distinguish firm appointments from estimates.

Price replacement equipment for the work that must continue. A delayed construction activity has a cost only if it really affects the programme. A substitute hire that covers the work already captures its cost; do not also charge the entire lost activity as an additional consequence. State which fees survive a transfer from diagnosis on site to repair in the workshop. A diagnostic report that travels with the asset can prevent a second provider charging for the same accepted assessment.

For hired equipment, the hire contract identifies who can authorise work and when the returning asset changes custody. Where the fleet owner can dispatch a released substitute, price the exchange that restores the customer's work first and the specialist recovery of the defective asset afterwards. Compare that interruption with waiting on site for the repair to finish. Record the substitute unit's actual delivery, hire start and acceptance separately from the removed machine's off-hire and workshop release.

## Two mistakes that leave a repaired platform unavailable

**Mistake one — comparing a complete workshop service with a field call-out.** A hypothetical owner chooses the smaller number. Investigation then reveals specialist testing missing from the initial site offer. Ask both bidders for the same return-to-use result and a priced escalation path.

**Mistake two — booking a haulier for a normally mobile machine.** The asset cannot reach the agreed collection point in its current condition. Name a competent recovery provider and obtain its accepted transfer arrangements before fixing the collection price or promising a return date.
