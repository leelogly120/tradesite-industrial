**Choose integrated forming and curving when its accepted product range serves committed orders and a complete trial/programme meets the required dates at lower whole-cell cost. Choose fully resourced separate stages when the integrated offer cannot serve the required input range or overlapping straight/curved schedule, or the separate cell costs less for the same accepted output. Compare finished panels through handling and release.** Two machine frames still need separately available people, handling and working space.

This decision begins after the business has chosen to manufacture mechanically curved panels. It compares two owned process architectures. The earlier [curving equipment versus specialist supply decision](/blog/roof-panel-curving-machine-vs-specialist-panel-supply/) establishes whether owning the process fits the accepted order book at all.

## Integrated and separate are real process categories

[WUKO's Master 0420 description](https://www.wuko.at/en/products/wuko-master-0420) identifies a roof-panel machine with integrated curving. [Schlebach's RBM description](https://schlebach.de/en/product/round-arch-curving-machine-rbm-25-rbm-32-rbm-38/) describes curving pre-profiled panels. These establish different equipment categories. Their product ranges and features belong to the specified maker/configuration; they are not capabilities assigned to an unidentified former.

Write down the section-forming function, the longitudinal-curving function and the accepted finished output for each proposal. Identify where the panel changes from one stage to the next and which provider owns the complete product acceptance. A combined frame and two separately quoted machines still need their own complete supported scope.

The roller-train photograph below makes the section-forming stage visible. Describe the supplied functions explicitly in the proposal. The purchasing comparison needs the separately defined curving result as well as the controlled cross-section, regardless of how the equipment is packaged.

## Start with the order calendar, not the floor plan

Group committed work into straight panels, the accepted curved-panel families and any other products served by the proposed curver. Record the required delivery periods and the resources allocated to each. Keep prospective orders outside the funded base case.

An integrated proposal ties its offered functions to one machine's real availability. Ask the provider to define how the quoted configuration handles the required straight and curved products and the transitions between them. Use a representative complete-order trial and the actual operating calendar to test that proposal.

Separate stages provide useful scheduling independence only when they have the required inputs, people, controls, support equipment and receiving space at the same time. Two machines sharing one unavailable operator or one blocked handling route are not two independently resourced production streams. Enter those shared dependencies in the programme before claiming a capacity advantage.

If the curver is intended to serve panels from more than one former, identify each accepted input product. Compatibility belongs to those actual product/configuration combinations. A similar-looking seam from another line is insufficient input definition for the purchase.

## Complete a whole-cell comparison

Use the same accepted output schedule and ownership period in both columns. Enter real provider responses, measured layout information and quoted amounts. The decision is between complete production cells, rather than a combined machine amount and an incomplete pair of equipment prices.

| Purchase field | Integrated route entry | Separate-stage entry | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted products | Actual sections, materials and finished curve families: ___ | The same output family and each accepted upstream input: ___ | Product lead fixes comparable sellable output |
| Straight orders | Required quantities, dates and offered production route: ___ | Allocated former/calendar and supporting resources: ___ | Planner tests straight-work availability |
| Curved orders | Complete cycle and changeover allowance: ___ | Former/curver calendars and intermediate movement: ___ | Production lead tests the committed curved schedule |
| Handover state | Defined material state between offered functions: ___ | Identified pre-profiled input and its acceptance: ___ | Equipment integrator closes the process interface |
| Physical layout | Whole supplied outline, input and finished-output envelopes: ___ | Both machines, transfer, supports and intermediate storage: ___ | Plant planner measures the complete floor requirement |
| People and support | Named duties, attendance and service scope: ___ | Separate and shared resources by stage: ___ | Owner establishes workable simultaneous/sequential duties |
| Trial release | Finished section, curve, surface and handling criteria: ___ | Matching criteria through both supplied stages: ___ | Quality lead accepts the same complete product |
| Full cost basis | Quoted cell, support, changeover and ownership items: ___ | Quoted stages plus transfer, storage and integration: ___ | Buyer compares the same output and planning period |

Keep the measurement datum for the finished curve in the product acceptance records. The equipment providers define the actual supported input and output arrangements; the production planner measures their occupied and moving envelopes on the receiving plant layout. Neither a machine footprint nor a short sample describes the entire moving panel.

## Count the transfer before choosing separate stages

Draw the pre-profiled panel's route from the former to the curver, including the nominated receiving, temporary storage and handling arrangements. Assign their supply and acceptance to named parties. Include the whole material movement in the trial and programme, rather than beginning the comparison with a panel already waiting at the second machine.

For the integrated route, use the actual provider's supplied input/output and handling requirements. Keep any external support equipment and rejected or exceptional products in its scope and cost schedule. A combined package still has a complete production boundary that the buyer must resource.

The truck/workshop photograph below shows a large forming assembly carried as a separate physical item. It is a useful reminder to price the offered production package through installation or relocation. Include the real equipment modules, receiving space and transfer responsibilities in the plant plan.

Choose the supported lower-cost route when both deliver the accepted schedule. Retain separately resourced stages when the required independent workload or input-product range cannot be served by the integrated proposal. If neither cell can deliver the committed work, change the proposed scope or purchase the uncovered output externally before ordering equipment.

## Two hypothetical failures in the architecture choice

**Hypothetical failure case: straight-panel orders lose their production window.** A buyer commits the combined machine to a curved campaign without allowing for overlapping straight orders and complete product transitions. Build one real calendar for the integrated proposal and compare it with the fully resourced separate-stage alternative before allocating the work.

**Hypothetical failure case: a cheap curver has no usable receiving path.** Another buyer retains its former but omits transfer and intermediate support from the new curver budget. The offered cell has an unassigned material movement between the two machines. Close that interface on the plant drawing and include its handling and acceptance in the complete comparison.
