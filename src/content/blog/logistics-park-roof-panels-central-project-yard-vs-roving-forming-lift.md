Choose a temporary central production yard when an accepted internal distribution route can deliver the specified panels to every assigned building. Choose a roving elevated forming package when each building has an accepted production position and receiver, and the complete moves fit its release programme. Use a defined mixed route for exceptions rather than assuming one arrangement covers the whole logistics park.

This is a production-and-material-flow decision within one project. A central yard makes panels for several buildings; a fleet depot stores or prepares equipment for dispatch. A permanent regional factory is another business again. Compare the two temporary construction arrangements against the same park-wide panel order.

## Give each building a production-to-receiver row

Use the current site plan, roof sections and main contractor's building-release schedule. For every building, identify the panel product, longest required pieces, quantities, first receiver and receiving dates. Then map the internal connection from a proposed central yard and the candidate equipment position for roving production.

An internal road on the master plan is not automatically an available panel route. Buildings, laydown areas, gates, active deliveries and later road closures can change it. The logistics specialist supplies an actual handling and movement proposal for the ordered lengths. Procurement records that proposal, its permitted occupation and the included resources.

For the roving option, obtain the full equipment configurations used for production and transfer, including any separately transported former. Count coil supply, site preparation, receiving and removal as part of each building's package. The existence of tracks or a road vehicle alone establishes neither loaded travel permission nor a transfer duration.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Building demand | Panel IDs, profiles and lengths ___ | Same released product schedule ___ | Roofing planner keeps the output equivalent |
| First receiver | Coordinates and level from project datum ___ | Same receiver plus proposed outlet point ___ | Surveyor and receiving team define the required endpoint |
| Production area | Reserved yard, services and whole period ___ | Accepted stand area and period at each building ___ | Main contractor establishes real occupation |
| Material movement | Actual finished-panel route and handling scope ___ | Coil/equipment approach and replenishment route ___ | Logistics specialist tests the different loads and envelopes |
| Release dates | Production and delivery slots by building ___ | Arrival, production and full departure events ___ | Programme lead exposes conflicting building demands |
| Support inputs | Yard equipment and distribution cases ___ | Proposed lift/former cases at each position ___ | Equipment providers and site engineers define support assessment |

| Whole campaign cost | Yard, forming, handling and final removal ___ | Forming, repeated moves, occupation and removal ___ | Commercial lead compares the same completed supply work |

Where the route differs between buildings, retain separate rows. Averaging all distances or all panel lengths can conceal the one building that the nominated central arrangement cannot serve.

## When a central project yard earns its place

Central production is a practical candidate when the main contractor reserves the yard and the accepted material route serves the required lengths and building receivers. It can consolidate the manufacturing location, but the distribution resources remain part of the purchased package.

Specify how panels are allocated and labelled by building and roof run. Record who releases the final cut list and who accepts material into temporary holding. Producing early against a changing master schedule can fill the yard with correctly formed panels for the wrong revision.

Include the yard's end condition. A temporary station can occupy land intended for parking, utilities or final landscaping. Identify the agreed removal event and the resources needed to clear equipment, unused material and supports. A temporary-yard comparison should not quietly assume permanent factory occupation.

## When the production package should follow the buildings

Roving production deserves comparison when suitable building-side positions eliminate an otherwise difficult finished-panel distribution connection. The offer must establish the actual outlet-to-receiver relationship at each assigned building, with its material and support scope.

Use a resource calendar for the full moves. The package is ready at the next building only after release, transport or relocation, configuration work and site readiness are accounted for by their providers. A short internal distance cannot compensate for an unavailable receiving team or an occupied stand area.

## Price exceptions openly instead of overstating coverage

A mixed arrangement is appropriate when one group of buildings suits central production and another needs an accepted elevated or delivered-panel route. Assign every panel mark to one supply route and identify shared resources so neither material nor equipment cost is counted twice.

## Two hypothetical park-wide supply failures

**Failure case: the central station cannot deliver the longest assigned panels.** Procurement reserves a yard using the equipment footprint but omits a constricted internal connection to a later building. Have the distribution specialist address the actual length envelope and route; change the allocation or price another supply route before committing those panels to the yard.

**Failure case: moving the former is booked as moving the whole operation.** The calendar relocates the machine while coil supply, support acceptance and the next building's receiver remain unavailable. Price the complete building-to-building production package and use its actual readiness events, rather than a presumed travel time.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the park-wide decision

1. Building register with roof products, panel marks, lengths and quantities.
2. Site plan showing the proposed yard and every candidate equipment position.
3. Roof sections and receiver coordinates tied to surveyed support levels.
4. Actual internal panel-distribution and equipment-transfer proposals.
5. Building release, receiver readiness and equipment-departure events.
6. Yard reservation, temporary storage, material identification and final-clearance scope.
7. Coil supply, utilities, support assessment and shared-resource responsibilities.
8. The central, roving and exception routes to quote at one delivery boundary.
