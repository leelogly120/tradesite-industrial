**Choose workshop overhaul when the crane specialist requires off-site facilities or the complete removal, transport and return package beats its on-site alternative. Choose on-site repair when the defined work is deliverable at the facility and the owner can release the required work and access zones.** Compare the same repaired and accepted outcome. Keep component handling, personnel access and specialist repair as separately assigned resources in either route.

The repair location decides more than the number of days a platform remains in the factory. It changes the route of the hoist or other named assembly, the owner of each handover and the visits needed before the crane returns to service. Purchase those boundaries together.

## Specify where the work actually happens

[Konecranes' CXT general-overhaul brochure](https://www.konecranes.com/sites/default/files/download/generaloverhaul_brochure_en_2018_konecranes_0.pdf) describes repair-centre and on-site routes for that service. It is an example of two repair locations, not a universal scope for every crane. The appointed crane specialist must issue the actual repair proposal for the identified asset and its condition.

On-site work can occur at the installed assembly or at an agreed repair station within the facility. Write that location into the order. A quote for on-site labour does not by itself allocate the work needed to remove a component, bring it to the repair position or return it to the crane.

For workshop overhaul, identify the assembly that leaves, its delivery condition, the receiving workshop and the return boundary. State which work remains at the facility. An off-site repair still has a site interface: removal, preservation, dispatch, receipt, reinstallation and specialist acceptance need assigned owners.

Ask the service provider to distinguish the initially priced work from condition-dependent repair options. Agree how newly identified work is authorized and how it changes the programme. The owner then compares quotations using the same defined repair boundary instead of mixing a complete overhaul with a limited initial repair.

## Keep the component route separate from the people

Have the crane and handling specialists identify the actual assembly dimensions, masses, connection interfaces and intended presentation positions. Their plans determine the component route and the resources assigned to it. The personnel-access order covers the people, tools and permitted items specified for the offered equipment.

For the off-site route, include every transfer between the crane, dispatch area, carrier and workshop. For the on-site route, include the actual movement to and from any local repair station. Count the transfers named in the offered method, rather than treating the building boundary as the end of all handling work.

## Two hypothetical orders that stop before return to service

**Failure case: workshop repair is bought without site removal.** A factory accepts a competitive overhaul price, but no contractor owns disconnection, removal, packing or the return installation. The repaired assembly reaches the gate with no funded route back to its working position. Buy the workshop scope together with a named site handover and return package before comparing its total with on-site work.

**Failure case: on-site work is assumed to occur on the personnel deck.** Another buyer books a broad platform because the service engineer will attend the factory. The actual repair plan requires a separate component-removal operation and a ground-level repair position. Draw the people and component routes separately, then order only the access and handling resources those routes need.

Use the facility's real interruption cost for the same comparison period. Record the removal window, workshop period, reinstallation window and any separately priced standby resources. Obtain the repair provider's actual dates and dependencies; do not create a generic turnaround promise from a product brochure.

## Survey the positions left at the crane

The access survey must show the specific faces where the service personnel work. Record elevations and offsets from the actual supporting floor, along with the approach route, obstructions and space for the offered base and supports. Include the building condition during the repair, not just an empty-shell plan.

*Locate each personnel workface against the real overhead structure instead of buying access from the hall's maximum height.*

The deck and steelwork in the photograph illustrate that geometric relationship. For the crane job, the specialist supplies the task positions and the facility supplies the supporting-floor and route records. The equipment provider must describe a configuration that serves those positions. Keep isolation, work-zone control and return-to-service responsibilities within the crane owner's and appointed specialists' plan.

A broad deck is useful only where the actual repair stages need a shared work surface and the building accepts the arrangement. For a few isolated visits, compare a defined access service before purchasing equipment. Do not retain a construction-sized platform solely because the crane remains high above the floor.
