**Choose an engineered timber packing design for a one-way shipment when it meets the actual machine support, handling and destination wood requirements at the justified complete cost. Compare an engineered steel-base or processed-wood alternative when repeat use, receiving access or the destination packing requirements give that accepted design a measurable purchasing advantage.** Select from issued designs for the same machine and route. The material label alone settles neither structural suitability nor compliance of every packing component.

A former's base, outer enclosure, corrosion protection and securing system do different jobs. Keep them separate in the RFQ. The buyer needs a packing design for the purchased configuration, not a request for the cheapest crate wrapped around a generic machine name. Include the lift modules, tooling and auxiliaries actually supplied.

## The support design is the first comparison

Ask the equipment supplier to issue the transport support locations and constraints for each assembly. Give those records to the proposed packer. The packer identifies its designed base, handling interfaces, restraint and protection arrangement for the route. Compare the resulting packed dimensions and gross mass because the packing choice itself changes transport cost and receiver resources.

A steel base can introduce reuse or later handling value only if those uses are designed and accepted. A timber base can be a practical shipment component when its structure and treatment requirements fit the actual transaction. Neither material provides a universal engineering answer for long forming beds, controls and separate tooling.

![Silver rolls, black adjustment blocks, a blue motor and wooden cases sit along a forming line.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261101-roller-line-detail.webp)
*Include vulnerable tooling and projecting adjustments when defining the packing envelope and protective access.*

The rolls, shafts and adjustment blocks show the equipment detail that must remain clear of packing contact and handling damage. Ask for the actual supplier constraints. A strong outer enclosure loses value if its fasteners, restraints or removal process interfere with protected machine surfaces.

## Wood compliance follows the actual material list

For imports into Great Britain, the [Forestry Commission's guidance](https://www.gov.uk/wood-packaging-import-export) covers solid-wood packaging such as pallets, cases and dunnage, and distinguishes processed materials such as plywood. Use that destination-specific example to frame the packing bill of materials. The appointed import specialist and packer establish requirements for the actual destination and all wood present in the shipment.

A plywood outer case can still contain solid-wood bearers or loose dunnage. A steel base can also use wooden blocks elsewhere. Obtain the complete packing material schedule and the required treatment and marking records for controlled components. Changing the cover material does not remove obligations for other pieces.

This is a compliance check alongside structural packing design. Required treatment does not certify that the base supports the machine correctly. Likewise, an engineered base drawing does not satisfy plant-health requirements for every wood item in the shipment. Assign a responsible provider to both subjects.

## Unpacking is part of the purchase

The receiving facility needs to remove packing without improvising around a heavy assembly. Obtain package-opening information, required receiving equipment and the remaining support condition after the outer enclosure comes off. The equipment and handling professionals establish the actual method. Procurement ensures that the facility and resources are booked for that method.

If a buyer intends to reuse a base for transport between projects, include that duty explicitly in the design enquiry. An export support frame and an accepted repeated-use transport frame are different purchased scopes. Price inspection, storage and return movement of reusable items instead of treating retained steel as an automatic saving.

![Grey and red frames, loose shafts and wooden cases are stored together in a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261030-stored-line-frames.webp)
*The receiver needs package identities and an opening plan for large frames as well as small components.*

The mixed items in the storage scene illustrate why a single packing description leaves gaps. Give each shipped package its own contents list and handling basis. Reconcile every component against the equipment order before releasing the shipment and again at receipt.

## Two hypothetical packing mistakes

**The case is called plywood, but its bearers are untreated solid wood.** The buyer checks only the outer panels. The shipment's actual material list includes controlled wood components under the machine. Require the packer's full material schedule and destination compliance response before dispatch.

**The steel shipping base is assumed to be a permanent lifting fixture.** A buyer plans repeated local moves using an export support frame that was never offered for that duty. Obtain the equipment and transport parties' accepted repeated-use design or procure a separate suitable frame. Credit only the duty actually purchased.
