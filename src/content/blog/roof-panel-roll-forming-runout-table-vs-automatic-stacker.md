Keep a staffed runout arrangement when irregular panel orders fit a defined receiving method and the available team can complete them within the required work period. Choose an automatic stacker when repeat panel and bundle demand justifies the complete exit cell, including packing and bundle removal. If finished packs have nowhere to go, buying a stacker alone leaves the production problem in place.

The decision concerns how an accepted panel becomes an identifiable, removable order. A receiving table supports part of that journey. A stacker performs a defined stacking function. Neither description automatically includes wrapping, dunnage, labels, a forklift or the people needed for the remaining work.

## Buy a removable bundle, not an isolated machine cycle

Write the required exit condition before requesting equipment. State whether the purchase ends with loose accepted panels, a counted stack, a secured bundle or a labelled pack at a collection point. Quote both alternatives to the same endpoint.

A staffed arrangement still requires specified equipment and a competent handling plan for the actual panel. Manual does not mean that any panel can be carried by available workers. Conversely, automated does not mean unattended: identify the tasks and attendance retained by the proposed cell, including changes, inspection, packing and fault response.

## Where automation earns a place on the shortlist

Investigate an automatic stacking cell when recurring orders require consistent counted bundles and the current receiving operation repeatedly holds up accepted production. Record the actual waiting events and why they occur. Separate stacking time from waiting for packing materials, labels, inspection or a collection vehicle.

If the delay is finding somewhere to put completed bundles, improve that route before buying more stacking capacity. If changing many small orders dominates the work, compare the proposed change tasks and retained attendance. A machine selected from one continuous demonstration does not yet represent that order book.

Choose the staffed alternative when its complete work package serves the committed output and the automated route adds unsupported equipment or unused capacity. Choose the automatic route when its representative order trial and full cost address the recurring constraint. This is a purchase comparison, not a rule that one method suits every small or large factory.

## Fill in the exit-cell worksheet

Use the same order sample and finished-pack definition for both suppliers. The final column records information that the responsible owner must supply.

| Buying question | Staffed runout route | Automatic stacking route | Responsible owner and actual project entry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product envelope | Defined receiving equipment and task method | Offered profile and length capability | Product planner: enter profiles ___, shortest ___ and longest ___ to establish covered orders |
| Bundle identity | Count, order separation and labelling tasks | Included counting and order-change functions | Dispatch lead: enter pieces per pack ___ and label requirements ___ to define the endpoint |
| Surface condition | Contact and separation arrangements | Offered stacking contacts and separators | Quality lead: record finish criteria ___ and accepted sample ___ to define inspection |
| Packing scope | Staff, tools and consumables included | Included equipment and remaining manual tasks | Packing lead: list dunnage ___, wrapping ___ and strapping ___ to complete the price |
| Pack removal | Actual collection method and occupied area | Actual discharge and collection interface | Logistics lead: enter route ___, collection period ___ and buffer positions ___ to prevent a blocked exit |
| Whole-order result | Accepted packed orders during the work period | The same representative order mix | Buyer: record trial result ___, retained staffing ___ and complete quote ___ to choose the cell |

The worksheet also exposes mismatched quotations. One bidder offering counted stacks and another offering wrapped, labelled packs have priced different results. Add the missing work before treating their totals as alternatives.

## Keep changes and exceptions visible

Some orders require a different packing arrangement or a separately reviewed receiving method. Assign those orders explicitly. Do not buy a stacker for the regular product and then assume that the unusual profile, delicate finish or short batch uses the same process.

Ask the equipment supplier to describe the proposed change sequence and the product information it requires. The buyer supplies actual orders; the supplier identifies the functions and limitations of the offered cell. Test the commercially important transitions, not every imaginable future product.

## Test the purchase against two practical mistakes

These are illustrative purchasing mistakes, not customer case studies.

**Failure case: the stacker produces a queue of uncollected packs.** A factory automates panel stacking while keeping a collection route that is unavailable during its busiest period. The machine stops behind completed bundles. Include the actual collection resources, buffer and working periods in the order trial and price the missing logistics before purchase.

**Failure case: an irregular order mix is sold as a repeat run.** The demonstration uses one profile, length and bundle count. The buyer's daily work includes frequent changes and separately packed special orders. Retained attendance and change work erase the assumed advantage. Trial the actual commercial mix and obtain a defined exception route rather than extrapolating from the demonstration.

## Request a complete production-exit offer

Send these eight attachments with the RFQ:

- Panel profiles, material specifications and actual finished-length range.
- Representative order list with batch sizes and change frequency.
- Required bundle count, order identification and labelling information.
- Finished-surface acceptance criteria and approved packing materials.
- Existing or proposed former outlet drawing and control interface scope.
- Measured floor layout with receiving, packing, storage and collection routes.
- Required work periods, available personnel and bundle-removal resources.
- Destination, power supply, trial endpoint and delivery/support responsibilities.

Buy the complete exit cell only when its covered orders and measured result justify the investment. Otherwise keep the defined staffed route and correct the specific bottleneck.
