Keep full-length production when the issued roof design requires uninterrupted panel runs. Compare shorter sections only when the roof designer has specified an allowable end-lap system and released its component schedule. Then choose between complete production, delivery and installation packages: shorter transport lengths do not eliminate the additional joint work, and longer panels do not automatically require an elevated former.

The purchasing decision starts after the roof-system choice. Procurement must not introduce a joint simply to make an available machine or truck convenient. If only one panel schedule is permitted, buy equipment and services for that schedule. If two designs are accepted alternatives, compare their actual whole-project requirements.

## One roof area can create two different supply packages

A full-run schedule defines each panel from its required start to its required finish without an intermediate end lap. A segmented schedule creates shorter ordered pieces and a defined connection between them. The roof designer supplies the locations and system details; the machinery and logistics parties respond to the resulting products.

An end lap is a system-specific connection, not just spare panel length. [Kirby Building Systems' installation resources](https://www.kirbybuildingsystems.com/builder-dashboard/installation-videos/) identify it as the transition between upper and lower roof panels. [MBCI's panel-planning guidance](https://www.mbci.com/tag/installer-tips/) connects panel breaks and lengths with actual roof conditions and manufacturer details. Use the appointed roof-system provider's documents for your project, rather than importing a detail from another product.

Put the two released bills of materials beside one another. Include panels, required connection components, packing and installation tasks. That reveals the full commercial difference before equipment suppliers price the production scope.

## Full-length panels: choose where the length is created

Full-length supply has more than one production route. Buy finished full-length panels when the producer, carrier and site can deliver and receive the ordered product. Use a ground-level site line when its output and subsequent roof transfer have a complete handling arrangement. Investigate elevated forming when moving the outlet near the receiver addresses a real transfer constraint and the whole line-and-lift arrangement fits the site.

Keep these as alternatives for the same required panel. For each route, record the former location, supported discharge path, roof transfer method and receiving position. Select the route that delivers the specified uninterrupted panel through the accepted site layout, then enter its complete cost alongside material, packing, transport and installation resources.

The longest product is not the only planning input. Record where panels first appear, the receiving direction and the complete route to their assigned work area. An order that can be produced still needs a deliverable site arrangement. Obtain that arrangement before selecting the line simply because it accepts the required length.

## Shorter sections: price the connection work that remains

Where the designer has released an end-lapped system, shorter ordered pieces change the material and logistics schedule. Compare them using their actual dimensions, quantities and connection components. Do not substitute building run length for the finished lengths ordered from the producer.

Include the specified joint-related production work. If the panel requires a particular end condition or other fabrication, assign it to the actual supplier and place it in the price. A basic forming-and-cutting quotation is insufficient when it excludes a required finished feature.

The installer provides the labour and access scope for the accepted design, including its inspections and completion checks. Procurement compares those real offers. No generic labour allowance or claimed reduction in roof risk belongs in the calculation.

## Compare two released schedules with this worksheet

| Cost or scope | Full-run panel package | Designed segmented package | Owner, entry and purchase decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Permitted roof arrangement | Issued uninterrupted-run schedule | Issued joint system and locations | Roof designer: enter drawing revisions ___ and permitted alternatives ___ to define the comparison |
| Ordered panels | Actual finished lengths and quantities | Actual segment lengths and quantities | Quantity surveyor: enter both schedules ___ to prevent building dimensions becoming purchase lengths |
| Production | Proposed forming and cutting route | Forming plus required end-related fabrication | Panel supplier: name included operations ___ and quote ___ to establish finished-product scope |
| Connection components | Required system accessories | Required accessories plus designed end-joint items | Roof-system supplier: enter component schedules ___ and costs ___ to compare complete materials |
| Delivery and receipt | Full-length transport and site route | Segment transport and site route | Logistics lead: record delivery quotes ___, receiving positions ___ and handling scope ___ to compare logistics |
| Installation completion | Actual work package for full runs | Actual work package including joints | Installer: enter priced tasks ___, access period ___ and inspection scope ___ to compare completed roofs |

Use actual quoted costs for both packages. Keep equipment ownership separate from the cost assigned to this project: a newly purchased line will also need a credible workload beyond one roof. If the project is the only committed demand, compare a complete supplied-panel or operated-production offer before assuming machinery ownership is necessary.

## Two illustrative purchasing mistakes

**Failure case: transport convenience becomes an unauthorized roof change.** A buyer divides full-run panels to fit an available delivery route without a released joint design. The resulting supply no longer matches the specified roof. Keep the required run intact or obtain a formal designer-issued alternative before comparing segmented production.

**Failure case: the shorter-panel quote excludes the joints.** A contractor compares shorter-piece freight with a full-length production package but omits connection components, end preparation and installation tasks. The apparent saving is incomplete. Price both released systems to the same finished-roof boundary before selecting equipment.
