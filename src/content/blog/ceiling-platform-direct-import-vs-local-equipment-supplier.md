**Choose direct import when your business has named import, transport, receiving and qualified support resources for the proposed ceiling platform. Choose a local supplier when its actual offer provides the work your organisation lacks at an acceptable complete cost.** Compare the same equipment and destination condition. A lower machine price and a nearby seller address each leave important parts of that comparison unanswered.

Direct import means buying across the border and assigning the remaining delivery and support tasks. It does not automatically mean buying from the manufacturer. A local supplier also needs to identify what it sells, what it imports to order and which services it actually provides.

## Use one equipment requirement for both channels

Issue the same ceiling task, working positions, material schedule and site information to each bidder. Identify the offered machine configuration, included deck arrangements and equipment documents. A comparison between different task coverage is an equipment-selection decision as well as a channel decision.

For a shared large work face with an established floor route, use the [ARC-F25 crawler ceiling platform](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/) as a catalog shortlist entry. Ask the supplier to return the offered deck layout, permitted load distribution, support reactions and transport envelope against the site brief. Compare an alternative local offer against those same task requirements.

Model labels and photographs are useful for identifying the enquiry, but the actual scope belongs in the technical schedule. If a local offer proposes another machine, retain its separate configuration and compare its coverage explicitly. Do not label different machines equivalent merely because their quoted height labels look similar.

## Find the work that changes hands

A buyer with an established import organisation can compare a direct equipment offer with separately obtained logistics and local service quotations. A first-time importer should first price the resources needed to perform those tasks. Leaving them out makes the direct route appear cheaper without making it complete.

A local supplier earns its place in the comparison through defined work. That includes a named receiving location, delivery to the agreed point, specified completion tasks and identified support. Obtain the provider and scope for each service. If the local seller simply passes on an overseas quotation, compare that narrower contribution honestly.

| Purchase element | Direct-import route | Local-supplier route | Responsible party and buyer entry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment scope | Overseas seller identifies the supplied configuration | Local seller identifies the configuration it will deliver | Equipment supplier: enter equipment schedule and drawing revision to compare task coverage |
| Import process | Buyer appoints its import specialists | Seller states which import work is included | Import lead: name destination, required document set and import-service quote to include this cost |
| Inland delivery | Buyer obtains the actual carrier scope | Seller states the final delivery point | Logistics lead: record cargo envelope, route and delivery quote to select transport resources |
| Receiving and completion | Buyer arranges the required local work | Seller identifies its included work and provider | Receiving manager: enter arrival condition, completion tasks and provider to price the receiving package |
| Familiarisation and support | Buyer purchases the services it needs separately | Seller identifies included attendance and ongoing services | Equipment owner: name service provider, attendance scope and price to compare support cost |
| Final comparison | Sum equipment and all separately purchased work | Add every excluded task to the supplier total | Commercial buyer: record complete totals and exclusions for the same completion state to choose the channel |

Use the table with actual quotations. Tax treatment, import classification and destination obligations belong with the buyer's appointed specialists. Include their conclusions and costs in the comparison rather than importing assumptions from another buyer's country.

## A local address is not a support package

Ask who will attend the site, what tasks that provider performs and which equipment information it needs. An office near the buyer does not establish personnel competence, spare-part applicability or a completion date for repairs. Compare the actual commitments instead of the distance to the seller.

The direct route needs the same discipline. Remote advice, parts supply, local attendance and maintenance are separate services. Assign each required service to an identified provider before choosing a package that relies on it. A buyer with competent in-house resources should list the tasks that team will perform rather than paying twice for overlapping work.

Distinguish warranty terms from purchased support. Record the seller's actual terms and refer their interpretation to the buyer's commercial advisers. Neither the word warranty nor the word local identifies who will carry out a particular site activity.

## Compare dates at the same delivery boundary

Ask what equipment condition each quoted date represents. Dispatch from an overseas supplier, arrival at a local yard and delivery ready for the agreed commissioning work are different milestones. Compare the date your project needs with the complete route leading to that milestone.

If the local supplier imports to order, include that import stage in the programme. If it offers an identified stock unit, examine that unit's actual configuration and completion scope. Location of stock does not settle suitability for the ceiling task.

## Two channel choices that conceal missing work

These illustrative purchasing mistakes compare the complete purchase with the quoted channel label.

**Failure case: the direct price excludes the buyer's local completion task.** A contractor compares an overseas equipment total with a local delivered package, then discovers that it must source unloading and assembly resources separately. Obtain those quotations before choosing the direct route. Compare both proposals at the same completed purchase boundary.

**Failure case: the local seller has no named site-support provider.** A buyer assumes local purchase includes attendance because the seller is nearby. The offer covers equipment delivery and telephone contact only. Either purchase the required service explicitly or choose a supplier whose defined package includes it.

## Send the same RFQ to each purchase channel

- Ceiling and wall tasks, with drawings and required working positions.
- Supporting-floor references, access routes and the responsible site engineer.
- Crew, tools, materials and any separate handling equipment.
- Requested deck arrangements and the equipment included in the purchase.
- Destination, import lead, final delivery point and transport scope.
- Receiving, assembly and commissioning resources already available.
- Familiarisation, maintenance and site-support services the buyer needs.
- Required project milestone and the technical documents expected at handover.
