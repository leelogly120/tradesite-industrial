**Buy access for the released complete wall system, including its actual preparation, support, finish and inspection stages. When comparing externally insulated rendered construction with a ventilated rainscreen proposal, use those retained operations to establish equipment occupation.** A rendered appearance alone does not identify the construction behind it, and a rainscreen label does not eliminate every wet finishing task.

The contractor's investment decision is the complete access package that serves its accepted wall work. It is separate from the designer's selection of the wall's thermal, structural, moisture and fire performance. The buyer should receive that released design, then compare the resources and dates needed to build it.

## Read the whole section behind the finish

Obtain the actual existing substrate, new layers, supports, junctions and product references. Identify which work your contract retains and which is handed over by another trade. A finish sample and insulation thickness are only parts of the construction order.

[Sto's published system information](https://www.stocorp.com/faq/) describes its externally insulated finish-system components. Its [StoVentec Render page](https://www.stocorp.com/sto_systems/stoventec-render/) identifies a rendered, back-ventilated rainscreen system. These examples demonstrate why appearance is a poor shortcut for identifying the complete assembly. They do not specify your wall or establish compatibility with an ARCLIFT platform.

For the actual comparison, have each wall-system provider identify the installation operations retained by its released proposal. Include substrate work, nominated support or attachment, insulation, outer construction, opening/junction completion and specified inspections. Use the product and project documents for applicable conditions and stage requirements rather than generic curing assumptions.

## Price access occupation through the stages

An installation stage requiring later acceptance or finish work can leave equipment waiting or returning. Another stage can need a different working position, material presentation or external process interface. Ask the installer to describe those actual states before selecting one platform arrangement for the whole elevation.

For a multi-stage rendered package, obtain the selected system's actual programme and release requirements from its provider. For a rainscreen package, retain its actual backing, subconstruction and facade operations. A complete proposal can contain a combination of those tasks; the price should follow the real assembly rather than a wet-versus-dry slogan.

## A complete-wall purchasing worksheet

Give every access provider the same assembly and stage schedule. Fill the entries from actual design, survey and specialist records so one equipment price does not rely on a different construction boundary.

| Purchase decision | Fillable project record | Responsible source and datum | Effect on the access package |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted assembly | System ___; full section revision ___ | Wall designer; released project specification | Defines the work being served |
| Existing substrate | Condition ___; preparation/repair scope ___ | Building and wall specialists; located survey | Establishes the starting condition |
| Retained operations | Support, insulation, outer construction and finish ___ | Installer; actual system work schedule | Defines elevated stages and resources |
| Junctions/openings | Detail references ___; provider boundary ___ | Design coordinator; issued elevations and sections | Prices completion beyond the main field |
| Stage releases | Required checks/intervals ___; owner ___ | System and quality leads; project acceptance plan | Establishes waiting and return requirements |
| Personnel tasks | Faces ___; levels ___; exceptions ___ | Installer and surveyor; local ground/building datums | Tests actual access coverage |
| Material interfaces | Objects ___; process equipment ___; handler ___ | Relevant specialists; task and delivered-object schedules | Defines resources beside personnel access |
| Completed cost | Preparation ___; construction ___; access ___; handback ___ | Buyer; identical accepted wall endpoint | Supports the complete comparison |

The table should lead to an installation package with explicit dates and responsibilities. If the wall design changes, revise the access task schedule before carrying the old equipment allowance into the new tender. A similar finished elevation can conceal a substantially different resource purchase.

## Choose the equipment arrangement for the actual elevation

A broad crawler deck deserves comparison where sustained adjacent personnel tasks fit its proposed work positions and an accepted ground route. For prolonged vertical facade work or difficult positions, obtain a specialist alternative with its own complete site arrangements. The [crawler-versus-mast-climbing guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-vs-mast-climbing-work-platform/) addresses that separate architecture comparison.

Require the actual offered geometry, task-load limits, reactions and site requirements. The responsible building parties assess support and route against the real structure and ground. The wall specialist owns its material and process requirements, including separately handled components and any external equipment interfaces.

Compare retention and remobilisation through the same accepted completion. A machine with a low day rate can be an expensive purchase if the project leaves its released work waiting or requires several unpriced returns. Use actual stage dates and quotes rather than invented installation rates.

## Two hypothetical facade budgets built around the wrong label

**Failure case: a rendered surface is assumed to identify one wall construction.** A buyer copies an access allowance from another project without reading the backing and support arrangement. The accepted system retains different operations. Rebuild the task and resource schedule from the complete released section before ordering equipment.

**Failure case: the platform leaves after the main field is installed.** Another budget omits junction finishing and specified later checks. The apparent saving creates an unpriced access return. Include all required stages, exceptional positions and handback duties when comparing the complete equipment period.
