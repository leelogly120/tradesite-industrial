**Choose high-wall jet nozzles when the mechanical designer accepts the throw, occupied-zone conditions and sound, and the perimeter positions remain accessible in the finished hall. Choose distributed ceiling diffusers when the accepted design needs supply points across the interior and the site can release those positions. If both layouts qualify, choose the lower complete installation, commissioning and service cost for the same duty. Overlay the terminal service faces and post-fit-out floor routes before booking access. Compare ARC-F25 for adjacent personnel positions and a local access quote for isolated outlets.**

A central air-handling system can feed either layout. This choice concerns the final supply-air outlets and the local ducts, controls and visits needed to support them. [TROX's jet-nozzle data](https://www.trox.de/en/nozzles/tjn-5e20bbbd487bffd0) describes long-throw supply for applications such as production halls and gymnasiums, with manual and actuator-adjusted variants. [TROX's DUE nozzle sheet](https://cdn.trox.de/362106860460d9ef/8feb94357b07/DUE-Jet-Nozzle.pdf) describes sidewall placement where conventional ceiling diffusers are impractical. [Its VDL ceiling diffuser range](https://www.trox.de/en/ceiling-diffusers/vdl-924acd99ba48f598?format=html&slug=ceiling-diffusers/vdl) gives examples of overhead swirl diffusers for high rooms, including suspended and ceiling-mounted arrangements. The designer accepts the proposed outlet arrangement for your hall.

## Where will a technician need to stand?

A high-wall supply layout puts the outlet faces at the perimeter. Find out whether the fittings, local duct connections and any actuators can be reached from the same accepted position. A wall outlet above shelving, glazed frontage or a mezzanine edge can need an approach other than the floor immediately beneath it. A diffuser distributed across the ceiling creates overhead work in the specific bays it serves. If those bays later contain racking or production lines, plan the maintenance approach at the same time as the installation approach.

Obtain an outlet schedule with each terminal ID, mounting condition, actual access face and service task. Ask the supplier what service is required from the room side and what parts need access above or behind the visible face. For the VDL example, the removable face is a variant associated with a plenum box. Include the exact variant in the service schedule, then ask the supplier which work can be completed from below and which requires access at another face.

## Keep setting and acceptance in the equipment period

The number of visible outlets is an incomplete measure of return access. The commissioning lead identifies which actual settings and checks need physical attendance at a terminal and which are observed from other points in the hall. Mark any required acceptance visit tied to heating and cooling modes. The quote needs to cover access through the last retained elevated task.

Price a required controls interface when the proposed actuator needs one. Obtain the actual device list, service faces and responsibility for later faults. Any power, control or balancing work belongs with the relevant specialists. The elevated platform quote covers the assigned personnel tasks and the materials allowed by its proposed configuration.

## Two purchasing mistakes that hide outlet access

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A contractor budgets based on room-side access to a removable face but leaves the required actuator checks on the far side of the enclosure. Have the mechanical and controls providers record the work side for each specific assembly before accepting the access quote.

**Mistake (hypothetical).** A hall owner buys perimeter jets to release the roof bays, then installs fixed equipment in the proposed approach beneath the wall terminals. The buying team priced the empty hall rather than the service layout. Reserve the accepted service positions on the final floor plan or price the alternate access at award.

## Issue one package for the mechanical access RFQ

1. Accepted outlet plans and occupied-zone requirements for each qualified design.
2. Terminal IDs, dimensions, product variants and controls responsibility.
3. Heights from local floor, offsets and marked work and service faces.
4. Branch, actuator and nearby obstruction details on dimensioned sections.
5. Installer and commissioning attendees, visit dates, tools and equipment supplier's task-load basis.
6. Separate handling and presentation duties matched to the outlet packages.
7. Installation and post-fit-out approach, support records and release windows.
8. Required operating handback, destination, delivery and withdrawal boundary.
