Choose an accepted modular cable transit when repeated planned cable changes justify re-entry and the complete frame arrangement fits the opening. Choose an accepted sealed-penetration system for fixed services when its installed and later reinstatement scope gives the better qualified offer. Compare complete systems accepted for the actual wall, cables and project requirements. A frame and a sealant cartridge are components, not competing finished firestop assemblies.

Have the responsible firestop design and installation team select the documented assembly before purchasing access. When both systems qualify, select the lower complete cost over the buyer's stated installation and cable-change period. Include the opening, system components, installation, inspection, identification and the defined future-change work in that comparison.

The upright work frame below shows the spatial issue around a high wall. The enquiry needs the actual opening and the working side available to the crew, together with the route beneath them.

## Re-entry is a service requirement, not a product slogan

[Hilti describes its CFS-T transit system](https://www.hilti.com/c/CLS_FIRESTOP_PROTECTION_7131/CLS_CABLE_TRANSIT_SYSTEMS_7131/r11735586) as modular, adaptable and reusable, with frames holding modules around cable or pipe penetrations. Its product page directs approval questions to individual items. That distinction matters: select the exact documented system against the actual construction and required performance, rather than transferring a category description to every opening.

For the other route, [Hilti's CP 606 information](https://www.hilti.com/c/CLS_FIRESTOP_PROTECTION_7131/CLS_FIRESTOP_SEALANTS_SPRAY_7131/r361) identifies a firestop sealant used for specified through-penetration applications. The procurement boundary remains the complete accepted assembly, including its associated materials and installation requirements. A cartridge description alone is insufficient for awarding a firestop package.

The comparison is therefore planned modular re-entry versus a selected sealed-opening arrangement for the actual service pattern. Modular systems can contain additional sealing materials. Sealed penetrations can also be changed through a documented repair or reinstatement process. Avoid presenting either family as universally maintenance-free or permanently unchangeable.

## Put the opening record ahead of the unit price

Create an opening schedule with stable identifiers. Tie each opening to the current wall or floor assembly, the cable schedule and the accepted system documentation. The specialist design team records the governing technical details; the buyer uses those records to compare the complete scope and prevent substitutions by appearance.

Keep future changes concrete. “Flexible for later” has no useful price boundary. State the expected additions or replacements, the decision period and the facility's actual change process. Ask bidders to describe what their documented systems require for those changes and who completes the work. Use the same scenario for both offers.

## A small opening can still create a large access package

An opening above fixed plant can require a different crew position from an identical opening in an empty bay. Record the working sides, existing services and installation-stage floor route. Ask the specialist installer to mark its required workfaces; the equipment provider then prices an actual configuration against them.

Separate personnel access from moving cable drums, lifting assemblies and supporting permanent services. Give the platform provider the crew, tools and intended deck materials with their distribution. Have the installation planner show the sequence across opening groups, including the inspection and final records required by the project.

For future changes, retain opening IDs, accepted system records and the as-installed service schedule together. Price the actual access and specialist reinstatement scope when comparing change scenarios. A reusable component does not eliminate the work of reaching, documenting and completing the changed opening.

## Two hypothetical penetration-package mistakes

**A modular frame price is compared with a complete sealed opening.** Modules, associated materials, installation and records are outside the frame quote. The buyer corrects the comparison by requiring one complete accepted assembly per opening ID, with identical installation and inspection boundaries.

**Future cable changes are expected but have no priced scope.** The contractor selects the lowest first-installation amount and the facility later discovers that access and reinstatement were omitted from its comparison. State the actual change scenario, obtain the specialist's documented scope for both systems, and include those amounts over the buyer's chosen comparison period.

These are hypothetical procurement failures. They support a bid structure, not a claim that one sealing technology is always cheaper or suitable for every fire-rated construction.
