A useful request for quotation does more than name a lifting height and ask for a price. It gives the technical reviewers a controlled picture of the roof work, forming inputs, access route, interfaces and decision owners. The immediate goal is not a final configuration. It is a reviewable question set that separates known conditions from estimates and open items. That distinction prevents an early commercial response from being mistaken for site acceptance, structural review or operating approval.

## Define the RFQ decision before listing equipment

Start by stating what the buyer needs the review to decide. A complete RFQ may ask whether crawler or truck architecture deserves further study, which forming data is missing, or which parties must close an interface. It should not ask one document to settle transport, structural, machinery and work-at-height questions at once.

### Write a one-sentence duty statement

Describe the intended task in ordinary project language: where material starts, where forming occurs, where panels are received and how the equipment reaches the work area. Add the expected operating pattern and any known changes between zones. Avoid promotional labels such as heavy duty because they do not define the work. A reviewer needs the sequence, boundary and unresolved conditions. When the task statement changes, treat that change as a new review input rather than a harmless wording edit.

### Separate selection from approval

Technical selection can identify a configuration path worth investigating. It cannot approve a roof, route, lifting arrangement, vehicle integration or local operating method. Put those decisions in a responsibility table with one named owner and one expected closure record for each. This keeps a preliminary proposal useful without allowing it to drift into authority it does not have. If an owner is unknown, record the gap directly and hold the affected decision.

### State what a quotation may assume

Every assumption that affects scope should be visible: application state, destination category, access condition, power availability, material data and document language. Mark whether the assumption came from a controlled record, a buyer estimate or a temporary placeholder. A quotation built on placeholders may support budgeting, but the placeholders must remain open. The RFQ should also say what event forces re-review, such as a changed panel profile, route, chassis concept or roof handover point.

## Collect the inputs that drive technical review

Organize inputs by decision rather than attaching an unsorted folder. Each item should carry a revision, source owner, date and status. The review team can then see which questions have controlled evidence and which remain dependent on site verification.

### Describe the work zone and access route

Provide a plan or marked survey that covers delivery, entry, internal travel, setup, material staging, panel handover and withdrawal. Record widths, overhead restrictions, gradients, turns, gates, floor or ground changes and adjacent operations as measured fields, not conclusions. Show which conditions are temporary. Photos can clarify context, but they should be dated, located on the plan and supported by measurements. The route owner should identify unresolved hazards and the review date.

### Provide forming and material records

The forming review needs the intended panel profile, controlled drawing status, material type, thickness range, coil data, finish constraints and required panel handling sequence. Do not infer tooling or output from a product name. If samples or drawings conflict, open a technical query and state which record currently governs. The RFQ should also identify who approves the roof system and who owns any profile change after the initial equipment review.

### Map equipment and utility interfaces

List the boundaries among lifting structure, forming module, carrier or crawler base, electrical supply, controls, coil handling and roof receiving team. The list should identify who supplies data, who reviews it and what signed record closes the interface. Preliminary envelopes are useful for coordination, but reactions, stability, attachment details and final utilities require configuration-specific documents. Avoid copying values from a similar model into the open fields.

## Turn the RFQ into a controlled review package

A review package is easier to act on when it has an index, an open-item register and a clear revision state. The following checklist is intentionally broader than a sales form because high-altitude roll forming crosses site, equipment, material and handover boundaries.

- Name the requested decision, commercial deadline and technical hold points separately.
- Attach an anonymized responsibility matrix for roof, route, forming and receiving interfaces.
- Record working height and outreach assumptions without treating them as an approved operating envelope.
- Describe roof slope or building geometry, nearby edges and any changing work-face condition.
- List task material, panel length, coil or feed information only where it affects the reviewed interface.
- Assign weather and wind monitoring to the site team under its approved work method.
- Provide ground or floor records and name the competent party responsible for support review.
- Map access and route states from delivery through setup, task work and withdrawal.
- State destination, transport and chassis constraints that belong to local or integration review.
- Record voltage, power, control and documentation requirements with revision status and owner.

### Use an input register

Give every important record an identifier, title, revision, date, owner and status. Link technical questions to the record that created them. This simple discipline prevents a later email attachment from silently replacing the drawing used for selection. It also lets commercial and technical reviewers work from the same baseline without exposing private customer or site details in public material.

### Maintain an open-item log

An open item should say what is missing, why it matters, who owns the answer, the due date and which decision remains on hold. Avoid entries such as confirm later. A useful entry names the exact roof-zone survey, coil record or interface drawing required. Close the item only with a traceable response, then record whether the new information changes scope or triggers a fresh review.

### Issue a review snapshot

Before a quotation or technical note is released, freeze a snapshot of the input index, assumptions, exclusions and open items. The snapshot does not make the inputs correct; it shows which inputs the response actually used. If a revised record arrives, compare it against the snapshot and identify the affected interfaces. This makes change control practical during procurement rather than a paper exercise after equipment is committed.

## Judge RFQ completeness without inventing certainty

### Balance response speed with evidence quality

A fast budget indication can be useful when the project is still forming, but it should use explicit assumptions and a short validity boundary. Waiting for every final document may stall early coordination; pretending estimates are final creates a larger risk. A staged RFQ solves the tension: first screen the architecture, then close critical interfaces, then request a configuration-specific commercial response. Each stage should identify what has become more certain and what remains unresolved.

The practical trade-off is between early coordination and false precision. A useful record exposes each constraint, interface and unresolved owner; it does not convert preliminary information into an approval.

For general planning context, see the <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/geis6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE guidance on selecting and managing mobile elevating work platforms</a>. It supports general work-planning principles only. It does not verify an ARCLIFT configuration, settle destination rules or approve a site.

### Recognize where an RFQ cannot decide the project

This method may not fit a project that lacks controlled site information, named decision owners or a safe way to close open items. An RFQ cannot replace structural assessment, route authorization, local compliance review, a lifting plan, vehicle integration, a roof-work method or site acceptance. It also cannot establish performance from editorial images or a neighboring project. If the intended profile, material route, support condition or handover sequence is still unknown, selection should remain preliminary and the affected scope should stay open.

### Send a decision-ready data package

Provide an anonymized input index, duty statement, route and work-zone survey, forming and material records, utility list, responsibility matrix and open-item log. Ask for a response that repeats the governing inputs and exclusions. That format gives procurement a usable comparison while keeping engineering, site and destination decisions with the parties appointed to make them.

Send the site, destination, transport, work-zone and ground or floor records through a controlled project channel. The final, signed, project-specific package must identify open items and responsible reviewers. ## FAQ

#### What should be sent before asking for a firm configuration?

Send the controlled task statement, work-zone and route data, panel and coil records, interface list, destination information and decision-owner matrix. A firm configuration still depends on project-specific reviews, so the package should show which records are final and which are conditional.

#### Can photos replace a site survey?

No. Dated photos can explain context, but they rarely establish clearances, gradients, support conditions, temporary obstructions or the route in each equipment state. Locate photos on a plan, add measurements and name the party responsible for verifying the actual condition.

#### Should the RFQ include a preferred model?

It may identify a model or architecture for comparison, but it should not force the technical answer. State why the preference exists and allow reviewers to show which inputs support it, which conflict with it and which remain open.

#### When should the quotation be reviewed again?

Review it when the governing profile, material, destination, access route, roof zone, carrier concept, power supply, handover method or responsibility map changes. The original input snapshot should make those triggers visible.
