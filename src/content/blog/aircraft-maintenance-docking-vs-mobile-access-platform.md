**Buy aircraft docking when repeated maintenance visits need several technicians at defined aircraft zones and the dock supplier can show a suitable interface for the fleet. Choose mobile access for intermittent, separated tasks when the maintenance organisation accepts every proposed approach and the aircraft can leave the bay on schedule.** A mobile platform for changing hangar lights is a separate purchase from equipment that brings people close to an aircraft.

Start with the last completed heavy visit and the next contracted visit. Identify the work cards that really need simultaneous access. At one extreme, nose, wing and tail crews occupy the bay together. At the other, one technician visits a short list of local positions with relatively little equipment. The hangar dimensions can be identical while the number, shape and occupation time of the platforms are very different.

A deck gives a group of technicians room to work only if its usable surface reaches their assigned positions. In the yard photograph, the red mesh runs to perimeter rails, with booms and cables occupying part of that space. On a particular aircraft the operator needs an accepted boundary between those components and the airframe.

## Count aircraft visits by maintenance configuration

List aircraft variants, nose or tail positions, access panels that open during the work and maintenance configurations used in each bay. Have the maintenance manager group cards by where technicians stand and when. A wing inspection completed before a separate tail task creates a different access demand from overlapping teams. The operator should identify how an aircraft change during the visit affects relative positions. Give configuration and clearance decisions to the aircraft maintenance authority.

[NIJL describes aircraft docking systems](https://nijlgroup.com/docking-systems/) arranged around nose, wing, fuselage and tail work, with fleet compatibility and facility workflow informing the design. Obtain a drawing for each covered configuration from the proposed dock supplier. A dock described as universal still needs a coverage list for the actual fleet. Include how each section enters, is stored, is brought into position and is withdrawn before the aircraft moves.

For a mobile offer, mark each accepted task position, how tools or components reach it, the plan for adjacent crews and the time to release each bay. The provider and operator should specify which work remains inside the machine and which separate component-handling equipment the visit requires. Price excluded work independently. Give no commercial credit for points the proposed machine cannot serve within the accepted limits.

## Buy completed work cards, then price the occupied bay

A dock has an initial project cost and a recurring cost to configure, inspect, move and store its sections. Credit it with a shorter visit only where the proposed work-card sequence releases the aircraft earlier or frees a genuinely constrained bay. Moving technicians onto more positions has little financial value during a long independent parts delay. Put visit frequency against the contracted fleet mix. Present a speculative aircraft programme separately.

For mobile equipment, include moves between stops, technician waiting where work fronts conflict, any second unit for simultaneous work and hire for exceptions. A practical third offer is dedicated docking for recurring heavy checks plus a mobile unit for dispersed hangar-building work. Separate those duties on the cost sheet so the owner can see which one earns the purchase.

The photographed industrial hall has a raised deck and open floor beside it. A maintenance hangar adds parked aircraft, service vehicles and controlled tow routes to that floor. Compare the offered machine's travel dimensions and deployed support drawing against an occupied bay plan, including the path out.

## Two mistakes that leave the aircraft in the bay

These hypothetical purchasing errors put the bay release at risk.

**Mistake one — counting every fleet type as a dock visit.** A buyer forecasts full utilisation from the airport movement list. Only one operator has ordered heavy checks and its variant needs a different tail arrangement. Rebuild the base case from awarded visits and price the verified tail section separately before committing capital.

**Mistake two — drawing the work position but losing the exit.** A mobile deck can stand beside the required area at the start. A component trolley and another contractor's stand close the return aisle before the tow-out slot. Assign a reserved route and withdrawal time, or quote equipment and sequencing that the hangar controller accepts.
