**Buy standing-seam tooling against a controlled product definition and a documented route for the sales claims your customers require. If an order specifies a named roof system or warranty route that does not cover your proposed production, source the required panels instead of buying a lookalike former.** Invest in your own production only when the product owner, customer requirements and equipment acceptance plan support that route.

The machine's job is to form the agreed panel. It does not automatically give the buyer permission to manufacture a third party's system, use another company's performance evidence or promise that company's roof warranty. Settle the commercial and technical product route before turning a familiar seam outline into a tooling order.

## Read the deliverable in the customer's order

Start with what the customer is actually buying. An order can specify panels to a controlled drawing, a named roof system, a complete component package or an installed roof with particular warranty conditions. These are different commitments. Record the applicable specification and identify who accepts the proposed product and any substitution.

Do not substitute a visual resemblance for that acceptance. Two panels with similar standing edges can have different mating components, closure requirements or documented applications. The responsible roof designer and system owner define the required assembly; the equipment provider develops a manufacturing proposal for its accepted panel definition.

As one concrete vendor example, [MBCI's warranty information](https://www.mbci.com/resource-center/resources/warranties/) states that its weathertightness warranties concern roof systems and require approval before the buyer orders materials. Its single-source route also describes geometry review and inspections. Those are MBCI's stated conditions, not a universal rule and not a warranty available through buying an ARCLIFT machine.

Use that distinction to ask a precise commercial question: whose product and whose stated evidence or warranty will appear in the sale? The answer determines which documents, permissions and technical acceptance the buyer needs before investing in tooling.

## Choose among three practical routes

**Manufacture within a named system owner's permitted route.** Obtain the actual written scope covering the intended manufacturing and sales arrangement. Identify the controlled drawings, materials, components and production or acceptance conditions that apply. Do not assume that the owner offers licensing or approves third-party forming; the route exists only when that owner provides it for the proposed arrangement.

**Develop and supply a separately supported product.** Assign responsibility for the product definition, engineering evidence, customer acceptance and ongoing quality arrangements to competent named parties. Price that development work alongside the machine. Keep the product's actual supported claims separate from a competitor's catalogue or a panel sample used for discussion.

**Purchase the specified panels while serving the project another way.** Use this route when the current contract needs an established system and the buyer lacks a supported production route for it. The contractor can continue to provide its agreed installation or project services without buying tooling that cannot satisfy the material specification. A future manufacturing investment should be justified by separate eligible orders.

## A product-route worksheet for the equipment buyer

| Decision field | Buyer entry | Responsible party and effect on the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer deliverable | Named system, drawing-based panels or complete roof package: ___ | Commercial and technical leads; identifies what the order actually requires |
| Product definition | Controlled section, revision, materials and component references: ___ | Product owner; establishes the output the tooling must produce |
| Manufacturing route | Written permitted scope or separately developed product route: ___ | System owner or buyer's appointed product team; determines which orders support the investment |
| Performance evidence | Required evidence, exact assembly covered and permitted use: ___ | Responsible designer and evidence owner; checks relevance to the proposed product |
| Customer acceptance | Named approving party and required submission or trial: ___ | Project technical lead; fixes the product-release milestone |
| Supply basket | Panels, clips, closures, trim and completion tools included: ___ | Procurement and installation leads; exposes equipment and purchased-component gaps |
| Production acceptance | Critical dimensions, datums, material and sample requirements: ___ | Quality lead and line provider; defines the manufacturing trial |
| Sales commitment | Warranty issuer, offered conditions and excluded claims: ___ | Actual issuer and commercial lead; prevents unsupported promises entering the equipment case |

Fill the evidence row with the exact document and assembly it covers. An attractive test-report title without its product, material and configuration is not a usable match. The responsible specialists decide whether the evidence supports the intended use and what further work is required. The equipment buyer should carry their actual conclusion into the order, rather than interpreting a report independently.

This worksheet is not a checklist for one jurisdiction's legal certification. Destination requirements belong in the project review with appropriately qualified advisers. The immediate purchasing decision is narrower: can the proposed machine make a product that the identified customer is entitled and willing to accept under the stated sales scope?

## Freeze the tooling after the product route is settled

Give the forming-line provider one controlled input set. Include the profile drawing, mating interfaces, material specification, surface requirements and agreed panel inspection method. Identify who can approve a product change. If a proposed tooling alteration changes the accepted section or components, return it to that authority before treating it as an equivalent manufacturing shortcut.

Separate machine acceptance from acceptance of the roof product in use. A forming trial can establish whether the agreed output features are achieved under the specified trial conditions. It does not automatically establish the performance of the completed roof assembly or fulfil every requirement of a named system's production route.

The panel-edge sample below illustrates why the trial needs an identified drawing and material. A white shaped component is held against the blue panel edge; the relevant interface belongs in the controlled product definition. Samples are useful comparison objects when their origin and measured features are recorded. An anonymous sample should not become the entire specification for a commercial roof product.

## Two investment mistakes caused by the wrong product assumption

**A named-system order is used to justify an unrelated lookalike profile.** In this illustrative case, the buyer selects similar-looking tooling and counts the project's entire panel quantity as future machine utilisation. The customer has not accepted the substitute. Obtain the required product decision before purchasing; otherwise source the specified panels and exclude that order from the machine's demand case.

**A machine trial is presented as proof of a roof warranty.** Another buyer receives dimensionally accepted panels and assumes it can advertise a third party's weathertightness cover. The trial and the warranty have different issuers and conditions. Keep the production acceptance record and the actual warranty commitment separate, and remove any unsupported sales promise before taking the customer order.
