Target hail-specified orders with the exact accepted roof assembly and a funded qualification route. Choose heavier coil only when that accepted product requires it and the complete production package supports the change. Keep established panels for their documented duties when the business has no accepted route into the new market. Thicker sheet alone is not an impact-performance qualification. Set the accepted impact-product and specimen schedule before purchasing another gauge.

Build the new product offer around the customer's required impact performance and the funded assembly-evidence route. The customer needs defined performance evidence for a particular roof. The factory needs the material, tooling, components and traceability required to manufacture its assigned part consistently.

## Separate visible dents from the performance the project buys

A customer concerned about appearance and a customer requiring a stated assembly impact classification are buying different deliverables. Ask the responsible roof designer and evidence recipient to state the actual requirement, accepted method, product condition and reporting. Put a separate agreed appearance criterion into the offer where the owner requires it.

[FM Approvals explains](https://www.fmapprovals.com/resources/insights/hail-damage-that-goes-unseen-can-be-the-most-devastating) the importance of evaluating roof assemblies as systems and identifies panel-roof testing within its programme. It also discusses damage beyond the immediately visible surface. Its [panel-roof examination standard](https://www.fmapprovals.com/-/media/Feature/Approval-Standards/4471-pdf.pdf) calls for production-representative samples. Use the appointed programme provider's specimen requirements for the actual product route.

Do not write dent-proof or hail-proof into a panel range without a supported product claim and agreed meaning. Use the actual accepted evidence language and the stated application. Have the responsible specialist resolve what the customer needs before purchasing material for a supposed upgrade.

## Define the assembly behind the heavier coil proposal

List the complete configuration relevant to the accepted evidence route: panel section, material and thickness, support arrangement, attachment and other specified components. Record the manufacturing and assembly revisions. The technical parties determine what the evidence covers and whether a proposed variation requires additional assessment.

A material supplier offering a thicker coil solves a feedstock purchase. The forming provider must still address the new input and controlled section. The system owner must still accept the roof configuration. Put all three decisions on the same programme so that an available coil does not become stranded stock while product acceptance remains unfunded.

## Put product qualification ahead of utilisation forecasts

Separate committed eligible projects from general enquiries mentioning hail. Obtain actual prices for the required evidence services, specimens, components and product-development work. Include trial material, tooling changes, staff time, transport, repeat specimens where quoted and the accepted reporting deliverable.

If a recognised product owner already offers an accepted component-manufacturing route, compare that defined opportunity with developing your own product. Record the actual permissions, production controls and commercial terms. If neither route fits the funded programme, buy the specified finished panels for the project and retain the current machine range.

Owning a test rig is a separate investment. The [internal versus outside laboratory guide](/blog/metal-roof-assembly-testing-own-facility-vs-independent-lab/) compares how evidence services are purchased. This decision defines which impact-performance product the producer intends to sell before choosing where any required tests occur.

## Carry the qualified definition into normal production

Give the forming-equipment provider the accepted material and drawing, not an informal instruction to make a stronger panel. Trial representative lengths and relevant product features on the offered configuration. Agree the dimensional, surface and cut-end acceptance with the product owner. Identify the coil and tooling revision attached to each retained sample.

Short samples next to the equipment below illustrate useful manufacturing records. A real impact programme needs specimens made and assembled to its agreed schedule. The responsible specialist specifies that schedule; the factory supplies traceable parts and records rather than selecting a convenient anonymous sheet.

For repeat orders, control material and component substitutions through the product owner. A new coil source, gauge, profile or attachment proposal belongs in the evidence-applicability review. Keep the documentation burden and purchased component route in the recurring cost, not only the first development budget.

## Two hypothetical hail-market purchases that fail

**Heavier coil arrives before a product route exists.** A producer purchases stock expecting an impact class from thickness alone. No accepted assembly or evidence programme covers the proposed panel. Establish the accepted product and funded qualification route before committing the material lot.

**An attractive loose sample replaces the scheduled assembly.** A factory sends a convenient panel from another order while its supports and attachments differ from the programme. The required evidence concerns another construction. Release the specimen and manufacturing identities through the specialist's actual schedule.
