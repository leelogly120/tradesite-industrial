**Choose a planned manual cable-placement method only when the contractor accepts the effort and control for the actual cable and route. Buy a complete engineered powered-pulling package when the contractor's route assessment rules out its planned manual method.** Have the electrical contractor issue its route, pull assessment and work method first, then price the full placement operation and separate personnel access. Purchasing a stronger puller alone does not complete that method.

The cable-support system has already been selected in this comparison. Ladder, basket, conduit and route interfaces belong to the released electrical design. The new purchasing decision concerns how the specified cable reaches its accepted installed position, including reels, guides, pulling equipment, people, inspection and site occupation.

The lowered crawler deck below remains a separate machine under the work area. Its base or guardrails should not become convenient pulling anchors. Keep the pulling system's load path with the appointed contractor's engineered arrangement and the access machine within its own documented duty.

## The cable route defines the assessed work

The electrical designer and contractor supply the actual cable schedule, route, changes in direction and placement boundaries. The appointed specialist assesses the proposed method using the cable and equipment requirements. Procurement needs that accepted output, not a pull-force guess derived from cable length alone.

The manual route should identify crew duties, control, material presentation and accepted working positions. A label such as hand pull is not a complete plan. Record the equipment and site resources that the contractor's method actually retains, including any guides or reel handling.

The powered route needs its complete component schedule and reaction interfaces. [Greenlee's pulling-equipment category](https://www.greenlee.com/us/en/cable-pulling-fishing) distinguishes powered tools within cable installation. Its [cable-puller manual](https://cdn.greenlee.com/resources/media?key=0712107e-308d-4f23-b8d1-78bdbdb55f3e&languageCode=en&type=document) explains that pulling forces act through multiple components and supports, and that the puller's rating is not a substitute for component ratings and support assessment. Use the actual selected equipment's instructions through qualified personnel; this comparison supplies no rigging or operating procedure.

## Purchase all the powered system's interfaces

Obtain the actual puller, ropes, connectors, guides, anchors and other components specified by the responsible contractor. Identify which are owned, hired or supplied under the specialist service. Include their receiving, setup, operation, inspection and removal in the quote.

Have the responsible parties identify and accept the supporting structures and forces. Require explicit acceptance of a proposed pulling anchor even when the same structure already carries cable containment. Likewise, a personnel platform in the same area does not accept pulling or side loads by proximity.

If the selected platform documents expressly include a relevant interface, require the specific configuration and task acceptance before including it in the method. Otherwise keep all pulling reactions on the separately engineered system. Do not resolve a missing anchor by purchasing extra nominal platform capacity.

## Count the personnel tasks that remain

Both methods can retain elevated feed, guide, connection or inspection positions according to the accepted plan. Ask the contractor to mark those faces and work stages. Do not remove access from a powered-system budget because the motor sits at floor level.

Conversely, do not buy a large deck simply because the cable is overhead. A small number of isolated positions can suit localized access. A sustained series of adjacent tasks justifies a large-deck comparison when the offered configuration and building route fit. Use the actual crew and task schedule to compare those resources.

## Compare completed placement, not theoretical pulling speed

Obtain actual contractor estimates for preparation, setup, pulling or manual placement, required pauses, inspection and demobilisation. Include the resources they specify rather than applying an unsupported productivity percentage to cable length. Keep setup and completion costs beside the quoted pulling stage in both totals.

Where a specialist service supplies the complete powered system, compare its accepted deliverables with an owner-supplied equipment route at the same boundary. Allocate responsibility for components, competence, inspections and site attendance. An equipment-only rental price and a completed electrical operation are different purchases.

The site team defines released areas and interference with other trades. Reserve reels, equipment and personnel zones together. Keep inspection and testing attendance inside the completion budget where the actual contract requires it. The installed cable and the returned usable building area are the common finish line.

## Two hypothetical cable-placement errors

**Mistake (hypothetical): The puller rating becomes the system rating.** A buyer hires a powerful unit while connectors, guides and anchor structure remain unspecified. The package is incomplete regardless of motor size. Obtain the contractor's complete component and reaction schedule before procuring the powered route.

**Mistake (hypothetical): The platform becomes an improvised anchor.** Another budget omits an accepted pulling support because a broad-deck machine is already available below the cable route. Keep the reaction system separately engineered and use personnel access only within its specifically documented task arrangement.
