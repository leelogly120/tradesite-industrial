**Choose a documented compatible retrofit when the assessed retained lighting assets can deliver the accepted design with the nominated upgrade. Buy complete renewal when the required layout, controls or equipment condition calls for a different system.** Compare the complete elevated campaign, including installation, specialist checks, removal and building handback. Fitting counts alone do not determine the access investment.

For a factory owner, the expensive mistake is to buy equipment before deciding what remains in service. For an installation contractor, it is to price a bounded component change as though it completes a new lighting system. Establish the accepted technical route first, then purchase the personnel and material resources that route requires.

## Find the retained asset boundary

Obtain the existing lighting inventory and the appointed lighting/electrical specialists' assessment. Identify actual product references, mounting arrangements, condition and the documents available for a proposed upgrade. Locate exceptions separately rather than treating every fixture visible from the floor as one identical asset.

[Signify's spare-parts and upgrade information](https://www.signify.com/global/signify-services/spare-parts-and-upgrade-kits) distinguishes replacement components, retrofit kits and connectivity options within its own offering. That establishes a documented renewal category. It is not proof that an unidentified luminaire accepts a particular kit or that ARCLIFT provides lighting design or electrical services.

Ask the responsible specialist to state the accepted retained equipment, new components and completed-system requirements. For full renewal, obtain the actual new layout and distribution/control scope. Procurement should price those outputs without prescribing electrical procedures or treating personnel access as permission for energized work.

## Compare the whole lighting result

Keep the same building use and accepted lighting outcome in both proposals. A compatible component upgrade can retain significant assets. A new system can change the mounting locations, controls and associated work. Neither route wins from a generic product claim or an assumed percentage energy saving.

Obtain actual project operating assumptions and the specialist's accepted design information for the business case. Separate the product/electrical investment from access cost, production-area possession and disposal. A machine purchase should be supported by the retained work pattern, not by attributing the entire lighting contract value to platform utilization.

## Build an asset-to-task worksheet

Fill this schedule from the actual asset records and accepted renewal proposal. Use fixture references and drawing revisions so the price remains linked to the system being retained or replaced.

| Purchase decision | Fillable project record | Responsible source and datum | Effect on the order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required outcome | Accepted lighting/design brief ___ | Owner and lighting designer; current building-use brief | Defines a comparable result |
| Existing assets | Product IDs ___; condition groups ___ | Electrical/lighting specialist; located inventory | Establishes the retained scope |
| Retrofit compatibility | Nominated upgrade ___; accepted application ___ | Actual product/system provider; written proposal | Determines whether the bounded route proceeds |
| Full-renewal scope | Fixtures, mounts, distribution and controls ___ | Responsible designer; issued layout/interface documents | Prices the complete alternative |
| Access positions | Task faces ___; levels ___; exceptions ___ | Trade and surveyor; local floor/building datums | Tests personnel-access coverage |
| Material services | New/removed objects ___; provider ___ | Installer and handling specialist; object schedule | Defines separate handling and waste costs |
| Acceptance stages | Checks ___; returns ___; handback ___ | Electrical/system specialists; project acceptance schedule | Establishes the equipment period |
| Full campaign cost | Product ___; trade ___; access ___; disruption ___ | Buyer; same completed building boundary | Supports the renewal decision |

The worksheet should produce a clear purchase: accepted retrofit by asset group, complete replacement by zone, or a mixed scope with identifiable boundaries. A vague instruction to upgrade the lights is not enough to justify a single equipment allowance for the building.

## Count the visits that remain overhead

The actual trade defines its work positions for removal, installation, specialist checks and any required return. Ask which stages can follow within one released zone and which depend on another provider. Price equipment retention and remobilisation against that actual sequence.

Where the facility remains occupied, the operator identifies the space and time it releases. Include access preparation and reinstatement in the programme. The fact that lighting work is scheduled outside the main production shift does not make the entire floor available to a broad platform.

## Two hypothetical lighting investments with the wrong scope

**Failure case: a generic kit description becomes compatibility approval.** A buyer assumes similar-looking fixtures accept the same upgrade and books a platform for a simple exchange. The actual specialist proposal has not identified the retained assets. Complete the product and condition assessment before purchasing a retrofit campaign or compare complete renewal.

**Failure case: the access budget ends after fixture installation.** Another quotation includes the new fittings but omits elevated tasks required for accepted system checks and closing work. Allocate those duties and visits before demobilisation. A completed hardware order and an accepted lighting result are different endpoints.
