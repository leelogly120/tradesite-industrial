Choose inline sealant application only when the accepted standing-seam product permits that manufacturing route and a supported integrated package serves repeat orders. Retain a separately defined application process when it meets the same product requirements and better fits the actual workload. If neither offered process matches the specified system, buy the accepted finished panels while the production route is resolved.

The first question is what sealant, location and delivered panel condition the product authority requires. “Sealant included” is too broad to describe the purchase. Separate manufacturing application from installation sealants, seam-closing tools and the completed roof's acceptance.

## Follow the specified product through the process

Obtain the roof-system or product owner's controlled requirement for the relevant panel. Identify the material reference, application location, permitted production stage, storage conditions and finished-output checks. The responsible product and material providers define these requirements; procurement carries them into both machinery alternatives.

An inline route connects the application function to production of the panel. A separate route has its own transfer, identification and release between forming and application. Define the accepted finished-product requirements for either route. Compare the complete sequence offered for the actual material and profile, including who accepts the resulting panel before packing.

## Put the hidden operating work into both offers

An integrated proposal needs the complete application equipment, material supply arrangements, controls and the interfaces with the former and cutter. It also needs an offered basis for pauses, changes of material or product, cleaning and maintenance. Obtain that scope from the responsible providers rather than inventing settings or assuming the line operator absorbs every extra task.

A separate process needs identified handling between stages, space for work awaiting application, product identification and inspection before release. Count the associated people and occupied area. If separate application is permitted but the workshop cannot preserve the required product condition between stages, choose a different supported layout or buy the specified finished product.

The cost comparison must include accepted production. Price equipment, application material, handling, cleaning resources, maintenance, inspection and rejected output over the same order programme. Do not count every metre leaving the former as completed saleable product when the application stage has not accepted it.

| Complete cost | Equipment, materials, cleaning, people and rejection ___ | Procurement and operations | Compares the two complete operating arrangements |

The section datum is the reference on the actual drawing, not a generic seam height. The process providers supply their proposed arrangement against that requirement. Any departure goes back to the product authority before it becomes an apparent equipment saving.

## Buy repeat production, not an unpriced accessory

Inline equipment deserves investment when a defined repeat product occupies enough of the actual order programme and the full supported arrangement fits the business. Keep exceptional materials or systems outside the base case unless their own production route is included.

A separate process is a useful deliberate choice where product requirements permit it and eligible demand is intermittent or mixed. Its inspection and transfer work should be visible in the cost and staffing plan. “Offline” should not mean unidentified work left to the packing crew.

For either route, use a representative trial with the specified metal, profile and application material. The product authority defines acceptance of the finished panel. Record the actual equipment and material used, including what was absent. A trial on another formulation or a bare formed sheet answers a different question.

The workshop photograph shows forming machinery occupying a substantial production area. Place application, material storage and inspection on that same layout so their space and service requirements are included before the machine order.

## Two hypothetical process failures

**Failure case: the quotation contains an interface but no complete application system.** A buyer treats a listed connection as a pump, material delivery, controls and commissioning package. The production team receives an incomplete process. Obtain an itemised responsibility list and a representative finished-panel acceptance scope before choosing integration.

**Failure case: the application route changes the specified product.** A producer chooses a convenient separate operation without the product authority accepting its location, material or delivered condition. The panel now differs from the customer requirement. Preserve the released specification and obtain the product decision before changing the manufacturing route.

## Eight RFQ attachments for a complete application proposal

1. The accepted roof-product identity and relevant production requirements.
2. Controlled panel section showing the application location and drawing datum.
3. Application-material reference and provider-issued handling/process requirements.
4. Metal specification, eligible profiles and representative order schedule.
5. Existing former records and the proposed application equipment basket.
6. Workshop layout showing separate-stage transfers, storage and utilities.
7. Required finished-panel observations, sample records and acceptance authority.
8. Included integration, cleaning, maintenance, training and destination support scope.
