**Choose ground or offsite preassembly for repeat components with released dimensions and a designed route to their installed position. Retain individual overhead assembly where the geometry is site-specific or a larger assembly cannot reach its position through the planned route.** Buy personnel access for the work left overhead, and purchase module handling as a separate function. A broad ceiling deck is not a substitute for a module-lifting proposal.

The useful question is where to perform each assembly task. Preassembly changes the work package; it does not remove every overhead connection, adjustment or inspection. A platform quotation based on the original piece-by-piece sequence can therefore include equipment time and work areas the revised method no longer needs.

## Three ways to deliver the same ceiling zone

The first route supplies individual components for assembly at the final position. The second delivers kits organised by area, with specified preparation already completed. The third supplies assemblies built to released drawings before installation. These routes sit at different points between material delivery and completed work.

Kitting is useful where component selection and preparation are repetitive but the final arrangement still follows local dimensions. It changes the supply package without requiring a large assembled object to pass through the building. Full preassembly deserves comparison when repeated geometry and coordinated interfaces justify moving more work away from the final position.

These are established procurement categories. [Hilti describes separate preassembly and kitting services for modular supports](https://www.hilti.com/content/hilti/W1/US/en/business/business/productivity/prefabrication-services.html), while [NG Bailey describes offsite assembly and testing of MEP modules](https://www.ngbailey.com/services/osm/). Those examples establish the distinction between the routes. Your ceiling or services designer must specify which components belong in your own assemblies.

## Freeze the interfaces before buying larger assemblies

Preassembly relies on dimensions and connections that survive delivery to the final position. Release the component drawing, building connection locations and permitted adjustment through the responsible designer. If those interfaces remain subject to site measurement, keep that work in the site package until the measurements are available.

An assembly is also a different handling object from its loose components. Obtain its complete dimensions, mass, handling provisions and delivery condition from the assembly supplier. The appointed lifting specialist then designs the movement and positioning arrangements. Do not derive those arrangements from the apparent size of a personnel deck.

## Compare tasks instead of counting fewer pieces

Use the same completed ceiling or services zone for every quotation. Fewer delivered objects are useful only when the complete route and installation work are addressed.

The output of the table is a task allocation. Some components belong in kits, others in repeat assemblies, and exceptional locations remain individually assembled. That mixed approach is a valid purchase if drawings and delivery labels keep the boundaries clear.

## Rewrite the access brief after the method changes

For each work zone, list what people actually do overhead: install individual supports, connect a positioned assembly, complete local joints or inspect finished work. Record the working position from the supporting floor and the space required beside the component.

Then specify the people, tools and materials that belong on the access equipment during those tasks. Keep the separately handled assembly outside that schedule unless the actual platform proposal expressly includes an applicable handling function and the project adopts it through its engineering process.

For a retained task needing a shared large work face, shortlist the [ARC-F25 crawler ceiling platform](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/). Ask for the offered deck layout, permitted load distribution and support reactions against the revised tasks. Use those issued values in the site engineer's floor and setup assessment.

## Two ways preassembly leaves a larger equipment gap

The following illustrative purchasing mistakes show what a complete assembly package must include.

**Failure case: workshop completion hides the positioning task.** A contractor orders assembled service sections and reduces its overhead labour allowance, but no offer includes moving those sections into position. Price the specialist handling and residual personnel access before selecting preassembly. A reduced component count alone is not a complete installation route.

**Failure case: the platform order follows an obsolete task schedule.** The project switches to prepared assemblies after equipment selection, but the access brief still assumes full overhead assembly everywhere. Reissue the actual connection and inspection tasks. Retain broad-deck access where the revised work requires it and purchase a separate arrangement for different task positions.

## Send a method-linked RFQ package

- Approved ceiling or services system and the completed work zones.
- Components assigned to individual supply, kits and preassembled units.
- Released assembly drawings and building connection locations.
- Assembly dimensions, masses and supplier-issued handling information.
- The appointed specialist's material positioning and delivery scope.
- Remaining overhead tasks, working positions and supporting-floor references.
- Crew, tools, materials, floor records and access-route drawings.
- Required access-equipment dates, transport destination and handover documents.
