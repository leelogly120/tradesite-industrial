Choose a consolidated site rental package when the main contractor can control equipment allocation, custody and cost recovery across the participating trades. Keep separate trade hire when subcontractors retain independent equipment obligations and the project cannot administer a common service reliably. Compare complete multi-asset packages; consolidation does not mean every crew shares one ceiling platform.

This decision concerns who buys and manages access across a project. It is different from deciding how many crews fit a deck or whether trades can share one machine in sequence. A central contract can include several platforms working simultaneously, while separate subcontractors can still coordinate their deliveries.

## Put the management service beside the equipment list

A consolidated offer needs more than a volume discount. It requires a named allocation process, asset records, equipment handover, support responsibilities and an agreed charging basis. Without those services, the project has merely moved several rental bills onto one purchaser.

[United Rentals describes a consolidated rental model](https://www.unitedrentals.com/project-uptime/expertise/introducing-united-rentals-consolidated-rental-program) in which contractors source project equipment through one provider and trade partners request it through a common schedule. That is an example of the commercial architecture. Price the actual local provider's scheduling and service commitments. ARCLIFT supplies the equipment package described below.

The low platform in the photograph below shows a specific physical asset occupying a defined area. A useful central record identifies that asset, the accepted duty, its current custodian and the next commercial action. A general booking reading “ceiling lift on site” is too vague for allocation or charging.

For separate hire, identify the equivalent responsibilities inside each trade package. The comparison is not central administration versus no administration. It is one managed project system versus several subcontractor-managed systems, with the coordination costs each arrangement actually requires.

## An allocation worksheet the commercial team can use

Complete one asset line for each required configuration, not just one line for each supplier. Use drawing positions and actual release periods for physical demand. Use the proposed agreements for commercial responsibility.

| Buyer input | Entry and controlling record | Responsible owner | Effect on the sourcing decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible work duty | Trade, workface/grid and accepted equipment configuration ___ | Trade supervisor and equipment provider | Keeps allocation within the task the asset actually serves |
| Concurrent demand | Required independent positions and dates ___ | Main-contractor planner | Sets the asset count; central purchasing cannot erase real overlap |
| Custody | Issued asset ID, receiving person and handback record ___ | Site equipment coordinator and trade lead | Makes possession and condition traceable |
| Cost allocation | Charge period, cost code and treatment of idle time ___ | Commercial manager and participating trades | Tests whether the central purchaser can recover the agreed cost |
| Service boundary | Inspection, support, delivery and collection inclusions ___ | Actual rental provider and site manager | Exposes work retained by the project |
| Change authority | Person authorised to extend, exchange or off-hire each asset ___ | Contract manager | Prevents unpriced occupation and conflicting instructions |
| Exceptions | Specialist tasks, incompatible configurations and separate-hire allowances ___ | Trade package managers | Preserves necessary specialist equipment outside the common pool |
| Final comparison | Full equipment/service, site administration and removal costs ___ | Estimator using actual offers | Ranks two complete sourcing models |

A custodian record does not replace operator competence or task acceptance. Keep the equipment's permitted use and the site's management responsibilities intact whichever party pays the invoice. Central purchasing changes the commercial organisation, not the machine's capabilities.

## Count assets first, then compare purchasing arrangements

Start from the simultaneous workfaces that require independent positions. Group tasks only where the actual configuration and sequence support it. The [shared-platform sequencing guide](/blog/shared-ceiling-platform-multitrade-fitout-sequence/) addresses that physical sharing decision. Use its output as demand data here rather than repeating a one-machine assumption.

Next, compare central and separate sourcing for the same demand schedule. Include mobilisation, collection, actual support services, equipment occupation and the project's administration. Keep the treatment of waiting and extensions explicit. A lower rental rate is not a saving when the central contract leaves the project carrying unallocated idle occupation.

Separate hire is the clearer choice when trades must control incompatible equipment packages and the main contractor has no workable allocation or recovery process. Coordination can still set delivery slots and site rules without taking over every rental contract.

A consolidated package earns its place when named management resources can release redundant assets, administer changes and assign costs to the agreed records. The project does not need to promise a utilization percentage to make this choice. It needs an enforceable commercial process and a realistic asset schedule.

The folded crawler equipment below illustrates another allocation issue: assets have different delivery and receiving envelopes. Keep physical mobilisation requirements on the asset record even when the purchase is centralised.

## Do not centralise away specialist work

A common pool is not a universal equipment class. Isolated access, a broad shared work surface, wall installation and component handling can require different configurations or separate providers. Identify those exceptions before negotiating the common contract.

Keep task-specific products and services in their own lines when they are not included in the central provider's actual offer. This avoids a trade discovering that its nominal access allowance buys a machine suitable for another package. The central buyer should be able to explain which duty each asset serves and which duties remain separately procured.

Set an off-hire owner for every line. Completion of one subcontractor's task does not automatically end the equipment's project occupation, and a central coordinator's booking does not automatically extend a provider's commitment. Use the actual contract's change and return process, with one named commercial instruction path.

## Two consolidation plans that fail

**Failure case: one purchaser, several unassigned costs.** A hypothetical main contractor takes the rental contract but leaves trades free to retain machines without recording custody or extensions. Equipment stays on site after its original task, and nobody accepts the idle charge. Establish the allocation, change authority and recovery records before centralising the purchase; otherwise keep accountable trade hire.

**Failure case: a common pool omits an incompatible duty.** Another project replaces separate trade allowances with a generic ceiling-platform line. A specialist task needs a different accepted working arrangement and is not covered by the common offer. Restore the specialist asset or service line and compare the complete packages rather than treating the missing duty as a rental saving.

Both are purchasing failures, not evidence that all pooled equipment underperforms. Fix the management and scope conditions, then select the sourcing model that meets them.
