Buy a containerized roof-panel plant when repeated contracts require the forming equipment to work inside a purpose-designed enclosure and the bidder can show how coil, personnel, spares and panels pass through it. Buy an open trailer roll former when the offered machine suits the site's operating conditions, the crew can reach its service points and a compatible towing arrangement is available. Compare a complete production day at the destination before deciding which package to tow or deliver.

A container and a trailer describe different parts of the purchase. A containerized plant can itself travel on a semitrailer. A trailer can also carry enclosed equipment. For this comparison, one bidder supplies a production enclosure with the former installed inside it. The other supplies a machine on an open trailer with its specified covers and accessories. Write those definitions on the bid sheet so that a quotation headed “mobile machine” has an unambiguous scope.

## Walk one coil through the offered package

Start at the delivery point. Who provides the coil stand, who brings the coil to the machine and where does the feed unit sit during production? On an enclosed package, mark the clear loading opening and the route beside it. Include room to access the actual reel and controls. On an open trailer, mark equipment that stays on the frame and items that must be set out separately. The working footprint includes these items and the route between them.

BEMO's site instructions distinguish a container with a built-in panel former from the semitrailer vehicle. They also identify separate requirements for lateral coil loading and panel runout. New Tech Machinery describes a trailer designed for particular roof-panel machines. These are two real supply arrangements. Request the drawings and transport details for the exact offer under review. [BEMO mobile forming requirements](https://bemo.com/wp-content/uploads/BEMO-Rollforming-Conditions-on-site_EN.pdf) and [New Tech Machinery's trailer overview](https://newtechmachinery.com/learning-center/video/roof-panel-machine-trailer-video/)

*Ask the supplier to mark the service side of each stand and the space needed to remove the longest service part.*

Now follow the panel out. The longest accepted sheet still needs a supported runout and a receiving crew, wherever the walls of the plant end. Compare jobs with a clear end-on lane against jobs where the output would block a delivery crossing. An enclosure can protect a specified part of the forming process while the coil queue or finished sheets remain outside. Buy separate weather protection where the material supplier or site plan requires it.

## Mobilisation has two prices

The first price is the recurring move. Request outward transport, unloading or setting up, return transport and the time the carrier stays dedicated to the job. A demountable enclosure can release its carrier only where the offered system and site unloading arrangement support that plan. Where the plant operates on its carrier, carry the occupation cost for the production period. For the open trailer, identify the towing vehicle, connection, route restrictions and the party responsible for local transport acceptance.

The second price is the cost of getting back into production. Record which connections, guides, runout supports or control equipment have to be restored after each move. Give the supplier a project-specific first-piece acceptance requirement. If your contracts include a succession of small orders at different sites, the hours before the first accepted panel can matter more than the hours spent rolling. If the plant stays at one location through a long package, space for maintenance and replacement parts takes a larger share of the purchasing decision.

## Two ways a portable purchase can lose a job

**Mistake 1 — Accepting a packing description as the operating offer.** Imagine an importer reads “container included” and expects a repeatable production station. The offer places loose machinery inside a freight box for shipment. The buyer has no drawing for production inside that box. Request a revised installed-plant offer or compare the loose machine with a correctly specified open carrier and site setup.

**Mistake 2 — Reserving space only for the trailer.** Suppose the contractor can park beside the building, but the designated coil delivery side faces a permanent fence. Moving the trailer solves the coil approach and blocks the long-sheet runout. Price the site in its usable orientation, including the alternative delivery and receiving arrangements. If the representative jobs have the same conflict, choose another package or contract the panel supply.

## RFQ attachments for a mobile forming plant

- Order example with profile drawing, material properties, coating, lengths and acceptance criteria.
- Representative site plans showing gate, setup area, coil approach and full panel discharge path.
- Installed equipment layout identifying fixed, removable and separately supplied components.
- Offered transport dimensions, masses and carrier or tow-vehicle responsibilities.
- Loading, unloading and site setup scope with named provider for each activity.
- Service-access drawing including removable tooling, drive parts and storage for spares.
- Operating conditions for the actual machine, enclosure, power and outside material staging.
- Cost breakdown for one move, site attendance, first-piece acceptance and return collection.
