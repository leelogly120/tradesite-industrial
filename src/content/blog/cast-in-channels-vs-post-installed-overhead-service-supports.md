**Order cast-in channels where the services designer can release support zones before the concrete pour and the structural designer accepts the proposed system. Choose designed post-installed anchors for released fixing positions that must be set after the structure exists.** A mixed schedule works when repeat runs are stable but specialist equipment layouts remain open. Buy access against the actual fixings left to install, inspect and correct beneath the slab.

A contractor comparing two anchor prices can miss the most expensive difference: when survey information becomes binding. An embedded channel needs a location before the concrete conceals it. A post-installed support transfers more work to the finished underside, where other trades can occupy the same bay. Neither purchase removes the need to install the actual hangers and services.

For a concrete project, request sections that show the soffit, installed services, planned fixing level and crew position together. The access supplier needs those differences even if the building elevation is unchanged.

## The pour release is a commercial deadline

Ask the services coordinator for a list of stable support lines and areas reserved for later tenant changes. Have the structural designer identify accepted fixing systems, load cases and allowable positions. The concrete contractor then states when inserts must arrive, who sets them out, and what information will be recorded before concealment. If the service line changes after that date, give the change an engineering and access allowance of its own.

[HALFEN describes its cast-in channels](https://www.halfen.com/en_GB/product-ranges/concrete/fixing-systems/cast-in-channels) as fixings for concrete structures. A channel gives adjustment only within its documented system and orientation. The relevant evidence is the project fixing schedule and the actual as-built position. A channel crossing the general service zone does not necessarily provide an accepted support for every item subsequently placed there.

For the post-installed route, obtain the fixing design, substrate information, restrictions around reinforcement or embedded services, and the inspection requirements from the responsible specialists. The quotation should identify survey, equipment, consumables, installer competence and records. A late request to move an anchor goes back to the design authority. The site team needs an instruction and an accepted position before it spends the access shift attempting installation.

## Price the work that remains after the concrete is placed

For the cast-in quote, count set-out, supply, placement, pre-pour checks, as-built survey and any explicitly priced correction work. Then add the under-slab visits to fit hangers, install the services and inspect them. An embedded component is an interface for later work. Quoting zero access cost after the pour leaves a missing trade in the programme.

For the post-installed route, arrange the estimate by fixing groups that can be completed from the same accepted machine position. Include survey, location restrictions, installation, any project-specified inspection or testing and the return visit after correction. Give the programme manager a marked list of bays that will close when ceilings, ducts or partitions arrive. Two identical groups of hangers can have different access costs if one is released after fit-out.

The lowered deck in the workshop photograph shows equipment that occupies floor area before it rises. Plot the offered unit at the door, along the travel route and at each working bay using the manufacturer's drawings. The machine supplier provides machine data and the building team accepts the floor and supporting positions.

## Two mistakes that change the anchor purchase

**Mistake one — buying inserts from a coordination model with open plant selections.** The eventual unit shifts outside the embedded band. The contractor has paid for both cast-in material and a later designed fixing. Limit the initial release to settled bays and price an approved later solution for the remainder.

**Mistake two — counting every drilled fixing as uninterrupted production.** A preferred fixing line conflicts with embedded services and the rest of the ceiling crew moves in before a replacement line is released. Separate production positions from exceptions and allow for the return access only on an agreed programme.
