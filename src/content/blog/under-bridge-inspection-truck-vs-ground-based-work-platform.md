Buy a specialist under-bridge truck package when the work must reach from the bridge deck around its edge and back beneath the structure, and the accepted road occupation supports that package. Compare ground-based access when usable positions below the bridge connect to the required workfaces. Price the complete access route, not the bridge's overall height.

The deciding distinction is where the equipment is supported and how its working surface reaches the task. Water, traffic or an inaccessible area below can remove the ground option. A restricted bridge deck or an edge outside the truck's offered envelope can remove the deck-side option. If both are excluded, commission a different specialist access proposal.

## Put the inspection task on a bridge section

Ask the inspection lead to identify the exposed surfaces and actual worker positions included in the assignment. Separate the accessible underside from internal spaces, pier faces and other tasks requiring a different method. A contract to inspect a bridge is not a single uniform platform position.

Use a section through each materially different span or edge. Mark the roadway, parapet, underside geometry, nearby services and the proposed supporting level. For the ground alternative, show the actual ground or supporting structure beneath the task. For the truck alternative, show the route around the edge into the under-bridge working position.

[MOOG's truck-mounted under-bridge product information](https://www.moog-online.com/us/products/mbi-70-1-s-truck-mounted/) distinguishes horizontal reach, lowering depth, edge-crossing geometry and space occupied on the bridge. Those separate categories explain the purchasing problem. Its dimensions and capabilities belong to that specific equipment, not to ARCLIFT's crawler platform.

## Two supporting locations create two complete quotations

A deck-side package includes the specialist vehicle and the accepted space it occupies above. Its offer must address the actual edge and underside positions, mobilisation and the resources needed for the proposed occupation.

A below-bridge package includes the equipment, its arrival path, accepted support positions and any site work retained by the customer. A visible area of ground is not enough. The proposed machine must reach the required working positions from the specific support locations the site can provide.

Keep the workface schedule common to both offers. Use the same physical task and completion boundary so a quotation covering only an easy outer strip is not ranked against one serving all requested positions.

Use the bridge's issued drawing datum and relate every support position to it. A ground-to-underside dimension and a truck's downward reach describe different parts of the route. One “working height” entry cannot replace the section.

## Keep the space below in the comparison

For a ground-based proposal, identify what occupies the area during the actual work period. Include roads, water, utilities, stored equipment and access controlled by other parties. Establish the accepted route and working footprint before requesting a platform solely from the vertical dimension.

The unfinished industrial route in the next photograph makes a general site issue visible: the equipment's destination and the ground crossed to reach it are separate inputs. Use current records from the real bridge location rather than adopting that photographed surface as a suitable setup area.

If the below-bridge area cannot supply accepted positions, remove that ground offer from the cost ranking. If the truck requires deck occupation the project cannot obtain, remove that truck arrangement. Seek an actual alternative access scope instead of allowing the lower price to conceal an inaccessible workface.

When both routes are eligible, compare the complete occupation needed for the same task. Include the project's own preparation and coordination costs and the actual providers' delivery, attendance and removal offers. Retain a separately priced package for surfaces outside the chosen arrangement.

## Separate the inspection service from the access asset

The inspection specialist defines the work and its acceptance criteria. The access provider defines the offered equipment and its coverage. The bridge owner and appointed reviewers establish the accepted interfaces with the structure and available occupation. Keep these roles distinct in the purchase.

A larger platform does not buy a qualified inspection team. A specialist truck quotation also needs an explicit service boundary: equipment supply, operated attendance and inspection services are different commercial items. Compare the actual proposed package without assuming that every item is included.

A ground-supported large deck is relevant only to working positions that the actual offered configuration can serve. Keep bridge-edge deployment, unusual reach and internal inspection tasks with the specialist equipment and methods specified for them.

## Two access bids that omit the deciding geometry

**Mistake: the buyer specifies bridge height but omits the parapet.** A hypothetical truck enquiry gives the road-to-water height and no section through the edge or the underside workface. The resulting class-based quotation does not address the actual route around the edge. Add the surveyed section and the specialist's proposed envelope before purchasing that access package.

**Mistake: a ground platform is priced below a span without a route.** Another tender identifies ground near the bridge but omits the barrier and operating road between the delivery point and the proposed support position. The equipment price leaves site access outside the offer. Price an accepted below-bridge route or compare a different specialist arrangement serving the same task.

These are hypothetical procurement failures. The correction is a complete, accepted support-to-workface path for every position included in the order.
