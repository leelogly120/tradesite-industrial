A truck-mounted forming lift needs more than a parking position. The project must connect public or controlled-road arrival, the site gate, internal route, setup envelope, material staging, support condition and roof receiving zone. A base vehicle that can reach the gate may still conflict with turns, overhead services, temporary works or the final operating boundary. The setup review records those interfaces and their owners without declaring the vehicle road-authorized or the site suitable.

## Define the setup state and route boundary

Start with the exact vehicle and equipment state expected at each part of the journey. Separate road movement, gate entry, internal travel, setup and work. The distinction keeps a road dimension or concept sketch from being treated as the final operating envelope.

### Describe arrival and transition states

Record whether the forming module, supports, material-handling items or other components remain in their road state at the gate and where any transition is expected. Identify the party that controls each transition and the document that defines it. Do not create operating steps from a sales image. The technical review needs only a controlled state description, dimensions and interfaces appropriate to the proposed configuration.

### Survey gate-to-work-face access

Measure gates, turns, gradients, crossfalls, overhead restrictions, shoulders, temporary crossings and approach space. Show traffic, pedestrians, emergency routes and construction deliveries. A base chassis route check is not enough because the integrated state may have a different envelope and movement restriction. Mark every value as measured, drawn or estimated and assign local review of the completed configuration.

### Preserve a withdrawal plan

Show how the equipment leaves after panels, materials and temporary structures alter the site. A route that is open during mobilization may close later. Record schedule dependencies and alternative gates before committing to the setup concept. If withdrawal needs partial disassembly or a special transport state, identify the responsible party and keep the final method subject to controlled configuration information.

## Lay out the operating and support zones

The work-face plan should show the vehicle, equipment, support boundary, material route, receiving team and surrounding exclusion area. Use separate overlays for setup and operation when their envelopes differ.

### Draw the setup envelope as a planning area

Use controlled preliminary data to mark the area needed for positioning, transition to work state, access, inspection and withdrawal. Do not label the area with unverified dimensions or imply that a drawn support location is approved. The plan should identify nearby edges, trenches, structures and traffic. Final geometry must follow the actual configuration, issued drawings and the site method.

### Coordinate material and panel routes

Map coil or sheet delivery, staging, transfer to forming, completed-panel support and roof receipt. Keep delivery vehicles and people outside the controlled equipment boundary unless the approved method states otherwise. The setup choice should reduce avoidable crossings, but no layout eliminates the need for communication and isolation. Name custody owners at each material handover and show the stop condition.

### Connect support review to vehicle integration

The site specialist needs configuration-specific loads or reactions for the proposed state, while the vehicle integrator needs the equipment and support interfaces. Keep these exchanges linked. A ground conclusion for one setup state should not be reused after the carrier, module or operating geometry changes. Likewise, a chassis review does not approve the slab, soil, temporary platform or nearby underground feature.

## Release a setup package with named owners

A setup package should make it obvious which party controls the vehicle record, integrated envelope, site route, support review, forming task and roof handover. A single supplier box hides too much responsibility.

- Show road state, gate state, setup state and work state on separate controlled overlays.
- Assign vehicle integration, route control, support review and roof receiving to named roles.
- Record working height and outreach assumptions without treating them as an approved operating envelope.
- Describe roof slope or building geometry, nearby edges and any changing work-face condition.
- List task material, panel length, coil or feed information only where it affects the reviewed interface.
- Assign weather and wind monitoring to the site team under its approved work method.
- Provide ground or floor records and name the competent party responsible for support review.
- Map access and route states from delivery through setup, task work and withdrawal.
- State destination, transport and chassis constraints that belong to local or integration review.
- Record voltage, power, control and documentation requirements with revision status and owner.

### Issue a route and zone index

List each drawing, survey, photograph and interface sheet with its revision and owner. Link open questions to exact route segments or setup zones. This prevents an updated gate plan from silently replacing the work-face record used by the technical team. Remove traceable customer, vehicle and site identifiers from material that travels outside the controlled project channel.

### Use hold points before irreversible actions

Typical holds include ordering a chassis, completing attachments, mobilizing to site, entering the work route, transitioning to work state and starting forming. The project should decide which holds apply and who releases them. A commercial deadline does not close a missing structural, vehicle or local-rule review. Show the required document beside each hold point.

### Define changes that reopen setup review

A new chassis, body arrangement, module state, route, support area, roof elevation, panel handover line or adjacent activity can affect the setup. Record change triggers in the issue package. When one occurs, compare the new condition against the approved baseline and route affected questions back to their owners instead of revising only the drawing that is easiest to change.

## Compare setup concepts with visible constraints

### Balance road mobility and site-space demand

A truck-based concept can combine transport and equipment interfaces, yet the completed arrangement may need more controlled setup space, a destination-specific vehicle path and disciplined transition between road and work states. A crawler concept may change those boundaries but introduce its own delivery and site-route questions. Compare the trade-offs against the real gate, support zone and roof workflow. Do not let road access alone decide the work-face architecture.

The practical trade-off is between early coordination and false precision. A useful record exposes each constraint, interface and unresolved owner; it does not convert preliminary information into an approval.

For general planning context, see the <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/geis6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE guidance on selecting and managing mobile elevating work platforms</a>. It supports general work-planning principles only. It does not verify an ARCLIFT configuration, settle destination rules or approve a site.

### Know what a setup review cannot authorize

This method may not fit a project that lacks controlled site information, named decision owners or a safe way to close open items. This review cannot approve road movement, registration, vehicle modification, ground support, lifting, work at height, panel handling or site operation. It may be unsuitable where the actual chassis or equipment state is unknown, the route is changing or the project has not assigned vehicle and site decision owners. Keep those interfaces open rather than turning a concept plan into an authorization document.

### Send a gate-to-work-face setup record

Provide the proposed road and work states, controlled envelope, gate and route survey, setup-zone plan, material journey, support records, roof interface and responsibility matrix. Ask reviewers to identify configuration-specific data and local approvals still required. Keep vehicle identifiers and private project details in the controlled channel only.

Send the site, destination, transport, work-zone and ground or floor records through a controlled project channel. ## FAQ

#### Is a truck that reaches the gate suitable for the work face?

Not necessarily. The integrated state, internal turns, overhead restrictions, support zone, transition envelope and roof workflow all need project-specific review.

#### Should the setup drawing show support locations?

It may show preliminary planning zones from controlled information, but final support geometry and loads require configuration-specific documents and competent site review. Label every preliminary area clearly.

#### Who checks road and site requirements?

The project should appoint the relevant vehicle integrator, local advisers and site roles. ARCLIFT can coordinate selection inputs, but the supply relationship does not decide destination road or site authorization.

#### What changes require a new setup review?

Changes to chassis, body, equipment state, route, ground or floor, roof zone, panel handling, utilities or adjacent operations should reopen the affected interfaces.
