A proposed truck-mounted forming-lift layout is ready for coordinated review only when it shows one continuous boundary from gate arrival to withdrawal. Each route segment must name the equipment state, controlled evidence, interface owner and current status; the material path, support-review zones and roof handover must connect to the same layout. A missing state, contradictory drawing or unowned boundary is the blocker. “Ready for review” does not mean road-authorized, structurally approved or released for work.

This is a layout-coordination decision, not an initial RFQ, a chassis-integration decision, a ground-support conclusion or a roof-panel receiving method. Use the [truck chassis interface review](/blog/truck-mounted-roll-forming-chassis-interface-review/) to establish the vehicle/equipment integration record, the [ground-support review](/blog/crawler-roll-forming-lift-ground-support-review/) to organize evidence for the appointed support specialist, and the [roof-panel handover guide](/blog/elevated-roll-forming-roof-panel-handover-zones/) to define custody at the receiving boundary. The [ARC-T truck-mounted lift page](/products/arc-t18-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/) remains a reference entry, not proof that a configuration fits this site.

## Decide whether the boundary is reviewable

The useful output of this review is not a coloured site plan with no evidence behind it. It is a traceable answer: either the proposed layout is connected enough for the responsible reviewers to assess, or a named interface remains on hold. That answer lets a buyer return one precise question to the right owner instead of circulating another generic checklist.

### Apply three readiness tests

First, test continuity: can a reviewer follow the proposed state and controlled zone from gate entry, along the internal route, into setup, through material delivery and forming, to roof handover and eventual withdrawal? Second, test evidence: does every segment point to a current drawing, survey, interface record or assigned project input? Third, test ownership: is one party named to resolve each open item and issue the closure record?

A row can remain “Open” and still be useful when the missing evidence and owner are explicit. It becomes a blocking “Hold” when the missing or conflicting item prevents the next interface from being assessed. A blank row is neither open nor accepted; it means the layout boundary has not been defined.

### Keep four neighbouring decisions separate

Chassis-interface review answers whether the proposed vehicle, body, module and attachment information can enter integration review. This page should use the resulting controlled transport/setup state and envelope; it should not choose the chassis, allocate axle loads, design attachments or decide road legality.

Ground-support review belongs to the appointed ground or structural specialist. The setup register identifies which travel and setup zones need an input or output from that review, but it cannot turn surface observations into allowable support values. An initial [RFQ data review](/blog/high-altitude-roll-forming-lift-rfq-data/) comes earlier and asks whether a configuration discussion can begin. Roof-panel handover comes later at the custody boundary and defines receiving readiness, not the gate-to-workface layout.

### Define the review boundary in one sentence

Write a project-specific boundary before comparing layouts. A useful pattern is: “This review begins with the proposed integrated arrival state at the controlled site gate and ends when the equipment, material route and roof receiving boundary are connected to a defined withdrawal state.” Then list what it does not release.

The exclusions normally include public-road authorization, vehicle modification approval, support engineering, operating procedures, panel-support design and roof installation. They are not boilerplate. Each exclusion points to a separate decision owner and prevents a site-layout mark-up from being mistaken for that owner’s authorization.

## Trace route and equipment states

A route line is reviewable only in relation to the state moving along it. The base chassis, an integrated transport state and a proposed setup state can have different evidence boundaries. Record the governing state reference instead of assuming one outline represents them all.

### Break the route at decision points

Create a new segment wherever control, geometry or evidence changes: controlled gate, security or induction area, internal traffic route, constrained turn, overhead interface, transition area, final positioning zone and withdrawal route. The project decides the actual breaks. The article does not prescribe a driving path or movement method.

For each segment, identify whether width, height, turning space, gradient, crossfall, surface condition, overhead services, temporary works, pedestrian interface or delivery traffic is relevant. Mark the source as measured survey, controlled drawing, issued state envelope or unresolved project input. A photograph can help locate a question, but it does not establish the equipment configuration or close a dimension.

### Name the state at every handoff

The gate row should identify the proposed arrival state. The internal-route row should identify the state intended to travel. The setup row should identify the controlled transition and work-state references. The withdrawal row should identify what is expected to leave after stored materials, temporary works and completed roof areas have changed the site.

This is state documentation, not a sequence of operating steps. If a transition needs configuration-specific instructions, authorisation or supervision, the register names the required document and owner without recreating that instruction in editorial text. The issued equipment and project documents govern the actual method.

### Read site hazards within their source scope

The <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UK HSE construction page on mobile elevating work platforms</a> says vehicle routes should account for pedestrians and traffic, and it identifies overhead cables and nearby vehicles among collision hazards in its MEWP context. These are useful prompts for a UK site-route review. They do not classify a truck-mounted forming-lift concept as a MEWP, define destination law, verify an ARCLIFT configuration or approve a route.

The <a href="https://www.ipaf.org/en/resource-library/site-assessment-mewp-selection-checklist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IPAF site-assessment checklist for MEWP selection</a> also offers general prompts for access restrictions and overhead hazards. Its scope must remain visible: it is not a substitute for the actual integrated-state drawing, local rules, support evidence or the project’s competent review.

## Complete the zone/state readiness register

Copy the compact register below into the controlled project workspace. It is deliberately blank: the prompts organize project evidence without presenting an invented completed record. Add project identifiers only inside the secure project record, never to public material.

On smaller screens, swipe the table to see all columns.

| Zone or state | Evidence and owner | Status / blocking interface |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Gate arrival state | State reference: ___; gate survey owner: ___ | ___ |
| Internal travel state and route | Route record: ___; traffic owner: ___ | ___ |
| Transition and setup zone | State drawing: ___; setup-zone owner: ___ | ___ |
| Material delivery, staging and feed boundary | Material-route record: ___; logistics owner: ___ | ___ |
| Support-review zones | Required input/output: ___; specialist owner: ___ | ___ |
| Roof handover boundary | Receiving-zone record: ___; handover owner: ___ | ___ |
| Withdrawal state and route | State/route record: ___; release owner: ___ | ___ |

Use only project-defined statuses. A workable starting set is “Ready for review,” “Open,” “Hold” and “Not applicable.” “Ready for review” means the listed evidence exists for the named reviewer; it is not equipment approval. “Open” names an obtainable input and owner. “Hold” identifies the exact interface that prevents connected review. “Not applicable” needs a recorded reason.

### Populate each row with decision evidence

- For the gate-arrival row, attach the controlled transport or chassis state reference, site access survey and destination routing requirement; do not substitute the base-vehicle brochure.
- For internal travel, identify the route drawing, overhead-height and turning evidence, pedestrian or traffic owner, and every segment whose access status is still open.
- For transition and setup, reference the proposed equipment state, setup-zone geometry, adjacent operations and the support or outrigger information required from controlled configuration documents.
- For material flow, mark delivery, coil or feed staging and crossing points, plus the site logistics owner responsible for keeping that route compatible with the reserved work zone.
- For support-review zones, name the ground or floor records, changing surface conditions and the appointed specialist’s required output; do not enter an editorial bearing conclusion.
- For roof handover and withdrawal, link roof geometry, weather or wind ownership, receiving-zone status, control or power interfaces, documentation requirements and the final route-release owner.

These prompts are specific to the register rows; they do not claim that every field applies to every site. Where a field is irrelevant, record why rather than deleting the interface without review.

### Test the joins between rows

Read down the first column as one physical and state sequence. Then read across each row to confirm that the evidence describes the same state and the owner can issue the needed answer. Finally, test the joins: gate to internal travel, travel to setup, delivery to feed, setup to support zone, and forming output to roof receipt.

A row-by-row package can still fail at a join. For example, the route survey may end before the transition area, or the roof receiving plan may use a different workface reference from the setup drawing. The blocker belongs at that join, with both source records named.

Test three types of join separately. A **state join** confirms that the departing state on one row is the arriving state on the next; it catches a gate survey based on a base vehicle when internal travel assumes an integrated configuration. A **space join** confirms that two zones meet without an undocumented gap or overlap; it catches a delivery lane crossing the reserved setup area. An **ownership join** confirms that the outgoing owner’s closure record is an accepted input for the incoming owner; it catches a roof team waiting for a handover definition that the setup package never requests.

Then check material and equipment paths against the same drawing basis. The truck route can be continuous while the coil, feed or completed-panel route is not. Likewise, a receiving zone can be defined while its relationship to the forming output remains unresolved. Record these as interface blockers rather than inferring that one clear route proves the whole workface arrangement is ready.

Support status should travel with the relevant state and zone. An opinion about one parking area does not automatically cover an internal route, a transition area or a changed setup position. The register needs only the specialist record reference, applicable boundary and status. It must not reproduce calculations, choose support measures or broaden a conclusion beyond the source reviewer’s stated scope.

### Report one outcome, not a vague score

Use one of three conclusions. “Connected for review” means the complete boundary and evidence owners are visible, while approvals remain with their respective reviewers. “Blocked at [named interface]” means the layout is defined but a specific contradiction or missing decision prevents review from continuing. “Boundary not yet defined” means the state or zone sequence itself is incomplete.

Do not convert the register into a numeric readiness score. A high score can hide one decisive missing interface, while a low score can exaggerate several minor document requests. The named blocking interface is more useful to procurement and project coordination than an average.

## Resolve conflicts and control changes

The central trade-off is not early coordination versus accuracy. It is between preserving visible uncertainty and hiding it inside a polished composite drawing. A useful review may carry open items, but it must show which constraint they affect, who owns them and what controlled record will close them.

### Resolve evidence conflicts at the source

Consider a hypothetical gate plan based on a base-chassis outline while the integration record shows a different proposed arrival-state envelope. Do not select the more convenient outline or copy one value into both files. Place the gate row on hold, retain both document references, ask their issuing owners to reconcile the state, and record the controlled revision that resolves the conflict.

Another hypothetical conflict is a delivery lane that occupies the proposed setup zone. This is not solved by telling one team to “coordinate.” The site logistics owner and setup-zone owner must decide which boundary or schedule changes, then reissue the affected route/material records. The register records their decision and its consequences; it does not instruct vehicle or material movement.

### Reopen only connected decisions

A changed gate, truck/equipment state, internal route, support area, material staging position, roof handover line or adjacent activity can reopen the affected rows. Trace the change through the joins rather than automatically declaring the entire package invalid or revising only the easiest drawing.

For example, if temporary edge works move the planned roof handover line, the handover owner must revise that boundary. The setup owner then checks whether the output-to-receiving join and proposed equipment position remain coherent; the support specialist is re-engaged only if the applicable zone or state changed. This example explains routing of decisions, not an installation or operating method.

Schedule changes deserve the same trace. If stored materials or another contractor occupy a previously reviewed route, the physical survey may remain valid while access status changes. Keep the survey reference, reopen the route row, identify the site-logistics decision, and check whether the alternative changes a support zone or transition boundary. Separating unchanged evidence from the changed decision avoids both unnecessary rework and silent reuse of an unavailable route.

Withdrawal is a genuine change check, not a copy of mobilization. The route may be narrower after materials, temporary works or completed building elements are in place, and the proposed departure state may differ from arrival. The register should show the later site-layout basis, state reference and release owner. If that boundary cannot be defined, the layout remains blocked even when entry and setup appear reviewable.

### Stop when the layout cannot support review

This review may not fit a project that has no controlled integrated-state reference, no stable site-layout basis or no named owners for route, setup, support and roof interfaces. It cannot authorize public-road movement, vehicle integration, ground support, work at height, material handling, forming operations or roof installation. It should also stop where a private identifier or customer record would have to be published to explain the decision.

Keep unknown product parameters, reactions, clearances, permissions and configuration limits unresolved until the responsible source issues them. A generic HSE or IPAF page, editorial image or neighbouring ARCLIFT article cannot replace that evidence. Where local rules or competence requirements apply, the destination project must appoint the relevant reviewers.

## FAQ

#### Is the layout review complete when the truck reaches the gate?

No. Gate access closes only one row. The review must still connect the proposed integrated travel state, internal route, transition/setup zone, material path, support-review zones, roof handover boundary and withdrawal state. A base-chassis route check does not establish that full boundary.

#### What makes an open item a blocking interface?

An item becomes blocking when its absence or contradiction prevents the next route, state, material, support or handover interface from being assessed. Record the affected join, evidence owner and expected closure document. Do not hide it behind a general “coordination required” note.

#### Does this register replace a ground-support review?

No. It identifies the travel and setup zones that need specialist input and records the status of that interface. It provides no support reaction, bearing value, temporary-work design, slab conclusion or approval. Those outputs belong to configuration-specific documents and the appointed competent reviewer.

#### How should two drawings with different boundaries be handled?

Keep both controlled references visible, place the affected row on hold and return the conflict to their issuing owners. Record which revision becomes controlling and which connected rows must be checked again. Do not reconcile a contradiction by silently editing an editorial copy.

#### When should the setup review be reopened?

Reopen the affected rows when a gate, integrated state, route, setup/support zone, material path, roof handover line, adjacent activity or withdrawal condition changes. Trace the change through connected interfaces; the project’s change process decides the actual release authority and required rechecks.
