Buy coil already slit to the agreed input width when your roof-panel orders are intermittent, material combinations vary, or your team is still establishing production. Consider a separate slitting investment when repeat demand, a workable width mix and a supported handling operation justify it together. Choose the roof-panel former against its confirmed input material first; a cheaper wide coil is not a complete reason to buy another production line.

For a contractor buying a roof-panel roll-forming lift, this is an upstream supply decision. The lift positions a compatible forming machine near the roof outlet. It does not turn every available coil into suitable feedstock. The coil-transfer photograph below shows why storage, receipt and handling belong in the equipment budget even when a processor supplies the finished slit coil.

## Separate three purchases before comparing prices

A roof-panel former shapes the agreed strip into the specified panel profile. Slitting divides wider strip into narrower widths. Lifting equipment positions or handles a defined load within its approved arrangement. One quotation can coordinate several interfaces, but those functions need separate equipment descriptions.

Bradbury lists slitting as a coil-processing equipment category with its own coil storage, cutting and handling features. That separation supports treating slitting as an additional production investment, rather than an assumed feature of a roof-panel former. ## Fill a width-and-demand worksheet

Use actual repeat orders, not every profile you hope to sell. Keep materially different grades, coatings and thicknesses on separate rows in the detailed version of this worksheet.

| Decision input | Bought-in slit coil | Separate slitting investment | Buyer entry / confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Required strip widths | Order confirmed widths from the processor | Develop a supported slit plan for the actual mix | Forming supplier: issues strip width by approved profile; buyer uses this to order ready-to-feed coil |
| Order pattern | Match deliveries to repeat or project orders | Need sufficient repeat workload for a second operation | Sales planner: enters committed quantity and date by material to establish slitter workload |
| Usable output | Agree supplied quantity and acceptance boundary | Include leftovers, setup material and rejected output | Quality lead: defines usable output unit, inspection and remnant disposition for both routes |
| Coil dimensions and mass | Specify coils the receiving system can accept | Check both incoming parent coils and outgoing slit coils | Coil supplier: enters ID, OD, width and mass; receiving supplier returns compatible limits |
| Space and handling | Receive, store and feed supplied coils | Receive wider stock, process it and manage multiple outputs | Site planner: measures storage, line and transfer space to accept or reject the extra operation |
| People and quality checks | Check incoming material against the purchase | Add an operated and maintained processing stage | Operations manager: names staffed inspection, handling and maintenance roles to price readiness |

The last two rows often decide the purchase before a machine price does. Where several widths are required but no workable handling layout exists, retain bought-in slit coil. Where a local processor already meets the required material schedule, price the actual savings before duplicating its operation. Conversely, a sustained production business with a suitable site can obtain a separate slitting proposal and test it against its real material programme.

## Calculate usable material, then the whole extra operation

Compare both routes over the same production period and at the same boundary: usable material accepted for the agreed forming job. Include purchase and processing charges, transport, receiving effort, handling, storage, inspection and the treatment of remnants. Keep costs for the roof-panel forming stage separate if they are common to both options.

For an owned slitting operation, add its premises, staffing, maintenance and planned ownership cost over that period. Avoid charging the full purchase price against one month while comparing it with one month's outsourced processing. Equally, do not omit ownership cost simply because the equipment has already been paid for.

A width combination that looks efficient on paper can still create an unwanted strip. Count an offcut as reusable only when a real order or documented stock use exists. Label uncertain demand separately. The decision should survive a conservative case in which that strip remains unsold, rather than depending on every remnant finding an immediate buyer.

## Two purchases that fail for avoidable reasons

**Failure 1 — attractive parent-coil pricing hides unwanted material.** A buyer compares the purchase price of wide coil with the price of slit coil but assumes every resulting width is saleable. One repeated roof profile uses only part of the planned output. Storage and cash remain tied to the rest. Compare usable output for confirmed jobs and give speculative remnants their own line.

**Failure 2 — the new processor feeds the wrong coil arrangement.** A buyer orders upstream machinery before agreeing the downstream receiving equipment. The proposed output coil data do not match the forming package's handling arrangement. Resolve the shared coil schedule before ordering either side; a product category name is insufficient interface information.

## Eight attachments for a coherent RFQ

1. Panel profile drawings and the intended finished roof-panel products.
2. Material grade, coating and thickness information for each repeat product.
3. Required strip widths and any existing forming supplier's input schedule.
4. Confirmed order quantities and timing, separated from speculative demand.
5. Proposed coil dimensions and mass, with receiving equipment information.
6. Workshop or site layout showing storage, access and material-transfer areas.
7. The intended ground-level or roof-level production route and required outlet position.
8. Destination, power information and a clear included/excluded equipment list.
