Choose an engineered modular channel-and-connector support when its complete accepted arrangement carries the specified services and the project values its documented adjustment or alteration scope. Choose a designed fabricated welded-steel support when that accepted construction fits the required geometry and gives the lower complete installed cost. Compare both at the same assembly location and support duty. Deciding where a frame is assembled is a separate choice from deciding how its permanent members connect.

Give the structural and MEP designers one service-load and geometry brief before requesting alternatives. When both arrangements qualify, select the lower complete installed offer including design, material, connections, finish, fabrication, handling, installation and acceptance. Raw steel weight or a channel price alone cannot identify the better purchase.

## Hold the assembly location constant

Compare shop-assembled modular frames with shop-fabricated welded frames, or compare the two accepted constructions at the same site assembly boundary. Include the actual delivery and final installation in each offer. Otherwise, a comparison of connection technology becomes mixed with an unrelated transfer of labour from one place to another.

[Hilti's modular-support description](https://www.hilti.com/content/hilti/W1/US/en/business/business/engineering/modular-supports.html?country=US) identifies channel-and-connector systems for services and discusses their adjustment and assembly. Its [comparison with welded methods](https://www.hilti.com.hk/content/hilti/A1/HK/en/business/design-center/strut-and-modular-steel-system-design-center/modular-support-systems-vs-traditional-welding-methods.html) distinguishes modular construction from traditional welded supports. Those sources establish the alternatives; their marketing savings are not a project estimate for your building.

Ask each system designer to issue the actual support arrangement, connections, building interfaces and component schedule against the same brief. The accepted design determines eligibility. A collection of channels with an unpriced connector schedule is not an engineered modular support, and a fabricated-steel material list without its connections and finish is not a completed welded rack.

## Modular construction: buy the complete component family

Name the accepted channel profiles, connectors, connection arrangement and applicable finishes by document revision. Keep the components and their supported interfaces identifiable in the purchase order. Substituting a similar-looking connector changes the proposed system and requires the design team's accepted revision.

Where adjustment is part of the business case, state what the project actually needs changed and within which accepted arrangement. Examples include accommodating surveyed service positions or a defined later service alteration. Obtain the designer's supported adjustment scope and the contractor's priced work. Do not value unlimited future flexibility that the offered design never provides.

Include component availability and replacement identification in the commercial review. Ask the supplier to state the actual parts included, the order boundary and its offered support terms. Compare those written terms with the facility's needs rather than assuming every future connector will remain interchangeable.

## Welded construction: buy a defined fabrication package

For the fabricated alternative, require the accepted member arrangement, joint details, fabrication requirements and finish specification. Identify the contractor responsible for the specified fabrication quality and records. Include the inspection, coating completion and any site repair work actually required by the project.

Keep shop and site work visible. If the offered frame is welded in a workshop and only attached at site, do not charge it for hypothetical overhead welding. If the accepted construction includes field welding, have the contractor price the actual permitted work, inspection and finish completion under the site's requirements. The comparison must follow the offered construction.

For unusual geometry, obtain a complete designed fabricated proposal before deciding that modular construction cannot serve it. Conversely, do not force a proprietary component family into a duty outside its accepted design simply because the buyer already owns those parts.

## Translate the selected connections into crew workfaces

The connections and final attachment arrangement determine what the crew must reach. Have the installer mark each working position and the required sequence. The access provider then evaluates its offered equipment against those positions, the entry route and the building team's floor information.

The underside view below shows a broad deck among steel roof members. Record those surrounding members and other services in the access section. Give the provider the intended crew, tools and deck materials with their distribution; keep permanent loads and assembly handling in their separately designed scopes.

**Mistake (hypothetical): a modular price omits its connectors.** A buyer compares cut channel material with a finished welded frame. Complete the modular bill of materials, design, fabrication and installation boundary before ranking the offers.

**Mistake (hypothetical): shop welding is priced as field welding.** An estimate adds site welding occupation to a frame delivered fully fabricated. Use the actual delivery condition and remaining task list for both alternatives, then compare the real installation costs.

## Eight RFQ attachments and the access shortlist

1. Common service-load schedule and support design brief.
2. Support IDs, geometry and building-grid references.
3. Accepted modular arrangement and complete component schedule.
4. Accepted welded arrangement, fabrication and finish requirements.
5. Common assembly location and delivery-condition boundary.
6. Actual adjustment or future-alteration scenario with its priced scope.
7. Crew workfaces, floor-route information, intended deck loading and separate handling plan.
8. Itemised installed-support comparison, destination and supply responsibilities.
