**Choose diffuse wall panels when the accepted room design needs distributed daylight without a clear outside view. Choose transparent glazing when a clear view is a required room function and the accepted solar and glare design supports it.** Have the daylight and facade designers compare complete assemblies in the already-selected high wall openings. Then buy installation and replacement access for the chosen panel and frame arrangement.

The aperture location is fixed in this comparison. The purchase changes the optical function and complete wall assembly, not whether daylight belongs in the roof or facade. An industrial buyer should state the room's work, visual tasks and operating layout before comparing a translucent-panel rate with a clear-glass rate.

## Separate useful daylight from a clear view

The owner defines occupied areas, work hours, visual tasks and the value of an outside view. The design team includes orientation, shading, nearby buildings, racks and internal obstructions. Ask it to compare the proposed wall assemblies under the same room-use conditions.

[Kalwall's daylighting explanation](https://www.kalwall.com/products/benefits/perfect-daylighting/) distinguishes diffuse transmission from transparent glazing by scattering incoming visible light. Its [panel-technology information](https://www.kalwall.com/products/panel-technology/) describes a composite translucent sandwich construction. These sources establish a different optical and physical product category. They do not establish daylight performance or a glazing specification for an unidentified factory.

Avoid treating all translucent materials as one assembly. Obtain the actual product, framing, finishes and documented application from each provider. Transparent glazing also needs a specific glass make-up and accepted support/perimeter design. Compare the optical result and complete assembly together rather than treating equal aperture area as equivalence.

## Let the room brief qualify the offers

A diffuse assembly qualifies when the responsible designer accepts its room-light distribution and the owner accepts the absence of a clear view. A transparent assembly qualifies when the specified view and accepted glare/solar treatment serve the room. If the owner needs both functions in different zones, release a located mixed design rather than averaging two rates across the elevation.

Keep thermal, weather, acoustic, fire and other applicable requirements in the complete facade brief. The responsible specialists select and accept the actual construction. An optical advantage should not remove another required wall function from the quote.

Obtain the maintenance requirements for the selected surfaces and frame assembly. The facility should identify the events included in its commercial comparison, such as cleaning or replacement access. Use actual adopted tasks and supplier documents; do not invent a lifetime maintenance advantage from the material category.

## Make handling and personnel access separate purchases

The panel/glazing provider supplies actual object information. The handling specialist defines receiving, staging and presentation for those objects. Keep its equipment and responsibility explicit beside the personnel-access proposal. A broad deck is not assigned a panel-support or glass-lifting function merely because the installer can stand on it.

The installer identifies the remaining framing, joint, seal and inspection tasks from the selected assembly. Map continuous strips and exceptional positions separately. A large deck deserves comparison where sustained adjacent tasks fit its actual configuration; isolated high-window positions can justify another access arrangement.

## Carry wall closure and replacement access into the award

Compare both offers through accepted wall closure. Reconcile framing, perimeter details, interior finishes, weather interfaces, checks and reinstatement. An offer ending at panel delivery cannot be ranked beside an installed transparent-glazing package.

For later replacement, locate the actual service side and removal path from the selected provider's documents. Include the completed surroundings: landscaping, process equipment, barriers and occupied areas. If the installation equipment cannot return after fit-out, obtain an accepted alternative service arrangement before fixing the permanent layout.

Where daylight performance is the reason for selection, retain the designer's acceptance output separately from the platform's equipment handover. One establishes the wall's intended result; the other defines the personnel-access configuration. Both belong in the project records with their named owners.

## Two hypothetical daylight orders

**Mistake (hypothetical): Diffuse light is assumed to preserve a view.** A buyer selects translucent panels for their light distribution, while the occupant brief also requires a clear external view at a particular zone. Release a mixed or revised accepted design before the panel order; a platform change cannot repair the wrong optical function.

**Mistake (hypothetical): Equal wall area hides a different installation.** Another comparison uses square-metre prices but omits one system's framing, perimeter closure and component handling. Reprice complete assemblies through the same accepted enclosure and include their actual personnel positions.
