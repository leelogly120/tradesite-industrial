Choose a roll-forming offer by comparing the cost of accepted roof-panel output alongside the machine price. A lower-priced line can become the more expensive purchase if start-up waste, rejected panels, surface damage or repeated adjustment consumes the saving. The better offer is the one whose agreed material trial supports your profile, quality standard and production pattern at a credible whole-package cost.

Keep the calculation tied to accepted panel coverage at the same inspection point. Installed roof cost is a later measure that also includes clips, seams, labour and other project items. Mixing those two boundaries makes the comparison harder to use.

The workshop line below shows the stations that the material passes through. A useful acceptance record connects the input material to the resulting panels; a photograph of an assembled line is only the beginning of that evidence.

## Put every offer on the same output basis

A metre of coil strip and a metre of roof coverage are different measures. The strip width includes material formed into the ribs and seam. Compare the approved profile's effective cover width with the accepted panel lengths; do not use nominal coil width as installed coverage.

For a manufacturer example of this distinction, New Tech Machinery's [material-usage guide](https://newtechmachinery.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Material-Usage-Guide-Metric-Version.pdf) lists profile-dependent material usage separately from finished panel width. Use your own approved profile dimensions in the ARCLIFT enquiry.

For a single approved profile, accepted panel area equals the accepted lengths multiplied by the effective cover width. If a batch contains different profiles, calculate each profile separately. Use the project's drawings and actual accepted quantities rather than a catalogue illustration to supply the inputs.

| Comparison input | Buyer record | Measurement or commercial basis | Treatment in the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profile and cover width | Drawing revision and effective width: ___ | Quality lead supplies the approved profile; supplier identifies its matching tooling | Quality lead: enters approved drawing revision and effective cover width to fix the accepted-area denominator |
| Material consumed | Issued material less usable returned stock: ___ | Choose one consistent unit and record returns | Material controller: records issued less reusable returned material in one unit, including trial/reject use |
| Accepted panel output | Accepted length by profile: ___ | Same inspection stage and acceptance method | Quality lead: enters accepted lengths by profile at the same inspection point to count usable output |
| Scrap and rejects | Start-up, cut-offs and rejected panels: ___ | Record reason and disposition | Production lead: records startup, cut-off and rejected quantities with disposition to separate stock from waste |
| Rework | Labour, energy and extra material used: ___ | Actual trial record or explicit estimate | Estimator: records non-material rework cost; consumed rework material stays in the material total once |
| Purchase and running scope | Quoted line, tooling and operating items: ___ | Buyer combines itemized equipment offers with local material, labour, energy and disposal costs | Buyer: enters line/tooling scope and local running costs over the same period for a complete comparison |

Do not combine different units into one yield percentage. A mass-based material-yield calculation needs accepted-product mass and consumed material on the same basis. For purchasing, material cost divided by accepted panel area is often easier to compare because it uses the roof output the buyer needs.

## Record the losses a short demonstration can hide

A demonstration can show a stable section of output while omitting the material consumed before that section. Ask for a trial record that starts with the agreed material issue and ends with accepted panels, reusable remainder and identified waste. Keep the material grade, coating, thickness, profile and drawing revision with the record.

Separate losses by cause. Start-up and changeover losses affect small production batches differently from long repeated runs. Cut-offs depend on the length schedule and cut arrangement. Compare the surface at material receipt, line exit and downstream handling to identify where damage first appears. Treat the whole route as evidence to investigate, rather than assigning every defect to the machine.

## A usable cost comparison without a guessed saving

Use two calculations. First, divide consumed material cost by accepted panel area. Include all material consumed in trials, rejects and rework in that first figure, once. Then calculate **production cost per accepted area = (consumed material cost + non-material production, changeover, inspection and rework costs) ÷ accepted panel area**. Use the same batch and output boundary throughout. Do not add rework material again. Keep scrap recovery as a separate line so its assumed value is visible rather than silently reducing consumption.

For the purchase decision, add each offer's quoted line, tooling, agreed accessories and integration scope over the production period you are evaluating. Use your own expected workload and financing basis. Label uncertain workload as an assumption and test what happens if it is lower or if the product mix changes.

This approach can change the shortlist. A cheaper line with an unproven profile is not automatically a saving. A higher-priced package is not automatically better either: it must justify its difference with defined scope and relevant acceptance evidence. Compare an accepted trial and a clear offer, not an unsupported promise of low scrap.

Tooling belongs in that comparison. The separate shafts in this photograph illustrate physical tooling components that need an identified supply scope. Establish what is included for the selected profile, what is supplied for changeover and what remains a separate purchase.

## Two failures that distort the buying decision

**Failure case: the comparison counts every formed metre as saleable output.** One trial includes panels that need rework or fail the agreed inspection. Its apparent unit cost looks lower because the denominator includes rejected material. Recalculate using accepted output at the same inspection stage, with the rejected quantities and rework shown separately.

**Failure case: a long single-profile trial is used to price frequent short runs.** The demonstration spreads start-up material across one extended run, while the buyer expects repeated changes in profile or material. Ask for an acceptance sequence that represents the intended product mix and compare changeover scope as well as steady running. Keep the trial's measured yield separate from assumptions about a different production schedule.

These are illustrative purchasing errors. The supplier and buyer should agree the trial materials and criteria in advance. The [FAT and SAT evidence guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-fat-sat-acceptance-checklist/) explains how to keep that acceptance scope explicit.

## Match the line and lift purchase to the same profile

Shortlist **[ARC-RF8](/products/arc-rf8-roll-forming-machine/)** as the catalog line to discuss against the approved roof-panel profile and material. Where the line will work at roof level, compare the complete integration proposal with **[ARC-T25](/products/arc-t25-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/)** or **[ARC-C25](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/)**. The forming-line provider supplies the installed footprint, mass, input/output direction and control interfaces; the lift provider uses that schedule to define the offered integrated configuration.

Choose the line on accepted panel requirements first, then confirm the lift interface. A convenient lift label should not force the purchase of tooling that is wrong for the roof system. For the multi-profile decision, use the [dedicated-versus-multi-profile line comparison](/blog/multi-profile-roof-panel-roll-former-vs-dedicated-lines/).

### RFQ attachments for a yield-based comparison

- Approved profile drawing with effective cover width and critical inspection dimensions.
- Material grade, coating, thickness tolerance and representative sample availability.
- Expected profile mix, batch sizes, panel lengths and changeover pattern.
- Agreed definitions of accepted output, reusable remainder, scrap and rework.
- Proposed trial sequence and the input/output records to be returned.
- Tooling, accessories and spare items to include in the equipment offer.
- Local material and operating-cost inputs you want kept separate in the comparison.
- Lift-integration requirement, destination power and intended site or workshop layout.
