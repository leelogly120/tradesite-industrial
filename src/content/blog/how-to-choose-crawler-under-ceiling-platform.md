## Start With Your Ceiling Height

The single most important specification is your working height. Not the ceiling height — the working height, which is the ceiling height plus the reach your workers need above the platform surface to actually perform the installation.

For a 20m ceiling where workers need 2m of reach above the platform, you need a platform that reaches 22m. Always add a 2–3m safety margin on top of that.

**ARC-F height coverage:**
- ARC-F20: up to 20m working height
- ARC-F25: up to 25m working height
- ARC-F31: up to 31m working height
- ARC-F35: up to 35m working height

If your ceiling sits at 18m, the ARC-F20 gives you plenty of margin. At 28m, you need the ARC-F31.

## Calculate Your Load Requirements

Load capacity determines how many workers and how much material you can have on the platform at once. This directly impacts productivity.

The ARC-F20 through ARC-F31 carry 0.3–0.5 tonnes. That's enough for 6–10 workers plus tools and a reasonable amount of material.

The ARC-F35 carries up to 3 tonnes — enough for heavy acoustic panels, lighting arrays, or rigging equipment.

**Rule of thumb:** Calculate the weight of your heaviest single work operation (workers + tools + one full material load). Your platform's safe working load should exceed this by at least 30%.

## Platform Size Matters More Than You Think

A 2m × 1m spider lift basket gives you 2 square meters. You can fit one worker and a small toolkit.

The ARC-F standard platform is 12m × 3m (36 square meters). Extended, it's 12m × 8m (96 square meters). That's 50–48 times the workspace of a spider lift.

Why does this matter? Because ceiling work isn't individual tasks — it's continuous operations. You need space for material staging, tool access, worker movement, and simultaneous operations. A cramped platform slows everything down.

## Power System: Diesel vs Electric vs Dual

**Diesel only:** Works outdoors and in well-ventilated spaces. Cheaper to operate. Noisier, produces exhaust.

**Electric only:** Zero emissions, near-silent. Limited to finished indoor spaces with power access. No outdoor capability.

**Dual power (ARC-F standard):** Diesel for outdoor travel and rough terrain. Electric for indoor zero-emission operation. One machine covers both environments.

For most ceiling projects, dual power is the right choice. You'll stage the machine outdoors (diesel), drive it into the building, switch to electric for indoor work, then drive it back out when the shift ends.

## Ground Pressure: Protect Your Floors

Crawler tracks distribute weight across a large contact area. This results in low ground pressure (PSI) that's safe for most indoor flooring — polished concrete, tile, even some carpeted surfaces.

Outrigger-based lifts concentrate load on four small pads. On a 50-tonne machine, that's potentially hundreds of PSI at each pad — enough to crack tile or dent polished concrete.

If floor protection is a concern (airports, stadiums, finished warehouses), crawler tracks are the safer choice.

## Site Access and Maneuverability

Consider how the machine gets to the work area:
- **Door widths:** Can the machine fit through standard entrance doors?
- **Floor level changes:** Are there ramps, steps, or level changes?
- **Column grid spacing:** Will the platform fit between structural columns?
- **Turning radius:** Can the machine navigate corners and aisles?

The ARC-F series has a minimum turning radius of 16m. It fits through standard industrial doorways (2.95m width) and handles 30% gradeability for ramps and slopes.

## Remote Control vs Platform Controls

Remote control keeps the operator on the ground, away from overhead hazards. On ceiling projects, this is a significant safety advantage — falling objects, structural elements, and electrical systems are all risks at height.

Platform controls put the operator in the basket, which is the traditional approach but limits visibility and exposes the operator to height-related risks.

ARC-F platforms include full wireless remote control as standard. We recommend using it for all operations.

## Certifications and Compliance

Before purchasing, verify that the platform meets the certification requirements for your market:

- **CE (EN 280):** Required for European Union
- **ANSI A92.20:** Required for United States
- **ISO 16368:** International standard for mobile elevating work platforms
- **AS/NZS 1418:** Required for Australia and New Zealand

ARC-F platforms are designed to meet ISO 16368 and are currently undergoing CE certification.
