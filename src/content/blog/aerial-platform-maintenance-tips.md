## Daily Pre-Operation Inspection Checklist

Every aerial platform should undergo a thorough pre-operation inspection before each use. ANSI A92.20 standards mandate that operators complete a pre-use inspection following the manufacturer's guidelines. This daily ritual takes 10-15 minutes but can prevent catastrophic failures and serious injuries.

**Visual Inspection Components:**

Start with a complete walk-around of the equipment. Check for visible damage to the chassis, platform, guardrails, and toe boards. Look for hydraulic fluid leaks — even small puddles under the machine indicate a problem that needs immediate attention. Inspect tires or tracks for wear, damage, and proper inflation. Examine all structural welds for cracks or corrosion, paying special attention to stress points around the scissor arms or boom sections.

Inspect the platform floor for damage, slippery surfaces, or missing anti-slip coatings. Verify that all guardrails are secure and that self-closing gates function properly. Check the hydraulic cylinders for scoring on the ram surfaces, which can damage seals and cause leaks.

**Functional Testing:**

Before elevating the platform, start the engine or power unit and allow it to reach operating temperature. Test all ground-level controls to ensure they override platform controls — this is a critical safety feature. Cycle the platform through its full range of motion at ground level, checking for unusual noises, vibrations, or jerky movements in the hydraulic system.

Test the tilt sensor (if equipped) to verify it triggers an alarm and restricts platform movement when the machine exceeds its rated slope. Check the load sensing system by verifying it prevents operation when the platform exceeds its rated capacity. Test the emergency lowering system — every operator must know how to safely lower the platform in the event of a power failure.

**Documentation:**

Record all inspection findings on the equipment's daily inspection form. Any deficiency that affects safe operation must be reported immediately, and the equipment must be taken out of service until repairs are completed. ## Weekly and Monthly Maintenance Procedures

Beyond daily inspections, aerial platforms require more detailed maintenance on a weekly and monthly basis. These procedures address wear items and system checks that don't need daily attention but are critical for long-term reliability.

**Weekly Maintenance Tasks:**

Check all fluid levels including hydraulic oil, engine oil, coolant, and fuel. Hydraulic oil should be checked with the platform in the stowed position and the cylinders fully retracted. Use only the manufacturer-specified hydraulic fluid — using incorrect fluid can damage seals and void warranties. For ARCLIFT equipment, the recommended hydraulic oil specification is typically ISO VG 46 or as specified in the operator's manual.

Lubricate all grease fittings according to the manufacturer's schedule. Most aerial platforms have 15-25 grease points that require attention weekly. Pay special attention to scissor arm pivot points, boom section slide pads, and turntable bearings. Use a high-quality lithium-based grease that meets the equipment specifications.

Inspect all safety devices including limit switches, tilt sensors, load sensors, and alarms. Test the functionality of each device and verify proper calibration. Check the condition of all hydraulic hoses and fittings for signs of wear, cracking, or abrasion. Replace any hoses showing damage before they fail.

**Monthly Maintenance Tasks:**

Perform a more detailed hydraulic system inspection. Check the hydraulic filter condition indicator and replace filters as needed. Most aerial platforms require hydraulic filter replacement every 500-1000 hours of operation. Inspect the hydraulic reservoir for contamination — milky oil indicates water contamination, while dark or gritty oil suggests oxidation or particle contamination.

Test the structural integrity of the platform by checking for cracks, corrosion, or deformation. Use a torque wrench to verify that all critical fasteners remain at the manufacturer's specified torque values. Check the boom or scissor arm wear pads and replace them if they've reached their wear limit — worn pads allow excessive play that accelerates structural wear.

Inspect the electrical system including wiring harnesses, connectors, and control panels. Look for damaged insulation, corroded connectors, or loose connections. Check battery condition and clean terminals if applicable. For diesel-powered units, inspect the air filter and replace if the restriction indicator shows it's needed.

## Hydraulic System Maintenance: The Heart of Your Aerial Platform

The hydraulic system is the most critical and maintenance-intensive component of any aerial platform. Proper hydraulic maintenance ensures smooth operation, precise control, and reliable performance at height. Neglecting the hydraulic system is the single most common cause of premature equipment failure.

**Hydraulic Fluid Management:**

Hydraulic fluid is the lifeblood of the system. It transmits power, lubricates internal components, removes heat, and protects against corrosion. The fluid must be kept clean, cool, and at the proper level. According to industry data, approximately 75% of hydraulic system failures are caused by fluid contamination.

Implement a fluid sampling program to monitor contamination levels. Send samples to a laboratory for analysis every 500 hours or at least annually. The analysis should measure particle count, water content, viscosity, and acid number. ISO 4406 cleanliness codes should be maintained at 18/16/13 or better for most aerial platform hydraulic systems.

Change hydraulic fluid at the manufacturer's recommended intervals, typically every 1,000-2,000 hours. Always flush the system when changing fluid types or if contamination is detected. Use only filters that meet or exceed the OEM specifications — aftermarket filters with lower micron ratings may not provide adequate protection.

**Hydraulic Hose and Fitting Inspection:**

Hydraulic hoses have a finite lifespan and should be replaced proactively before they fail. The Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE) recommends replacing hydraulic hoses every 6-10 years, regardless of visible condition. Hoses exposed to extreme temperatures, UV radiation, or chemical exposure may need more frequent replacement.

Inspect hoses for cracking, blistering, abrasion, or kinking. Check that hose routing hasn't changed — hoses that rub against structural components will fail prematurely. Verify that all fittings are tight and that O-ring seals are in good condition. A single hydraulic hose failure at height can result in uncontrolled platform descent, making this inspection critical for safety.

**Cylinder and Valve Maintenance:**

Inspect hydraulic cylinders for rod scoring, which can damage seals and cause leaks. Minor scoring can be polished out, but significant damage requires cylinder rechroming or replacement. Check cylinder mounting pins and bushings for wear — excessive play affects platform stability and control precision.

Test all hydraulic valves for proper operation. Solenoid valves should click firmly when energized, and spool valves should move freely without sticking. Check relief valve settings to ensure they're at the manufacturer's specified pressure — incorrect relief pressure can cause system damage or unsafe operating conditions.

## Electrical System and Battery Maintenance

Modern aerial platforms rely increasingly on sophisticated electrical and electronic systems for control, diagnostics, and safety. Proper electrical maintenance ensures reliable starting, accurate controls, and dependable safety systems.

**Battery Maintenance:**

For electric aerial platforms, battery condition directly impacts performance and runtime. Lead-acid batteries should be checked weekly for proper electrolyte levels and topped up with distilled water as needed. Keep battery terminals clean and coated with anti-corrosion spray. Check specific gravity with a hydrometer — readings below 1.225 indicate the battery needs charging or replacement.

Lithium-ion batteries, increasingly common in newer ARCLIFT models, require different care. Avoid deep discharges below 20% state of charge, and don't leave batteries in a fully discharged state for extended periods. Store lithium-ion equipment in moderate temperatures — extreme heat accelerates degradation, while extreme cold reduces available capacity.

**Wiring and Connector Inspection:**

Inspect wiring harnesses for damage from abrasion, heat, or chemical exposure. Check that all connectors are fully seated and that locking mechanisms are engaged. Apply dielectric grease to connectors in areas exposed to moisture or corrosion. Look for signs of rodent damage, which is surprisingly common in equipment stored outdoors.

**Control System Diagnostics:**

Many modern aerial platforms, including [ARCLIFT's advanced control systems](https://arclift.com/technology), include built-in diagnostic capabilities. Use these systems to identify potential problems before they cause failures. Download diagnostic logs regularly and review them for error codes or abnormal operating parameters. Some systems can alert fleet managers remotely when maintenance is needed.

## Structural Inspection and Corrosion Prevention

The structural integrity of an aerial platform is fundamental to safe operation. Structural failures, while rare with properly maintained equipment, can have catastrophic consequences. Regular structural inspections and proactive corrosion prevention are essential elements of a comprehensive maintenance program.

**Structural Inspection Points:**

Focus inspection efforts on high-stress areas: scissor arm pivot points, boom section connections, turntable mounting bolts, and platform attachment points. Use dye penetrant testing (DPT) or magnetic particle inspection (MPI) on critical welds during annual inspections. Look for cracks initiating at stress concentration points such as bolt holes, weld toes, and geometric transitions.

Measure scissor arm and boom section dimensions to detect deformation. Even minor bending or buckling can indicate overstress events that may have compromised structural integrity. Compare measurements to manufacturer specifications and consult with the manufacturer if dimensions fall outside tolerances.

**Corrosion Prevention:**

Corrosion is the enemy of structural integrity. Implement a corrosion prevention program that includes regular cleaning, application of protective coatings, and prompt repair of paint damage. Equipment operating in coastal environments, chemical plants, or areas where road salt is used requires more aggressive corrosion prevention measures.

Apply rust-inhibiting primer and paint to any bare metal surfaces immediately. Consider additional protective measures such as galvanizing, epoxy coatings, or sacrificial anodes for equipment operating in corrosive environments. Store equipment indoors when possible, or use fitted covers to protect against the elements.

## Seasonal Maintenance Considerations

Aerial platform maintenance needs vary with the seasons. Cold weather, hot weather, and wet conditions each present unique challenges that require specific maintenance attention.

**Cold Weather Maintenance (Below 32°F / 0°C):**

Cold temperatures thicken hydraulic fluid, reduce battery capacity, and can cause seals to harden and leak. Switch to a lower-viscosity hydraulic fluid if operating in sustained cold conditions — consult your ARCLIFT operator's manual for cold-weather fluid specifications. Allow the engine and hydraulic system to warm up before operating at full capacity.

Check antifreeze concentration in liquid-cooled engines. Use a battery maintainer or trickle charger during cold storage to prevent battery discharge. Inspect all seals and hoses for cold-weather cracking. Store batteries in a heated area when possible — battery capacity drops approximately 20% at 32°F and 50% at 0°F.

**Hot Weather Maintenance (Above 95°F / 35°C):**

High temperatures increase hydraulic fluid degradation rates and can cause overheating. Monitor hydraulic fluid temperature during operation and avoid sustained operation at maximum capacity in extreme heat. Ensure cooling systems are clean and functioning properly — clean radiator fins and check coolant levels frequently.

Check tire pressures more frequently in hot weather, as pressure increases with temperature. Inspect hydraulic hoses more often, as heat accelerates rubber degradation. Consider using synthetic hydraulic fluids that maintain better viscosity characteristics across a wider temperature range.

## Building a Preventive Maintenance Schedule

A well-structured preventive maintenance (PM) schedule is the backbone of any successful maintenance program. The schedule should be based on manufacturer recommendations, operating conditions, and actual equipment usage patterns.

**Usage-Based vs. Calendar-Based Scheduling:**

The most effective PM schedules use a combination of usage-based (hours of operation) and calendar-based intervals. Some maintenance items wear based on usage — hydraulic filters, for example — while others deteriorate with time regardless of usage — such as rubber hoses and seals. Track both hours and calendar time to ensure nothing falls through the cracks.

**Creating Your PM Schedule:**

Start with the manufacturer's recommended maintenance intervals as your baseline. Adjust intervals based on your operating conditions — harsh environments, heavy usage, or extreme temperatures may require more frequent maintenance. Use fleet management software to automate scheduling and track completion.

A typical PM schedule for an aerial platform includes:

| Interval | Key Tasks |
|----------|-----------|
| Daily | Pre-operation inspection, fluid level check |
| Weekly | Lubrication, safety device testing, detailed visual inspection |
| Monthly | Hydraulic system inspection, electrical system check, structural inspection |
| Quarterly | Fluid sampling, filter replacement, calibration checks |
| Annually | Comprehensive inspection, structural NDT, major fluid changes |
| Every 2,000 hours | Major overhaul of hydraulic system, replacement of wear items |

## FAQ: Aerial Platform Maintenance

**Q: How often should I change the hydraulic fluid in my aerial platform?**
A: Most manufacturers recommend changing hydraulic fluid every 1,000-2,000 hours of operation or annually, whichever comes first. However, fluid condition should be monitored through regular sampling, and fluid should be changed immediately if contamination is detected. [ARCLIFT equipment](https://arclift.com/products) includes fluid condition monitoring in some models that can alert you when fluid change is needed.

**Q: What are the most common maintenance mistakes that cause equipment failure?**
A: The three most common mistakes are: (1) neglecting daily pre-operation inspections, (2) using incorrect hydraulic fluid or filters, and (3) ignoring minor leaks or unusual noises until they become major failures. These three issues account for over 60% of preventable equipment failures according to industry data.

**Q: Can I perform aerial platform maintenance in-house, or do I need a certified technician?**
A: Daily inspections and basic maintenance (lubrication, fluid top-offs, cleaning) can typically be performed by trained operators. However, annual inspections, structural assessments, and major repairs should be performed by manufacturer-certified technicians. ANSI A92.22 requires that maintenance personnel be trained and qualified on the specific equipment they service.

**Q: How do I know when my aerial platform needs major repairs vs. routine maintenance?**
A: Key indicators that major repairs are needed include: hydraulic system contamination that persists after fluid changes, structural cracks or deformation, persistent electrical faults, and excessive play in scissor arms or boom sections. When in doubt, consult with the manufacturer or a qualified service provider. Learn more about [ARCLIFT's service and support programs](https://arclift.com/service).

**Q: What maintenance records should I keep, and for how long?**
A: Maintain records of all inspections, maintenance performed, repairs, and parts replacements for the life of the equipment. OSHA and ANSI standards require that maintenance records be available for inspection. Digital record-keeping systems make this easier and provide better data for fleet management decisions.
