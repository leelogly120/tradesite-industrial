**Specify exterior shading when the building design needs solar energy intercepted outside the glass and the owner can install and maintain the selected equipment on the outside face. Choose interior blinds when the accepted daylight, glare and thermal brief can be met from inside and tenant access is practical.** Obtain a priced access route for replacement as well as installation. A façade screen can solve the daylight problem yet leave the owner unable to reach its headbox.

Begin with a particular room, orientation and season of use. An auditorium darkening requirement, a workstation glare complaint and a cooling-load target are different purchases. The façade or daylight designer should state the result the proposed combination of glazing and shading must achieve. Where shading is part of a wider façade renewal, distinguish the costs of the glazing itself from the incremental cost of choosing one shading location.

The practical question for an exterior screen is whether the working arrangement can reach its service face after landscaping, gates and adjacent buildings are complete. Measure the passage at the narrowest obstruction, not midway along an open elevation.

## Set the performance target room by room

Ask the designer to document the glazing, window orientation, occupancy and acceptance criteria used to compare the two proposals. State which hours matter, which rooms need views out, where glare must be controlled and what thermal result the building requires. Use project modelling or supplier-supported performance data for the selected installation. A percentage from another glazing-and-shade combination is a weak basis for underwriting a cooling saving.

[Hunter Douglas Architectural describes its exterior roller shades](https://www.hunterdouglasarchitectural.com/windowcoverings/rollershades/exteriorrollershades/index.jsp) as intercepting solar heat and glare before they enter the interior, with external headboxes and guides. Those external components also create service points on that face. The supplier must provide the operating limits, weather response and maintenance method for the precise system being offered. Include the time the shade is unavailable if the proposed controls retract it under defined conditions and that affects the owner's requirement.

For an interior offer, identify brackets, control or drive locations, clearance from furniture and how the complete shade or motor is removed. A room can offer easy daily operation yet require a closure and special equipment to replace an assembly above a reception hall. Ask the tenant or facility operator for access permission and permitted hours. A fit-out drawing showing a clear floor today is insufficient if the approved future layout fixes a counter directly below.

## Tender a representative replacement, even on a new building

Choose an actual upper opening and ask each bidder to describe the replacement of the component its own product needs to service. The external bidder should identify the working side, access equipment, material handling, public protection and weather constraints. The internal bidder should identify room protection, tenant coordination, service equipment and the route for removing the part. A glass-replacement route elsewhere in the building does not automatically serve a sunshade mounted beyond it.

Compare two complete costs over the owner's selected service period. Include initial supply, installation, scheduled maintenance, agreed service visits and accommodation of the access equipment. Enter expected quantities from the provider's maintenance plan or the owner's records. Avoid assigning a universal repair interval to either shade type. If both options meet glare requirements but only the exterior solution meets the accepted thermal brief, resolve that technical difference before comparing their prices as equivalent bids.

## Two mistakes behind a low shading price

**Mistake one — valuing identical cooling performance by fabric colour.** A hypothetical buyer compares exterior and interior fabrics that look alike and credits them with the same heat reduction. Ask the designer to evaluate both systems with the actual glass and operating conditions. Keep only eligible bids in the cost comparison.

**Mistake two — providing exterior access for construction only.** The installer reaches the elevation from temporary ground, then permanent planting removes that standing area. Reserve a maintainable route in the completed design or price a different accepted service method before signing the shade order.
