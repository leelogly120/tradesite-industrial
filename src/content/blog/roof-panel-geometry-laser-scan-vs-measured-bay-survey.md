Buy a three-dimensional survey and panel-detailing service when many roof pieces depend on the as-built shape of the same irregular structure. Choose a measured bay schedule when each run can be defined and checked independently from accessible references. In both cases, contract for dimensions that the panel producer can use.

## Start with a drawing the shop can cut from

Ask the fabricator to show the information it needs for one difficult panel. For a panel that changes shape across its run, require the detailer to identify the mark, developed dimensions, orientation, end geometry and adjoining references the producer needs. A point cloud is a collection of measured positions. It becomes useful to the shop when somebody registers the observations to a known reference, interprets the support geometry and issues the correct production records. Price that translation in the survey package or name the detailer who will perform it.

## Identify where an error would affect adjacent panels

Mark each roof change on a plan. Does an observed offset change one end of one panel, a succession of tapered panels or the closing position of a complete roof field? Locate support lines across adjoining measurement zones. For direct measurement, define the check dimensions and how two crews agree on the common reference. For a three-dimensional survey, ask the provider to identify missing views, obstructions and the practical limit of the accepted deliverable. Give reconciled dimensions priority over impressive visual output.

Select a representative area with a transition, not just the easiest rectangular bay. Ask each bidder to describe how its deliverable would reach the machine planner. Include the time for field access, preparation of references, processing, drawing revision, resolving discrepancies and a repeat visit if the structure changes. Compare both offers at the same accepted output. Add the detailer's processing, checking and release fee to a data-only offer before comparing it with a price for approved production drawings.

## Make survey scope follow construction sequence

An early scan can support design decisions but still precede final alignment of the supports. For a staged release, name the survey milestone, the construction activities permitted afterward and the party paying for remeasurement if those activities change the geometry. On a mixed project, price specialist measurement on interdependent transitions and direct checks on independently defined repeated fields. Define the shared boundaries in one coordinate reference. Without that link, two individually credible datasets can leave the installer with a gap or overlap at their junction.

**Mistake: Purchasing a model without the roof detail.** A contractor buys a detailed scan, then sends the file to a panel shop that accepts only released panel schedules. The file contains the building but nobody has allowed for converting support positions into developed panel dimensions. Assign the geometry interpretation, checking and drawing release and price those steps before comparing services.

**Mistake: Repeating a typical bay measurement across a transition.** A project records a single representative run and orders identical pieces through a changing roof area. The closing pieces need different geometry. Draw the transition boundary, measure its actual references and release a separate set of marks while keeping the repeated bay schedule for genuinely identical runs.

Value the specialist survey against the specific remake, delay or detailing work it can remove. Avoid credit for every panel on the building when only a transition depends on three-dimensional geometry. Conversely, a short transition can determine the layout of a long adjacent field, which makes its checked references valuable beyond its own area. Ask the roof designer and fabricator to identify those dependencies and sign the release path together.

## Eight RFQ entries for usable production geometry

1. Provide the roof design and construction-stage drawings.
2. Mark which supports exist and the date of final alignment.
3. List straight, tapered, curved and transition panel groups.
4. State common references, measurement access and blocked views.
5. Attach an example of the fabricator's accepted production record.
6. Name survey, design, interpretation and release responsibilities.
7. Define checking, revision, file ownership and revisit terms.
8. Request separate prices for survey data and completed panel schedules.
