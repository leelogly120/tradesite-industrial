**Keep a metal-sheet supply business when the project uses a separately specified PV installation and your contract ends at accepted roof panels. Enter a roof-integrated solar package only with an appointed system provider and an agreed route through panel production, PV integration, electrical completion and future replacement.** Buy forming equipment for the metal work retained in that route. Base a forming-line investment on the metal-sheet work your business retains.

For an established panel producer, this is a choice about the product sold to the customer. A completed solar roof carries interfaces beyond producing a suitable section. The buyer needs to decide which of those interfaces the business will own, subcontract or leave within the customer's separate solar contract.

## Two sales offers with different completion points

In the separate-PV route, the roof-panel order identifies the accepted roof product. The PV provider specifies its own assembly and its connections to the roof through the responsible designers. The panel producer's manufacturing case follows the released sheet schedule and the agreed roof-system requirements.

An integrated route joins the roofing and solar functions in a named product package. [Kalzip's solar-system range](https://www.kalzip.com/en/products/roof-systems/solar-systems/) distinguishes adhered thin-film PV on aluminium standing-seam sheets from retrofitted and raised PV arrangements. These are specific system architectures, rather than evidence that any metal panel becomes a solar product after an attachment is added.

Select the route that matches the customer's accepted specification and the business's actual supply agreements. If the order requires an integrated product, selling ordinary sheets plus an unallocated PV allowance leaves an incomplete offer. If the customer already retains a separate PV contractor, buying additional integration processes needs its own supported demand.

## Integration does not determine the factory location

Write down where each operation occurs. Rolling, PV bonding, electrical connection and roof installation can sit with different providers and locations in the actual system.

Kalzip's [Prestwick project account](https://www.kalzip.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Press-release-AluPlusSolar-in-Prestwick-0721.pdf) describes sheets rolled to length on site, with specialist on-site bonding of the PV product. The useful procurement fact is the division of work. A roof-integrated product did not require every integration operation to happen inside the panel producer's factory.

Ask the proposed system provider to identify the supported process and its required production conditions. Then decide whether the business is buying equipment, buying specialist processing or supplying formed sheets into that provider's operation. Price the route actually offered. A speculative factory integration cell is a separate investment from a contract to form the metal substrate.

For site production, retain the logistics of both trades. Establish where accepted sheets transfer to the integration operation, who stores and protects them, and how the completed objects reach their next stage. These boundaries belong in the programme before booking a former or lift.

## Use one responsibility map before committing capital

Complete this map with the customer, roofing system provider and PV specialist. Each row needs one accepted output and a named recipient; an unallocated row is an incomplete commercial package.

| Product stage | Fill in the actual supply boundary | Responsible owner and evidence | Equipment decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sold product | Roof-only panels or named integrated product: ___ | Commercial lead; customer specification | Defines the order being supported |
| Metal manufacture | Material, section, lengths and accepted output point: ___ | Panel process provider; released drawings | Defines tooling and forming demand |
| PV integration | Provider, location, incoming sheet condition and output: ___ | Solar-system provider; supported process | Separates owned machinery from purchased processing |
| Building interfaces | Roof connections, PV interfaces and accepted revisions: ___ | Responsible designers and system providers | Fixes the product the line must serve |
| Electrical completion | Included installation, inspection and handback work: ___ | Appointed electrical specialist | Includes the work outside sheet production |
| Material custody | Transfer location, protection and rejection ownership: ___ | Operations leads; agreed handover records | Prices handling between processes |
| Replacement route | Required access, replacement object and responsible provider: ___ | System provider and facilities owner | Defines the after-sales scope being sold |
| Investment basis | Firm eligible orders, process quotes and exceptions: ___ | Buyer; same finished-product endpoint | Establishes whether capital belongs in the business |

A forming order should reference the metal-production row precisely. The commercial offer to the customer should cover every row its promised endpoint includes. Keeping these two documents aligned prevents the equipment quotation from silently becoming a promise to deliver the complete solar system.

## Separate machine utilization from solar project value

Count the accepted sheet runs your business will manufacture. Exclude panels purchased as part of another provider's integrated package. Identify projects for which the same tooling and material route are genuinely accepted; a common label such as solar roofing is insufficient.

For a roof-only supplier, investment rests on repeat metal-panel orders and their actual production route. For an integrated-package supplier, add the costs and resources of the retained integration, handling and support work. Compare that complete business with buying the named finished package from a specialist.

This decision comes earlier: whether the business will sell those roof panels or own the wider solar-roof package.

## Retain a practical replacement route

Have the system provider describe the actual replacement item and the access needed to exchange it. That item can be different from the plain metal sheet sold by a conventional panel business. Record who diagnoses the issue, supplies the replacement and restores the completed assembly.

The building owner also needs the relevant product identities and provider contacts at handover. Include the agreed support deliverables in the integrated offer. For the separate-PV route, preserve the boundary between roof and solar providers so a future service request reaches the responsible party.

This is a commercial scope decision, not a reason to promise a generic service life or savings figure. Compare offers using the declared obligations and project-specific replacement arrangement.

## Two hypothetical supply failures

**Hypothetical failure case: the former is bought as a complete solar factory.** A panel producer wins an integrated-roof enquiry and budgets only for rolling. No provider owns the supported bonding process or electrical completion. Secure the complete system partnership and its process allocation before approving the machinery investment.

**Hypothetical failure case: all solar area is counted as owned forming work.** Another buyer includes panels supplied within a specialist's finished package in its machine-utilization forecast. Remove those bought-in panels and calculate the investment from the released metal work the business actually retains.
