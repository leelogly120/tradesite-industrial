**Retain a serviceable owned platform when its OEM supports the required guarding configuration for that exact unit, a qualified upfit provider can deliver whole-machine acceptance by the work date, and that route costs less for the same required functions, work and comparison period. Buy an equipped new machine when those conditions fail or a qualifying new offer has the lower complete cost. Shortlist ARC-F25 only against the required personnel workface and documented equipped-configuration response. First, obtain a specialist-approved function schedule and an identity-specific OEM eligibility response.**

This purchase starts with a new site requirement, not an assumption that the existing machine is worn out. It also starts with functions, not an accessory name. A device acting at the controls and a system detecting nearby objects do not necessarily satisfy the same requirement.

## Write the required function before selecting the hardware

Have the responsible specialist state the relevant task conditions and the required additional function. Separate contact at the operator-control area from non-contact object detection, and distinguish overhead/platform coverage from chassis-area coverage. Ask which requirement each offered system actually addresses. Do not exchange one function for another because both brochures use the word protection.

[HSE research RR1180](https://www.hse.gov.uk/research/rrhtm/rr1180.htm) appraises secondary guarding and reports that protection varies with the incident scenario. Its findings do not establish a universally protective device. For procurement, the useful output is a traceable match between the accepted requirement, the actual system function and the exact machine configuration; the specialist retains risk assessment and system selection.

Map the elevated work as well. An acceptable functional response still needs the crew, tools and working region required by the installation. Give each bidder the same overhead obstructions, personnel positions, floor datums and surrounding trades. A guarded small basket and a shared broad deck are different access purchases unless the remaining personnel resources are included.

## Treat OEM eligibility as a configuration decision

Collect the owned unit's model, serial number, market version, controls configuration and fitted options. Give that identity to the OEM and proposed upfit provider. Buy an explicit applicable kit and integration scope, rather than treating a kit for the same brand as permission to modify every machine in the fleet. Require the response to address the unit's existing options and final control/software configuration, with their whole-machine acceptance owner named.

[JLG's November 18, 2026 release](https://www.jlg.com/en/press-releases/jlg-introduces-skysense-with-collision-avoidance) illustrates the specificity needed: it announces a system for selected scissor and vertical-lift models, with named aftermarket eligibility in the United States and Canada. It also distinguishes coverage around the platform and chassis. That announcement is evidence for those listed configurations and markets, not availability on ARC-F25 or an unnamed fleet unit.

[JLG's explanation of its safety technologies](https://www.jlg.com/en/directaccess/safety-technologies-and-accessories-for-jlg-mewps) separately describes enhanced control-panel protection and object detection. Preserve that distinction in the quotation comparison. An offer labelled secondary guarding without the function, covered region and eligible configuration does not identify what the buyer receives.

## Price the completed machine, not a box of parts

The retained route includes the applicable equipment, qualified integration, whole-machine acceptance, updated applicable instructions and handover, and the substitute access needed during the real unavailable period. Name the party responsible for each deliverable. A parts invoice with installation excluded is not equivalent to a new equipped machine ready for the accepted work.

For new supply, specify the required functions in the actual offer and request their configuration records. Then price delivery, destination acceptance and any other personnel resource needed to serve the same installation. Retaining an owned unit for other work is a separate fleet-use decision; do not hide that work's replacement equipment in the guarding comparison.

![A low red personnel deck above grey lifting structure, tracks and supports stands in a workshop, with a softened figure nearby.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261109-low-deck-workshop.webp)
*Request final configuration and acceptance records alongside the actual deck layout and entry data for the complete machine.*

When both routes satisfy the same required functions, task programme and comparison period, choose the lower complete cost. If the existing identity has no applicable supported configuration, remove that upfit from the tender rather than pricing an improvised equivalent. If its release misses the project date, include a complete cover arrangement or choose a new offer that meets the requirement and date.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two misleadingly complete offers

**Mistake (hypothetical): platform sensing is accepted as chassis coverage.** A buyer sees a detection-system name and marks every site requirement satisfied, although the actual requirement includes a distinct machine region absent from that configuration. Correct the tender with separate function/region entries and the provider's configuration response, assessed by the responsible specialist. Reject the unmapped requirement instead of marking it complete from the system name.

**Mistake (hypothetical): a kit price is compared with delivered new equipment.** The kit fits the model family, but the quotation excludes identity checks, integration acceptance and substitute personnel access during the work. The apparent saving disappears from the actual project budget. Obtain the exact-unit eligibility response and a completed-machine offer with the missing responsibilities and dates priced.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Eight attachments for the new-platform RFQ

1. Specialist-approved functional schedule distinguishing the actually required contact, proximity and covered-region requirements.
2. Owned-unit identities, controls and market versions, with the OEM response and applicable upfit proposal.
3. Personnel task plan and workface sections with floor-to-task datums, offsets and overhead obstructions.
4. Simultaneous crew, tools and materials inventory with the proposed shared working-region layout.
5. Entry dimensions, turns, fitted envelope, standing positions and supporting-floor records.
6. Required integration/whole-machine acceptance records, handover documents and named assessment responsibilities.
7. Work calendar, actual upfit/new acceptance dates and separately priced substitute-access allocation.
8. Destination, site power and transport boundary, requesting the exact supplied functions, deck geometry, simultaneous task/load limits, reactions and configuration/acceptance records.
