Use the Machinery Directive route only when the individual machine's lawful placement on the EU market before 20 January 2027 is established. For first placement when Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies, buy against that applicable regime and the actual machine's conformity route. If credible earlier-placement evidence is absent, select a unit and offer planned for the later regime, with the responsible parties and deliverables in the order. Establish the actual event from its facts; the buyer's preferred date cannot choose the law. Start the unit-specific timeline before signing.

An order date, an earlier model launch and a booked vessel answer different questions from lawful first placement. This matters for a roof-panel former supplied alone and for a defined former-and-lift assembly. Freeze the purchased equipment boundary first, then have the responsible EU parties assess its classification and dated supply event.

## The transition belongs to the individual unit

The [27 July 2026 consolidated Machinery Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/2026-07-27/eng) gives 20 January 2027 as the general application date in Article 54 and the Directive repeal date in Article 51. Article 52 preserves further market availability for products lawfully placed under the Directive before that date. Some provisions apply earlier, so the general date is not the start of every article.

The buyer's useful distinction is between an identified product already lawfully placed before the transition and a product first placed when the Regulation applies. The same model name can appear on both sides. An earlier sale of another unit cannot establish the route for the machine now quoted.

The workshop below contains two forming beds and separate loose shafts. Give each purchased unit and included module its own identifier in the contract and records. A catalogue family or shared workshop scene is too broad a boundary for the timeline.

*Keep the same identified former and included modules across the order, completion records and conformity schedule.*

## Separate the events before comparing offers

The Commission's [Blue Guide, sections 2.2, 2.3 and 2.5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52022XC0629(04)), explains first availability by individual product. The relevant offer or agreement follows completed manufacture; physical handover is not essential to that event. An agreement to manufacture a later product is different. For imports, customs release and market placement can occur at different times.

Therefore record order acceptance, manufacturing completion, the asserted first supply, transport, customs and destination receipt separately. Ask the accountable party to identify which event is relied upon and why it qualifies for that particular unit. Keep its evidence with the event instead of using the earliest convenient date on the file.

Match the manufacturing record's completed unit and configuration to the product in the asserted supply transaction. Completion is one necessary input to that placement assessment; it is not the supply event itself. Name the responsible reviewer and resolve inconsistent identities or dates before committing to the claimed earlier route. Keep packing, payment and freight records on their own timeline entries.

## Buy the route with its actual responsibilities

An earlier-placement offer needs a coherent unit record, lawful original placement and the documents applicable to that supplied machine. Evaluate its actual condition, included equipment, instructions and handover alongside that legal history. The transition alone gives no answer about a used machine's condition or suitability for the next roof-panel order.

A later-route offer needs the responsible parties to define the product classification, applicable assessment process and the required documents and marking for the complete purchased boundary. List their deliverables and due stages in the order. Separate conformity work from freight and local installation work so that an apparently complete transport offer does not hide an unfinished machinery obligation.

For a combined package, retain the connection between the former, lift, controls and any vehicle or carrier. Changing a module during manufacture can change the product being assessed. Carry that change to the conformity owner and the commercial schedule before accepting an unchanged delivery description.

The port scene below belongs on the transport line of the record. Its visible equipment, cranes and people give shipping context. Use the actual unit records and supply-chain assessment for the legal event.

## Two timing mistakes to remove from the order

**Mistake (hypothetical): a December deposit is treated as earlier placement.** The buyer pays a December 2026 advance for a former scheduled for completion in February 2027. At the advance date, its configuration is still being manufactured. Specify the applicable later-route deliverables and programme for that actual unit, instead of treating the payment date as a completed-product supply event.

**Mistake (hypothetical): an old model number is used for a new assembly.** A supplier cites a previous unit's documents for the familiar model, while the purchase now combines a different former, control arrangement and carrier under a new unit identity. Reidentify the complete purchased boundary and have the responsible parties determine its route. Keep the earlier unit's record attached to that earlier unit.

If the commercial plan relies on earlier placement, give the evidence review a decision point before the purchase becomes committed to that route. Where it fails, choose a properly scoped later-route offer or a different identified unit with established history. Rewording an invoice cannot repair missing product history.

## RFQ attachments for a transition-date purchase

1. Identified individual machine and complete assembly boundary.
2. EU destination, intended use and actual commercial parties.
3. Order, manufacturing, first-supply and transport timeline.
4. Evidence supporting any claimed earlier lawful placement.
5. Product classification and assigned conformity-route responsibilities.
6. Unit-specific declaration, instructions, marking and record-delivery schedule.
7. Contract treatment of a changed machine or delayed event.
8. Named EU technical and commercial release owners.
