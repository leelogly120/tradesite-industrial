Choose an inside-glazed curtain wall for bays where the selected system supports the required replacement from inside and a replacement pane can reach the bay after fit-out. Choose the outside-glazed alternative where the facade specialist can deliver the glass and crew externally over the building's service life. Decide bay by bay before awarding a single access allowance for the whole elevation.

An owner can buy an attractive curtain-wall system and discover years later that replacing a broken pane requires dismantling permanent interior work or occupying a neighboring property. The first installation happens in an unfinished building with access benefiting several trades. Replacement happens around occupants and a completed landscape. Ask how one replacement unit will reach its final position under the latter conditions.

## Start with the glazing detail, then trace two routes

Obtain the facade specialist's current drawing for each offered bay type. Identify from which side the particular system can be glazed, which tasks take place on the opposite side and what access is needed to remove and replace one unit. Identify opening lights, opaque spandrel areas and fixed replacements individually where their details differ.

Kawneer's architectural manual identifies one captured curtain-wall system as offering inside and outside glazing. That establishes a genuine design choice within a framing family. The detail and manufacturer documents for the actual job govern which side serves each task. A general label such as stick-built or unitized describes another part of the supply decision. [Kawneer 1600 Wall System 3 architectural manual](https://www.kawneer.us/kawneer_files/shared%20files/97911-Arch_Manual/ADMD030EN.pdf)

Draw a route for the pane and a route for personnel. Start the glass route at the delivery vehicle, continue to temporary holding and end at the opening. Start the personnel route on the supporting floor or exterior position and end where the specialist must work. The two routes can begin on the same level and still require different equipment, trade attendance and permission to enter occupied space.

## Inside glazing spends interior space

On an inside-glazed proposal, ask the specialist to trace a replacement unit through the completed building. Record loading access, goods-lift or other vertical transfer, door turns, corridor pinch points, ceiling heights and the final approach to the bay. A pane that fits the opening can still be too large for a return at the end of a corridor. Obtain an accepted alternative where a fit-out drawing removes that route.

At the workface, show the room use, fixed partitions, plant, furniture and floor protection. Identify where the glass-handling equipment can stand and where the crew can carry out the named work. An inside-glazed system can still involve tasks on the outside. Have the facade specialist identify those tasks and their positions so the owner buys the required attendance.

Put the continuing right of access in the owner's documents. If a future tenant can permanently subdivide the only transfer corridor, decide whether to protect that route, reserve a removable interface with funded reinstatement, or select a different glazing arrangement for those bays. A proposed restriction on future tenants has a real commercial consequence even when it adds little to today's glazing invoice.

## Outside glazing spends perimeter access

For the exterior alternative, draw the approaches after landscaping, loading bays, canopies and adjacent buildings are finished. Record the building owner's rights over the relevant land and the times when an exterior access provider can occupy it. Identify bays above setbacks where the workface sits beyond the usual exterior standing positions. Obtain an accepted equipment proposal for those locations individually.

Separate the cost of bringing a replacement pane to the elevation from the cost of positioning the glazing crew. Price any recurring occupation, service disruption, specialist equipment and protection of people using the building. The owner can then compare genuinely accessible interior and exterior alternatives for the same pane and occupancy condition.

## Two hypothetical mistakes at tender

**Mistake: accepting an interior route on shell drawings.** The first panes arrive before permanent partitions and an internal stair enclosure are fitted. The maintenance schedule promises the same internal delivery route later. A replacement-size unit cannot turn at the completed lobby. Trace the representative pane through the fit-out plan, then price an agreed removable interface or a different system detail.

**Mistake: assigning every exterior bay to the same personnel platform.** A buyer obtains a rate for the flat facade and applies it to a setback above a finished canopy. Neither the crew workface nor the glass transfer is in the provider's offered envelope. Obtain a separate price for those bays and compare an inside-glazed detail where the facade designer accepts it.
