**Share one forming package when the projects can use it sequentially and the transfer period fits between their committed windows. Investigate a second complete package when both sites need production at the same time. Buy a second lift alone only if the missing resource is the lift and the remaining production equipment is genuinely available.**

The number of buildings is not the number of machines required. Nor does a machine that physically fits several projects necessarily serve them within their programmes. Decide asset quantity with a resource calendar alongside the [project-by-project equipment fit matrix](/blog/roll-forming-lift-multiple-project-fleet-fit/).

## Put occupation dates beside production dates

A lift can be unavailable to the next project while it is being transported, assembled, inspected, integrated with the former or released from the previous site. Record the actual proposed activities and obtain durations from the responsible providers. Do not insert a universal setup allowance.

| Calendar entry | Site A | Site B | Information owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Earliest equipment arrival | Enter agreed date/window | Enter agreed date/window | Site team: supplies the arrival window used to reserve transport |
| Site ready for the proposed assembly | Confirm access, support and services | Confirm access, support and services | Site and equipment parties: name who releases access, support and services before assembly |
| Production must start | Enter committed requirement | Enter committed requirement | Programme owner: supplies the latest acceptable production start |
| Required production/receiving periods | Enter accepted project plan | Enter accepted project plan | Production and roofing leads: agree matching production and receiving windows |
| Earliest equipment release | Include outstanding dependent work | Include outstanding dependent work | Site team: names the final task and handback that release the equipment |
| Transfer and next-site preparation | Obtain actual route and service plan | Obtain actual route and service plan | Carrier and equipment providers: supply route-specific transfer and preparation durations |
| Uncertain dependency | Name it and its deciding party | Name it and its deciding party | Programme owner: names each deciding party, decision date and fallback if readiness misses the next start |

The one-package test is straightforward: **earliest credible readiness at the next site must be no later than that site's required start.** If those dates conflict, identify an agreed scheduling change or another supply route. A hoped-for early finish is not a resolved conflict.

## A second lift and a second production system are different orders

Compare the following packages before requesting a price for “two units.” Duplicate forming equipment when independent simultaneous production is required; use another panel-supply route when only one project's output needs temporary coverage.

| Resource arrangement | What it can address | Dependency that remains | Useful buying condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One complete package moved between sites | Sequential demand | Release, transport and next-site readiness | Work windows can be coordinated |
| Two lifts sharing one former | Separate lift locations with sequential forming | Former transfer, integration and availability | A documented sequence makes sharing practical |
| Two complete forming packages | Concurrent production requirements | Separate material, operators, services and receivers | Both sites have independent ready work |
| One owned package plus outside panel supply | A temporary or exceptional overlap | Purchased-panel delivery and onward handling | Duplicate ownership is not yet supported by repeat demand |

Two lifts sharing one former should be treated as a specific transfer proposal. It cannot produce at both sites simultaneously. Before selecting it, obtain the scope for removing and reinstalling the former and confirm that each proposed pairing is reviewed.

Similarly, two complete packages still need two functioning project routes. A shared operator team, unavailable coil supply or one receiving resource can leave the second machine idle. Write these dependencies beside the equipment count so the purchase solves the right shortage.

## Use the calendar to choose truck or crawler candidates

A truck-mounted route deserves comparison where the buyer expects repeated road movements and can establish the chassis, road and setup requirements. A crawler route deserves comparison where its site arrangement addresses the work positions and a separate transport route is available. Neither architecture carries an assumed transfer time.

![Red truck-mounted lifting apparatus beside a building with a long blue sheet.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261027-truck-roof.webp)

*A truck-mounted configuration connects the forming assembly to a vehicle; confirm both the road arrangement and the working position for every project.*

The vehicle-mounted arrangement in the image explains why chassis scope matters to the calendar. An offer for the lifting module alone leaves local vehicle supply and integration as separate activities. Do not schedule it as a completed truck delivery.

For a crawler candidate, request the actual transported configuration, carrier availability and site unloading arrangements. These details affect readiness, even when the machine's height band is suitable for both roofs. A common model does not eliminate the second site's access and support review.

## Two scheduling traps that equipment quotations miss

**Failure case: the second lift still waits for the first former.** A contractor orders another lift to cover an overlapping roof, but both offers assume the same buyer-owned line. The calendar contains two lift locations and one production resource. Either change the sequence, price a compatible second line or obtain panels through another source.

**Failure case: the first site's “finish” is not equipment release.** Main-roof production ends, but a remaining area still depends on the package. The buyer books transport using the main-roof date. Record the full release condition and identify how outstanding panels will be supplied before promising the package to Site B.

Where a date remains uncertain, show the alternatives and their triggers. For example, the project owner can decide when it will commit to outside panel supply if a preceding site is not released. Obtain the actual supplier availability before relying on that alternative; the calendar itself cannot reserve equipment or production.

Use a matched pair only when both duty sheets support it. A mixed fleet can be worth comparing if the projects have different height or mobility needs. Conversely, matching machines for convenience does not justify buying more height or a different chassis arrangement than the work requires.

### RFQ package for one versus two systems

- Site-by-site roof plans, outlet datums and receiving positions.
- Panel profiles, material requirements and production quantities.
- Required start, work window and full equipment-release conditions.
- Current former ownership and the equipment expected at each site.
- Proposed truck/chassis or crawler/carrier arrangement for each move.
- Access, support-area and ground information for both sites.
- Operators, power, coil supply and any resources shared between projects.
- The sequential, duplicated and outside-panel alternatives to price.
