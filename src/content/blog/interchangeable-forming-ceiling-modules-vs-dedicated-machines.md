**Choose dedicated forming and ceiling-access machines when the duties overlap or no documented conversion is offered for the proposed host. Compare an interchangeable arrangement only when its provider explicitly covers both duties, the complete conversion and the acceptance required before each assignment. Share one asset only when the realistic work calendar accommodates those events.** Similar lift structures and a broad deck in a photograph do not establish an interchangeable product.

The purchase is not simply one machine against two invoice lines. A shared host introduces modules, transfers, storage, technical records and periods when neither duty is available. Dedicated assets introduce separate transport and support commitments but can serve their accepted assignments independently.

## Begin with two outputs and two equipment boundaries

For roofing, identify the compatible former, its material input and the panel outlet/receiver relationship. For ceiling or wall work, identify the personnel tasks, deck arrangement, crew, tools and materials. Keep those physical duties separate even if one supplier coordinates their purchase.

ARCLIFT's catalogue separates the [forming-lift duty](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/) from the [large-deck ceiling-platform duty](/products/arc-f25-crawler-ceiling-platform/). These references do not establish that either model converts into the other. An interchangeable proposal needs its own identified host, modules and complete supported configurations.

The existing [PEB equipment-package guide](/blog/peb-roof-forming-and-cladding-access-equipment-package/) helps allocate production and access duties. This decision goes further: should the buyer own a separate asset for each duty or purchase a documented shared arrangement?

## Put conversion on the programme as real work

Ask the actual provider to define the conversion boundary. Identify removed and fitted objects, the responsible competent service, handling resources, location, applicable records and acceptance deliverables. Request the scope and duration from that provider's proposal rather than inventing a standard changeover allowance.

Add delivery, preparation and accepted return to the next duty. Include any offsite travel needed to reach a suitable conversion location. A gap between two project start dates is not necessarily free conversion time: the first site still needs equipment removal and the second needs its complete receiving and setup period.

## Complete a two-duty asset worksheet

| Decision input | Shared arrangement entry | Dedicated-assets entry | Owner and purchase consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Actual configurations | Host and each accepted module/configuration ID: ___ | Forming package and work-platform IDs: ___ | Equipment providers; establishes the real alternatives |
| Duty positions | Outlet/receiver and ceiling workface drawings: ___ | Same drawings assessed for each asset: ___ | Surveyor; uses building and local support-floor datums |
| Work calendar | Each duty plus complete conversion/transfer: ___ | Each independent equipment occupation: ___ | Planner; reveals concurrency and unavailable periods |
| Conversion resources | Named service, handling, location and deliverables: ___ | Any initial assembly or task-change work: ___ | Provider and buyer; prices actual work rather than an assumed swap |
| Stored objects | Removed modules, former, tools and location: ___ | Spare/unused equipment and location: ___ | Equipment manager; fixes storage and custody costs |
| Support and transport | Issued data for every proposed configuration: ___ | Separate issued data and carriers: ___ | Site engineer and logistics lead; determine accepted routes/support |
| Acceptance | Required handover before each duty: ___ | Initial and site-specific acceptance scope: ___ | Technical and project leads; defines when the asset is actually available |
| Complete ownership | Asset, modules, conversion, idle and support costs: ___ | Two assets, support and transport costs: ___ | Finance lead; compares the same awarded work period |

Record task elevations from their proper datums. An outlet height for a former and a personnel workface level answer different questions. The providers must assess each offered configuration against the relevant task; one catalogue height class cannot establish both working arrangements.

## Concurrent work is the strongest reason for two assets

Where roof production and interior installation both require equipment during the same released period, a single host cannot satisfy both assignments at once. Purchase a second accepted capability or a separately offered project service for the uncovered duty. Price that additional resource before calling the shared arrangement cheaper.

Sharing deserves comparison where the duties are sequential, each configuration is supported and the full transition fits the programme. Preserve a recovery allowance based on the actual provider's plan and the project's consequences of delay. Do not fund a shared asset from the assumption that every conversion will finish before the next contractor needs it.

The broad deck in the yard photograph has a crawler base and deployed supports. Its complete arrangement needs its own delivery and setup information. A roof-former interface drawing cannot replace that personnel-platform information.

## Two failures in hypothetical shared-host budgets

**Hypothetical failure case: construction phases overlap.** A buyer expects roof production to finish before ceiling work, but released work in another building requires both duties together. Rebuild the schedule from actual awards and buy the uncovered capability before selecting one shared host.

**Hypothetical failure case: a deck photo becomes a conversion commitment.** Another buyer budgets only an accessory price without an identified host/module combination or accepted conversion service. Obtain a complete documented offer or select separate catalogue machines. Do not purchase an assumed compatibility relationship.
