Choose consigned coil when the metal supplier offers the exact roofing material your shop repeatedly consumes, lets you draw it against actual jobs and sets an acceptable price for the unused balance. Buy a lot outright when it belongs to a specific awarded roof, or when owning it gives you more useful freedom over timing and allocation than the proposed release agreement.

Start with one finish and width. A panel business can buy the special colour for a school project and use a release agreement for the standard finish on its repeat industrial orders. The decision belongs to each material reference. Combining them can make a slow colour look as liquid as the best seller.

## Follow the coil from reservation to invoice

A release programme and a consignment agreement answer different questions. Reserving a supplier's stock addresses availability. The consignment terms set out who owns identified material, where it stays, when the buyer incurs an obligation and how the parties settle. Metal Master describes positioning master coil against a forecast and processing it to size on release. A buyer who needs slit, identified roofing coil already at the forming shop should request that specific arrangement. [Metal Master's steel distribution and release programme](https://metalmaster.com/steel-distributor)

Put four events on the bid sheet. When is material allocated? When can it arrive at the former? What event fixes the price and triggers billing? When is the cash actually due? A supplier who bills the entire coil on dispatch offers a different cash profile from one who bills documented withdrawals. The quoted unit price alone will hide that difference. Ask for an example invoice for a partial withdrawal and the statement that accompanies the remaining stock.

## Test the agreement with a real part coil

Pick a recent job whose last panel used just part of a coil. Trace the material reference from goods receipt to the job ticket, the amount consumed, the usable remainder and the recorded scrap. Ask each bidder to settle that same example under its terms. A quote that requires payment for the entire opened coil has a different consequence from a quote that accepts measured consumption. Include the cost of identifying and storing the remainder in either case.

Establish who can authorise a transfer between jobs or sites. If a roof specification requires material from a named finish lot, give the scheduler that requirement and reserve matching coil before reallocating it. A nominally identical colour on the stock list is insufficient evidence that two deliveries can be mixed on the same visible roof plane. Use the customer's approved finish and lot policy.

A damaged delivery also tests the arrangement. Record who examines incoming material, the evidence required to separate transit damage from later handling damage and who has authority to release a replacement. If the supplier retains title while the buyer has custody, enter the agreed loss allocation separately. Ownership and day-to-day responsibility belong in different columns.

## Compare two cash calendars against the same accepted output

Use the same awarded orders, material requirement and customer payment schedule in each bid. Enter deposits, delivery payments, draw invoices, service fees and final settlement on their due dates. Compare the largest cash deficit during the period as well as the total procurement cost. A bidder can offer a useful reduction in peak funding while charging more overall. The finance lead can then put a price on that benefit.

Repeat the calculation with the largest customer draw delayed. Show who still pays storage, whether an aging purchase falls due and how much residual coil can actually serve another order. Credit a recoverable balance at the value the business can support from another job or a written return offer. A booked coil carrying a discontinued colour seldom deserves the same credit as material already allocated to the next roof.

## Two purchasing mistakes that change the result

**Mistake one.** Suppose a distributor reserves master coil at its own warehouse, and the roofer books a former on the assumption that project-width strip is ready on site. Slitting and dispatch are still open. Base the machine booking on the agreed arrival of released, usable coil and price the intermediate processing with the supply bid.

**Mistake two.** A buyer models its standard finish and three special colours as a single stock pool. The overall draw looks steady, but the special runs miss the contractual buyout date. Model separate material references, fund the dedicated colours against the customer orders and negotiate consignment only on the repeat references that pass the delay test.

## RFQ for the material and the forming interface

- List approved coil references, permissible sources and project-specific finish restrictions.
- Attach awarded orders and separate the unawarded sales forecast.
- Identify the holding location, dispatch obligation and receipt inspection owner.
- State the release event, pricing date and payment due date requested.
- Include one partial-coil job with its scrap and remaining balance.
- Request the aging, cancellation, damage and residual purchase terms.
- Mark each destination, change of custody and job transfer permission.
- Attach the controlled panel drawing, material specification and acceptance sample for the former.
