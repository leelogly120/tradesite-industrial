**Buy a personnel platform alone when an accepted panel-handling arrangement is already identified and its interaction with access has been reviewed. Otherwise procure the access and specialist handling scopes together, with named providers and a shared layout.** A broad deck is not a substitute for a wall-panel lifter, and a vacuum lifter does not provide the workers' access positions.

For a cladding contractor, the expensive gap appears when both quotations sound complete but neither supplies a necessary function. The platform supplier prices people and tools at the workface. The panel supplier prices delivery to a receiving area. Nobody has priced the accepted equipment and resources that present the panel for installation. Resolve that gap before comparing equipment totals.

## Two functions, two evidence sets

The personnel-access proposal should identify the actual work positions, platform configuration, task loads, route and site requirements. The panel-handling proposal should identify the specific panel and the equipment and resources needed for the handling function defined by the responsible installation planner. The project needs both proposals to address the same building stage.

Vacuum lifting is one specialist equipment category to investigate where the panel and handling specialists support it. For example, [VIAVAC describes its CladBoy](https://www.viavac.com/panels/cladboy/) as a vacuum lifter for sandwich roof and wall panels. Select a specific proposal only after the specialists have addressed the actual panel, surface, orientation and site arrangement.

Send the panel manufacturer's issued information to the specialist provider and ask it to identify the applicable equipment and conditions in its proposal. Do not select suction pads, clamps or attachment arrangements from a generic photograph. Where another handling method is specified, procure the equipment for that accepted method rather than changing it to fit an attractive vacuum-tool price.

Great Britain's [HSE guidance on MEWPs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm) identifies material dimensions, load distribution and the possible need for additional lifting equipment as planning considerations. The practical buying consequence is straightforward: keep material presentation in the purchase schedule even when personnel access has already been selected.

## When an access-only order is the complete missing purchase

Choose access-only supply when the project already has a defined, accepted material-handling package for the actual panels and work locations. Identify its provider, availability, included resources and operating-space requirements. Give that information to the access provider so that both arrangements can be reviewed together.

Check the difference between owning a tool and having a complete handling package. A contractor can own a vacuum device while still needing an appropriate supporting machine, competent resources, inspection arrangements and a project-specific plan. The responsible specialists define those requirements. Procurement records and prices them; it should not assume they arrive with the tool.

Likewise, a panel supplier's delivery service is not automatically an installation-presentation service. Read the actual delivery endpoint. If its obligation ends at the receiving area, the remaining route and workface functions still need an identified provider.

## When to coordinate both purchases before either order

Choose coordinated procurement when the handling method is new to the project, the wall layout constrains the available space, or the two equipment arrangements have not yet been assessed together. Coordination can use separate contracts. What matters is a named project owner for the shared requirements, with each specialist retaining its defined technical responsibility.

Ask for one marked layout showing the proposed personnel-access positions, material equipment positions, delivery and staging areas, and the space reserved for other work. The actual project planners decide movements and work sequencing. The buyer uses the resulting accepted arrangement to establish whether both offers can be delivered within the same site and time constraints.

Do not merge the two load schedules. People, tools and permitted materials on the access equipment belong in its specific task schedule. The panel and handling equipment belong in their own specialist assessment. No panel should be assigned to a platform deck, guardrail or accessory simply to make a procurement diagram look complete.

## A purchase matrix that exposes the missing function

| Package field | Project entry | Access-provider deliverable | Handling-provider deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panel identity | Product, dimensions, mass and manufacturer handling information: ___ | Uses the defined task and material interface | Identifies the panel covered by its proposal |
| Work positions | Wall elevation, fixing faces and floor-to-task datums: ___ | Marks positions addressed and exceptions | Addresses the defined presentation requirement |
| Supplied equipment | Existing assets and required additions: ___ | Lists platform, options and included documents | Lists specialist equipment and separately required supporting resources |
| Shared space | Measured approach, staging and working areas: ___ | Provides the proposed equipment envelope and site requirements | Provides its space requirements for the joint project review |
| Task resources | Named crews and specialist appointments: ___ | States included familiarisation or other offered services | States the resources included and those provided by the project |
| Delivery endpoint | Receiving, assembly and availability dates: ___ | Defines access-equipment handover | Defines handling-equipment handover and availability |
| Acceptance | Project review owners and agreed evidence: ___ | Supplies configuration-specific records and acceptance scope | Supplies its equipment-specific records and acceptance scope |
| Excluded work | Functions still bought elsewhere: ___ | Identifies excluded material-handling activities | Identifies excluded personnel access and installation work |

The installation contractor owns the task description, the surveyor supplies the measured geometry, and the project coordinator maintains this combined schedule. Each equipment provider answers for its proposed scope. Blank functions remain a purchasing gap until the project assigns them to a real, capable provider.

## Compare the assembled proposal on the same endpoint

For an access-only purchase, include the cost and availability of the already selected handling package in the project comparison. For a coordinated purchase, include both equipment scopes, the required supporting resources, delivery and the accepted shared-site arrangement. Do not compare a platform price with a total that also includes panel-handling equipment and then call the latter expensive.

Use the same panel family, wall areas and completion boundary in both alternatives. If one proposal excludes long panels or a recessed area, obtain the separate solution and price before ranking it. Do not let an unpriced exceptional task disappear behind the phrase “complete wall solution.”

## Two package mistakes that stop installation

**The vacuum tool is priced without the resources that make it usable on the project.** In this illustrative order, procurement buys the device and assumes the platform completes the package. The specialist's required supporting equipment and project arrangements are missing. Obtain the complete handling scope and its accepted interaction with personnel access before committing to either purchase.

**Both machines fit individually, but their proposals claim the same working space.** Another buyer checks the access machine against the wall and the handling arrangement against the panel, each on a separate drawing. The combined layout conflicts with the available staging area. Require the responsible planners to resolve the shared layout before treating both offers as a workable package.
