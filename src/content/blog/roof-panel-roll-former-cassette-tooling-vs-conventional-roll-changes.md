**Buy a supported cassette or raft-style tooling system when repeated eligible product changes justify the complete exchange, storage, handling and accepted-restart package. Retain conventional roll changes when their actual work fits your order schedule and competent resources. Buy occasional alternative profiles externally when neither ownership case is supported.** Compare complete changes between accepted products, rather than a short demonstration of moving one tool assembly.

This decision starts after the business has selected a machine serving several products. It differs from choosing one shared line versus dedicated lines. Here the buyer selects the physical tooling-change architecture for the supported product mix.

## Identify what the proposed exchange unit contains

Request a configuration showing which tooling and associated parts belong to each exchangeable unit, which stay with the host and which require separate work. Record its identity against the product drawing. The provider defines compatibility and permitted conversion; the buyer should not infer either from a similar frame.

[Bradbury's panel-equipment overview](https://info.bradburygroup.com/bradbury-group-metal-building-equipment) lists rafted roll-former configurations within its panel-line range. This establishes an offered architecture category. Use the actual provider's configuration and proposal for compatibility, change duration and available equipment scope.

For a conventional proposal, obtain the corresponding description of retained and exchanged tooling. Ask both providers to name the complete equipment and competent work needed to return the machine to accepted output. Avoid treating an unspecified set of spare rollers as equivalent to a complete supported product configuration.

## Measure a change from accepted output to accepted output

Choose representative transitions from the actual order schedule. Record the last accepted output of one product and the first accepted output of the next as the comparison boundaries. The provider's scope should identify preparation, handling, conversion and acceptance within that event.

Separate work performed before the line stops from work that requires the host to be unavailable. Include the people, equipment and space needed for advance preparation. Moving work away from the stopped machine still consumes resources; it should not disappear from the ownership budget.

## A tooling-change investment record

| Decision field | Buyer record | Provider's offered scope | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supported products | Controlled sections, materials and revisions: ___ | Configuration mapped to every required product: ___ | Product lead; establishes eligible demand |
| Transition frequency | Actual product sequence and release dates: ___ | Changes used in the representative comparison: ___ | Planner; tests whether added architecture is justified |
| Exchanged objects | Tooling-unit and loose-part identities: ___ | Included host interfaces and retained parts: ___ | Equipment provider; prevents incomplete product kits |
| Handling | Available location and resources: ___ | Issued unit dimensions, mass and handling requirements: ___ | Competent handling provider; prices the complete movement scope |
| Storage | Allocated locations and product identification: ___ | Specified protection and storage requirements: ___ | Equipment manager; establishes usable off-machine readiness |
| Restart acceptance | Critical dimensions, datums and sample criteria: ___ | Complete transition and acceptance deliverables: ___ | Quality lead/provider; defines productive availability |
| Support | Competent personnel and local service resources: ___ | Documentation, parts and actual service commitment: ___ | Maintenance lead; checks continued ownership capability |
| Complete cost | Host, tooling, handling, storage and interruption: ___ | Quoted inclusions and separately supplied work: ___ | Finance lead; compares the same order schedule |

Use the actual provider's dimensions for exchanged objects. Link output measurements to the controlled product drawing. A tooling-unit outline and a panel inspection datum are different records and should not be combined into a generic dimension field.

## A stored cassette is not automatically a ready product

The ownership case needs controlled identification, applicable documentation and a supported condition for the next assignment. Assign custody and the competence needed to prepare the configuration. Price the supplier's real requirements; do not substitute an assumed maintenance routine.

Include all product-specific objects that the proposal requires. If the next product also needs a different cutter, input arrangement or another separately identified item, carry that work into the comparison. A fast tooling exchange alone is not the full transition between finished products.

For roof-level production, have the integrator assess the complete proposed former configuration with the lift provider. Tooling architecture changes do not authorize additional lifted objects, altered mounting or unreviewed service arrangements. The final machine and configuration records must describe the assembly actually being purchased.

## Two failures in hypothetical quick-change budgets

**Hypothetical failure case: the quoted change ends before accepted production.** A buyer compares a cassette movement with the competing provider's complete restart, including inspection. Align the boundaries and resources. Recalculate both alternatives from the last accepted product to the next accepted product.

**Hypothetical failure case: the next product kit is incomplete.** Another owner stores an identified tooling unit but omits a separately required product-specific item from its purchase. The host cannot fulfil the planned order. Obtain a complete configuration-to-product map and inventory the supplied scope before committing the production schedule.
