Buy a bounded prototype or pre-series programme while the new roof product or production route still has a decision-critical question. Release full line capital when the responsible product parties have accepted the required definition, representative production evidence supports the proposed process and actual demand justifies the complete operation. If those gates fail, revise the product, buy an accepted alternative or stop the investment.

This is a choice about the next capital commitment. It is different from viewing an existing machine demonstration or accepting an already ordered line. A pilot stage should answer named questions before the business pays for a complete plant, with a clear decision at its end.

## Write the decision that the first samples must support

Start with the customer requirement and the product owner. Identify the controlled features already fixed and the unresolved question that prevents investment: a required section feature, the intended material combination, an interface with another component or a representative production concern.

Define the evidence needed for that question with the responsible specialists. An anonymous attractive sample is a weak basis for a large commitment because its material, process and measured features are unknown. Conversely, a short identified sample can be useful where the question is local mating geometry rather than full-length handling.

## Separate three funding gates

**Product-definition work** establishes what the business intends to sell and who can accept its geometry, material and interfaces. Obtain a scoped deliverable for the unresolved product work. Do not bury an undefined design programme inside a machine price.

**Prototype or pre-series work** addresses the agreed production questions using identified materials, samples and observations. Its result should identify what was demonstrated, what changed and what remains outside that evidence. Buy the representative scope required for the decision instead of assuming that either one display section or an entire full-scale run is always necessary.

**The complete line investment** includes equipment, tooling, material handling, utilities, staffing, inspection, maintenance and dispatch. Commit when the accepted product, supported process and order case justify that complete operation. A successful pilot is an input to this decision, not a reason to omit the surrounding plant.

Keep each gate's price and decision owner visible. The buyer needs an agreed point at which it can continue, revise or stop. An early payment should purchase a defined output rather than a vague promise that a usable commercial roof product will eventually emerge.

## A learning-to-investment worksheet

| Decision question | Representative input and record | Responsible party | Next funding decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is the product sufficiently defined? | Controlled section ___; fixed/open features ___ | Product owner and designer | Fund the remaining definition or release a bounded pilot |
| Does the intended material belong in the trial? | Material/finish IDs ___; source lot ___ | Material and product authorities | Use representative inputs or limit the conclusion explicitly |
| Which features must be observed? | Drawing datums ___; agreed checks ___; sample IDs ___ | Quality lead and process provider | Accept the defined result or require a revised proposal |
| Are mating interfaces addressed? | Component references ___; responsible acceptance ___ | Product/interface owner | Retain the section or approve a controlled change |
| What differs from the proposed plant? | Trial equipment ___; intended line differences ___ | Process and line providers | Price remaining production work before full commitment |
| Is output saleable under the intended scope? | Required product evidence and customer decision ___ | Product and customer technical parties | Release eligible orders or retain bought-in supply |
| Does demand fund the operation? | Confirmed orders ___; supported repeat demand ___ | Commercial lead | Approve the complete plant or hold the investment |
| What remains after the pilot? | Open questions ___; owner ___; priced next stage ___ | Project sponsor | Continue, revise or stop with a defined remaining scope |

The table should contain actual decisions. A list ending only with “more testing” is incomplete: identify the unanswered product or process question and the scope needed to decide it. Equally, reject an unconditional pass label that ignores a different material or an absent production function.

## Price what the pilot does not buy

A development specimen does not itself create a staffed factory or an accepted customer product. Separate its value from tooling ownership, future use of drawings, the proposed production machine and the remaining product evidence. Obtain actual terms and technical scopes from the parties offering those deliverables.

For a new panel family, include the associated components and completed-product requirements in the launch plan. Producing its main section while lacking a supported mating component or an accepted customer route leaves the business with an incomplete product. The pilot should expose those dependencies before the full equipment order.

Use committed and demonstrated repeat demand for the plant's base case. Keep prospective orders in a separate scenario until the relevant customer accepts the product and supply scope. A successful sample can support a sales discussion; it should not silently turn every enquiry into financed machine utilisation.

A staged pilot decision precedes that full purchase. Preserve its useful records, but negotiate acceptance of the eventual production line against its actual equipment, material and contract. Differences identified during the pilot belong in the final technical schedule rather than disappearing behind an earlier sample approval.

## Two hypothetical investment failures

**Failure case: the pilot specimen uses a material unrelated to the intended orders.** The section looks satisfactory, so the buyer releases a full line without a responsible decision on the required grade and finish. Identify the material difference, obtain the necessary representative work and limit the earlier conclusion to the specimen actually produced.

**Failure case: a promising sample funds an unsupported market launch.** The tooling works for its defined trial but the business has no accepted customer product route or sustained orders. Separate process success from the complete product and demand gates; retain a bounded development stage or bought-in supply until those conditions are met.

## Eight RFQ attachments for the next justified stage

1. Customer product requirement and the named product decision owner.
2. Controlled section drawing with fixed and explicitly open features.
3. Intended material, coating and representative sample identification.
4. Specific prototype or pre-series questions and required observations.
5. Mating-component, product-evidence and customer-acceptance requirements.
6. Actual pilot output records and identified differences from the proposed plant.
7. Confirmed orders and a complete operating and material-flow plan.
8. The next-stage deliverables, ownership/use boundaries, destination and utilities.
