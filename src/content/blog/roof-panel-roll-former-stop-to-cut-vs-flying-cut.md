Choose stop-to-cut when a complete order trial meets your required dispatch output and finished-panel criteria. Choose flying cutting when repeated cutting stops prevent that output and the proposed moving cutter completes your actual length mix within the required time. Start with the shortest saleable panel and the number of cuts per order, then compare the installed production cells.

This purchase is about cutter motion. A stop-to-cut line pauses material movement for the cut; a flying system moves the cutting assembly with the material. The separate question of cutting before or after forming belongs in the [pre-cut versus post-cut line comparison](/blog/roof-panel-roll-forming-pre-cut-vs-post-cut-line/). Keep the chosen process location fixed while assessing whether continuous cutting earns its additional equipment and support cost.

## Count cuts, not just metres

Two orders with the same total panel length can create very different cutting workloads. A batch of many short panels requires more cutting cycles than a batch of fewer long panels. A demonstration dominated by long pieces therefore hides the condition that frequently makes a cutter purchase important.

For each representative order, record accepted pieces, lengths and the number of cutting cycles. Include first-piece checks, scrap cuts and replacement pieces separately. The production planner should identify the orders that miss the dispatch window, rather than simply choosing the machine with the larger advertised running speed.

Measure elapsed order time from the agreed ready-to-run condition until the last accepted piece reaches the defined receiving position. Use the same starting condition in both trials. If one demonstration starts with material already threaded and the other includes setup, the comparison is not usable.

## The shortest panel tests the moving cutter

A flying cutter must complete its cutting motion and prepare for the next required cut. Ask the equipment engineer to state the offered minimum piece length for the selected profile, material and running condition. Test the shortest recurring length and the transition into it from the preceding job. A demonstration at a different length does not answer that requirement.

[Formtek's explanation of flying die systems](https://blog.formtekgroup.com/methods-of-measuring-for-flying-die-systems) describes travelling dies and identifies profile shape, material, line speed and run length among the selection inputs. That supports treating the cutter as a matched system, not as a speed upgrade that fits every existing former.

The buyer supplies the finished-length tolerance and cut-end acceptance criteria from the product drawing. The supplier provides the proposed measurement and cutting arrangement. Inspect the actual pieces against that drawing, including the first and last accepted pieces after an order change. Reject a proposal that reaches its headline output only by excluding a regular product from the order book.

Do not adjust cutting controls or bypass guards to reproduce a sales demonstration. The trial belongs under the equipment supplier's approved operating procedure with the buyer observing and recording results.

## Use one comparison sheet for both offers

Fill the blanks from your order records, the trial and the two complete quotations. Retain the original units; do not mix elapsed minutes with running minutes or accepted pieces with total cut pieces.

If both systems finish the committed workload inside the available window, compare full cost and supportability before choosing flying cutting. If only the flying proposal passes, include the receiving equipment and staffing used in its successful trial in the purchase scope. Removing them after acceptance invalidates the output comparison.

## Put a value on the time that can actually be sold

Use the measured difference in accepted output to identify additional orders the business can fulfil. Do not multiply saved cutting seconds by every hour on the calendar. Coil changes, profile changes, inspection and dispatch still occupy the production period.

The commercial comparison is: additional contribution from deliverable orders, minus the extra recurring operating and support cost, against the incremental installed purchase cost. Enter actual quotations and your own accounting figures. If demand is absent, a higher possible output is capacity held in reserve, not a forecast sale.

For an existing line, include the retrofit interruption and all interface work. New controls, a moving assembly and guarding must be evaluated together by the integrator. Buying a standalone cutting unit without that complete offer leaves the most important installation work outside the quoted saving.

## Two mistakes that erase the purchase benefit

**Failure case: the short products arrive after acceptance.** A buyer tests only long roof sheets and then adds a regular short-panel order to the schedule. The proposed flying cycle cannot produce that order under the demonstrated conditions. Keep the shortest recurring product in the contractual trial mix; if a separate process is required, price it before choosing the system.

**Failure case: running speed is booked as saleable output.** A shop buys flying cutting using the former's uninterrupted speed, but the receiving crew stops the line repeatedly to clear panels. The planned extra dispatch never appears. Repeat the complete order trial with the actual receiving arrangement and count only accepted output reaching its collection point.
