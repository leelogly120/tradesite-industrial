Start with project-led orders if prospective customers need different chassis, profiles, power arrangements or working positions. Buy a demonstration unit when several credible buyers share a repeatable equipment package and you have a suitable demonstration location, a support plan and a credible resale route for that exact unit.

A demonstration machine is a sales asset with a specification. It is not a substitute for defining the market. The practical test is whether showing one configuration answers the main purchase questions of the buyers you can actually reach. If each serious enquiry needs a different arrangement, stocking early can lock money into the wrong example.

## First define what the demonstration must sell

Choose the decision a visitor should be able to make after the demonstration. For crawler-led buyers, demonstrate the proposed site-forming arrangement. For truck-led buyers, show the vehicle, setup position and forming assembly together. Write down the representative profile, material, installation context and equipment scope.

Then separate visible demonstration functions from order-specific engineering. A buyer can observe the arrangement and an agreed trial with stated material. That trial does not settle a different chassis, coil, roof geometry or destination requirement. Keep the test conditions with the resulting evidence so the demonstration remains useful after the visit.

## A stock-versus-order worksheet

Use actual qualified enquiries rather than general market interest. Fill the evidence column with named internal records; keep customer details private in published marketing material.

| Commercial question | Demo stock is stronger when… | Project-led orders are stronger when… | Distributor evidence to enter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer overlap | Several prospects need the same core package | Serious enquiries differ in major interfaces | Sales lead: enters common configuration and qualified enquiry references to justify one demonstration unit |
| Demonstration purpose | A defined trial can resolve recurring objections | Each decision needs a different site or material | Technical sales lead: names trial objective, profile and material so the demonstration answers recurring objections |
| Carrying cost | Storage, care and financing can be budgeted | Cash must remain available for project execution | Finance lead: enters monthly costs and holding period to budget capital tied up |
| Local support | Named people and agreed resources cover the unit | Support arrangements still need to be built | Service manager: names local roles and quoted supplier attendance/parts to fund the resale commitments |
| Resale route | The exact unit fits an identified buyer group | Qualified buyers require substantial configuration changes | Sales lead: records target resale configuration and required changes to test a credible exit route |
| Physical location | Delivery, demonstration and secure storage fit | Suitable space has not been secured | Site manager: measures delivery route, working area and storage dimensions to test the exact unit |

The answer need not be permanent. A distributor can begin with project-led sales, document recurring requirements and later stock a more representative unit. Equally, an existing demonstration unit should be reviewed when actual enquiries move away from its configuration.

## Compare the cash commitment without inventing a payback promise

Build the decision from quoted and local costs: purchase scope, transport, import handling, storage, demonstration preparation, insurance where applicable, maintenance responsibilities and expected resale preparation. Record financing and tax treatment using your own advisers and quotations.

Compare that commitment with the commercial benefit you expect from a physical unit: qualified demonstrations, shorter decision cycles or sales of the same package. Treat those benefits as assumptions until measured. A large number of visitors is not the same as demand for the stocked specification.

For project-led orders, include the cost of producing clear configuration proposals and arranging representative evidence. If a catalogue omits the required interface information, budget the technical proposal work needed to answer qualified buyers. The [importer supply-package guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-importer-distributor-supply-package/) helps separate equipment scope from the distributor's own local delivery and support responsibilities.

## Two expensive ways to buy the wrong first unit

**Failure case: one dramatic demonstration, little repeat demand.** A distributor chooses the most imposing configuration but most qualified enquiries need another transport arrangement and a different forming profile. Visitors remember the equipment, yet few can order that unit. Define the representative customer and trial first; buy against the recurring requirement, not the most impressive image.

**Failure case: the unit is sold with support promises that were never purchased.** The sales team offers local commissioning, training or spares availability without agreeing who supplies them. The margin comparison excludes the resources needed to deliver those promises. Put each obligation, responsible party and quoted inclusion into the resale package before taking an order.

These examples are hypothetical commercial risks, not reported customer outcomes. ARCLIFT's quotation must state its actual supply commitments; the distributor's local services belong in its own documented offer.

The US International Trade Administration's [export-potential guidance](https://www.trade.gov/how-analyze-product-or-services-export-potential) also treats a distributor's ability to service equipment as part of partner selection. Use that principle to assess your own resources before including support in the resale offer.

The transport image below adds another practical question: can the stocked unit reach the demonstration location and leave again for resale without an unplanned transport change? Include both movements when comparing the first-unit cost.
