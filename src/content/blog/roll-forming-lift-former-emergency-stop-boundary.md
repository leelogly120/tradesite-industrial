A statement that the lift and former have emergency stops does not explain the relationship between their separate control systems. If a forming-lift proposal claims coordinated stopping, ask which machines and interfaces the statement covers, who is responsible for that scope and what documentation supports the actual proposal. Do not infer a combined function from the presence of similar controls on adjacent machines.

This guide turns an ambiguous stop-control statement into a supplier-document question. It supplies no circuit design, safety level, stopping sequence or testing instructions. The [electrical interface guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-electrical-control-interfaces/) covers broader integration; this page isolates the boundary of a proposed cross-machine stop claim.

The <a href="https://www.iso.org/standard/59970.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ISO 13850 public abstract</a> concerns emergency-stop functional requirements and design principles. It provides context for requesting the responsible designer's configuration-specific explanation of a claimed relationship. It supplies no circuit, verification result or evidence that separate lift and former provisions constitute a coordinated function.

## Identify the machines behind the stop statement

### Preserve the supplier's actual wording

Copy the relevant statement into a private purchasing clarification with its quotation reference and issuer. Ask whether it describes individual machine provisions or an expressly proposed relationship between machines. Words such as coordinated, common or complete need an equipment boundary before the buyer can understand them.

Do not improve the wording on the supplier's behalf. Changing separate stop controls into integrated emergency stopping in a comparison spreadsheet introduces a claim the offer may never have made. The first useful action is accurately identifying what remains ambiguous.

### Separate the lift and former identities

Name the actual lift and forming machine, including the provider of each. A [crawler forming-lift arrangement](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) raises compatible forming equipment near the roof receiving level, but the shared task does not establish a common control architecture. The equipment identities determine whom the buyer must ask.

If the former is already owned, its actual records belong in the enquiry. If the seller proposes a new former, request the identity behind the combined statement. A generic reference to any former cannot describe a specific interface response.

### Include other equipment only where proposed

Feed equipment or another powered subsystem may belong in the purchase, but its relationship to a stop-control statement must be explicitly identified by the responsible parties. Do not add an assumed system connection merely because an item supplies material to the former.

The buyer's equipment list should distinguish included, buyer-supplied and optional items. That lets the provider explain its actual boundary without allowing an illustration of a complete line to extend the scope of a narrower quotation.

## Ask for a boundary description rather than a circuit

### Draw the commercial boundary as a question

Use labelled boxes for the lift, former and actual additional equipment. Identify the provider of each box. Between them, mark where the quotation claims a relationship and where it gives no answer. The sketch is a map of questions, not a functional design or a statement that any machines must share a particular stop arrangement.

Ask the suppliers to identify the applicable scope and responsible designer. This keeps the buyer focused on missing ownership while leaving the technical arrangement to the actual equipment and integration parties.

### Request documentation appropriate to the claim

Ask which configuration-specific records explain the proposed relationship and its boundaries. The issuer should identify the equipment covered, document purpose and remaining dependencies. A generic manual for one machine may be relevant to that machine while leaving the cross-machine statement unanswered.

Distinguish a proposed design deliverable from a completed verification record. A promise to provide information later is purchase scope, not evidence that the combined arrangement has already been assessed or verified. The buyer should retain the stated stage and conditions of each deliverable.

### Keep restart and other behaviour outside assumptions

When the buyer asks about a stopping statement, do not assume the answer also defines every related machine behaviour. Ask the responsible provider which applicable documents address the relevant conditions and limitations for the actual configuration. Do not write a sequence, predict a response or propose a practical test from this article.

The distinction matters because a short commercial phrase can appear to answer more than it says. A clear response identifies its scope; it does not invite the purchasing team to fill technical gaps by analogy with another machine.

## Compare individual provisions with a combined claim

### Work through a two-provider example

In a hypothetical project, the lift seller states emergency stop included and the former seller states the same. The buyer combines those lines into a complete-system compliance row. Neither seller has actually described the relationship between the machines. The spreadsheet therefore contains a broader conclusion than either source supports.

The buyer sends both actual equipment identities and asks who owns the proposed cross-machine question. One provider may offer a defined integration scope, or both may identify a separate responsibility. This example assumes neither the availability nor adequacy of any resulting arrangement.

### Use a claim-boundary comparison

| Statement or record | What it identifies | What remains unanswered | Supplier question |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Lift stop provision listed | A lift-side quotation item | Relationship with the former | Which actual equipment boundary does this statement cover? |
| Former stop provision listed | A former-side quotation item | Relationship with the lift | Is any combined scope included or excluded? |
| Coordinated system wording | A claimed relationship | Equipment identities and responsible party | Which configuration and integration scope are proposed? |
| Individual machine manual | Information for its stated machine | Uncovered interfaces | Which additional record addresses the claimed relationship? |
| Proposed verification document | Planned evidence deliverable | Actual result and later changes | What is its purpose, stage and configuration basis? |
| Buyer-owned feed equipment | A separate project item | Inclusion in the supplier statement | Which provider must answer this boundary? |

The table evaluates the scope of claims and documents, not stopping performance. It contains no required circuit, function category or test criterion. Its value is making the unanswered relationship visible before the buyer relies on a combined package description.

### Keep commercial completion distinct from technical acceptance

A supplier can answer that it includes a defined coordination deliverable while the actual engineering work remains outstanding. Record that as an included scope with a future deliverable, not a completed technical result. Likewise, a supplier can exclude integration without invalidating its narrower machine information.

The trade-off is how much coordination the offer actually undertakes and what remains for the buyer to arrange. Compare those boundaries before treating two prices as equivalent. No amount of documentation alone permits the buyer to declare an unspecified combined arrangement technically sufficient.

## Send the unresolved relationship to its actual owner

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Avoid using a button count as system evidence

A proposal may not fit a final combined-system decision when the claimed stop relationship has no identified equipment scope or responsible party. The number or colour of visible controls cannot resolve that omission. Photographs of machinery and coils do not show the relevant design or establish what any stop function affects.

This article cannot specify a required safety level, judge circuit adequacy, describe stopping behaviour or authorise testing. Those matters require the actual equipment, design and project responsibilities. The buyer's task here is to obtain an attributable answer about the scope being purchased.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Prepare the stop-boundary clarification

- Identify the actual lift, former and feed equipment, keeping buyer-owned and optional items separate from confirmed seller scope.
- Attach the precise control statement from each offer and ask whether it addresses one machine or an expressly defined relationship.
- Describe roof receiving geometry, outlet height and intended equipment arrangement as context for the providers' review.
- Include site access and ground or floor restrictions where they affect the proposed layout, without specifying control positions or methods.
- State destination and transport assembly boundaries so the combined claim is tied to the equipment actually delivered and assembled.
- Request the responsible integration party, applicable power and control records, exclusions and the stage of any promised documentation.

Keep the resulting replies beside the original statement. If a provider narrows the claim, update the purchasing comparison rather than retaining the broader phrase because it is easier to present internally.

## FAQ

#### Do separate emergency stops establish system integration?

Their presence identifies separate provisions, not the relationship between machines. Ask the actual providers which combined scope, if any, is proposed and which records address it. Do not rewrite individual equipment statements as a system-level conclusion.

#### Should the buyer specify how every machine stops?

This guide does not provide that design. The buyer can describe the equipment and request the responsible parties' response to a claimed interface. Technical behaviour, design requirements and verification belong to the actual equipment and integration review.

#### Can a demonstration video answer the boundary question?

It cannot replace the configuration-specific explanation and applicable records. A visible event does not identify every machine state, interface or design responsibility. Ask what equipment the record concerns and keep its evidence scope separate from the broader purchasing claim.

#### What if no supplier includes the combined scope?

Record the unassigned responsibility and seek a defined project response before relying on a complete-system statement. This does not establish that a particular technical solution is required or available. It identifies a purchase and coordination question that remains open.
