Reserve public street space for a roof-level former only when the authority's written occupation terms and the project's approved plan cover establishment, production, receiving work and clearance. Choose private-site production when the complete delivery route from that site can supply the roof more reliably at an acceptable cost.

## Price the period you need to control the street

The street bid starts before the first panel. List delivery of the equipment, agreed preparation, access controls, establishment, material deliveries, working periods, interruptions, dismantling, removal and any required reinstatement. Record which of these phases can share the same occupation and which need separate arrangements. A licence allowing equipment to stand on a section of carriageway does not by itself reserve a delivery queue, adjacent pavement or another contractor's unloading position. Map each resource and name the party obtaining it.

Local authorities determine the applicable permission. For example, Transport for London explains its highway licences for its own road network. Use the responsible authority and actual road classification for the project. Obtain the authority's applicant eligibility, permitted activities, reservation dates and extension terms for the proposed occupation. Engage the appropriate street-works and traffic professionals for the design and permission process. Include their priced scope, authority charges, conditions and reinstatement obligations in the street-side offer. [Transport for London highway licences](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/urban-planning-and-construction/our-land-and-infrastructure/highway-licences)

## Build a second bid from the private parcel to the same roof

Give the private-site supplier the same panel marks and installation dates. Locate the forming position, coil deliveries, panel exit, finished-sheet hold, vehicle access and the route to the building. Secure the landowner's actual terms and usable dates. If the parcel is nearby but separated by a street, the route still has a public interface. Identify permits and traffic management that survive in this alternative. Where forming occurs off site, add finished-panel delivery, a separately contracted roof handover and protection while the crew waits.

| Complete for both route plans | Accountable party | Effect on purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter named street authority or landowner, dates and permitted uses | Project manager | Exclude a location that cannot be reserved for the duty. |
| Mark equipment delivery, support positions, coil laydown and exit | Plant supplier and site team | Establish the actual occupation envelope. |
| Record access for residents, adjacent businesses and other trades | Logistics coordinator | Price interfaces and restricted working periods. |
| List panel marks, longest pieces and roof receiving points | Roof contractor | Test the complete private-site transfer or direct outlet. |
| Enter restoration, inspection, demobilization and closeout obligations | Commercial manager | Compare return of each occupied site. |
| Name the extension, relocation or replacement supply option | Buyer | Quantify the risk of losing the reserved period. |

Use the same weather and installation holds on both schedules. A pause on a public street can consume a paid day whether or not production resumes; it can also consume private rent or vehicle reservations on the other route. Ask what is genuinely refundable, what can move to another date and which people would still be present. Separate sunk charges from costs caused by an extension. This gives the buyer a specific decision when one roof area falls behind rather than a generic contingency percentage.

## Select the route that can finish and vacate

A street route can bring production close to several consecutive workfaces and remove finished-panel trips. It can also create a hard end to production if the road must reopen on a particular date. A private compound can buffer sequencing and support later runs but impose an extra material transfer for every panel. Count actual panels and permitted transport groups in each phase. Value closer production against the transport, receiving work or waiting it genuinely removes. Keep a line for public interfaces required by both bids.

**Mistake: Buying machine days against a shorter permission.** A buyer books a mobile former for the full roof campaign. The proposed street occupation ends after the production shift with no time available for collection and reinstatement. Obtain an accepted start-to-clear schedule or use the private production location; a longer plant hire alone cannot extend the street reservation.

**Mistake: Calling a nearby parcel a complete alternative.** A supplier offers a low rent for a private yard. The finished panels still need to cross an intervening route, and no party has priced suitable transport or the roof handover. Obtain a continuous transfer package with accepted panel lengths and dates before valuing the saving in street occupation.

Award the permitted street route for the released roof area only if all phases fit and the corresponding private alternative costs more or cannot serve the same handovers. For residual roof work after the occupation expires, request a separately dated price. This avoids committing the entire roof and machine to a finite window on the strength of the first uncomplicated bay.

## RFQ for two complete production locations

1. Attach the street plan, property boundary and responsible authority.
2. State the requested activities, working dates and clearance deadline.
3. Provide machine delivery, support, coil and exit envelopes.
4. List panel lengths, roof zones and receiving dates.
5. Mark all vehicle, pedestrian and adjacent-property interfaces.
6. Provide the private site's permission, footprint and transport route.
7. Request establishment, restoration and extension terms for both.
8. Name the party accepting the finished roof handover and site return.
