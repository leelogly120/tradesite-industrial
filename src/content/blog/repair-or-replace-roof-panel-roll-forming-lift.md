Compare a defined repair proposal when your existing roll-forming lift still fits the future work and competent assessment can establish its condition, repair scope and supported return to service. Shortlist replacement when the required work geometry or equipment interfaces have changed, or when an adequately supported repair cannot be established. Choose between those complete outcomes, not between a spare-part price and a new machine price. Suspected safety-critical defects require withdrawal from use until the competent assessment and required corrective work establish readiness for service.

An owner can face two different problems at once: deterioration in a machine and a business that now needs another production arrangement. A repair that restores the old duty does not resolve a changed production requirement. The forming-line photograph below makes another distinction visible: the production machine and the structure lifting it have separate functions ; assess each against its own condition and future duty.

![A blue and yellow roof-panel forming line with partial protective wrapping stands on a workshop floor.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261028-wrapped-line.webp)
*Separate the condition of the former from the condition and suitability of the lifting system before defining replacement scope.*

## Start with the next jobs, not the previous purchase

Write down the work the asset must perform after the proposed investment. Include the required outlet position, intended forming machine, material route, site access and transfer arrangements. Compare those requirements with the existing machine's issued documents. A similar model name or a remembered past job is a weak basis for a new duty.

If the future work fits, a condition assessment can support a repair-versus-replacement comparison. If the work no longer fits, keep repair and modification separate. Restoring the existing arrangement is different from changing its intended duty, interfaces or operating envelope. Any proposed modification needs its own competent engineering and approval route; a replacement component does not itself enlarge the machine's permitted use.

The UK HSE explains inspection as a way to identify deterioration and determine whether equipment can be used and maintained safely. It also distinguishes routine deterioration from events such as significant modifications or suspected serious damage. Those are useful assessment triggers; local requirements and the specific equipment documentation determine the applicable review. [HSE work-equipment inspection guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/inspection.htm)

## Compare documented outcomes in one table

| Decision area | Repair proposal must define | Replacement proposal must define | Owner entry / verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Future duty | Which existing duties remain supported after work | Which requested duties the offered configuration covers | Owner and site surveyor: enter support-surface-to-outlet geometry, former interface and next-job constraints |
| Condition and scope | Assessment findings, included work and unresolved items | New equipment scope and treatment of retained components | Competent assessor: issues condition findings and bounded repair scope; buyer lists retained/new items |
| Return to use | Inspection, testing and release responsibilities | Delivery, assembly, commissioning and acceptance scope | Owner: names assessment, commissioning and return-to-service parties and their deliverables |
| Availability | Repair schedule assumptions and parts dependencies | Purchase and readiness dependencies | Planner: enters provider-supported readiness windows and parts/supply dependencies |
| Ongoing support | Identified parts, documentation and responsible support | Included support scope for the offered configuration | Maintenance lead: records actual documentation, parts and service inclusions for both outcomes |
| Whole investment | Assessment, repair, movement, lost availability and follow-up | Purchase, integration, movement and transition costs | Estimator: totals assessment, equipment, integration, transport and lost availability over the same period |

Keep uncertainty visible. If photographs cannot establish condition, obtain a competent inspection before accepting a bounded repair scope. If the retained former lacks issued interface data, obtain those data before accepting a replacement-lift configuration. Neither should receive a fictional fixed total merely to complete the comparison.

## Three useful purchase scopes

**Retain the supported package and repair defined defects.** This is worth evaluating when the existing duty remains suitable and the assessment can establish a bounded repair. Include the evidence and responsibility needed before the equipment returns to work. A low initial parts bill is not the decision boundary.

**Replace one subsystem and retain a verified compatible subsystem.** Retain a serviceable former when the new lift proposal accepts its issued interfaces; retain a supported lift only when the proposed former is accepted in the reviewed configuration. Treat compatibility as a fresh integration scope. Supply the retained component's actual data and documents; do not assume matching paint, mounting appearance or model family resolves it.

**Replace the complete package.** This can be the clearer comparison when the future job requires a materially different arrangement or the existing package has too many unresolved interfaces. It still needs a scoped offer, including what happens to any existing equipment you intend to keep. ARCLIFT's new-equipment quotation should not be read as a promise to repair, upgrade or take back the old asset.

## Downtime belongs on both sides

For repair, list assessment, quotation, parts, work, inspection and the planned return to use. For replacement, list selection, confirmed supply, transfer, assembly and commissioning dependencies. Use current information from the responsible parties. Use provider-supported readiness windows. If a route lacks a credible completion window, obtain an actual temporary panel-supply proposal before relying on it for an awarded job.

Include the temporary production arrangement needed during either route. Reschedule only when the client accepts the revised dates; otherwise obtain a finished-panel supply offer covering the required window. Compare the resulting project cost and availability consequences consistently. A repair is not automatically faster; a replacement is not automatically ready for immediate use.

Transport is one easily missed cost on both sides. The trailer photograph below illustrates the carrier interface for assessment collection, workshop work or replacement delivery. Allocate each actual movement once in the relevant option.

## Two costly mistakes to avoid

**Failure 1 — repairing wear is treated as approval for a new duty.** A buyer replaces worn components and then plans to carry a different forming machine at a new outlet position. The repair scope addressed condition, not the changed configuration. Separate the future-duty review from the repair and obtain the applicable integration decision before committing to that use.

**Failure 2 — a provisional repair is compared with a complete replacement.** The repair price excludes assessment findings, transport and return-to-use checks, while the new offer includes an assembled package. The apparent saving disappears when those omissions surface. Align the included work and unresolved allowances before selecting an option.

### Attach these eight inputs to the RFQ

1. Existing equipment identification and its available issued technical documents.
2. Competent condition findings and the exact proposed repair or modification scope.
3. Required future work positions and representative site drawings.
4. The retained or proposed forming machine's data and interface information.
5. Panel profiles, material requirements and the expected production tasks.
6. Access, transport and available workshop or assembly information.
7. Required availability dates and the temporary production arrangement being considered.
8. Destination market and a clear list of equipment to retain, replace or exclude.
