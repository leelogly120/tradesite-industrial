Choose a freestanding loading-bay canopy when its foundations and column positions fit vehicle operations and an independent structure meets the weather-cover brief. Choose a building-attached extension when the engineer accepts its connections to the existing building and the complete junction and construction package suits the operation. Draw the truck movements and required sheltered area before selecting either roof. Where neither a workable column position nor an accepted building connection exists, revise the shelter design before buying either nominal roof area.

A loading canopy is bought to serve a working dock. The comparison must preserve loading clearance, doors, pedestrian routes, drainage and the site's actual operating periods. Compare roof area with the actual sheltered loading outline and usable vehicle clearances.

## Begin with the operation under the roof

Record the vehicles using each bay, their actual approach and manoeuvring envelopes, loading equipment and door operation. Have the logistics lead mark the areas that need cover during ordinary tasks. Include the location of stored goods and the route used by staff without turning a shelter into an assumed enclosed warehouse.

Obtain the client's weather-cover brief. Identify what protection is required and the conditions under which the bay operates. The building designer converts that brief into an eligible roof geometry and complete structure. Avoid promising that an open-sided roof excludes wind-driven rain from every position.

The low-position forming line below illustrates the metal-sheet production operation, which itself needs working space. Mark its location separately from live loading movements if site production is proposed. A dock with room for a completed shelter does not automatically have room for coil delivery and panel runout during construction.

*Keep production equipment, coils and panel receiving outside the loading operations retained in the construction plan.*

## Freestanding structure: test the ground and columns

An independent canopy still needs an engineered foundation and support arrangement. Obtain the actual column positions, foundation requirements and clearances from its designer. Coordinate buried services and the pavement work required to construct and reinstate each support location.

Choose this architecture where those positions fit the dock and the independent building arrangement satisfies the brief. Include the junction or gap to the existing building in the weathering design. Freestanding describes the structure; it does not answer how people and goods remain covered at the transition.

[Canopies UK's loading-bay range](https://www.canopiesuk.co.uk/products/loading-bay-canopies/) illustrates purpose-designed loading shelter systems and discusses site-specific calculations. It establishes a specialist building-product category. The new metal-roof design still needs its own accepted structure, geometry and cladding specification.

Price column protection or other traffic-related work where the accepted site design requires it. Have the facility's logistics owner assess the final arrangement with the actual vehicle fleet. A roof price that assumes an unusable column position is not a low-cost operational solution.

## Attached extension: buy the existing-building interface

Appoint the structural designer to assess the proposed extension connection and the existing construction receiving its loads. Obtain the accepted connection package before treating the wall or roof as available support. Include any investigation, strengthening and making good identified by that assessment in the attached option.

Coordinate the roof-to-building junction, drainage and movement details with the roof designer. [Lysaght's metal-flashing guidance](https://lysaght.com/support-technical/support/design/guide-metal-flashing) describes flashing roles at roof edges and intersections. For the actual extension, obtain the project-specific detail linking the old and new envelopes.

An attached route is appropriate where the complete accepted arrangement gives the useful covered operation the owner needs. Price temporary access, any roof opening or disturbance and the work restrictions required by the contractor. A shorter list of new columns does not by itself establish lower installed cost.

## Programme the shelter around the dock

Separate ground work, structural installation, roof laying, junction completion and handback. The operator identifies when each bay can close. The contractor identifies the resources and work areas needed for each phase. Include temporary loading arrangements in the project price where required.

For site-made panels, compare the production window with these releases. A short canopy project with irregular profiles can favour bought finished sheets. Own production earns consideration when the contractor's repeat accepted panel demand supports it, rather than because a canopy happens to have a metal roof.

The exposed roof framing below shows why structural and sheet schedules need separate release points. Order the roof sheets against the issued structure and final junction geometry. Keep unfinished site dimensions attached to a named measurement milestone.

![Profiled roof sheets overlook uncovered steel framing and construction work below.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-roof-framing.webp)
*Release sheet lengths from the accepted shelter structure and its final building transition.*

**Mistake (hypothetical): the freestanding columns occupy the turning route.** A canopy quote matches the sheltered loading area, but its rear column sits in the regular articulated-truck turn between the yard and the dock. Revise the engineered support arrangement or choose the eligible attached option before comparing the final operational packages.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the attached price assumes available wall support.** A buyer awards an extension before the existing structure is assessed. Required connection and strengthening work is outside the price. Complete that assessment and include its work before selecting the attached route.

## Eight RFQ attachments

1. Loading operations, vehicles and required cover outline.
2. Surveyed dock, door and traffic plans.
3. Accepted freestanding and attached structural alternatives.
4. Existing-building and buried-service information.
5. Roof junction, drainage and movement details.
6. Metal profile, material, lengths and quantities.
7. Closure windows, production location and receiving routes.
8. Destination, power and complete supply responsibilities.
