Build an internal indoor-drone inspection capability when the facility can resource qualified personnel, an accepted sensor-and-report workflow and enough real recurring work to justify its complete cost. Contract accepted survey deliverables when demand is intermittent or the business cannot maintain that complete capability. In this comparison both options already use an accepted drone inspection method. Choose who operates and delivers it, then budget the physical testing or repair tasks outside that method separately.

A hardware price is only one part of an internal inspection operation. The business also needs people, a defined work programme, usable records and the support arrangements stated in its actual supplier offers. When both complete models meet the accepted output and required timing, choose the lower resourced programme cost over the same comparison period.

The deck underside photograph shows the separate crew workface for physical maintenance. Retain those tasks in both business models so a drone investment is not credited with removing repair access that remains necessary.

## Define the operating capability being purchased

[Flyability's insourcing and outsourcing discussion](https://www.flyability.com/webinar/indoor-drone-inspections) explicitly distinguishes building an inspection team from hiring inspection professionals. The practical decision goes beyond owning an aircraft. A facility needs an accepted method and a delivery organisation, whether internal or contracted.

[The manufacturer's Elios 3 information](https://www.flyability.com/elios-3) distinguishes visual inspection, surveying and additional payload options, including ultrasonic thickness measurement. Specify the actual configuration and method in both alternatives. Do not compare an internal imagery-only purchase with a specialist offer that includes a different accepted test or report.

Have the facility's responsible inspection team issue one output specification. Identify assets, required evidence, location references, reporting format and the person who accepts the result. The internal team and the contractor then price the same inspection work. The separate decision between remote survey and crew inspection is already addressed in the [camera-survey versus platform guide](/blog/industrial-ceiling-camera-survey-vs-platform-inspection/).

## Count work that the facility will actually commission

Build an asset-and-inspection calendar from approved maintenance needs. Separate recurring accepted surveys from speculative work that has no budget, operating window or decision owner. Use the actual scope to obtain specialist prices and to estimate the internal team's workload.

Group comparable tasks by method and location. A second site adds useful demand only when the offered equipment, qualified team and travel arrangement can serve it. Price travel and lost availability instead of treating every building in a corporate estate as free utilisation for one internal asset.

Where the internal team requires specialist help for some methods, retain that contracted scope in the ownership model. Where the survey contractor excludes follow-up visits or a particular reporting requirement, price those additions in the service model. Both totals must end at the same accepted output.

## Use complete cost per accepted survey programme

For the internal model, obtain actual offers for the required equipment and payloads, software or reporting tools, training and support. Add the facility's own personnel, supervision, planning, data review, storage, travel and equipment-care costs. Include the backup arrangement that the facility needs for its required availability.

For the contracted model, obtain a scope-based price for the same asset calendar and deliverables. Record mobilisation, site visits, reporting, data delivery, exclusions and the terms for revised scope. Ask the contractor to state the availability it actually offers; do not assume immediate attendance from a generic service description.

Compare the complete programmes over one stated period. Use the buyer's actual finance assumptions and supplier terms. No universal number of inspections or payback period establishes the answer. If the internal model is cheaper but has no resourced qualified team, it is an incomplete offer and should not receive the award.

## Capability ownership also needs a handover owner

For an internal purchase, name who accepts the equipment-and-workflow handover and who accepts each inspection record. Buying training does not itself establish the facility's complete technical competence or authority for every proposed task. The responsible inspection and operations teams define and resource those requirements before the investment is approved.

For contracted surveys, keep the same technical acceptance owner inside the facility. State the data formats and delivery boundary so the maintenance team can use the findings after the supplier leaves. Align asset IDs with the existing maintenance register and specify how follow-up work orders refer to the survey output.

The raised deck below illustrates the later physical workface. Where an accepted survey identifies cleaning, testing or repair outside its scope, the maintenance plan still needs an actual crew-access proposal. Give the access supplier the marked positions and floor-route information supplied by the building team.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the aircraft becomes the entire internal budget.** A facility compares a hardware quote with a full year of accepted survey services but omits staff, reporting and support. Add the complete resourced internal programme before selecting ownership.

**Mistake (hypothetical): speculative sites create the payback.** An investment forecast counts surveys at other buildings without approved tasks or a deliverable travel schedule. Retain only commissioned workload, price actual travel and compare that programme with the specialist contract.
