Fund a continuous strip entry only when recurring orders allow adjacent coils to be joined, the line can consume the stored strip during entry work, and the downstream operation can sell the extra good panels. Retain planned stops between coils when grade, finish, profile, traceability or order changes already interrupt the runs.

## Find the changes the investment can actually remove

Take a completed production period that includes the normal variety of work. Mark every coil end in sequence and record the next job actually released. Distinguish time spent getting the next coil onto entry equipment from time spent changing the profile, cleaning a contact surface, clearing the outlet or waiting for a cut list. A continuous-entry package addresses a particular interruption. Minutes attributable to other work remain in the comparison unless the vendor's proposed scope covers that work too.

*The guide surfaces sit ahead of the forming rolls. On the proposed layout, distinguish the strip entry, joining equipment, storage and the first forming stand.*

A strip joiner connects successive lengths and an accumulator stores strip between the entry and the process it feeds. Metform describes this relationship in its roll-forming equipment FAQ. Treat the entry machinery as a complete coordinated system. Obtain the supplier's written route for the strip joint through the proposed process, including its identification, removal and disposition. A roofing customer can require intact single-coil pieces or coil-specific identification. Decide which panels around the joint would be segregated and how the shop would know when the following order can resume. ## Rebuild the business case from adjacent coil pairs

Pair the last piece of one coil with the first required piece from the next. A repeat colour on the sales list does not establish that the two coils can follow each other on the actual production plan. Finish batch restrictions, width changes and minimum usable lengths can break the sequence. Where rescheduling would make a pair eligible, also enter the customer delivery dates and the extra stock that would be required. Give that opportunity its own scenario.

Request a timed demonstration or an agreed model based on representative strip and planned production. It should start with an operating process, include a change at entry, follow the joint to its disposition and finish with traceable acceptable pieces at the outlet. Agree how the supplier will report a change that takes longer than the strip available for that production period. If the quoted storage cannot bridge that change, ask the integrator to state the required stop, scrap disposition and restart scope. Include the agreed outcome in the production calculation rather than counting every offered change as uninterrupted.

## Convert recovered time into a purchase decision

Place a value on recovered operating time only once. If the shop has unfilled orders and the outlet has room, use the contribution from additional accepted orders after their variable costs. If the same orders will be made during shorter shifts, use the hours of expense that can actually be removed. Show separately the floor area, foundations, electrical integration, supporting equipment, commissioning time, training, joint tracking and maintenance included with each bid. Price a straightforward planned-stop entry with its own realistic coil-change crew as the comparison.

**Mistake: Counting a profile change as a recoverable coil stop.** A shop records a long stop at the end of a dark coil. The following order uses a different profile with a tooling change that takes the crew through the same window. Buying a strip store leaves the profile change on the schedule. Reclassify overlapping minutes and use only removable entry time in the capital request.

**Mistake: Good strip reaches an occupied exit.** A continuous entry produces more strip while a finished-pack area waits for a collection. The sales estimate values the extra strip as dispatched panels. Rebuild the plan with the measured exit and dispatch windows and compare shorter working hours, a different packing plan or planned coil stops.

For a roof-level mobile operation, add the cost of moving and integrating the complete offered entry arrangement at each project. A continuous factory system requires its own layout and acceptance. For an intermittent site campaign it can be cheaper to order coils and cut lists around separate released zones. Use the same quantity of finished roofing and the same delivery dates in both bids.

## RFQ schedule for eight production inputs

1. Send representative consecutive coil and order sequences.
2. Attach the strip and finish specifications for each run family.
3. State the customer's traceability and joint disposition rules.
4. Provide actual entry stops separated from other line interruptions.
5. Give the allowed entry footprint and material delivery route.
6. List required output, cut lengths and downstream receiving periods.
7. Request an integrated test and agreed acceptance measures.
8. Ask for installed costs, project moves and ongoing support by package.
