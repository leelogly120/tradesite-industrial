Keep sheet stock and a guillotine when a varied, modest flashing order mix is served economically by purchased sheets and workable nesting. Buy a coil-fed cut-to-length cell when recurring compatible demand supports its complete stock, handling and changeover commitment while producing blanks the folder accepts. Compare cost per complete accepted order, including residual stock and delivery dates.

The roof-panel and flashing business is already chosen. The investment here prepares discrete flat blanks for folding. It therefore differs from opening a flat-sheet sales business or buying a slitting line to supply recoiled strip to a roll former. Keep the existing downstream folding requirement fixed while comparing the two preparation routes.

## Build a stock-to-order ledger

Take a representative period of committed flashing orders. Group blank requirements by material specification, finish, thickness and width, then retain their required dates. Annual demand can conceal small urgent lots in many finishes. Keep those lots dated: they determine the stock bought and the actual changes a coil cell must make.

For the sheet route, obtain actual sheet sizes and purchasing terms, then prepare a nesting plan. Identify the usable residual pieces and the orders that can consume them. For the coil route, obtain the offered coil sizes and purchasing terms and map each batch onto the intended stock. Keep unused balances visible as funded inventory. Charge the current order for material it consumes, then record the remaining stock and its funding separately so neither route receives a hidden credit.

A coil balance has practical value when the business can identify future compatible work, store it properly and retain its material identity. A short sheet offcut needs the same discipline. Price an uncertain future use conservatively in the comparison rather than granting one route a full credit for hypothetical recovery.

The physical route matters alongside stock price. Include receiving, moving stock to the machine, loading, collecting blanks and delivering identified batches to folding. The buyer should know which stands, supports, lifting resources and storage positions belong to each offer. A compact cutter price can exclude much of the work needed to use it.

Use the same dimensional reference edges in cutting and folding records. If the folder locates from one edge while the cutting trial measures from another, an attractive length report can fail to predict the resulting flashing. Record the measuring method, reference edges and agreed condition of the blank during inspection with each result. The cutting and folding quality owners must use the same released acceptance basis.

## Make acceptance and inventory change the calculation

Trial a recurring batch and a smaller changeover batch on each actual route. Observe setup, stock changes, cutting, collection and folder entry. Include startup and end-of-stock pieces in the material record. Have the quality owner separate accepted blanks, reworkable pieces and scrap against the agreed drawing and finish criteria.

A nominal cutting rate leaves out the time between different finishes and the disposition of a coil remainder. Likewise, a guillotine's cutting cycle leaves out sheet collection, nesting and repeated handling. Convert the trial into a complete order comparison using the actual production calendar and available people.

Build the cost from purchased material consumed, handling and preparation work, rejected output, continuing machine resources and the complete installed equipment investment. Retain inventory as a separate funding and storage commitment. This makes it possible to see whether lower material cost actually pays for the coil route, rather than hiding the stock balance in an average cost per blank.

Choose the sheet route when finish diversity and short lots make its flexible stock allocation more valuable than a coil cell's demonstrated gains. Choose the coil route when repeat demand consumes its input efficiently and the complete accepted-order result supports the investment. If the difference depends entirely on forecast orders, show those orders as a separate scenario beside the committed base case.

## Two hypothetical blank-preparation mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): counting a coil discount without funding the balance.** A workshop compares coil price with sheet price for one short flashing order, then buys several finishes whose remaining stock has no scheduled use. Repeat the comparison with the cash, space and identified later consumption of each balance visible.

**Mistake (hypothetical): accepting the cut count instead of folder-ready blanks.** A trial reports the requested quantity, but bowed pieces or inconsistent reference edges create rejects during folding. Set the folder-entry criteria first and count accepted blanks after the agreed check. Include the rejected material and additional work in both bids.

## Eight attachments for the preparation-cell RFQ

Send the same package to the actual sheet-cutting and coil-cell candidates:

1. **Demand schedule:** dated blank quantities grouped by specification and finish.
2. **Released drawings:** blank dimensions, reference edges and downstream folding requirements.
3. **Stock offers:** actual sheet and coil sizes, purchasing terms and available finishes.
4. **Material ledger:** nesting plans, residual stock and documented later uses.
5. **Movement layout:** receiving, storage, loading, collection and folder transfer positions.
6. **Acceptance basis:** flatness, squareness, edge and visible-face criteria with an owner.
7. **Complete cell scope:** machine modules, supports, controls and destination work.
8. **Trial record:** representative orders, elapsed time, crew and accepted output.
