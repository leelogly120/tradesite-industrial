Buy tooling for a water-barrier roof panel only when the designer's specified exposure and accepted assembly evidence cover the exact product your line will make. Choose a water-shedding panel package for roofs designed to drain under that system's conditions. Select on the roof's duty before comparing seam styles or forming machinery.

## Ask the designer what the roof assembly must do

Two roofs can show a similar raised seam while relying on different water-management principles. A hydrostatic or water-barrier design addresses the conditions stated for its particular assembly. A water-shedding roof relies on the designed drainage and details that direct water away. Ask the project designer for the applicable classification, drainage design, roof support, underlayment and interface details. A manufacturer's description of a water-barrier product is not approval for arbitrary ponding, a blocked outlet or another building's roof geometry.

The Metal Construction Association's roofing installation manual explains the distinction between water-shedding and water-barrier metal roof systems. Use the current system manufacturer's own documentation and the project's design to establish the exact admissible assembly. Do not transfer generic slope or exposure figures to a specific order. A mechanical closure, snap feature or applied sealant is a component or process detail. It cannot by itself define performance of the complete roof with its terminations, supports, accessories and installation. ## Qualify the roof order, then qualify the former

Build an order schedule that names the assembly and the evidence recipient. For each project identify the approved panel, material, support arrangement, underlayment where specified, joint treatment, ends, eaves, penetrations and drainage interfaces. Ask the system owner which elements can be produced by a local fabricator and which must come from an authorized source. A proposed line can be suitable for one released profile but leave closures, clips, sealant application, joining and installation to separate suppliers. Price the entire roof and the retained manufacturing work separately.

Ask bidders to work through a difficult zone, such as a change of roof plane or an area near a drainage interface. Record how the released assembly continues through it and which manufacturer detail has been accepted. A preferred panel shape in a simple field area can create costly special work where drainage paths change. Conversely a factory-built accessory can preserve a regular panel production schedule if it is included in the accepted assembly. Do not use an installation manual for one system as evidence that an alternate profile qualifies.

## Turn roof classification into a defensible investment forecast

Separate signed projects requiring a particular water-barrier system, signed projects accepting a stated water-shedding system and speculative projects where substitution remains open. For each signed group list the real panel metres or area after excluding other suppliers' products and special details. Deduct trial work, component obligations and commissioning costs once, then compare the contribution from actually eligible orders with the machine investment. Avoid counting every metal-roof enquiry as demand for a single seam simply because two brochures use the same terminology.

**Mistake: A mechanical seam is substituted for accepted system evidence.** A bidder proposes a mechanically closed profile for a water-barrier roof, assuming the closure method qualifies it. The drawings require a specific assembly and evidence that the offered producer cannot provide. Obtain written system eligibility or retain the specified supply. Remove the unaccepted quantity from the new tooling case.

**Mistake: A water-shedding panel is priced on the wrong drainage layout.** A contractor copies a unit price from an earlier clear roof field. The new building has transitions that change the designer's water-management requirements and the accepted details. Reprice the actual assembly by zone and identify who supplies the special pieces before making a machine commitment.

Award the panel package that the building designer and system owner accept for the specified roof duty. If an alternate assembly would require a design revision, show it as a separate conditional bid with its own evidence and adoption cost. Choose tooling after that commercial decision. This keeps a line capable of making attractive metal sections from being purchased against roofing orders the business has no accepted route to supply.

## Eight RFQ inputs for a system-qualified panel offer

1. Attach the designer's water-management duty and roof plan.
2. Give the exact system, evidence documents and accepted revision.
3. List panel geometry, material, support and underlayment requirements.
4. Mark drainage transitions, penetrations and edge details.
5. Name the system owner, authorized producer and installation party.
6. Identify retained forming work and separately supplied accessories.
7. Provide committed quantities, approved alternatives and release dates.
8. Request complete assembly pricing and a separate machine offer.
