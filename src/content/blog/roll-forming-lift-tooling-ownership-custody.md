An invoice line called tooling does not tell a buyer which physical items it receives, which records accompany them or what happens when it needs another order later. A provider may propose to deliver an identified set, retain it at its premises or supply only a product made using it. The buyer needs an item-by-item custody and agreed-record matrix before treating those offers as the same purchase.

The [line-specification guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-specification-long-span-roof-panels/) defines the intended product and installed assembly. This page asks about the commercial information surrounding the identified tooling. Discuss the [forming-equipment scope](/products/#roll-forming-machines) separately from these negotiated terms. Nothing here declares ownership, intellectual-property rights or standard supplier conditions.

## Identify the physical items and information being discussed

### Name the tool set rather than the invoice heading

Request an identity for the tooling or other physical items the actual offer addresses. Connect it to the intended profile, material and proposed equipment. If the provider's price concerns use of tooling rather than delivery of the set, ask that distinction to be stated. The buyer should be able to recognise which object the commercial term concerns without assuming that every item used to make a panel becomes part of the purchase. An unqualified word tooling is too broad to establish either physical scope or an agreed future relationship.

### List the records separately from the metal items

Ask which drawings, identification records, maintenance information or other documents the provider proposes to deliver for the purchased scope. These are questions about actual offered records, not a claim that any particular document is routinely included. Keep the physical item list and the information list separate. A buyer can receive an item without obtaining every associated design record; it can also receive useful identification information without being granted unrestricted use of a design. The actual terms and qualified advice must settle the rights involved, rather than this article.

The <a href="https://www.wipo.int/en/web/intangible-assets" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">World Intellectual Property Organization's explanation of intangible assets</a> distinguishes intellectual-property rights and related information assets from physical assets such as machinery. That distinction helps frame separate questions about the tool set and its associated records. It does not determine ownership, a licence or the effect of any payment in the buyer's actual agreement.

### Distinguish a tooling purchase from a panel order

If the buyer only orders finished panels, identify whether the commercial offer actually mentions a particular tool set at all. Do not carry assumptions from a machine purchase into a material-supply contract. Conversely, if an equipment order includes an identified set, ask which assemblies and records are delivered with it. The underlying roof product can be the same while the purchased asset and information differ. This makes the custody discussion relevant to the actual transaction rather than a general claim that paying for development automatically determines every later right.

## Separate custody from agreed future use

### Ask where the identified items will be held

Request the proposed delivery or custody arrangement for each item. If the provider retains a tool set, ask which identity and records let the buyer refer to it in a later enquiry. If it is delivered, ask how the actual scope identifies what arrives. Custody tells the buyer where an object is proposed to be held; it does not, by itself, answer ownership, permission to reproduce drawings or the ability to move the set into another machine. Keep those questions separate so a clear shipping statement does not appear to settle unrelated terms.

### Define the next-order question explicitly

A buyer expecting repeat orders should ask how the actual offer addresses future access to the identified provision. Name the intended relationship: another panel order, additional equipment or a request to discuss a different provider. Ask the contracting parties to explain what is proposed and excluded. Do not assume future capacity, pricing, availability or transferability from today's offer. A practical commercial record can identify the question and the provider's response without turning an editorial guide into contract advice or declaring what a buyer is automatically entitled to do.

### Ask about a changed provider without assuming transferability

If continuity with another provider matters, state that concern before commitment. Ask what physical items and agreed records would be available under the actual terms, and which further technical review a different equipment arrangement would require. Do not present possession as proof of compatibility with another former. A tool set, its supporting records and any proposed rights are different subjects. The buyer may need qualified commercial or legal advice for the actual agreement, while equipment specialists address the technical use; neither question should be silently answered by the other's document.

## Compare delivered tooling with provider-held provision

### Work through two hypothetical purchase boundaries

Imagine Offer A identifies a tool set proposed for delivery with a former, while Offer B describes provider-held tooling used to supply a defined product. These hypothetical routes could address the same panel requirement while buying different assets and services. A may give the buyer possession of identified equipment but still needs its actual documentation and terms. B may leave physical custody elsewhere and needs a clear repeat-order relationship. Neither route automatically grants design rights or continuity. Compare the deliverables and actual negotiated response rather than treating the word tooling as a common commercial unit.

### Use the custody and record matrix

| Question | Delivered-set proposal | Provider-held proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What physical object is identified? | List the supplied set and associated items | Identify the provision the offer actually addresses |
| Where is it held? | State the delivery boundary | State the proposed custodian and reference |
| Which records are delivered? | Name agreed records and exclusions | Name information available to the buyer |
| What is the next-order relationship? | Ask about future support or equipment requests | Ask about repeat-product enquiries |
| What rights are addressed? | Request actual negotiated terms | Request actual negotiated terms separately from custody |
| What remains technical? | Review the actual installed assembly | Do not assume transfer to another machine |

The trade-off is between different purchases and retained dependencies. A physically delivered set is not automatically the more complete information package; a provider-held arrangement is not automatically inadequate when its actual scope matches the buyer's need.

### Keep supporting evidence tied to the actual agreement

A catalogue photograph may illustrate tooling context but does not identify the object in a contract. Ask the provider to connect the agreed item list and records to the actual commercial offer. If correspondence changes a deliverable, obtain a clear incorporated response through the parties' agreed process. The buyer should be able to tell which terms were proposed and which remain unanswered. This is a documentation question, not a declaration about enforceability. Do not fill a missing answer with presumed industry practice, especially where the transaction, parties or destination are still being selected.

## Ask the commercial questions before commitment

### Send six connected custody questions

- Identify the tooling, profile, material and equipment to which the proposed terms apply.
- Separate physical delivery, provider custody and any finished-product supply within the actual purchase.
- List the records requested and ask the provider to state the records actually included and excluded.
- Describe future-use and repeat-order needs as questions for actual negotiated terms, without assuming rights.
- Give the intended outlet height, roof zone and site access context for the separate machine-interface discussion.
- Include destination documentation, transport boundary, ground and power assumptions where equipment is supplied.

Use this package to obtain a specific reply, not a generic statement that the buyer owns its tooling. The physical items, records and permissions should remain individually identifiable throughout the discussion.

### Keep unresolved terms visible in the comparison

Do not mark a commercial route complete merely because its equipment price is clear. If custody, delivered records or next-order access matters to the buyer, keep each unanswered question beside that price. Request clarification from the contracting parties and qualified advisers where appropriate. The [equipment-price guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-price-quotation-cost-scope/) helps compare different supply boundaries without inventing monetary allowances. Here the added value is the precise tooling-related dependency: what the buyer receives now and what it expects to request later must refer to the same identified provision.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise a custody arrangement that may not fit

An arrangement may not fit when the buyer needs identified physical delivery but the offer describes provider-held provision, or when important future-use questions remain outside the proposed terms. This guide declares no ownership rule, licence, exclusive right, storage obligation or standard transfer condition. It also cannot establish that tooling fits another machine. Ask the actual parties to resolve their agreement and the responsible specialists to address technical interfaces. A photograph, payment heading or earlier project assumption cannot settle those separate questions.

## FAQ

#### Does paying for tooling establish every associated right?

No conclusion about rights follows from this article. Ask the actual contracting parties to address identified items and records in their negotiated terms. Seek qualified advice for the agreement rather than relying on a general equipment description.

#### Is custody the same as ownership?

Keep those questions separate. A statement about where an item is held does not answer all proposed rights or obligations. Request a clear response about each subject relevant to the actual purchase.

#### Can delivered tooling be used with another former?

Possession does not establish technical compatibility or permission. The actual terms and equipment review need their own answers. Identify the intended future need without assuming that transfer is possible or included.

#### What should a repeat-order buyer clarify?

Ask what identified provision the next order would refer to, which records remain available and what the actual supplier offers. Do not assume future pricing, capacity or continued availability from today's transaction.
