A ceiling task can lie above the underside of a deep beam while the proposed platform sits below it. A general ceiling-height figure then hides the real question: **does the actual proposed arrangement address the recessed fixing face, rather than merely the open space beneath the structure?** A broad deck is relevant to ceiling work, but its width does not establish access around a beam or the position of people and materials.

This guide prepares a vertical task section and compares two workface descriptions. The [overhead-clearance survey](/blog/ceiling-platform-overhead-clearance-survey/) records the broader building volume. The narrower output here distinguishes below-beam space from the recessed task face without assuming a personnel reach, clearance allowance or special platform motion.

## Identify the face hidden by the beam-depth shorthand

### Locate the actual fixing or installation face

Ask the task owner to mark the surface where the work must occur, tied to the building grid and relevant section. A note saying ceiling works can describe a face beneath a beam, beside it or recessed above its underside. Those tasks can need different equipment responses even when the roof elevation is unchanged. Name the face and the intended installation component. The buyer's input is the actual task location, not an assumed body position or an equipment setting selected from the general building height.

### Show the beam as more than a low line

Record the relevant structural surfaces and their extent at the task location. A single beam-underside elevation does not describe the space alongside it or the recessed area beyond. If project drawings and observed conditions differ, retain the discrepancy for the responsible survey or structural party. Do not infer an accessible passage from an apparently open gap in a photograph. The section should make clear which surfaces are known and which geometry remains unavailable for the proposed task comparison.

### Separate task material from the building surface

Identify the actual panel, trim, fixture or other component involved, with its project-provided geometry and proposed task relationship. Material can occupy space distinct from the platform outline. The [task-load guide](/blog/aerial-platform-worker-tool-material-load-planning/) develops quantities and locations for load review; this page concerns their spatial relationship to the recessed face. Do not invent a way of turning or lifting the component around the beam. Ask the task owner and equipment reviewer to describe the actual proposed arrangement for assessment.

## Connect structure, task and proposed deck separately

### Tie floor and workface references together

Give the local floor reference, the relevant beam surfaces and the task face against a shared project datum. Ask the supplier to identify the proposed deck reference and configuration on its own actual information. Platform height, task-face elevation and personnel working-height language are different descriptions. Do not add a standard human-reach allowance to convert one into another. The responsible parties need the actual task and equipment arrangement to assess the relationship, while the buyer keeps the measured or required points identifiable.

### Request the actual position rather than a generic deck outline

The [crawler ceiling-platform family](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms) describes a broad work-deck architecture for ceiling and wall tasks. It does not establish the envelope of a selected configuration. Ask for the actual proposed deck, surrounding equipment and task relationship. An outer deck outline can sit below a beam while the recessed fixing face remains unanswered. Likewise, an optional extension should not be assumed to create access around structure. The applicable equipment information and task review must address what is genuinely proposed.

### Keep people and overhead structure in the same question

The task arrangement must account for the actual people, tools and materials as well as the machine. Great Britain's <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/mewp.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE personnel-platform guidance</a> discusses selection and hazards around fixed overhead structure. This provides context for asking about the complete task relationship, without classifying this configuration or supplying clearances. It does not establish a permissible person position, a protective function or a method of reaching a recessed face from below a beam.

## Compare below-beam and recessed-face requests

### Keep the hypothetical building section unchanged

Consider a **hypothetical ceiling project** with a beam deeper than the adjacent ceiling fixing zone. Request A says that the deck must be below the beam underside and gives a general ceiling elevation. Request B marks the same beam but identifies a fixing strip recessed above its underside, together with the component involved. Neither request establishes an approved task. B reveals the actual workface question that A's below-beam description leaves out. A supplier could answer A accurately while still not addressing the intended recessed installation task.

### Ask which task the proposed arrangement answers

If a bidder returns only a deck-height figure, ask whether its actual arrangement has been considered against the named recessed face and task objects. Do not interpret the figure as a promise that people can reach around the beam. The trade-off is between a simple low-point description and a more demanding but accurate task question. A clarified response may identify missing geometry or suggest that another access concept needs investigation. That is useful before purchase, even when it does not deliver the buyer's initially preferred platform choice.

### Preserve vertical depth separately from plan coverage

Several beam bays can create another problem: interrupted coverage across the plan. Keep that separate from this vertical section. A larger deck footprint cannot by itself answer the recessed-face relationship, while a response to one recessed face cannot prove coverage of all bays. The [work-zone layout guide](/blog/large-deck-crawler-platform-work-zone-layout/) develops the broader layout. The table below keeps the vertical comparison focused on the actual face, equipment and task references rather than an assumed reach dimension.

## Send a vertical workface brief

### Make the section answerable without choosing the method

Send the task owner's located section with the actual component requirement and available floor information. Ask the equipment provider to identify what its proposed configuration addresses and what remains with the project. A useful response can distinguish an equipment-information gap from an undefined task. It should not transform the buyer's drawing into a work authorization. The objective is to make the hidden recessed face visible early, before the quotation is judged only by platform height or the apparent width of the deck.

- Locate the actual ceiling fixing face and identify the roof or building section reference.
- Show beam surfaces, their extent and the local floor datum with measurement sources.
- Keep platform height and task elevation separate from any personnel working-height description.
- State the task-material requirement and the proposed component geometry supplied by its responsible party.
- Identify access, destination and transport boundary for the actual candidate platform.
- Ask for the configuration response to the recessed face, with missing site or equipment inputs marked.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognize a proposal that addresses only the clear space

A proposal may not fit the real task when its response concerns only passage below the beam, an unlabelled deck height or an assumed person-reach allowance. A broader platform or an extension is not an automatic remedy. This guide supplies no permissible clearance, task position, personnel count or operation around overhead structure. The actual equipment, task and floor/support conditions require responsible review. If another method is proposed, its geometry and scope need assessment in their own right rather than being inferred from the original section.

## FAQ

#### Is the beam underside the required platform height?

Not automatically. It is one structural surface, while the deck and task face have their own references. Ask the supplier to identify the actual proposed arrangement against the located section. Do not derive a deck setting or a person-reach allowance from the beam depth. The task may concern a surface that the below-beam measurement does not describe.

#### Does a broad deck solve access to a recessed face?

It describes a form of work area, not the complete spatial relationship around the structure. The actual deck, people, tools and component must be considered against the named face and beam surfaces. A wide outline beneath the beam does not establish access above or beside it, and this article does not authorize a reaching method or configuration change.

#### Can one nominal ceiling height represent every face?

It can identify a general building reference but may omit the local recess and beam relationship. Use the section at the actual task location and preserve any changes between faces. A supplier response to a general elevation should not be treated as coverage of a specific recessed installation task that was never shown in the enquiry.

#### How does this differ from a full clearance survey?

The full survey records the surrounding building volume, obstructions and relevant states. This brief isolates the vertical task face hidden by beam-depth shorthand. It uses that survey information to ask whether the actual proposed arrangement addresses the named face. Plan coverage, routes, floor support and the eventual work method remain separate inputs and decisions.
