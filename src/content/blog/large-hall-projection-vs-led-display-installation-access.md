**Choose projection when the accepted image and room-lighting design works with a protected, unobstructed optical path and a suitable image surface. Choose direct-view LED when its design delivers the required viewing result in the adopted lighting state and its supporting structure and service space fit the venue.** Have the display designer release the room-based alternatives, then compare complete installed and maintainable systems. Buy ceiling and wall access for their actual work positions instead of treating screen area as the equipment requirement.

This is a new-display project. It precedes decisions about servicing or replacing an existing LED wall. The owner can still choose the room arrangement, display location and service route. Use that opportunity to compare the complete construction and operating interfaces before selecting hardware.

For LED, locate the actual support and cabinet/service positions. Those tasks need distinct sections even when they present the same image to the same audience.

## Establish the image the room must deliver

The owner supplies content types, audience positions, operating hours and the required room uses. The display specialist defines qualifying image size, resolution, brightness and other performance using the project's own criteria. Keep the intended lighting states, daylight conditions and obstructions in that design input.

[Christie's projection-versus-LED discussion](https://www.christiedigital.com/spotlight/projection-vs-led/) presents both as active technology routes for different applications. Its [display-wall planning whitepaper](https://www.christiedigital.com/globalassets/help-center/whitepapers/documents/christie-top-considerations-when-choosing-display-walls-whitepaper.pdf) includes maintenance among purchase considerations. Both are planning sources; qualify the submitted design against the actual hall before ranking its installed total. Require each actual provider to demonstrate its proposed result against the same room brief.

Where the venue needs a particular viewing or lighting condition that one proposal cannot satisfy, remove that proposal before comparing construction costs. A lower hardware price is irrelevant when it serves a different accepted image or requires a room operating mode the owner will not adopt.

## Projection purchases a path as well as a device

Obtain the chosen projector, lens, image surface and mounting arrangement as a coordinated design. The provider identifies the required optical path and service geometry. Show them with moving equipment, suspended services and future venue installations on the accepted room drawings.

Assign any room or lighting changes needed by the proposal to actual contractors. If an owner must alter blinds, lighting or the display surface to achieve the accepted result, those costs belong in the projection alternative. Do not move them outside the comparison merely because another supplier delivers them.

Identify the installer and maintenance positions for the selected equipment. A projector located above an occupied floor creates an access task different from work at a wall-mounted image surface. The platform budget needs both if the accepted scope retains them.

## LED purchases support and service space

Obtain the exact display assembly and its structural, power, control and service requirements. Identify who accepts the supporting building interface and who handles the actual cabinets or components. Keep front-side and rear-side tasks as located positions; the visible image face is not the whole installation.

The selected product provider supplies its removal and replacement requirements. A design that uses a rear service corridor needs that usable corridor in the completed building. A front-service arrangement needs its actual workface and component route. Neither description allows the buyer to delete personnel or material resources without reading the actual proposal.

## Choose access from the retained installation work

A broad platform serves sustained adjacent technician positions where its documented configuration fits the building. Dispersed projector points or a restricted rear-display corridor need their own appropriate access comparison. Request explicit coverage of those locations rather than assuming the same machine completes every task in the hall.

The raised-hall photo below shows the broad deck with its base and supports. Its footprint is part of the room possession. Coordinate it with the separate display-handling equipment, storage and audience-area closure required by the accepted installation plan.

Give the access provider the crew, tools and permitted objects for each work phase. The equipment proposal must state its actual geometry, task limits and support cases. Do not assign cabinet lifting or projector suspension to a personnel deck through a general description such as complete AV installation.

## Compare through the first usable venue handback

Reconcile installation, control integration, image adjustment, acceptance and reinstatement. Identify which completion tasks need elevated access and when they occur. Use the accepted venue handback as the common completion boundary for both installed systems.

For the adopted service plan, use the selected provider's actual requirements and the venue's supported access windows. Include clearing and reinstating the occupied space where required. Keep unsupported life, energy or maintenance forecasts outside the ranked total; real service provisions still belong in the purchase even without a lifetime-cost prediction.

## Two hypothetical new-display mistakes

**Image size replaces optical-path design:** A buyer orders projection hardware for a large image while an operating crane and later suspended services cross the intended path. The hardware list never represented the completed venue. Resolve the coordinated room design before selecting installation access and equipment.

**Display cabinets arrive without a service route:** Another project selects LED by image and price while its actual rear-service requirement has no usable space behind the wall. Obtain the product-specific service geometry and an accepted room arrangement before awarding the complete system.
