**Choose a separately roofed in-plant room when the accepted brief requires a defined enclosure within the host building and its own complete roof, wall and service package. Choose full-height partitions when the required division uses the host building's envelope and the accepted design specifies connections to it.** Buy high-level access for the tasks that remain in that chosen arrangement. A low internal room does not justify a large elevated platform merely because the surrounding factory is tall.

The room's purpose comes first: an office, a controlled process enclosure or a division between operating areas creates different requirements. Compare designs accepted for the same purpose. A partial internal enclosure and a full-height separation are only commercial alternatives when the responsible design team accepts both.

## Buy an enclosure, not a wall area

A freestanding room package has its own enclosure boundary. Identify the supplied walls, supporting members, roof or ceiling, doors and service provisions. Define any interfaces with the host building and the work required to make the room operational.

[National Partitions' modular in-plant office range](https://www.nationalpartitions.com/store/modular-in-plant-offices) illustrates packages with different wall configurations and included roof and ceiling components. This is a real purchasing category: a room system with multiple components, rather than a quotation for loose wall panels.

For full-height partitions, identify the existing building elements that complete the enclosure. Obtain the issued top, base, end and penetration details. The partition supplier, building designer and relevant service specialists should allocate their respective interfaces.

The comparison must retain the required environmental, acoustic, fire and occupancy outcomes defined by the project team. Where a candidate arrangement fails that accepted brief, remove it from the purchasing shortlist. Its smaller installation bill would buy a different result.

## Establish what the room roof is for

The internal room's top can perform several specified functions within its actual design. Ask the provider to identify the included roof and ceiling assembly and any separately accepted use above it. Keep those uses in the room specification and structural review.

For access procurement, list where installers must stand and which face they must reach. Work below an internal ceiling differs from work above it or at the room's perimeter. A completed room top is not a general equipment parking location. Use the designated supporting floor and the equipment supplier's reviewed configuration.

Full-height partitions create a different task pattern. The installation can extend through the host building's upper connections and around existing services. Record those located tasks and the remaining clearance after the partition closes. The equipment route needs to work through the actual sequence, including removal.

## Compare work locations before fleet size

Prepare a task map for each accepted enclosure option. Separate work at ordinary floor level, work requiring elevated personnel access and work requiring specialist material presentation. Use the same finished-room or separated-area endpoint.

A separately roofed low enclosure can leave little high work. In that case, procure the access method suited to those actual positions and exclude a large-deck purchase from the room's capital case. The surrounding factory volume is irrelevant to a task that remains below it.

A full-height installation can create repeated adjacent work at the upper wall and roof interfaces. A reviewed broad-deck configuration belongs on the shortlist where those positions, the support surface and the routes suit it. Isolated connections or narrow spaces need their own access arrangement.

The following record should be completed before inviting equipment quotations. It turns the enclosure decision into a located work package.

## Keep fitout and later factory use in the same decision

The factory layout after installation matters to both enclosure options. Mark the machinery, aisles, racks and operating spaces around the new room or partition. Identify the access required by the system provider's maintenance and replacement tasks.

A freestanding room can create a separate perimeter and top to maintain. A full-height division can close routes previously used to reach the building's services. Price the actual future access route where the facility retains those duties.

If relocation is part of the business plan, ask the selected provider to define the supported dismantling, reuse and reconstruction scope for that actual system. Include the associated access and service reconnection work in the commercial comparison. A modular label alone should not determine the relocation allowance.

## Two hypothetical enclosure mistakes

**Hypothetical failure case: factory height becomes the equipment requirement.** A buyer orders a high-access package for a low internal office without identifying any retained task near the host roof. Map the room's actual installation faces and buy the smaller eligible access scope established by that map.

**Hypothetical failure case: a wall quote is compared with an operational room.** Another buyer ranks full-height partition panels against a room package that includes its own top and services. The partition offer omits host-building interfaces and later service attendance. Complete both scopes to the same accepted operational endpoint before deciding.

Attach eight inputs to the RFQ:

1. The accepted room or partition design and intended operational use.
2. The enclosure boundary and actual supplied components.
3. Located wall, ceiling and connection tasks retained on site.
4. Task elevations and offsets from the actual supporting-floor datum.
5. Component records and separately appointed material handling.
6. Routes, floor information, obstructions and enclosure-closing sequence.
7. Service installation, completion and future maintenance tasks.
8. Access dates, destination, removal route and acceptance owners.
