Use rear-ramp transport when the exact platform/carrier combination passes its loading-transition, deck-support and road-route assessment and both sites preserve the rear approach. Choose a front-loading lowbed when its offered deck and entry geometry resolve a clearance or transition constraint and both sites accommodate the detached neck and tractor movement. When both qualify, choose the lower complete delivery-and-collection cost. Shortlist ARC-F25 when the projects need shared ceiling or wall workspace; carry its offered configuration into the issued transport-state record. Assess both loading-area plans before awarding the machinery and transport package.

This decision fixes the committed personnel-platform fleet and the owned-versus-hired carrier policy. It selects a physical transport arrangement within that policy. A haulier can offer either architecture, and an owner can compare either asset; changing the operator's commercial model is a separate investment question.

The low-deck workshop photograph below shows why the machine itself needs a defined state. The deck, base and support assemblies occupy different parts of the envelope. Request the supplier's prepared-state drawing and item list for the arrangement that will actually travel.

## Draw both loading footprints at both ends

Start with a scaled origin and destination plan. Mark the arrival route, standing vehicle, loading space, receiving area and path taken by the unloaded machine. Include the construction or operating activities that occupy those areas on the movement date. A clear loading zone at the seller's yard does not create equivalent space at an occupied factory gate.

For rear-ramp transport, show the actual rear approach and the machine's entry or exit direction. Reserve the carrier provider's required space for its offered ramp arrangement and loading operation. Retain the same information for collection, when stored materials or new construction can occupy the former delivery area.

For front loading, show the actual detachable-neck arrangement, tractor movement, holding and reconnection space described by the provider. A front access corridor and a rear access corridor use different parts of the site. Select the arrangement that can be delivered at both endpoints, not the one with the easier demonstration at origin.

[Faymonville's construction transport overview](https://www.faymonville.com/activity/construction/) explicitly connects removable-gooseneck lowbeds with front loading. It also distinguishes those vehicles from semi-lowloader families. Use that physical distinction to obtain two real provider proposals; do not infer a universal loading height or payload from the category name.

## Three eligibility questions travel with the machine

**Can it enter and leave the offered carrier?** Obtain the machine provider's transport preparation, underside geometry, ground-clearance information and applicable loading limitations. The competent loading planner assesses those records with the actual carrier transition. Enough nominal deck length and payload do not establish that the machine can cross that transition.

**Can the carrier support and carry the identified configuration?** List fitted items, removed options and companion packages. Obtain the documented machine mass, contact/support information and other data the carrier engineer requires. The transport provider assesses its deck, loading distribution and axle arrangement for that specific cargo. A removed part outside the main machine's record still needs its own receiving and carriage scope.

**Can the loaded combination complete the route?** Use the provider's actual loaded envelope, axle/distribution assessment and turning arrangement with the road and gate survey. Record loaded height from the road surface with the prepared machine and its support arrangement included; bare trailer deck height is a different input. Assign applicable road permissions and route acceptance to the competent local transport parties. A low deck can solve one height constraint while the selected combination still fails another route or site requirement.

[Nooteboom's MCO(S) description](https://www.nooteboom.com/trailers/multitrailer/mcos-semi-low-loader/) identifies semi-lowloader applications including aerial work platforms, with different ramp and gooseneck configurations. Those are examples of offered transport equipment. Obtain the actual selected vehicle and its assessment of the ordered machine, rather than assigning another manufacturer's brochure dimensions to the fleet.

A lower quoted deck height is valuable when it removes an actual route constraint in the reviewed combination. It is not an independent reason to buy a trailer whose loading footprint cannot be reserved. Conversely, retain a rear-ramp route that passes all assessments and completes the movements at the lower cost; another loading architecture needs an evidenced benefit to justify its total.

## Keep the working platform useful after unloading

The personnel-access duty remains the reason for purchasing the machine. Select the proposed deck and working arrangement from the actual task heights, crew, tools and workface geometry. Then carry that identified configuration into transport preparation. A convenient trailer should not quietly change the work platform into an arrangement that cannot complete the awarded task.

The raised yard photograph illustrates the working arrangement, not the cargo state being priced. The supplier-issued transport drawing must identify what is lowered, retained, removed or separately packed for the selected machine. Reconcile that drawing with the destination's receipt and any required assembly before booking carriage.

## Complete movements reveal the commercial difference

Price the actual carrier, competent loading/unloading attendance, site occupation, road requirements, waiting and collection on the same dates and scope. Include the resources associated with each actual loading architecture. Keep common machinery purchase and ownership costs separate where they are unchanged.

[IPAF's transport guidance](https://www.ipaf.org/en-us/resource-library/safe-loading-unloading-transportation-mewps) calls for compatible vehicles, planned loading/unloading and trained, familiarised personnel. Put those responsibilities into the service or owned-operation budget. The comparison is a procurement decision; the appointed parties retain the equipment-specific operating and securing methods.

## Two hypothetical transport-selection mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): payload passes but the transition does not.** A fleet reserves a rear-ramp carrier using mass and overall length. The issued underside arrangement conflicts with its proposed ramp/deck transition. Obtain the prepared-state records first and secure the competent planner's acceptance of the complete transition, support and carrier arrangement before the collection date.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the lowbed fits the road but cannot unload.** Front loading solves a loaded-height constraint, but the destination has no reserved neck/tractor separation space and its receiving lane is occupied. Protect the full front-loading footprint at both ends or reprice another separately accepted carrier arrangement.

## Eight RFQ attachments for machine-and-carrier matching

1. Personnel-platform workface brief with actual task heights, crew/tools and required shared-deck duty.
2. Exact machine configuration and the intended fitted, removed and separately dispatched items.
3. Issued prepared-state dimensions, mass, clearance and support/contact information.
4. Identified rear-ramp and front-loading carrier proposals with competent assessment owners.
5. Origin/destination plans showing rear approach and neck/tractor movement spaces.
6. Route, gate, turning and overhead restrictions plus the haulier's combination assessment scope.
7. Delivery/collection dates, companion cargo and unloading, receiving and assembly responsibilities.
8. Destination power and storage/support, working-configuration records, prepared-state drawings and final machine/transport handover documents.
