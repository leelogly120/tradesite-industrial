The panel is not finished when it leaves the shear. Someone has to support it, point it, and decide who owns it before a fixer puts a screw in it. A roll-forming lift can put the former near the roof. The handover zone is the piece of the job that is often missing from both the lift quote and the former quote.

## Follow one panel across four boundaries

### Shear to first support
The strip is flexible. The first support is a roller table, a person, or nothing. Write which. If it is a person, say so honestly. That choice belongs in the method, and it may be the reason to change the outlet.

### Support to the roof edge
Direction, the side the coating must not drag on, and the place the panel is allowed to rest. [The long-panel guide](/blog/roof-level-roll-forming-long-panels/) is the selection context. This page is the receiving context. Use both if the panel is long enough to scare the crew.

### Edge to the fixing crew
Who takes the panel, with what words, and what they do if it arrives twisted. A radio plan is part of the zone. A lift catalog class is not.

### Fixing crew to the finished bay
Where offcuts go, and who is allowed to walk under the outlet while a panel is moving. Draw the exclusion. [ARC-T25](/products/arc-t25-truck-mounted-roll-forming-lift/) or [ARC-C25](/products/arc-c25-crawler-roll-forming-lift/) can be the machine, but the zone is still yours to describe.

## Choose direct receipt or a pause

### Direct
The panel runs from the former toward the roof and is taken by the fixing crew. You need the outlet aimed at that crew, a support for the middle of the panel, and a stop if the crew is not ready. This is the arrangement people imagine. It needs the most alignment and the clearest stop rule.

### Pause
The panel is cut, rested, and moved again by a second crew. You need a place to rest it that will not crease the coating, and a second handling method you are willing to own. The lift might be simpler. The roof crew might not be. Write the pause if that is the truth.

### Wind at the handover, not only at the former
A former can be happy while a long panel acts like a sail. Name who looks at the wind and who can shout stop. Do not copy a number from another site.

### The middle of the panel
Long panels fail in the middle, where nobody planned a support. Mark a rest point that is not a person's shoulder. Say whether that rest is a table you already own or something the quotation must include. If you do not know the length yet, send the longest and shortest bay you might build and keep the support as an open line. The [line specification guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-specification-long-span-roof-panels/) is where the length schedule belongs. This page only needs to know that the middle has an owner.

### Exclusion under the outlet
Draw where people may not stand while a panel is moving. Include the path a forklift would use if you pause panels on the ground. A crowded edge is the usual reason an elevated outlet looks good on a section and works badly on a Monday. If the public can walk there, say so in the first line of the RFQ.

## The trade-off is a short carry against a crowded edge

Putting the former at the eave shortens the carry and crowds the edge with the machine, the run-out, and the crew. Leaving the former on the ground clears the edge and lengthens the carry. The interface constraint is the same: the panel needs a support and an owner at every step. Photographs of a sheet beside a building are reference only. They are not your zone.

### Write the handoff as three names
Name the person at the shear, the person at the edge, and the person who can stop both. If those are the same crew, say that too. A zone with no names becomes a shout. Add the signal you will actually use: radio, horn, or eye contact, and what happens when the signal fails. This is still not a method statement signed by anyone. It is the minimum the quotation needs so the run-out is not drawn toward an empty roof.

### Coating and the face that must stay clean
Say which face of the panel may touch a support and which face may not. A rest table in the wrong place marks the weather side. If you do not know the coating rule yet, write "unknown, architect to confirm" and do not let a supplier assume either face is sacrificial. The same note belongs with the profile drawing. Repeating it on the zone sketch stops the lift quote and the line quote from disagreeing.

### Night, rain, and the half-finished bay
Say whether panels may be left on the rest overnight and who covers them. A pause method that looked cheap in the afternoon can mark a whole bay if the cover was nobody's job. You do not need a weather number from this website. You need the sentence "panels are not left out" or "panels may rest until the next shift, covered by the roof contractor." Put that sentence in the RFQ so the machine layout does not assume a clear table every morning. Add who removes the cover and who checks the face before the panel is fixed. Those two names are small, and they stop the zone from being redrawn on site by whoever happens to be holding the sheet.

### The first panel of the day
The first panel is the rehearsal. Say who watches it, how long the crew is allowed to stop, and what gets written down before the second panel starts. A zone that only works after everyone has learned it by surprise is not a zone. It is a hope. Put the rehearsal in the RFQ so the run-out length and the rest table are sized for the slow pass, not only for the fast one you wish you had. If the first panel is scrap by plan, say that too, so nobody treats the scrap path as the production path.

### Offcuts, oil, and the walkway
Name where offcuts land and who clears them before the next panel. A pile under the outlet becomes the exclusion you forgot to draw. If the former sheds oil or swarf, say which way that waste falls and whether the finished face can pass through it. The lift quotation will not invent a waste plan. The zone sketch can, in one sentence, and that sentence is worth more than a second photograph of the building.

### When the bay changes direction
A hip, a step, or a direction change is a new handover, not a footnote. Mark it on the sketch and say whether the former moves, the crew moves, or the panel is paused and turned. If you do not know yet, write unknown next to that bay. An unknown bay is easier to quote than a bay you described as if it were the same as the first. The catalog model still only tells you the machine family. The sketch tells the reviewer where the panel changes hands.

## The elevated handover may not fit

It may not fit when nobody will own the panel after the shear, when the edge is a public way you cannot close, or when the only support is a person you are not willing to put there. It may not fit when the specification demands a splice the crew did not plan. Change the panel schedule or change the outlet. Do not hope the zone will appear on delivery day.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
## Zone inputs

- Work height and the outlet the package must serve
- Roof geometry the module will stand beside
- Profile and former mass if a former travels in the same shipment
- Wind is a site issue, not a container issue, but name the site anyway
- Ground at destination after the box is opened
- Access from the port or yard to the chassis builder
- Outrigger spread you hope the site can take, or unknown
- Destination country and the port of arrival
- Chassis included, local, or unsure
- Container assumption stated as a question, not a result
- Voltage the destination will actually provide
- Documents the packing plan must match later

## Send the zone with the machine enquiry

Send the work height, the site, the destination, the transport question, the work zone, and the floor or ground at arrival. The signed schedule is project-specific. Editorial images do not prove a container result, a price, or a lead time. Attach the sketch to [request a quote](/contact/?request=quote) and name the lift family you circled. The <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-at-height/index.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE work-at-height guidance</a> is a Great Britain reference for the people who will stand at that edge. It does not draw your zone.

### Questions from the fixing crew

#### Is the run-out included with the lift?
Only if the quotation says so. Ask for it as its own line.

#### Can the fixing crew take panels straight from the shear?
Only if you have described the support, the stop rule, and the exclusion. The catalog page does not make that method safe.

#### Which machine page should I open?
The truck or crawler forming-lift page that matches how you move between pads. The zone sketch is the same attachment either way.

#### What if we must rest every panel?
Say that. The pause is a real method. Hiding it makes the quote look shorter and the site look surprised.
