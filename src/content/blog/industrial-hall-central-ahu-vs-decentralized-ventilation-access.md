Choose distributed supply-and-extract units when the accepted hall design permits direct air distribution, main duct routes conflict with operations, and roof interfaces and service access fit. Choose central air handling when the required zoning or treatment calls for that arrangement and its plant and distribution space are available. When both qualify, award the lower complete installation, operation and maintenance total for the same duty and period. Start by separating fresh-air ventilation from recirculation-only heating or cooling.

The purchase is a new ventilation system for an industrial hall. Fix the required occupied zones, air-quality outcome, operating periods and heat-recovery brief before comparing hardware. Exclude an offer that leaves an air path unpurchased or serves only part of that brief. Price the accepted system through operating handback, then its stated maintenance duties over the owner's chosen period.

## Fresh air is the first dividing line

A fan moving air within the room and an installation supplying outdoor air and extracting air perform different functions. A distributed proposal needs its actual supply, extract and recirculation roles stated by unit. The same discipline applies to a central proposal: trace its specified outdoor-air intake, treatment, distribution and extract route through the complete offered system.

[Hoval's industrial-hall example](https://www.hoval.com/en/solution/lagerhalle-logistikzentrum-interne) distinguishes its RoofVent supply/extract units from TopVent recirculation equipment. It describes direct air distribution from units positioned in the roof or at ceiling level. These are real architecture categories; the actual HVAC designer must accept the product combination for your hall.

Keep process extraction and fire-related systems separately identified in the coordinated brief. A general hall-ventilation offer should receive credit only for the duties it actually includes. Buying more recirculation equipment is not a remedy for an unpurchased outdoor-air requirement.

The red deck below stands beneath repeated roof members. Mark the actual connection and installation positions on your building section, with heights measured from its finished-floor datum. Map the proposed ventilation work onto the actual building rather than assigning one access allowance to every roof-mounted item.

## Central plant buys a distribution network

A central AHU proposal concentrates air handling in its nominated plant location and distributes air through the specified network. The quoted package needs the plant, building interfaces, ducts, supports, terminal devices, controls and required completion work. Place every separately purchased element beside the main unit price.

[Swegon's AHU guide](https://www.swegon.com/references-and-insights/technical-guides/air-handling-units-and-ventilation/) discusses plant placement, technical-room space and the larger ducts associated with centralized arrangements. The purchasing consequence is concrete: reserve and price the actual plant and distribution route on the coordinated building model. A usable plant room with an obstructed main duct path is an incomplete central option.

Central distribution deserves the order when it supplies the accepted zone and treatment requirements and its full installed case wins. Obtain the actual controls and commissioning boundary alongside the mechanical supply. A unit delivered to a roof or technical room is an earlier endpoint than an accepted operating system.

## Distributed units buy repeated building interfaces

Direct-distribution hall units replace the proposed main hall duct network with a population of located units. Price their actual roof or ceiling positions, supports, openings, weathering, energy connections and controls. Count the interfaces in the issued system layout rather than assuming that eliminating a main duct eliminates installation work.

[Hoval's hall-system brochure](https://cdn.hoval.com/indoor-climate-systems-en-hybris-original.pdf) identifies distributed ventilation equipment and distinct recirculation units. Its described direct-distribution architecture is useful where large ducts compete with the hall's working space. It does not make every distributed unit suitable for every air-treatment requirement.

Compare the repeated-unit proposal against the same operating brief as the central system. Where zones have different needs, have the designer identify which units serve each accepted duty. Keep the actual product and control groups visible in the price. A count of roof boxes hides differences between supply/extract equipment and additional recirculation resources.

## Complete the system-to-service purchase record

Enter proposal and drawing references so excluded work has a named owner and amount. Use the same accepted operating outcome and ownership period in both totals, including each actual proposal's energy case under that operating schedule.

| Input and reference datum | Fillable buyer entry | Responsible owner | Decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted ventilation duty | Zone, occupancy and operation brief revision ___ | Owner and HVAC designer | Establishes comparable system output |
| Central air path | Unit, plant and distribution drawing references ___ | Central-system provider | Prices the complete network |
| Distributed equipment | Located supply/extract and recirculation unit IDs ___ | Distributed-system provider | Separates actual functions and quantities |
| Roof/building interfaces | Opening, support and weathering detail revisions ___ | Roof and structural designers | Prices repeated construction work |
| Spatial fit | Plant and duct routes or direct-distribution layout ___ | Building coordinator | Admits a physically deliverable architecture |
| System completion | Controls, connections, checks and handback owner ___ | HVAC integrator | Closes the commissioned-system boundary |
| Service access | Task coordinates; height from finished floor; removal objects; routes ___ | OEM service and facility leads | Separates internal personnel tasks from exterior/lifting work |
| Complete ownership case | Installed, energy, care and interruption amounts; start/end dates ___ | Buyer | Awards the lower complete eligible total |

An excluded control connection or roof closure is still a project cost. Add the actual complementary quotation before comparing totals. Keep construction occupation, specialist component handling and personnel access identifiable; this lets the buyer see which architectural difference creates each expense.

## Buy maintenance access for the occupied hall

Ask the actual equipment provider to locate its service faces and replacement objects. Distinguish work performed from the roof from work retained inside the hall. An interior platform allowance should cover the latter tasks, while exterior access and lifting have their own providers and acceptance.

The construction photograph shows open framing, loose ground and a distant raised deck. The finished hall introduces a different operating layout. Mark future racks, machines and occupied zones on the service-access plan before using a construction-stage route to justify equipment ownership.

![An unfinished steel hall contains rough ground, a mobile machine and a distant raised red deck.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261031-hall-construction.webp)
*Price installation access and future service access against their respective building stages.*

Obtain the provider's actual service tasks, workface positions and required people, tools and materials. Price a retained access asset or a defined future service arrangement against those same internal duties. Do not assume that having more units automatically increases every service cost, or that one central unit makes all service work accessible from one position.

## Two hypothetical purchases with missing ventilation work

**Mistake (hypothetical): recirculation wins an outdoor-air comparison.** A warehouse buyer prices room-air heaters over the packing zone against a central offer supplying its required outdoor air and extract. The cheaper bid leaves that duty unpurchased. Rebuild the distributed schedule with accepted supply/extract equipment before comparing installed totals.

**Mistake (hypothetical): the AHU price becomes the system price.** A delivered central unit appears cheaper than an operating distributed scheme, but its quotation stops at the plant-room delivery point. Main ducts, supports and commissioning have no funded owner. Add those purchases, plant works and access occupation before comparing accepted operating handback.

## Eight attachments for the retained access RFQ

1. Selected ventilation layout with retained internal access tasks marked.
2. Workface heights from finished-floor datum and task coordinates.
3. People, tools and materials listed for each personnel task.
4. Entry width/height, turning space and route-obstruction dimensions.
5. Floor/support records and proposed operating-position drawings.
6. Roof-exterior and component-lifting work allocated to separate providers.
7. Installation/service dates, occupied-zone restrictions and available site-power records.
8. Required delivery configuration, destination and task-acceptance record.
