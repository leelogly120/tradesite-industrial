Ask whether the hydraulic power unit is on the lift, on the former, or not in this purchase. One pack does not automatically serve both machines. Screen the height class while that question is open, and freeze nothing about oil, duty, or who owns the hoses. ## Name which machine owns the power pack

### Split the lift from the former

Write two names: the lift you are screening, and the former if you already have one. Ask which of those names the hydraulic pack belongs to. ARC-T18 is the 18.5m truck screen. ARC-T25 and ARC-C25 are 25m screens, truck or crawler. ARC-RF8 is the forming machine, with a catalog sheet class of 0.3–1.0mm, not a statement about your coil and not a hydraulic schematic. If the offer uses one phrase for the whole "line," make the supplier split it before you compare prices in your own sheet.

### Say included, excluded, or unknown

Those three words are enough. Included means a line on this quotation. Excluded means you must buy it elsewhere, and you should say from whom if you know. Unknown means confirm on RFQ. A brochure that shows a power pack beside a similar machine is not included. An anonymous buyer once counted the pack twice, once inside the former price and once as a lift option, and still did not own the hoses between them.

### Ask what the pack is claimed to run

If a pack is included, ask which motions it is offered for: lift functions, former functions, or a list. Do not invent pressures or flow. This page has no duty numbers. The useful sentence is the supplier's list of functions, or a blank that says the list was not given. A pack that runs the lift and is silent about the former is a lift pack. Treat it that way until a drawing says otherwise.

### Keep sheet class away from oil

The 0.3–1.0mm band is a catalog sheet class for ARC-RF8. It does not size a pump. Your coil thickness is whatever you write. If you do not know it, write unknown. Mixing sheet class into the hydraulic row makes a technical-looking cell that answers neither question.

## Compare pack location on one offer

### One row per machine

| Machine on the offer | Where the pack sits | Hoses and who supplies them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lift only | On the lift, or unknown | Confirm on RFQ |
| Former only | On the former, or unknown | Do not copy the lift answer |
| Both named | One pack or two, as written | The gap between them is a scope line |
| Neither pack mentioned | Not in this purchase | Leave it blank rather than assuming |

### Look for the hose as its own line

Machines can each have a pack and still need a hose, a coupling, or a shared valve that nobody sold. Ask if any connection between lift and former is in the offer. If the answer is no, the functions stay separate. That is a clean scope. Pretending the oil is shared because the machines stand together is how a site discovers two plugs and no pipe.

### Match the pack to the setup floor

A pack on the ground needs a place that is not the coil path and not the outrigger pad. A pack on the machine moves with the machine and still needs protection from the weather you already described for cabinets. Write the location in words. Measure the clear space on site if the pack is separate. The catalog height does not reserve that space.

### Do not read the coil photo as a circuit

![Coils and forming equipment at the side of a steel building](/images/equipment/coil-real-cover.webp)

Coils in a photograph are material waiting. They are not evidence of a hydraulic layout. Use the offer's words. If the offer has no words, the cell stays unknown.

<!-- audit-section: evidence-tradeoffs -->
The trade-off is one shared pack against two simpler scopes. A shared pack can reduce hardware only when someone names the functions and the hoses. The limitation is that most early offers name neither. The constraint is the former you may not have chosen yet: its power demand is unknown, so a lift pack cannot be declared sufficient. The interface is the quotation line that says which machine owns the pack. Without that line, stop comparing duty and compare scope.

An anonymous roof-panel order showed the gap in one email chain. The lift seller wrote "hydraulic power included." The former seller wrote "standard hydraulics included." Neither email said the two phrases were the same pack, and neither listed the hoses between the machines. The buyer treated the overlap as a saving and later found two power units on two quotations and no line that connected them. The fix was not a new height class. The fix was three cells: pack on the lift, pack on the former, pack not in this purchase. Both sellers had to tick one cell. Until they did, duty, oil, and hose length stayed confirm on RFQ.

A second anonymous file tried to size the lift pack from a former that was still a blank. Someone had copied a power figure from a different project because the sheet looked serious. The copied figure then drove a request to "confirm the pack is enough." There was nothing to confirm. The former had no name, no feed side, and no duty. Screening ARC-T18 or ARC-T25 against the outlet height was still valid. Freezing a shared pack was not. The note that should have gone out said: height class to compare, pack owner unknown, former not selected, do not reuse another project's power number.

Weather belongs in the same note when the pack sits on the ground. Rain, wash-down, and dust are site facts. They do not become a pack specification by being omitted. If you do not know whether the pack is indoors, write unknown next to location. A photograph of coils beside the building does not locate the pack, and it does not say whose quotation pays for it.

## Where a shared hydraulic story may not fit

### Two prices, no owner

This shortlist may not fit when both the lift seller and the former seller assume the other one brought the pack. You then own a gap. Ask each seller the included / excluded / unknown question in writing. If both say excluded, the pack is a third purchase, not a detail.

### A former that is not chosen

It may not fit when you want the lift pack to be "enough for the former" and the former is still a blank. Mass, feed side, and power are confirm on RFQ until the former has a name. Screen 18.5m or 25m anyway. Do not freeze the pack.

### Weather on a ground pack

It may not fit when the only place for a separate pack is the wash-down lane or the open eave. The same exposure note you would write for a control cabinet applies. A pack is not happier in the rain because it is hydraulic. Say the exposure or mark it unknown.

### Sheet class copied into the power cell

It may not fit when 0.3–1.0mm is typed as if it were a pump rating. It is a catalog sheet class. Your coil is a different number. Using the band to look complete will not size a hose.

## Power lines to attach

- Work height to the outlet, and the catalog class to screen: 18.5m or 25m
- Roof geometry the panel must clear
- Profile, material, thickness, coil width, and feed side, or unknown
- Wind stop if the eave is open
- Ground or floor under the setup, and a place for a separate pack if there is one
- Access route for the coil and for a pack that ships loose
- Outrigger or track footprint
- Destination country
- Chassis or carrier included, local, or unsure
- Transport split if the former ships separate
- Voltage or other power for each machine, and who owns the hydraulic pack
- Documents: the offer lines that say included or excluded

## FAQ

#### Can one pack run the lift and the former?
Only if the offer says so and names the hoses. Otherwise count two scopes.

#### What if the former is not chosen?
Screen the height class. Mark pack ownership unknown. Do not borrow a duty from a different former.

#### Is 0.3–1.0mm a hydraulic rating?
No. It is the ARC-RF8 catalog sheet class. Your coil thickness is a separate line.

#### Who should answer the pack question?
The seller of each machine, on the quotation, not a caption under a coil photograph.
