A quotation describing a hydraulic roll-forming lift should identify the actual power arrangement it proposes. Ask whether the relevant power unit is included in the supplied equipment, separately proposed or assumed to come from another system. The word hydraulic describes an equipment concept; it does not tell the buyer which assemblies and responsibilities are included in the price.

This guide produces a power-package boundary sheet. It does not specify hydraulic ratings, circuits, connections or maintenance. The [mounting interface guide](/blog/lifted-roll-forming-line-mounting-interface-review/) examines the machine's physical relationship with the lift. Here the narrower decision is which provider supplies the proposed hydraulic power arrangement and what remains outside that purchase.

## Identify the power arrangement in the actual offer

### Ask which assembly provides the proposed power

Request the provider's description of the hydraulic power arrangement for the quoted lift configuration. Ask it to identify the relevant supplied assembly and the documents that describe its scope. Do not infer a power-unit identity or drive arrangement from a general photograph of the lifting structure.

The [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) concerns raising compatible roof-panel forming equipment near the receiving level. That architecture supports asking about the power subsystem, but it does not establish that every proposed configuration uses the same equipment package.

### Distinguish included equipment from an external assumption

If the quotation relies on power provision outside the supplied assembly, ask the provider to identify that assumption explicitly. The buyer needs to know which other equipment or party must answer it. A brief note saying by others can conceal a substantial unanswered scope question when the assumed external system has not been identified.

Do not suggest using an existing machine's system as a convenient substitute. The actual providers must assess any proposed relationship using the relevant equipment information. This guide does not establish a conversion or shared-system capability.

### Keep the former's equipment separate

A forming machine may have its own provider-described power equipment. Identify it separately from the lift package rather than assuming one system serves both. Ask which actual machine records the lift provider requires and whether any relationship is expressly proposed.

The buyer's scope sheet should therefore name the lift, former and any other relevant proposed subsystem, even when one seller coordinates the enquiry. Shared purchase timing or proximity does not establish shared power or an included interface.

<a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg271.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE's machinery-purchasing checklist</a> includes questions about hydraulic and other energy sources. This Great Britain context supports asking the provider to identify the actual power equipment and its documentation. It defines no supplied assembly, hydraulic rating or connection for this proposal; the offer must establish those boundaries.

## Locate the commercial boundaries around the power unit

### Identify the supplied unit and its equipment context

Ask the provider to show where the power equipment belongs within the offered assembly using its actual arrangement information. The purpose is to understand what is purchased and what occupies space, not to choose a mounting position. Keep the identity and configuration reference with the answer.

Where the proposed unit is separate from another delivered assembly, ask how the offer describes their relationship. A packing item and an installed subsystem can have different commercial boundaries, and the buyer should not treat a shipment description as a completed integration statement.

### Separate energy input from hydraulic distribution

Ask which provider supplies the information about the unit's own energy input and which equipment scope its hydraulic output is intended to serve. These are questions for the actual configuration. Do not assume an electrical drive, engine arrangement or connection to another machine unless the offer identifies it.

This distinction helps prevent a power-package discussion from drifting into guessed technical specifications. The buyer can request the relevant records without selecting a motor, pressure, flow, reservoir or circuit arrangement.

### Keep local completion visible

If the delivered package requires destination work, ask which parts of that work the seller includes and which are left to the buyer's appointed parties. Identify the required information handover and the point at which a configuration-specific response becomes available. Do not write assembly or commissioning steps into the purchase sheet.

The [destination receipt guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-destination-receipt-plan/) develops the broader arrival question. For this article, the important distinction is between receiving a power-related assembly and having the actual proposed system completed under a defined scope.

## Compare a supplied unit with assumed external provision

### Follow a hypothetical offer pair

Imagine Offer A identifies a power-unit assembly within the lift package. Offer B describes a lifting assembly and states that power provision is external, but gives no identity for the assumed source. The buyer initially compares both as complete hydraulic lifts because their lifting structures look similar.

The boundary sheet shows why the prices are not yet equivalent. A must clarify what its included assembly and integration scope cover. B must identify the equipment information and responsible provider needed to assess the external provision. Neither hypothetical offer establishes that a particular arrangement is available or suitable.

### Use a power-package boundary table

| Purchase question | Supplied-unit proposal | External-provision proposal | Clarification that changes the comparison |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Power equipment identity | Assembly named in offer | External system must be identified | What actual equipment does the response describe? |
| Scope serving the lift | Provider must define coverage | Provider must state required information | Which lift configuration is addressed? |
| Former relationship | Separate unless expressly proposed | Separate unless expressly proposed | Is any shared arrangement actually included? |
| Energy input information | Equipment-side record requested | Relevant external-provider record requested | Who supplies the missing requirement? |
| Delivery boundary | Included assembly and packing scope | Included and excluded items distinguished | What actually arrives under each contract? |
| Destination completion | Actual integration scope needed | Additional responsibility may remain | Which party owns the unresolved work? |

The table compares purchasing definitions, not hydraulic performance. No row authorises connecting equipment or concludes that an existing power unit can serve a new lift.

### Interpret the trade-off without inventing savings

An included power assembly may make some supply boundaries easier to identify, while an external arrangement may depend on equipment already owned or separately purchased. Those are conditional commercial differences. They do not establish a lower total cost, simpler installation or equivalent performance.

Ask each provider to state what its proposal leaves for others. If the external equipment is unidentified, the buyer cannot fill that gap by assuming an available unit will suffice. If the included package omits local integration, retain that exclusion beside the inclusion rather than calling the offer complete.

## Request the missing package information

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise an undefined power assumption

A proposal may not fit a final purchase comparison when its hydraulic power provision depends on unidentified external equipment or when the included unit's scope is unclear. The appropriate next enquiry is about that specific boundary. A larger-looking unit or a similar machine in a photograph does not resolve it.

This article provides no hydraulic rating, hose specification, connection method, pressure adjustment, bleeding procedure or maintenance instruction. The actual equipment providers and responsible project parties must define and assess the offered arrangement. Its power-unit identity is a purchasing input, not an engineering approval.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Prepare the power-package scope request

- Identify the actual lift, former and any proposed feed equipment so each power statement can be attached to its correct subsystem.
- Ask which hydraulic power assembly is supplied, separately offered or assumed external, keeping exclusions explicit.
- State roof receiving geometry and outlet height as task context without treating either as evidence of power-system capability.
- Include site ground or floor conditions and access constraints where they affect the proposed equipment layout and delivery discussion.
- Identify destination and transport module boundaries, including which local completion parties are appointed or still unknown.
- Request the actual power requirements, configuration records and documentation handover needed to clarify the package before price comparison.

Keep hydraulic connection endpoint questions separate from the identity of the power unit itself. Once the package is named, the responsible parties can define the connecting scope; this worksheet should not attempt to supply that technical answer in advance.

## FAQ

#### Does hydraulic lift mean a power unit is included?

The actual quotation must define that inclusion. Ask the provider to identify the supplied assembly and any external assumptions. A product-family description alone does not establish the delivered components, drive arrangement or remaining local scope.

#### Can an existing power unit be used instead?

This guide cannot determine that. Identify the actual equipment and ask the responsible providers what information is needed to assess any proposed relationship. Do not infer compatibility from appearance, ownership or a broadly similar application, and do not treat it as an available conversion.

#### Is the former's hydraulic equipment part of the lift package?

Only an explicit equipment-specific offer can define that boundary. Keep the former provider's records separate and ask whether a relationship is actually proposed. The forming task does not automatically make the machines share a power system.

#### What should a buyer compare before the price?

Compare the actual supplied assemblies, external assumptions, included coordination and destination completion boundary. If those differ, ask for clarified scope or separate missing-item responses. A comparison of nominally hydraulic equipment does not establish that both prices cover the same purchase.
