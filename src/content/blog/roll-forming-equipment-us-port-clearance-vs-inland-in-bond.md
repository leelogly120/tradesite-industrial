**Clear the equipment at the arrival port when that accepted route provides the needed customs services, inspections and onward delivery at a justified complete cost. Use an inland in-bond route when the broker and bonded carrier accept the actual movement to a named destination port and the inland clearance/receiving arrangement improves the complete purchase.** Obtain the broker's route assessment before booking. An inland factory address alone does not authorise moving uncleared cargo there.

This comparison is for a US import transaction. Other countries have their own procedures and responsible providers. The decision is about where an identified shipment undergoes its agreed customs-entry process and how it reaches the equipment receiver. It is separate from whether the importer buys through an overseas or local seller.

## Clearance location changes the purchased inland service

US [CBP guidance on clearance away from the first arrival port](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article-1154?language=en_US) distinguishes arrival-port entry from an immediate-transportation arrangement to another port. [19 USC 1552](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t19/s1552/) provides the statutory basis for transportation in bond to another designated port for entry. Have the appointed US customs broker and bonded carrier establish the actual eligible route and required filings. This article does not supply entry instructions or a tariff classification.

An arrival-port route needs a broker who can handle the actual machine and any required inspection arrangements there, followed by released inland transport. An inland in-bond route needs an accepted bonded movement, destination arrival reporting and final entry/clearance arrangements. Price the responsible services in both proposals rather than comparing one trucking amount with a complete customs package.

*A module and contents schedule lets the broker assess the actual equipment shipment.*

Separate shafts, frames and tooling can arrive under several package identities. Give the broker the actual invoice descriptions and packing list, linked to the bought equipment. Classification, valuation, origin and any other applicable requirements belong to that specialist's assessment of the actual goods, not to a generic model name.

## Use one final released receiving condition

Set the comparison endpoint at the same equipment receiving location after the required release. Include terminal and warehouse charges, inspections where required, customs services, bonds and bonded movement where relevant, released inland transport and any unloading or storage work. Obtain the actual provider quotations and the broker's applicable duty/tax assessment.

Do not treat in-bond movement as a way to remove import obligations. It changes the movement and entry arrangement; the named parties must perform its required steps. Ask the broker to identify any route-specific restrictions and the responsible party for each reporting and release event. Fund the accepted route's actual services.

## The inland terminal is not automatically your assembly workshop

Check how the equipment leaves the clearance location. A broker or terminal handling a customs event does not automatically unload a long former, perform technical inspection or assemble a lifting package. Name the receiving workshop and competent equipment/handling providers separately. Obtain their actual scope and the cargo data they require.

If an inspection requires package access, coordinate with the packer and preservation provider. Identify the instructions and resources for authorised opening and restoring protection. Keep package identities through that work so the final equipment receiver can reconcile the shipment and records.

The receiver still needs its own installation and acceptance plan after customs release.

## Two hypothetical customs-route mistakes

**An inland warehouse is assumed to be a customs destination.** A buyer books transport to its own building without an accepted in-bond and clearance arrangement. The freight proposal does not provide the required route. Have the broker establish the eligible entry location and carrier service before committing movement.

**The port quote is compared with an incomplete inland quote.** The port route includes handling, customs service and final delivery; the inland amount includes only line-haul movement. Reconcile both proposals at the same released receiving endpoint and include the actual procedure, handling and destination services.
