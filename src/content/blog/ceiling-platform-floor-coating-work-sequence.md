Complete ceiling-platform work before the final floor coating when the ceiling design, materials and supporting floor are ready. This keeps heavy access movements out of the finished-floor handover period. Choose access after coating only when the programme requires it and the floor specialist has released the actual finish for the proposed traffic and protection arrangement. A dry-looking surface is not that release.

Start by placing ceiling completion, platform removal, floor preparation, coating and floor handover on one programme. The sequence should include return visits for testing and defects. Moving installation earlier saves little if the same large platform must return across the newly finished floor for an unfinished overhead task.

The unfinished hall below shows why an earlier date alone is insufficient. Debris, uneven areas and other construction movements still affect the proposed route. The project team must deliver a usable, assessed route before bringing the equipment in.

## Make two separate floor decisions

First establish whether the structure and supporting ground accept the proposed equipment and support arrangement. The building engineer needs the actual route, slab records, openings, joints and candidate-specific support information. Second establish whether the finish accepts the intended contact, movement and protection system. The flooring specialist needs the coating specification, application record, environmental conditions and proposed traffic.

Neither decision replaces the other. A structurally suitable slab still has a surface to protect. Conversely, a scratch-resistant finish does not increase the slab's structural capacity. Keep those two answers separate in the platform purchase worksheet and in the responsibility list.

Sika's [Sikafloor-515 product information](https://usa.sika.com/en/construction-products/flooring/flooring-products/polyaspartic-urethaneuvresistantcoatings/sikafloor-515.html) distinguishes foot traffic, light traffic and full cure. The practical lesson is to use the installed system's own release conditions. Do not transfer a time from another coating or interpret pedestrian access as permission for a crawler platform.

Walk the full route when completing the table. Include the unloading point, entrance, turns, working positions and exit. Protecting only the operating bay leaves the most constrained part of the journey outside the plan.

## When coating must come first

A phased building sometimes needs an accepted floor before overhead work in a later zone. In that case, define one continuous route and one responsible party for maintaining it throughout the access period. Price the protection installation, inspections, removal, cleaning and any agreed restoration together with the platform package.

Do not prescribe generic boards or sheets as a universal solution. Protection changes the contact arrangement and can introduce its own movement, surface-contact and handling issues. The floor specialist and equipment planner must agree the actual system. Their agreed design is an input to procurement, rather than a material chosen by the purchasing team from a photograph.

If the accepted protection arrangement cannot accommodate the proposed platform, change the access method or the programme. Buying the platform first does not create permission to cross the finish. For a building-wide comparison of alternative access, use the [crawler platform, spider lift and scaffolding guide](/blog/crawler-platform-vs-spider-lift-vs-scaffolding/).

## Two mistakes that create a second access bill

**Mistake 1 — coating follows installation but precedes inspection.** In this illustrative project, ceiling panels are fitted and the platform leaves. An overhead inspection then identifies unfinished details after the floor has been coated. The correction is a completion schedule covering installation, inspection and agreed rectification before floor handover. Where a later visit is unavoidable, include its assessed access arrangement in the first purchase.

**Mistake 2 — floor protection is bought without a route assessment.** A buyer prices protective material for the work bay, then discovers that the entrance turn and support positions need a different arrangement. The correction is one route drawing used by the building engineer, flooring specialist and equipment provider. Compare their accepted complete scope, including entry and exit, rather than only the protected area beneath the ceiling.
