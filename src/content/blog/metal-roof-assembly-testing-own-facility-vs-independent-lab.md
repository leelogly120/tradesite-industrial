Choose a qualified outside laboratory when a specific roof assembly needs recognized evidence and your business has few repeat programs. Consider an internal facility when sustained development pays for the equipment, trained personnel and quality system, and the parties relying on each result accept that facility and the applicable witness arrangements.

## Separate development experiments from contractual evidence

Write down who needs the result and what decision it will support. An internal comparison between two design revisions can be valuable even if the resulting record has no contractual status. A project requiring a named test method, recognized facility, independent witness or formal report has a different deliverable. If product development and contractual reporting are both funded, assign each a separate workload and budget. Treat them as two service lines in the forecast. A test rig bought for frequent development work can coexist with an external laboratory used for assessments required by customers.

MBCI describes a witnessed in-house test at its facility. Intertek lists an assembly testing service under a stated roof testing standard. These examples establish two real procurement routes. Ask the intended evidence recipient which test, facility status, specimen selection and reporting it will accept for this project. Have the proposed laboratory identify any additional work outside its offer. Agree who will accept a revised assembly after a design change and which stages of the original program must recur. ## Specify the test program before costing a laboratory

Define an assembly revision, including the exact panel, clips or fixings, support arrangement and other components relevant to the method. Ask the test specialist for the required specimen and build sequence. Include representative production, freight, assembly labour and disposition after the test. The facility bid must identify who provides the test plan, instrumentation, calibration, supervision, data recording and report. For an internal bid also include the building services, installation, periodic maintenance and management needed to keep the facility ready between programs.

Model a successful first program and a revision requiring another specimen. The value of bringing a development loop inside the business depends on how quickly design and production teams can act on the result. An available rig saves little time if revised tooling, material or technical approval takes longer than the external testing appointment. Conversely a short experimental run can free the outside appointment for the version most likely to become the released product. Count that saving only if the customer accepts the sequence.

## Set a spending threshold for each route

Compare annual ownership and staffing costs with outside laboratory fees for the same accepted deliverables. Allow for transport, witnessing, travel, reserved time, repeat specimens and report preparation. Value additional internal development work separately using a specific decision, such as narrowing the next trial to one revision. Include idle time when development pauses. A producer with several product families should distinguish shared resources from fixtures and specialists unique to one family. Ask finance to keep evidence used for several customers in one cost centre so its value is counted once.

**Mistake: Pricing a chamber as an accepted report.** A manufacturer obtains a quotation for testing equipment and budgets all future assessment costs as savings. The project still requires a recognized independent report and the new laboratory has no accepted route for it. Retain external assessment costs, then test whether internal development activity alone justifies the equipment.

**Mistake: Sending a convenient sample.** A roll former sends attractive field sheets from another order. Their support, clip and production revision differ from the proposed roofing package. The report the customer needs concerns a different assembly. Agree the specimen schedule with the laboratory and evidence recipient before releasing coil or cutting fixtures.

Award outside testing when the known program can buy the required evidence on an acceptable schedule. Fund an internal facility when recurrent development and genuinely accepted in-house work pay for it after remaining external services. The equipment purchase for producing panels follows the released profile and manufacturing process regardless of which facility supplies the evidence. Identify any later changes that trigger an engineering review before promising further customers that the existing evidence covers their orders.

## Eight RFQ inputs for a comparable evidence program

1. Name the product owner and recipient of each report.
2. Attach the applicable contract, test method and recognition requirements.
3. Provide assembly revisions, support details and component schedule.
4. Identify specimen materials, manufacturing location and traceability.
5. List funded programs, optional iterations and target dates.
6. State who supplies fixtures, builds specimens and witnesses the work.
7. Request complete outside and internal costs including retained services.
8. Define report contents, acceptance, records ownership and repeat arrangements.
