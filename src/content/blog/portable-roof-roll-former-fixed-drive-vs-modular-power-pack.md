Choose a fixed drive when your portable roof former can produce every committed order at an accepted location with the required power. Pay for interchangeable power packs when a second, documented mode opens otherwise inaccessible production work and the same machine can reach those jobs without displacing more valuable orders.

A contractor can have an electrically supplied workshop, a remote job without suitable site power and just one former. Buying two drive options solves a power mismatch. The shop and remote job still compete for the same forming line, crew and tooling. Build the purchase comparison around the dated production orders that survive both tests.

## Reconstruct a month in the life of the machine

Choose a representative period containing actual shop orders and site work. Record the job, profile, promised delivery, permitted production location and length that can be received there. Mark orders that expressly require forming on site. A remote customer who can receive complete sheets from the shop belongs in a different group from a contractor who has bought local production because the completed lengths cannot be delivered.

For each location record who supplies the utilities, when they become available and which drive arrangements the site accepts. The permanent building supply on an electrical drawing can be commissioned after the roof is required. A remote site can have a suitable temporary supply already included in the principal contractor's price. Those entries can reverse the apparent case for a second pack.

New Tech Machinery describes modular electric and gasoline power on certain named portable forming machines. That is evidence for a manufacturer-designed purchase option on identified machines. Obtain documentation for the exact former and both offered packs before assigning either mode to an order. ## Compare complete ways of supplying the panels

Start the fixed-drive bid with the core group of orders that already fits it. For an outlying order, obtain a price for an accepted temporary electrical supply, production at another location or purchased panels delivered to the customer. Include the cost of the actual sheet transfer and any change to the delivery undertaking. An alternative only belongs in the comparison if the customer accepts the finished product and handover.

Start the modular bid with the same orders and repeat the exercise. Identify the permitted second mode, the complete equipment required, who owns and transports the unused pack and who is competent to perform the manufacturer's specified change. Agree where that change can occur and how the line is returned to production. Budget the stock, storage, servicing and mobilization associated with the additional unit. Use the offered documents for the operating requirements.

A quoted production drive also needs a boundary. Ask which forming functions, auxiliaries and controls it supplies. Identify separately any external equipment needed for coil handling, site lighting or moving material to the roof. A lift carrier or truck has a separate equipment scope. A second forming power pack cannot be credited with eliminating costs that remain in the material-handling or lifting contract under either bid.

| Production order input | Enter for the actual job | Decision and accountable owner |
|---|---|---|
| Required location | Name the permitted shop or site and record any mandatory site-rolling term | Contract manager allocates eligible work |
| Site utilities | Enter the accepted source, connection particulars from the provider and date available | Site representative releases the power basis |
| Offered machine | Record line ID, profile and maker's supported drive configurations | Supplier defines both bids on the same output |
| Dates and travel | Mark last shop run, dispatch, site runs and first possible shop return | Planner rejects simultaneous bookings |
| Auxiliary equipment | List separate power, coil movement, crew and sheet receiving arrangements | Project lead prices the complete production day |
| Outside alternative | Enter the accepted supply or hire offer, delivered quantity and margin | Buyer calculates what the modular option actually saves |

## Put a value on the second mode

Build a calendar for each bid. On a site assignment, block out dispatch, return, configuration change and the customer's release windows as well as the sheet run. A bidder's changeover figure describes a particular operation. It leaves travel, material release, crew scheduling and return inspection to the project unless those items appear in the offer.

Calculate each bid's contribution from the jobs it can fulfil on that calendar. For a displaced shop order, use its actual outside supply cost if you keep the sale, or leave the sale out if the customer goes elsewhere. Deduct the pack and support costs once, then compare the two net totals. If only a speculative tender requires the second mode, keep that tender in a separate scenario. Ask the supplier to price the base configuration, both packs together and the written terms for adding a pack later. Buy the option now when the committed order book pays for the difference within your investment period.

## Two hypothetical purchasing mistakes

**Mistake: valuing the same production day twice.** A buyer assigns a profitable remote job to the second mode and keeps all warehouse orders on the original calendar. The day the former leaves, the warehouse has no line. Move those orders to an available date or price an outside supply before counting the remote margin.

**Mistake: comparing an engine with a complete electrical package.** A fixed-drive quotation includes the site's temporary supply and ancillary services. The modular bid omits the equipment serving the coil-handling area. The comparison appears to favor the modular bid until identical attendance is included. Obtain the same job-by-job service boundary from both bidders.
