Buy a standby forming lift only when an interruption to the lift itself is the gap you need to cover and a compatible former, crew and recovery route remain available. Compare a complete reserve package when production must continue independently of both the primary lift and former. Choose outside panel supply when it can deliver the accepted product to the required receiver within the project's recovery window and duplicate ownership is not justified.

This is a decision about uncertain interruptions. The [one-versus-two package guide](/blog/one-roll-forming-lift-vs-two-concurrent-projects/) addresses known simultaneous project demand. A reserve asset has a different job: it must restore a defined output after a specific interruption, even while it spends much of the planning period unused.

## Name what has stopped before buying its replacement

A spare lift addresses a different event from a spare former. Neither resolves a rejected coil delivery, an unavailable roof receiver or a closure of the only site access road. Start with the interruption scenarios that the project wants to cover, then identify the capability missing in each one.

For a lift-only interruption, establish what happens to the retained former and what a second lift needs to accept it. The alternate pairing, transfer scope and relevant engineering records belong in the purchase. Similar model names or a common mounting appearance are not an interchangeable-machine commitment.

For a former interruption, another lifting assembly leaves the production fault in place. Compare a separate compatible line, an independent complete package or accepted panels from another producer. For a site-wide interruption, examine whether the alternative reaches an available receiving route at all.

## Buy recovery to a named point, not equipment standing nearby

Define the required recovery outcome as accepted panels at a specified handover point. Then work back through the tasks needed to reach it. A spare asset at the depot still needs release, transport, site receipt, integration and the applicable readiness checks. An outside producer still needs the accepted product definition, material, production allocation, delivery and onward handling.

The programme owner should set the latest acceptable time for that outcome. Obtain actual response and completion commitments from the parties supplying each route. If a route cannot meet the required window, remove it as the primary contingency or revise the project programme. Do not fill the gap with an invented mobilisation time.

## Independence matters more than the second serial number

Two machines depending on one unavailable crew are not two independent production routes. Two suppliers dependent on the same unavailable material are not two independent material sources. Trace only the dependencies relevant to the interruption being covered, then decide which must be duplicated or sourced differently.

A complete reserve package needs continuing care while idle. Allocate storage, records, inspection and service responsibilities under the actual equipment requirements. Also identify who can authorise deployment and who supplies the production team. Those are continuing ownership resources, not one-time accessories delivered with the spare machine.

Outside panels avoid owning every standby resource, but require an accepted alternative supply arrangement. The required panel length, material and finish must survive the delivery route. A shorter panel with a new joint is a roof-design change, not an automatic substitute when the main former stops.

## Use actual commitments without inventing a failure rate

For the standby route, compare purchase, storage, maintenance, keeping personnel available and the defined deployment movements over the same planning period. For the external route, record actual reservation, call-off, panel, delivery and receiving charges. Identify excluded events on both sides.

There is no need to guess how often the primary machine fails to produce a useful first decision. First remove alternatives that cannot cover the selected event or deadline. Then the business can judge the remaining cost using its own experience and accepted project exposure. Keep any optional future work separate from the expenditure required to maintain readiness.

## Two illustrative contingency mistakes

**Mistake: a spare lift is purchased for a former breakdown.** The second lifting assembly arrives while the only former remains unavailable. The buyer duplicated support capacity rather than the missing production capability. Match the reserve scope to the failure scenario and obtain a line or outside-panel alternative for that event.

**Mistake: the backup producer has no route for the accepted panel.** An informal supplier contact is listed as the contingency, but its delivery offer assumes shorter panels than the released roof design. The promised alternative requires a design change after the interruption. Resolve product acceptance and delivery before treating that supplier as the recovery route.
