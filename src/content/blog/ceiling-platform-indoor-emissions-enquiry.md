An indoor ceiling-platform enquiry should state the facility's actual air-related restrictions and ask the equipment provider to identify the power arrangement it proposes. Do not begin with an assumption that a product label makes the equipment emission-free or suitable for any interior. The building's requirements and the supplier's equipment response are different inputs that need to be compared by the responsible project parties.

This guide produces a building-restriction brief. The [dual-power platform guide](/blog/dual-power-crawler-platform-selection/) compares energy concepts more broadly. Here the specific buying question is whether the actual proposal has answered the facility's stated restrictions, including the building phase and zones to which they apply.

## Obtain the facility's own restriction statement

### Identify who owns the requirement

Ask the facility representative responsible for the relevant building conditions to provide the actual restriction or review requirement. A contractor's recollection that only clean equipment is allowed is not a precise basis for comparing power proposals. Record the issuer, applicable area and the question the facility wants answered.

This guide does not create a universal indoor-air rule. Different projects may have different stated requirements, and the buyer needs an attributable local response rather than a generic claim copied from another building's purchase file.

### Separate a restriction from a preference

The facility may state a requirement, request further information or express a preference for consideration. Preserve that distinction. A preference should not be rewritten as a technical prohibition, while a condition attached to permission should not disappear into a list of optional features.

Ask for clarification when the wording is broad. The buyer's useful contribution is making the requirement understandable to the equipment provider without interpreting its technical or legal implications independently. The responsible facility and project reviewers must decide what the actual proposal needs to demonstrate.

### Identify the exact interior being discussed

Name the intended work zone and describe the building phase using current project information. A partly enclosed construction hall, an occupied interior and a separate service area should not be treated as one generic indoor condition. The restriction brief should show which areas the facility statement covers.

For [crawler ceiling platforms](/products/#crawler-ceiling-platforms), the equipment task concerns a broad work deck at the ceiling or wall face. The facility's power-related restriction is one selection input, alongside the physical task, access and support questions that still need their own review.

<a href="https://www.safework.sa.gov.au/workplaces/plant-tools-and-vehicles/diesel-exhaust" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">SafeWork SA's diesel-exhaust page</a> identifies engines and stationary power sources as potential exhaust sources and highlights enclosed-area exposure concerns. This South Australian context explains why an enquiry involving such a proposed source needs the actual building conditions. It supplies no emissions result, ventilation design or conclusion about this platform's indoor suitability.

## Request the actual power-arrangement response

### Ask what equipment is proposed

Have the supplier identify the power arrangement and configuration behind its answer. Do not assume that electric, hybrid or dual power describes every source or function relevant to the intended task. A family-level feature label cannot replace the actual equipment record.

Where the option is conditional or not yet selected, retain that status in the comparison. The facility cannot assess an unidentified future arrangement merely because the buyer expects it to meet the restriction. The next useful question may be which actual proposal the supplier can provide for review.

### Separate equipment evidence from a suitability conclusion

Ask the provider what configuration-specific information it can supply that is relevant to the facility's stated question. Keep the issuer and conditions with that information. Its existence does not automatically establish that the building requirement is met; the responsible reviewer must assess the actual relationship.

This article offers no emissions value, exposure threshold, ventilation calculation or indoor-safety conclusion. It helps the buyer request attributable information and avoid expanding a narrow equipment statement into a general approval for occupied interiors.

### Include activities outside the immediate ceiling task

Ask the facility whether its restrictions apply only at the workface or also to delivery, waiting, parking and other proposed locations. Identify those as distinct project questions rather than assuming a permission for one area applies throughout the building. The actual provider should know which configuration and conditions the enquiry concerns.

Do not prescribe how the equipment should move or change source between areas. The restriction brief records the project boundaries; the actual equipment information and responsible site arrangements must determine what is appropriate.

## Compare two replies against one building restriction

### Follow a hypothetical occupied-hall enquiry

Imagine a facility issuing a restriction for an occupied hall and asking for information about the proposed equipment power arrangement. Offer A responds with the phrase suitable indoors but identifies no configuration or supporting record. Offer B identifies a proposed option and provides limited equipment information while leaving the facility's acceptance open.

The buyer should ask A for the basis and scope of its statement. B's response creates an identifiable review input but does not close the facility question. The purchasing comparison should show that difference rather than treating either phrase as completed environmental acceptance.

### Use a requirement-to-evidence table

| Brief item | Facility input | Supplier input | Remaining review question |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Restricted area | Actual named zone and building phase | Configuration proposed for that zone | Does the response address the same location? |
| Restriction wording | Requirement or requested information as issued | Response to that specific wording | Has the supplier answered the actual question? |
| Power arrangement | Facility needs stated without guessing equipment | Actual source and function scope | Which parts remain conditional or unidentified? |
| Supporting information | Reviewer identifies needed evidence | Available records and their limitations | Is more configuration-specific information needed? |
| Other project areas | Parking, waiting or delivery restrictions if applicable | Equipment context for those areas | Does the earlier answer extend to them? |
| Changed building phase | New condition issued by the facility | Revised or retained supplier response | Which earlier assumptions no longer apply? |

The table deliberately contains no recommended source, emission limit or ventilation measure. Its purpose is aligning the exact question with the exact response so the responsible reviewer can assess the real proposal.

### Avoid trading an unanswered restriction for another feature

A platform may have an attractive deck arrangement or an additional energy source while leaving the facility's essential restriction unanswered. Those features do not compensate for the missing response. Ask which actual configuration can be assessed against the restriction, or keep the affected selection open.

The trade-off is between a generic indoor claim and a bounded proposal with visible evidence and limitations. A limited but attributable answer can move the review forward, while a broad assurance may add no usable information if the equipment and conditions remain unknown.

## Keep the restriction brief current through selection

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise when indoor suitability remains unestablished

A proposal may not fit a final selection when the facility requirement is unclear, the offered power arrangement is unidentified or the responsible reviewer has not assessed the relevant information. A photograph of a platform beneath a roof does not establish acceptable emissions or building conditions for another project.

This article provides no health guidance, exposure limit, ventilation design or operating procedure. It makes no emission-free or indoor-safe claim. The actual equipment, facility and project parties must resolve the stated restriction using applicable information for the proposed arrangement.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Prepare the building-restriction brief

- Obtain the facility's actual restriction or evidence request, identifying its owner and the zones and building phase it covers.
- Describe the ceiling task, task height and workface geometry so the supplier can identify the proposed platform context.
- State people, tools and material requirements as task inputs, keeping them separate from environmental suitability conclusions.
- Provide floor and access information alongside the facility's restrictions for relevant delivery, parking or waiting areas.
- Identify destination and transport handover scope so a response for one stage is not assumed to cover the entire project.
- Request the actual power arrangement, configuration-specific documentation, conditions and unresolved information needed by the responsible reviewer.

When the facility changes a restriction or the supplier changes the option, update the affected row. Preserve the earlier response within its original scope rather than retaining a convenient indoor-approved label that no longer describes the actual proposal.

## FAQ

#### Does an electric label mean emission-free equipment?

Do not infer that conclusion from a label. Ask the provider to identify the actual power arrangement and the relevant configuration-specific information. The responsible facility reviewer must assess the stated restriction against the actual proposal rather than a general product description.

#### Can an indoor photograph prove the option is acceptable?

It cannot establish the photographed equipment's power system or the conditions applicable to another building. Use the image only as workface context. The actual equipment records and facility requirements remain the basis of the enquiry.

#### Should the buyer recommend ventilation to resolve a gap?

This guide does not provide a ventilation design or mitigation method. Identify the unresolved requirement and refer it to the responsible facility and equipment parties. A purchasing comparison should not fill a technical gap with an unreviewed remedy.

#### What changes when an area becomes occupied?

Ask the facility which requirements or conditions change and obtain the corresponding equipment response. Do not assume the earlier construction-phase review covers the new use. Keep the actual zone, building phase and stated restrictions linked to the supplier information being assessed.
