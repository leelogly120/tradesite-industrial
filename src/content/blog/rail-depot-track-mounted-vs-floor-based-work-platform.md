**Procure accepted rail-mounted or road-rail access plant when the workface must be approached along a track and the infrastructure operator can grant the required track access. Choose a floor-based personnel platform for building work beside the track when the depot can release a continuous floor route and verified support positions clear of pits and railway operations.** If the only plan puts an ordinary crawler on the track, obtain a different engineered access proposal.

Divide the work orders before choosing equipment. Railway infrastructure work above a track, vehicle maintenance beside a parked train and building maintenance at the outer wall of a depot can look similar on a height schedule. They belong to different asset owners, require different clearances and often have different permission windows. Group them by who can release the work area and when.

The low red deck in the photograph occupies an industrial floor under white roof framing. A depot can add inspection pits, rails, flangeways, fixed maintenance stands and parked rolling stock between two apparently open positions. Mark the actual depot floor and rail boundaries on the tender layout. This photograph illustrates the floor-supported option.

## An on-track booking is a complete railway work package

A rail access supplier needs the track identity, actual work coordinates, arrival and departure arrangements and the authority responsible for possession or other approved access. Ask the operator to define train movements, electrical and other controls, required competencies and rules of the relevant railway. Keep these decisions with qualified railway parties. A successful equipment quotation alone does not release the infrastructure or authorise work near live equipment.

[VolkerRail describes road-rail MEWPs](https://www.volkerrail.co.uk/en/rail-plant/mewps-and-access-plant) for railway work. [Network Rail explains its own product-acceptance process](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/industry-and-commercial/research-development-and-technology/product-acceptance/) for equipment on its infrastructure in Great Britain. Use the local infrastructure owner's requirements for your depot. Acceptance on one network, or for one plant configuration, cannot be transferred to another by citing a catalogue.

Price the rail route from mobilisation to handback. Include the accepted provider, rail access arrangements, operator and support team, allocated working period, required protection and tests, and the release back to traffic. Separate productive access time from the total time occupied by controlled entry and exit. A large nominal reach has little value if the short railway window cannot support the required task.

## A building-work route must survive the operating layout

For a floor-based platform, obtain the door opening, turning route, floor construction and each proposed operating position. Identify any pit covers, embedded rails, drainage channels and thresholds. The building engineer needs the reactions and travel loads for the exact offered machine. A track crossing on the drawing requires its own accepted arrangement and railway permission. A visible cover or continuous-looking floor finish cannot supply that evidence.

Ask the depot operator for a representative live shift layout: train present or absent, maintenance stands deployed or stored, escape routes kept clear and adjacent bays operating. Recalculate the number of accepted positions and the access window from that layout. A wide deck can support simultaneous building tasks along an unobstructed ceiling run if the offered configuration is suitable. Dispersed points over a live railway route need a separate authorised proposal.

The higher red deck below stands close to white framing while its tracks and supports remain on the ground. In the depot drawing, show the final crew position and the area below at the same time. A clear floor route into the building can still leave the required workface directly above a pit or occupied track.

## Two mistakes in a mixed rail-and-building bid

**Mistake one — treating the closed depot door as a railway possession.** A hypothetical building contractor books plant while adjacent track movements remain under another controller. Identify the infrastructure authority and approved occupation before pricing work that enters its area.

**Mistake two — assuming the railway unit serves every off-track room.** The accepted rail position is well placed for an overhead track task but cannot provide the wall crew's position beyond a fixed obstruction. Separate that work and obtain a compatible floor-based arrangement for the released building area.
