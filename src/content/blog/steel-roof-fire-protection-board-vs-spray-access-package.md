**Purchase personnel access for the fire-protection system specified by the appointed specialists. For board encasement, price component delivery, fixing access and joint work. For a site-applied spray system, price access alongside separately supplied application equipment, the specialist's environmental controls and inspection visits. Shortlist a large-deck ceiling platform for repeated accessible work faces when its proposed configuration fits the building and task.** Use a separately defined access arrangement for faces that the deck cannot serve.

This purchasing decision follows the fire design. A broad work deck cannot determine the required protection, make different systems equivalent or approve a coating application. The buyer's job is to connect the accepted protection scope to a complete and comparable access package.

## The material specification comes before the access calendar

Require the fire-protection specialist's released system, member schedule and work sequence. Identify the party responsible for application or installation, inspection and acceptance. Where the project permits an alternative protection system, obtain the responsible design decision before using that alternative to justify a different equipment purchase.

[Steel Construction Info's structural fire-protection overview](https://steelconstruction.info/topics/fire-and-steel-construction/fire-protecting-structural-steelwork) distinguishes board protection, sprayed non-reactive protection and intumescent coatings. It identifies board installation as a dry trade and notes the effect of spray application and overspray on other work. Those are useful package distinctions. They are not a specification for the protection required on your roof steelwork.

The [Association for Specialist Fire Protection's technical catalogue](https://asfp.org.uk/knowledge/asfp-technical-documents) also lists separate installation and inspection guidance for board systems and sprayed non-reactive coatings. Use the documents applicable to the selected system through the appointed specialist. Do not treat every product called a spray as the same process.

## Price the work face, including the parts behind it

A beam has more task faces than its underside. The installation brief should identify the member faces, connections, returns and adjacent obstructions included in the specialist's work. Mark the positions from which those tasks must be served on a coordinated section.

For board encasement, the material schedule must identify delivered component sizes and masses, preparation locations and the objects required at the work face. The access proposal needs the crew, tools and material distribution for the named tasks. Separately identify handling equipment where the project needs it.

For a sprayed product, use the application contractor's actual equipment and task requirements. Identify which items remain on the floor, which reach the work position, and which external hoses or cables interact with the proposed platform. The supplier and site team must review those interfaces before the buyer accepts a complete package.

## A worksheet for the complete protection campaign

Compare access offers against the same specialist-defined result. Use separate rows for work that changes when an approved system changes.

| Purchase item | Board installation entry | Site-applied spray entry | Responsible party and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted system | Product, member schedule and detail revisions: ___ | Product, member schedule and application requirements: ___ | Fire designer and system specialist; establish the permitted installation scope |
| Task positions | Fixing faces, joints, returns and connections: ___ | Application and inspection faces: ___ | Surveyor and installer; locate faces from building grid and local supporting-floor datum |
| Material and tools | Piece dimensions, masses and simultaneous deck items: ___ | Floor equipment, work-position items and external connections: ___ | Specialist contractor; defines the task load and handling interfaces |
| Work environment | Specified installation conditions and adjacent-work restrictions: ___ | Specified containment, environmental controls and adjacent-work restrictions: ___ | Specialist and main contractor; define included enabling work |
| Platform arrangement | Repeated faces and exceptional positions: ___ | Repeated faces and exceptional positions: ___ | Access provider; proposes coverage and identifies separately served tasks |
| Floor and route | Setup locations, route and floor records: ___ | Setup locations plus application-equipment space: ___ | Site engineer; assesses the issued equipment loading against the actual floor |
| Return visits | Joint completion, inspection and corrections: ___ | Inspection, accepted follow-on stages and corrections: ___ | Quality lead and planner; set the full access period |
| Handover | Removal, protection disposal and area release: ___ | Removal, cleaning and area release: ___ | Main contractor; makes the completion endpoint comparable |

The environment row should contain an actual specified requirement and an included provider. A quotation that prices access while another offer also includes containment and cleaning has a different boundary. Keep those costs visible rather than hiding them in a general site allowance.

## Buy access for inspections as well as installation

Ask the quality lead which hold points need physical access and when the relevant faces must remain available. Add the agreed visits to the equipment schedule. Do not assume that the application crew's last shift is the access package's last day.

Where the specified system includes offsite work, list the site tasks that remain after delivery and erection. Those tasks determine the remaining personnel-access requirement. Removing work from site changes the task schedule; it does not automatically eliminate every inspection or correction visit.

The route to those visits is part of the purchase. The ground photograph below shows wet, uneven construction-stage conditions inside a hall. Obtain the actual building records and supplier reactions for the proposed equipment rather than treating a visually open bay as an accepted support area.

## Two hypothetical gaps in an apparently complete quotation

**Failure case: board area replaces a member-by-member work schedule.** A buyer allows platform time from the visible underside area, while the accepted details also include joints and faces behind connections. The quote fails to address part of the work. Reissue the member schedule with all required task positions and obtain a combined proposal for repeated and exceptional faces.

**Failure case: the spray quotation ends at application.** Another buyer releases the access platform before the specified inspection and follow-on visits. The project then needs a second mobilisation. Put the quality lead's actual access milestones into the purchase and compare retaining the equipment with a separately priced return.
