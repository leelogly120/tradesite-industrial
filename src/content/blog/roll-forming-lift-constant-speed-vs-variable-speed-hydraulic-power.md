Choose a constant-speed hydraulic package when it fulfils the required forming-lift duty and the variable-speed alternative adds at least as much ownership cost as it removes. Buy a documented variable-speed package when actual partial-load or idle periods produce a demonstrated saving exceeding its complete extra cost while preserving required motion and response. Send the machine provider a representative work calendar and function sequence before comparing power-unit prices.

This purchase belongs in a new-machine order. The selected lift structure, former, required working positions and accepted electrical source stay fixed. The provider designs two complete hydraulic supply proposals against that same equipment duty; the buyer compares their usable result and cost over one ownership period.

The lowered crawler and blue forming bed below are separate assemblies within a complete purchase. List the lifting functions and production functions separately, then attach each to its actual power unit.

## Start with the machine's working day

Use an actual project programme containing the intended positioning, production, waiting and shutdown periods. The owner records when equipment is required and which functions are needed together. The machinery designer converts those requirements into the applicable pressure, flow and time duty for each proposed circuit. Keep the designer's duty reference with the purchase rather than sizing a pump from roof area or the former's sheet thickness.

Distinguish a function being available from a motor running continuously. A long period beside the roof contains different equipment states. The provider must describe how its offered architecture serves those states, including the required readiness and response when work resumes. Use the issued machine requirements for each state. Procurement should purchase the accepted functionality without prescribing control settings.

[Bosch Rexroth's variable-speed drive overview](https://www.boschrexroth.com/en/us/connected-hydraulics/products/sytronix/) describes coordinated motor-pump systems that match supply to demand and use standby functions. This establishes a real hydraulic architecture. Its advertised benefits belong to its designed systems and working cycles; the forming-lift comparison needs its own duty evidence.

## The idle period must exist in the funded programme

A contractor serving intermittent roof campaigns should record the real number and duration of available work periods, rather than extending one busy demonstration across a year. Separate an authorised waiting state from a shutdown and from active motion. The actual provider defines each state's hydraulic and electrical requirements.

The same OEM's [variable-speed drive whitepaper](https://2022461.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2022461/DC-IH_MKT/Sytronix/WP_Sytronix_Variable-speed-drive-solutions_EN.pdf) describes load-dependent motor speed and complete system sizing, while expressly treating continuous constant-load operation as an exception to its broad savings case. For purchasing, the implication is to compare the real duty and all design limits before valuing reduced consumption. Specify motor speed separately from pump displacement. The whitepaper describes constant-speed operation with a variable-displacement pump and variable-speed systems using their specified pump arrangement. The offered design must identify both, together with its pressure/flow control. A motor nameplate alone describes neither the cycle nor its energy bill.

If intended work remains close to constant load, retain that case in both offers. If the calendar contains substantial partial-load or idle periods, require them in the representative comparison. Choose from the resulting totals; a variable-speed label receives no assumed annual saving.

| Required response | Function and response criteria ___; acceptance revision ___ | Owner and provider | Rejects an option that loses required performance |
| Electrical source | Supply record ___; connection boundary ___; location ___ | Destination electrical provider | Establishes usable installed power |
| Complete assembly | Pump/motor/drive/control/cooling schedule ___; outline datum ___ | Equipment provider | Prices integration and occupied space |

| Comparable cost | Installed cost ___; energy/service cost ___; common period ___ | Procurement lead | Ranks complete qualifying packages |

Keep the outlet and receiver drawing beside the first row. The power architecture still serves a lift carrying an identified former at specific working positions. Changes to assembly mass, mounting, cabinets or service space belong in the complete configuration review before the technical schedule is signed.

## Buy the complete package around the drive

The comparison includes the proposed pump, motor, converter where specified, controls, cooling, electrical connection and handover. Record the actual retained and changed objects on the supplier's assembly schedule. A price for one drive component leaves the surrounding engineering work outside the purchase.

Use the same accepted duty for both consumption records. Agree which electrical input is observed, which functions run and where the observation starts and finishes. Keep separately powered forming or site equipment outside that boundary unless both offers include it. This avoids crediting one power unit with costs incurred by another asset.

Enter required noise and environmental conditions as project acceptance criteria. Obtain the response for the complete offered installation, including its working state and measurement basis. The assembled package, working location and project requirement determine acceptance; a general technology label is insufficient.

## Compare ownership on one stated period

For each eligible proposal, add installed equipment, integration, priced energy use and provider-defined service costs over the chosen period. Price any additional stocked assemblies, competent support and the owner's supported interruption cost. Keep expenses common to both alternatives in each total once.

Use the owner's actual electricity basis and the duty it expects to fund. Keep a busier future scenario separate from committed work. Where a variable-speed proposal changes cooling or enclosure requirements, use the designer's issued scope and actual prices for those changes. Calculate the variable-speed premium from its additional installed and support costs, then subtract the supported energy and service savings for the same period. Where an ancillary reduction is already included in the installed quotation, count that reduction there once. Choose variable speed when the resulting premium is negative and the required duty is accepted; otherwise retain the qualifying constant-speed package.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
## Two hypothetical power-package mistakes

**Mistake: a best-case percentage becomes the annual budget.** A buyer applies an OEM advertising maximum to a planned programme dominated by near-constant loading. The funded saving has no matching duty record. Recalculate from both actual packages under that programme and retain the constant-speed offer when the extra investment has no supported return.

**Mistake: the converter arrives without integration.** Another order buys a variable-speed component while controls, cooling and site supply remain in unrelated quotations. No provider accepts the combined duty. Require the machine provider's complete power-package scope and integrated acceptance before award.

## Eight attachments for the forming-lift RFQ

1. Selected crawler or vehicle-mounted scope, destination and equipment identities.
2. Controlled former section/material drawings, installed mass, mounting and utilities schedule.
3. Work positions, outlet/receiver datums and panel length/quantity programme.
4. Ground/support information, access routes and transport/packing boundaries.
5. Actual function sequence and working/waiting calendar for provider duty design.
6. Available electrical source and issued environmental/noise requirements.
7. Complete alternative power scopes, representative test and handover responsibilities.
8. Ownership period, service resources and common cost-comparison boundary.
