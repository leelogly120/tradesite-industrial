An enquiry for tooling can ask for two quite different purchases. One provider may be asked to supply equipment against an identified design; another may be asked to develop a proposed profile whose details are still open. Comparing their prices as if they were the same tool set hides the work and decisions included in each. The useful output is a deliverable comparison that separates the existing design, requested development and eventual machine interface.

The [profile-input guide](/blog/roof-panel-profile-material-tooling-data/) defines the panel and material information. This page addresses what service is actually being bought. Discuss the [related forming-machine scope](/products/#roll-forming-machines) alongside the intended lift. No current custom-design capability or standard development service is established by this article.

## Identify the starting point of the tooling purchase

### Separate a controlled design from a familiar product name

Ask whether the buyer has an identified profile drawing that the responsible project party controls. A named commercial panel type is not the same thing as a defined design basis. If the drawing exists, identify its revision, mandatory interfaces and permitted departures. If it does not, describe the project requirements that remain to be translated into a proposal and name the party who can decide them. This determines whether the provider is being asked to respond to an established product definition or participate in developing one.

### Identify what existing tooling actually means

An existing tool set could belong to the buyer, be documented by a proposed provider or be known only through a previous panel sample. Ask which case applies. Request the available identity and records rather than assuming the supplier owns or can reproduce a design. Previous use can support a conversation about that identified set, but does not establish suitability for the intended material or lifted-machine arrangement. The buyer needs the actual starting object and evidence before asking for a price for reuse, reproduction or replacement.

### Keep roof-product authority outside an undefined service label

When profile development is requested, state who decides the roof connection and product requirements. A tooling provider may be asked to propose geometry, but its commercial participation does not automatically appoint it as the roof-system designer. List the decisions the buyer retains and those it explicitly asks another party to undertake. This makes an unanswered product question visible before it is buried inside a tooling price. The distinction is a purchase boundary, not a conclusion about legal responsibility or design rights in an unknown contract.

## Name the development deliverables being requested

### Ask what the first proposal would contain

Define the output expected from the initial service: for example, a marked proposed section addressing specified project inputs, an explanation of unresolved features or a scoped equipment proposal. These are examples of requested deliverables, not services assumed available. Ask the actual provider to state what it will supply and what information it needs first. A promise to discuss a profile differs from an undertaking to deliver a defined proposal. Identify which result the commercial offer addresses before comparing it with a price for an already identified tool set.

### Separate review rounds from an unlimited result

If a provider proposes a development activity, ask how it describes the included submissions, buyer responses and change boundaries. Avoid assuming that any quoted service includes unlimited revisions or an assured acceptable roof product. The buyer can define which requirements are fixed and which alternatives it wants considered, then ask the provider for an explicit service offer. If a later requirement changes the requested product, both parties need to identify how that change affects the scope. This article sets no rates, timetable or standard commercial entitlement.

### Connect the final tooling proposal to a real assembly

A proposed section is not the same deliverable as supplied tooling, an installed former or an assessed elevated assembly. Request the sequence of commercial outputs in terms of what each offer actually includes, without prescribing the provider's design or manufacturing method. Identify which eventual equipment records must pass to the lift reviewer. The [line-specification guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-specification-long-span-roof-panels/) handles the resulting installed arrangement. A development service that ends with a drawing leaves different purchases outstanding from one offering identified equipment against an agreed drawing.

## Compare existing-design and development offers

### Use one hypothetical project with two starting conditions

Imagine a buyer with fixed mating interfaces but an unresolved part of the profile. Offer A proposes equipment against a provider's documented existing section and requires the project to accept its differences. Offer B proposes a separately priced preliminary development response before any final tool-set offer. These are hypothetical possibilities. A may provide a clearer existing equipment basis while leaving a product departure for the buyer. B may address the open requirement while leaving the eventual equipment and outcome unresolved. Neither should be presented as an assured shortcut to a compatible lifted former.

### Compare what each price actually buys

| Purchased output | Existing-design route | Requested development route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting definition | Identified existing section and material basis | Fixed project requirements plus expressly open features |
| Initial deliverable | Response against that identified design | Actual provider's proposed development deliverable |
| Product departure | Mark differences for the roof owner | Name who decides each proposed feature |
| Included review | State the actual review scope | State included submissions and response boundaries |
| Final equipment | Identify supplied tooling and machine scope | Separate any later equipment offer from the initial service |
| Lift interface | Request records for the identified assembly | Identify when a sufficiently defined assembly becomes available |

The trade-off concerns certainty of the starting design and the scope of additional work. A larger development fee does not prove a better panel; a lower existing-design price does not prove it meets the project's fixed connection requirements.

### Keep evidence and promised deliverables distinct

A provider may show earlier samples to illustrate its proposed service. Ask what those samples actually establish and whether their material and section relate to the current request. They do not substitute for an agreed deliverable. Likewise, a concept sketch can help explain an option without proving that a tool set exists or that its production result is accepted. Compare the actual undertaking, remaining decisions and evidence offered for the buyer's requirement. This prevents an attractive presentation from silently enlarging the service the provider has priced.

## Write a decision-ready tooling-service enquiry

### Include the six scope inputs that matter

- Identify the controlled profile or state exactly which product features remain open for a proposal.
- Attach intended material and sample records, with fixed mating interfaces and permitted alternatives clearly marked.
- State whether the purchase concerns an existing identified tool set, a new supply against a design or a separately requested development service.
- Ask for deliverables, included review scope, excluded work and the party deciding each product question.
- Give the intended outlet height, roof zone and site access context so eventual equipment records connect to lift review.
- Include destination documentation, transport, ground and power assumptions affecting the eventual supplied assembly.

This brief lets the provider decline, qualify or define the requested activity honestly. It does not ask procurement to fill the design gap by inventing a tool configuration.

### Keep commercial stages visible in the reply

Ask the provider to separate any initial service, proposed tooling supply and later machine integration in its reply. Identify which price is firm for an identified deliverable and which part remains a future proposal. Do not insert assumed costs to make the comparison look complete. If the buyer must decide a profile departure before equipment can be offered, make that dependency visible. A conditional route can still be useful when the buyer knows what it buys now and what remains to be decided, priced or technically assessed later.

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise a service offer that may not fit

An offer may not fit when it assumes the buyer has a controlled design that does not exist, or promises an equipment price while excluding the development the buyer expects. A procurement brief cannot establish custom-tooling capability, ownership rights, accepted tolerances or a design result. Tool custody and permitted future use are separate negotiated questions. This article supplies no tooling design, adjustment or manufacturing procedure; it helps the buyer identify the service and equipment boundaries that an actual provider must address before the purchase can be compared meaningfully.

## FAQ

#### Does custom tooling mean a complete new machine?

The actual offer must explain that. Development work, tooling supply and a complete machine are different deliverables. Ask which are included and which eventual assembly records will be available for the lifting-interface discussion.

#### Can an existing section be offered as an alternative?

If the project permits alternatives, ask for its departures to be marked and the relevant product owner to decide them. A similar name or attractive price does not establish equivalence to mandatory roof interfaces.

#### Is a development fee a promise of acceptance?

Do not assume so. Request the actual deliverable and commercial conditions from the provider. A service can propose and assess an option while leaving product acceptance and later equipment supply unresolved.

#### Who owns the resulting drawings and tooling?

That needs actual negotiated terms addressing the identified items and records. This article declares no standard rights. Keep ownership, custody and future-use permissions separate from the technical question of what the proposed tooling would produce.
