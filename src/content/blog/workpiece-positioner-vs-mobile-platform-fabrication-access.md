Buy a purpose-designed workpiece positioner when your fabrication team can put a repeat family of assemblies on accepted fixtures and bring the required faces to suitable working positions. Buy mobile personnel access when the assembly remains fixed and the crew must reach several elevated tasks. If only the early stages of a build can be repositioned, price the fixture for those stages and the platform for the actual work left afterward.

A fabrication director needs to know what the second shift will inherit. Will a recurring frame still be held in the same fixture while it cools, waits for inspection or receives parts? Will a crew have to move to a fixed station after the frame leaves that fixture? A positioner and a personnel platform change different parts of the sequence. Put both into the routing for a representative finished assembly.

## Where does the assembly stop being movable?

Take a common product family from release of its first subassembly to handover of the completed item. List the faces a worker must weld, inspect, fit or finish at each stage. Ask the process engineer which assemblies the shop can present to a positioning specialist and which have already been connected to fixed supports, services or adjacent parts. The finished product's dimensions alone can hide a large change in mass or balance between stages.

Pemamek describes positioners that hold and rotate workpieces and headstock/tailstock arrangements for long components. Koike's selection guidance addresses workpiece clearance and the location of its centre of gravity. Give a specialist the actual assembly and fixture data to select and engineer the arrangement. The procurement decision starts with which production stages are eligible for that workholding method. *For a fixed assembly, put the base position and deck approach on the shop plan together. They occupy space other stations also need.*

Now mark access after the last eligible fixture operation. A frame might receive its final connections after installation in a larger assembly. The personnel approach then depends on the completed shape, the floor and support positions, and any overhead obstructions. Price a broad deck when multiple retained operations can use a continuous, accepted working face. For a single isolated inspection point, request a narrower suitable access offer as a separate comparison.

## Count completed assemblies per fixture booking

A fabricator can mistakenly credit a positioner with every hour formerly spent working overhead. Some of those hours occur after the item leaves the station. Some are replaced by fixture changes, inspection holds or moves between the fixture and the next operation. Build a representative schedule from the actual sequence, including first-off jobs and the product variants your sales team has sold.

For each candidate family, enter the fixture design and validation cost, compatible part revisions, changeover time, floor occupation and handoffs for loading and unloading. Ask who owns the fixture drawings and changed-product review. A stable family that repeats across orders can justify dedicated fixtures. A sequence of one-off assemblies with different attachment points can give more useful work to a mobile personnel platform, provided the offered platform reaches the retained tasks from accepted positions.

A platform quote also needs a full shift on the same schedule. Include the route into the bay once components occupy the floor, people and tools to be accommodated, setup and removal, and any separate material handling. The personnel platform bidder needs the work environment and operations proposed at the deck. Where an operation needs specialist protection or a different access method, price it under that duty and keep it out of the broad-deck utilisation estimate.

## Make the fabrication routing a purchasing document

| Entry to complete for each product family | Who owns it | Effect on the award |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter part and drawing revisions, batch sizes and repeat orders released | Production planner | Shows which fixtures have recurring work |
| Record dimensions, stage-specific mass, centre-of-gravity information and approved attachment interfaces | Design engineer | Gives the positioning specialist a defined workpiece to assess |
| Name each face, operation, process requirement and approved inspection hold | Fabrication and quality leads | Separates repositionable work from fixed-stage tasks |
| Enter fixture occupation, changeover and external handling for one complete cycle | Industrial engineer and handling provider | Establishes how many assemblies the positioner booking can serve |
| Mark fixed-stage work heights from the supporting floor, lateral offsets and clear approach positions | Shop surveyor and supervisor | Defines the personnel platform's working envelope |
| Record accepted floor zones, adjacent bay access and time each resource is occupied | Facilities manager | Prices interference with other production and release dates |

## Two purchasing mistakes illustrated

**Mistake 1 — Buying for the empty frame.** Imagine an offer sized against a bare assembly. The shop wants to keep using the same fixture after adding a large offset component. The process engineer's data for that later stage differ from the original request. Obtain an accepted specialist offer for the changed workpiece or transfer that stage to a separately priced fixed workstation. Keep its hours out of the original fixture case.

**Mistake 2 — Buying a broad platform for a bay that closes around it.** Suppose an access trial uses a clear floor with the workpiece absent. Once the normal transfer trolley and adjacent finished assembly arrive, the proposed deck position blocks output from both bays. Survey the occupied production layout and compare a revised fixed station, a suitable alternative access machine or a separate access window. Include the actual interruption in the platform award.

## RFQ inputs for the two equipment specialists

- Product-family list with released variants, lot sizes and production schedule.
- Assembly drawings with mass, balance and interfaces at each requested positioning stage.
- Operation and inspection sequence identifying when the workpiece becomes fixed.
- Fixture design, review, ownership and changeover responsibilities.
- Material transfer and return arrangements with the relevant handling provider.
- Workface sections with floor datum, offsets, tool access and obstruction dimensions.
- Occupied bay plan showing routes, available support zones and adjacent operations.
- Comparison schedule for completed assemblies, fixture hours and retained personnel-access hours.
