**Choose mobile light towers where work areas change frequently, a released floor position is available and the lighting designer can meet the required illumination without blocking movement. Choose suspended temporary fixtures for stable work areas where an accepted support and supply layout keeps the floor clear through several phases.** Include installation, relocation and final removal. A tower bringing lights to height is lighting equipment; the crew still needs separate personnel access for suspended work.

Start with a dated construction plan. Mark task surfaces, circulation routes, work hours, daylight changes and when each zone moves from site lighting to the permanent installation. A concrete pour, late fit-out and a night delivery can need light in different places. A cheap daily tower rate makes little sense if the tower repeatedly occupies a transport route that must stay open. A tidy overhead installation has its own cost if it must be taken down soon afterwards.

The red mesh deck in the photograph sits beneath roof members. For a suspended lighting proposal, identify the competent installer's positions for supports, wiring and handback. Draw overhead work from an accepted personnel platform independently of the fixtures' light distribution.

## Define the lit task rather than a lamp count

Ask the responsible lighting and electrical parties for the accepted illuminance basis, measurement plane, glare limits and distribution needed for the work and routes in each phase. Include emergency lighting requirements separately under the appropriate design authority. A light tower or a temporary luminaire earns credit only for the areas the proposed arrangement actually serves. Two lamps of similar output can produce different results around high equipment or shadowed workfaces.

[Atlas Copco lists electric light towers](https://www.atlascopco.com/en-uk/construction-equipment/products/light-towers/electric) as a temporary lighting equipment class. [Milwaukee describes a temporary site light with a hanging provision](https://www.milwaukeetool.com/products/details/radius-led-70w-temporary-site-light/2155-ac). These are examples of two arrangements, not a specification for the site. Obtain data for the actual proposed products and power source. The site electrical authority selects and releases the temporary supply and installation.

For a tower proposal, mark the footprint, support condition, permitted route, mast or light-head clearance and power connection or charging arrangements. Have the planner locate the unit during its operating shift and while it is being moved or serviced. Select an indoor-appropriate configuration under the site's environmental and electrical requirements. A tower that fits an empty bay can interfere with the next trade's material route after delivery racks arrive.

## Compare one completed phase at a time

For mobile lighting, price the units required simultaneously, delivery and collection, energy, servicing, changes of position and any protected floor space. Where several phases use the same fleet at different times, avoid buying a separate tower for each phase on paper. Where the phases overlap, show the spare or additional hire explicitly. Test the revised layout after a move against the same acceptance criteria.

For suspended lighting, include design, accepted attachments, wiring, protection, installation access, inspection, relocation and final removal. Agree whether permanent supports or power supplies are genuinely available at the time the temporary work is installed. Price repeated platform visits where partitions or ceilings close at different dates. A building programme promising permanent lighting in one month has little value unless the electrical package and energisation date are actually released.

The wide hall photograph shows a continuous floor area beneath a raised work surface. At the end of the fit-out, newly built booths or stored materials can close that floor. Reserve the personnel route for removal or specify an alternative accepted method. Record the last takedown visit on the programme before the equipment is demobilised.

## Two mistakes in a temporary-lighting budget

**Mistake one — buying nominal output while losing the work plane.** A hypothetical buyer selects the smallest tower count from lamp data. A mezzanine shadows the actual assembly position. Require a phase-specific layout and acceptance measurements before treating the working area as served.

**Mistake two — paying for installation but overlooking strike.** Suspended lighting stays behind a completed service run and the original platform can no longer approach. Price the removal path and work window from the outset, or change the temporary layout before the permanent services close it.
