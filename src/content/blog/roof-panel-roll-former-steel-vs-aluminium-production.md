**Add aluminium production when repeat approved orders support the extra tooling, qualification, handling and stock commitments, and the offered line has demonstrated the required aluminium profile. Keep a qualified steel line focused on steel when those conditions are absent; buy the aluminium panels from a supplier with the required production capability.** Similar panel geometry or nominal sheet thickness is not a business case for treating the materials as interchangeable.

This guide addresses a roofing supplier's expansion decision. The roof designer selects the material and roof assembly for a building. The equipment buyer decides whether to manufacture that defined product internally, source it externally or defer the new product offer.

## Start with customers, then specify the new process

Separate committed aluminium orders from general enquiries. Record the specified alloy and temper, finish, thickness, panel section, length mix and required delivery period. Price the sellable product, including its nominated components and packaging. Do not combine every enquiry containing the word aluminium into one machine-utilisation forecast.

For existing steel work, use actual accepted production and the delivery commitments that the expansion must preserve. If aluminium changeovers displace profitable steel orders, include the cost of replacing or rescheduling that output. A new material family does not create additional machine hours by itself.

[New Tech Machinery describes testing material types, thicknesses and grades during machine development](https://newtechmachinery.com/learning-center/bad-metal-coil-portable-rollforming-problems/). The procurement lesson is to qualify the actual material/profile combination. Its machine material limits belong to that manufacturer's equipment; they are not ARCLIFT ratings.

## Three workable expansion routes

**Keep steel production and purchase finished aluminium panels.** Choose this route for occasional orders or where the internal process investment lacks a supported return. Agree the purchased panel's dimensions, finish, packing and delivery endpoint so the sales team offers a complete product.

**Qualify both materials on one production system.** Choose this route only when the supplier's defined configuration and trial support both product combinations, and the planned changeovers fit the order book. Price the necessary tooling, cutting arrangements, material handling, cleaning and setup procedure. The actual provider defines those tasks for the offered equipment.

**Purchase separate aluminium production capability.** Compare this route when committed volumes or simultaneous delivery periods justify dedicated resources. Include the feed equipment, cutting and runout arrangement, inspection resources and working space. A second former without supporting resources is an incomplete expansion.

These routes are commercial alternatives, not instructions to adjust existing machinery for a different metal. Select the route using the material supplier's records, product requirements and the equipment provider's configuration-specific proposal.

Keep surface expectations measurable through agreed samples and inspection criteria. “Architectural quality” alone leaves the material supplier, machine provider and panel customer pricing different outcomes. A short documented trial with the intended material is more useful than a long demonstration on an unrelated coil.

## Test the full order cycle

The trial plan should follow receipt of the identified material, setup, first accepted output, representative production and release of the finished panels. Where one line will alternate materials, include the transition back to the established steel product. Record accepted output, start-up loss and the resources occupied; do not convert a short uninterrupted run into a promised annual capacity.

The workshop frame below shows why a process proposal is more than a material label. The feed end, forming stands and output route have physical positions that must work with the proposed support equipment and plant layout.

![Grey forming-line frame with rows of silver rollers and blue bearing housings.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261030-line-frame.webp)

*Compare the complete line and supporting route for each material family, including the space occupied during changeover.*

For a site-based business, retain a separate comparison of transporting finished panels versus producing near the building. A forming lift belongs in the package when the required outlet location justifies it. Adding a lift does not qualify a new material or approve a roof assembly.

## Two expansion mistakes to avoid

**Using a steel success as aluminium acceptance.** In this hypothetical order, the buyer approves a steel sample and signs for both material families. The aluminium product has no representative trial or agreed finish criteria. Correct the scope before purchase: identify the aluminium material, profile and acceptance evidence, or keep that product externally sourced.

**Counting sales while ignoring new stock.** A distributor forecasts aluminium revenue but omits minimum coil orders, retained remnants and the time those stocks remain unsold. Recalculate the expansion using actual supply terms and committed panel demand. A small finished-panel order does not automatically justify a dedicated coil inventory.

### Eight attachments for an expansion quotation

1. Committed steel and aluminium orders, separated by product and delivery date.
2. Controlled panel drawings and surface-acceptance requirements for each family.
3. Intended material specifications and traceable trial-coil identities.
4. Existing machine details, production observations and supporting equipment.
5. Proposed changeover sequence and the production periods it must fit.
6. Plant or site layout with coil, line, runout and storage positions.
7. Coil supply terms and a comparable finished-panel supply offer.
8. Destination, power and any elevated-forming outlet and support datums.
