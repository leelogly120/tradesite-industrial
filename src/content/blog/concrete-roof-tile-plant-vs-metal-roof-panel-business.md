Choose metal roof-panel production when supported orders specify formed sheets and the complete coil-to-delivery business earns its investment. Choose a concrete roof-tile plant when customers require that tile product and the funded proposal includes material preparation, tile forming, mould circulation, curing, inspection and dispatch. Start with purchased supply when neither complete business has supported demand and a viable operating plan. A similar roof appearance does not make the factories interchangeable.

This is a choice between different products and production organisations. It is not a choice of a different roller profile on one machine. A metal tile-effect sheet still belongs to a metal-processing route; it does not become a concrete tile because the installed roof has repeated courses.

## Follow each product beyond its shaping machine

[ABECE's concrete-tile process overview](https://www.abece.se/products/the-process) describes batching and mixing, extrusion onto moulds, cutting, curing, separation and mould cleaning. That manufacturer's overview establishes the process category. Its equipment performance and particular curing technology are not assumptions for another plant.

The concrete investment therefore needs a coordinated wet-material and reusable-mould route. The metal-panel alternative needs the actual coil, forming, cutting, receiving and dispatch arrangement for the specified sheet. Each supplier must state where its offered equipment stops and which completion operations the investor obtains elsewhere.

## Choose the customer product before the factory

Obtain actual customer requirements for the proposed launch range. Separate awarded orders, attributable repeat demand and speculative enquiries. Use each product's own demand register. A distributor's existing metal-sheet customers are not automatically buyers of concrete tiles, or the reverse.

For each sold product, identify its governing roof-system requirements and the party accepting it. The building designer selects the roof assembly for its intended building. Factory procurement determines whether the offered process can produce the selected component. Keep those decisions connected without treating a production sample as acceptance of an entire roof.

Include the components sold with the main product. A customer ordering a roofing package needs the specifically agreed ridges, edges, fixings or other finishing items, whether manufactured internally or purchased. List that actual contract basket in the business case; do not invent a universal accessory set.

## Curing and mould circulation change the concrete purchase

For the concrete route, request a process layout showing wet output, its proposed movement into curing, product release, tile separation and return of the moulds. Identify the proposed stock and resources at every stage. The process designer supplies the curing method and required facilities; this procurement comparison supplies no recipe, temperatures or operating settings.

Include the mould range and its support boundary in the quotation. Determine who owns and maintains the moulds and which product changes require additional tooling. Price the specific launch range instead of assuming that a plant offered for one tile accepts every style in a sales catalogue.

Then compare the dispatch promise with the proposed production flow. A tile count at extrusion is not the same as a quantity of accepted packed roofing delivered to a customer. The same commercial principle applies to metal: forming speed alone does not complete cutting, protection and collection of a long sheet.

## Two hypothetical investments with the wrong endpoint

**Mistake: pricing concrete extrusion as a complete factory.** A buyer compares an extruder offer with a complete panel cell, omitting mixing, mould circulation, curing and finished-product handling. Obtain the full proposed process and operating scope before comparing the investment totals.

**Mistake: using one roofing customer list for two markets.** Another investor counts sheet enquiries as tile demand because both products cover roofs. Separate the purchase specifications and customer commitments. Choose the supported first business and purchase the other product externally until it has its own viable case.
