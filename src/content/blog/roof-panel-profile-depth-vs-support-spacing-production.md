## Replace the price-per-sheet comparison

A quotation for roof sheets and a quotation for an installed roof contain different quantities. The product's effective cover, support layout, roof edges, penetrations, flashings and attachments influence the total. Ask each eligible bidder to price the same accepted roof area, completion boundary and programme.

[Tata Steel's Trisobuild information](https://www.tatasteeluk.com/construction/products/roof/profiles/trisobuild) lists distinct roof profiles and provides profile drawings and load/span resources. Request the section drawing and applicable load/span data for each actual candidate so the designer can release its support arrangement.

Give the responsible designer the actual load, deflection, support and attachment brief. Obtain an accepted arrangement for each alternative. The buyer then requests quantities and prices based on those arrangements, rather than extrapolating from a catalogue photograph or the largest headline span.

## Deeper profiles spend material and tooling differently

Record incoming strip width, finished effective cover and relevant section dimensions separately. A changed profile changes how strip becomes installed roof coverage. Obtain the supplier's actual material and output requirements for each accepted section. Compare cost per accepted covered area through the same order mix.

Add tooling, trial material, cutting arrangement, runout supports and packing to the investment case. Include matching closures, flashings and other bought components where the selected product requires them. A new profile family that sells only on occasional projects needs a separate ownership case from the factory's regular range.

## Test the existing support layout first

A new building can allow competing designed support arrangements before structural procurement is fixed. An existing roof or a released frame often presents another decision: the support geometry is already given. Have the designer select eligible roof products for that actual arrangement. Do not charge an impossible support reduction as a saving in a replacement-sheet bid.

Where altering supports is an accepted option, include the actual strengthening, removal, new members, connections and construction access. Keep the completed roof and building constraints common to both bids. A lower panel count does not erase work needed to change the structure beneath it.

This distinction helps the factory choose its product range. Customers regularly roofing an established support geometry support different demand from contractors able to redesign each new frame. Segment the order book by accepted profile/support combinations rather than grouping every enquiry under industrial roofing.

## Two hypothetical complete-roof mistakes

**The panel price wins while support costs vanish.** An estimator compares cheaper shallow sheets with a deeper-profile roof while omitting supports required by the first arrangement. Obtain both accepted support schedules and compare completed roof totals.

**The new line assumes every customer can change the frame.** A producer buys a deeper-profile range based on support savings, but most orders concern fixed existing geometry. Segment committed demand first and invest in the product those roofs actually accept.
