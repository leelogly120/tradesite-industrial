**Choose roof-panel roll forming when repeat accepted orders require weathering sheets and your business can supply the specified panel product. Enter purlin manufacture only when structural product engineering, section and hole schedules, acceptance responsibilities and a complete production route support that separate business. Buy purlins externally when those resources are absent.** A roof-sheet line is not a general-purpose machine for every metal component in a building.

Both businesses use roll forming, but the sold objects perform different duties. The equipment buyer should compare those businesses from customer drawings through accepted delivery. Similar machine colours and the broad label “building profiles” do not establish interchangeable tooling or manufacturing capability.

## Follow the item on the customer's order

A roof-panel order specifies the weathering product, material, profile and cut schedule. The purlin order identifies secondary structural members through the engineer's section, material, length and connection-feature requirements. Preserve the role of the responsible structural designer; the production machine does not supply the building design.

[Bradbury's metal-building equipment overview](https://info.bradburygroup.com/bradbury-group-metal-building-equipment) lists panel production and purlin production as separate equipment families. Its [VTR purlin page](https://www.bradburygroup.com/bradbury-vtr-purlin-line) describes a named section/tooling system and an associated punching arrangement. Those manufacturer examples establish separate process categories. Their section ranges, material limits and output claims do not describe an ARCLIFT machine.

Obtain a representative order for each intended business. Use actual controlled drawings rather than a catalogue assortment that nobody has ordered. The equipment provider needs the complete sold part, including the features the customer expects beyond its basic cross-section.

## A purlin quotation must reach the finished member

Map the operations required by the released member drawings. Identify material preparation, section forming, required features, cutting, identification, inspection and delivery. The actual process provider determines the equipment arrangement for those operations. Do not assume a generic punching accessory completes every connection detail.

Then identify the people who release product changes and accept the manufactured output. Keep manufacturing acceptance distinct from the structural designer's acceptance of the member in the building. A dimensionally accepted sample is not a substitute for the required design responsibility.

## Compare complete factories against supported demand

| Investment field | Roof-panel business entry | Purlin business entry | Owner and decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer product | Controlled panel section and material: ___ | Controlled member section, material and features: ___ | Product/engineering lead; defines the manufactured item |
| Repeat orders | Accepted profile, lengths and release periods: ___ | Accepted member marks, quantities and releases: ___ | Sales and planning leads; separates real demand from an assumed range |
| Operations | Forming, cutting and receiving scope: ___ | Complete operations against every drawing feature: ___ | Process provider; identifies included and outsourced equipment |
| Measurement basis | Cover/profile and ordered-length datums: ___ | Section, length and feature-position datums: ___ | Quality lead; fixes inspection and trial requirements |
| Product authority | Panel/system design and substitution owner: ___ | Structural member/design responsibility: ___ | Customer technical team; prevents machine selection changing the product |
| Handling | Coil, long-sheet receiving and packing routes: ___ | Input, finished-member grouping and dispatch routes: ___ | Operations lead; defines premises and handling resources |
| Acceptance | Representative accepted panel order: ___ | Representative accepted member order: ___ | Customer and quality leads; sets the trial endpoint |
| Complete operating case | Staff, stock, support and delivery costs: ___ | Same cost categories for the full member process: ___ | Finance lead; compares complete businesses over one period |

Use drawing datums for every dimension. A member length and a connection-feature position need their specified references; a panel's feed width and effective cover have different meanings. The quality plan must name the correct features instead of calling all output “accurate profiles.”

## Buying one product externally can strengthen the other investment

A panel producer can purchase the specified purlins from an identified supplier and still offer a coordinated delivery scope where the customer accepts that arrangement. Keep product references, quantities, responsibility and delivery dates explicit. Do not describe bought-in structural members as output of the roof-sheet machine.

Likewise, a purlin business can source roof panels without buying a panel line immediately. The sensible investment is the operation supported by actual orders, competent people and complete resources. A larger invoice containing several building products does not mean that all those products justify in-house manufacture.

## Two hypothetical failures from treating building products alike

**Hypothetical failure case: the panel-line price funds an assumed purlin factory.** A buyer compares basic machine frames and leaves feature production, identification and member inspection outside the budget. Obtain a complete process proposal against the actual structural-member drawings before selecting that investment.

**Hypothetical failure case: coordinated sales become assumed manufacture.** A company sells a building-component package and counts every purchased item as demand for its new sheet line. Separate the products that the proposed equipment actually makes from the products supplied by partners. Test the machine case using only its accepted output.
