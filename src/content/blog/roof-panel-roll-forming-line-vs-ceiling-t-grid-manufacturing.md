Choose a roof-panel roll-forming line when committed roof orders fund its complete route; keep buying grids if roofing is your only supported demand. Choose T-grid manufacturing only with independent accepted grid orders and funded component production through packed dispatch. If both businesses qualify, choose the higher supported contribution after complete installed and continuing costs over the same period, within your cash, space and delivery calendar. First build each business's delivery bill of products and components.

For a contractor or importer allocating manufacturing capital, accepted demand is product-specific. Name the customer, completed output, buying commitment and delivery boundary before comparing machinery. The grid business needs its own accepted orders and complete supply plan. Component balance and completion resources determine saleable shipments alongside the forming rate.

## Start with the shipment sold to the customer

For roof production, name the accepted profile, material, panel lengths, quantities and releases. Keep the agreed accessories and purchased items on the delivery list. The proposed cell runs from actual coil receipt through accepted sheet, removal and the dispatch arrangement for those long products.

For T-grid production, obtain the controlled main-runner and cross-tee family, connector details and accepted mating samples. List the other components actually included in the sold shipment, including bought perimeter items where specified. A T-shaped length is an intermediate product when the customer buys identified compatible grid components in protected boxes.

[Dallan's T-bar manufacturing range](https://www.dallan.com/en/products/rollformers/t-bar-en/) distinguishes main runners, cross tees, galvanized bodies, painted coverings and connector work. This establishes a dedicated multi-component production scope. Qualify its matched grid output against the customer's controlled family; qualify the roof-line alternative against its separate accepted panel drawing.

The roll train below shows ordinary forming equipment, with shaped rolls on successive frames. Use the actual tooling schedule to connect each purchased section to its drawing. The separately sourced grid proposal needs its own specialist equipment and controlled component family.

## Buy completed grid components rather than isolated forming capacity

Write the grid process map by component. [Dallan's D59D main-runner description](https://www.dallan.com/en/products/rollformers/t-bar-en/d59d/) identifies decoilers, forming and press operations for connectors and perforations. Its [D57 cross-tee description](https://www.dallan.com/en/products/rollformers/t-bar-en/d57/) includes dedicated perforation and end-hook processing. Those are distinct purchased functions beyond the visible T section.

A second OEM, [KINGREAL, lists main-tee and cross-tee machinery](https://www.kingreal.org/product-ceiling-t-bar-roll-forming-machine.html), with material feeds and forming/punching/cutting arrangements. Use actual component drawings and acceptance evidence to define your package. A manufacturer's category page is a starting point for the scope, rather than proof that an unspecified line produces your approved grid.

Identify the body-strip and visible-cap feeds, connector operations and remaining completion work by component. Name the provider for every made or bought part. Retain the actual material/finish specification for both stock streams. Have the customer's product authority accept the mating main/cross components and their connection details as the controlled family before crediting them as saleable output.

## Balance the factory around complete deliveries

Build the production calendar from the component counts and due date of a representative grid delivery. Allocate the main and cross-tee routes, bought items and packing releases to that dated shipment. Extra main-runner output earns no dispatch value when the required cross tees or connectors remain unavailable. Include the purchase or bought supply that closes the actual missing scope before ranking the grid proposal.

[Dallan's Coil to Box system](https://www.dallan.com/en/coil-to-box/) shows quality checking and carton packing as real manufacturing functions. Your purchase needs the actual box contents, identification, surface protection and packing trial. Decide whether those functions are included equipment, a separately purchased cell or owner-provided work. Count their people, space and replenishment with the forming machines.

The roof-sheet outlet below represents a different dispatch endpoint. Long profiled panels need the receiving and packing route specified for their own orders. Allocate the separate stock, inspection and collection requirements of both proposals on the shared building plan.

*The roof-line alternative is accepted profiled sheet, with its own receiving and packing route.*

For this worksheet, compare committed completed-order revenue less the complete installed purchase and the continuing resources over the common period. Include trial stock, tools, changes, upkeep, component buying and dispatch. Allocate shared people and space to the actual calendar and count each cost once. Choose between funded positive cases within the available resources; keep the other product bought in when its independent demand fails to support a complete route.

## Two hypothetical capital mistakes

**Mistake (hypothetical): a main-runner sample becomes a complete grid factory.** An importer funds main runners for boxed grid orders but leaves the cross-tee end hooks and mating checks unassigned. The completed runners wait while the shipment lacks accepted cross components. Rebuild the offer from its delivery bill, assign every made or bought part, and qualify a representative completed and packed shipment before committing capital.

**Mistake (hypothetical): unlike line rates decide the investment.** A buyer ranks a grid machine against a roof former by their advertised length output while only roofing has committed customers. Build separate accepted-order calendars, include all completion and dispatch resources, and compare complete investments over the same period. Retain purchased grid supply until its independent demand supports a funded manufacturing route.

## Eight attachments for the roof-production RFQ

1. Accepted roof-panel section, system revision and material/appearance requirements.
2. Actual roof orders with panel lengths, quantities and dated releases.
3. Coil grades, thicknesses, finishes, widths and purchased-stock responsibilities.

6. Identified finished-panel samples, appearance criteria and dispatch acceptance records.
7. Named roof-line boundaries and separately funded grid-equipment or bought-grid scope.
8. Destination, delivery endpoint, common investment period and actual shared-resource limits.
