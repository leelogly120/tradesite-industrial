**Buy a portable roll former tied to one material supplier when enough signed or repeatable work accepts that supplier’s coil and accessories, and its complete delivered basket leaves the margin your business requires. Choose independently sourced equipment when important customers nominate other eligible materials and your team can qualify, buy and support those inputs.** Make this choice before agreeing a machine price or promising a customer that you can form its coil.

This decision concerns two workable production routes for products your customers already accept. The equipment invoice answers how much you pay to acquire the line. The material agreement determines what you can feed into it on every future job. A contractor can own the physical machine outright and still have a continuing exclusive-supply obligation.

## Sort the order book by who supplies the metal

Take actual quotations and repeat jobs from the last complete trading period. For each, identify the named panel, allowed material source, finish, accessory package and the customer’s right to supply coil. Mark three groups. The first permits the proposed exclusive basket. The second specifies a different source. The third needs commercial approval to substitute. In the committed case, count only the undelivered quantities from awarded orders in the first group. Use historical repeat work and open quotations in separate demand scenarios. Written customer acceptance can move a job from the third group into the eligible basket. A sales award is still required before its quantities enter the committed case.

Berridge offers a concrete example. Its licensee programme includes owners and long-term lessees of particular portable formers. It states that the licensee agrees to use Berridge coil and accessories exclusively on the particular machine. Its leasing information also distinguishes machines offered for different types of transaction. Read the written proposal for the exact former and market. Apply its actual obligations to the order book. Consider a customer-owned coil job separately. Even when the stock appears to have the correct width and finish, the job needs both an accepted material and permission to use it on the chosen machine. If the tied route cannot take the order, price a separate independent production source or exclude its contribution. Counting all such jobs as lost revenue also exaggerates the difference if an existing partner can deliver them profitably. Compare the net result after subcontracting.

## Price a complete basket at the delivery point

Quote the same lengths and finish through each source. Enter purchasable coil quantities, accessory pack sizes, freight to the actual forming location, duties where applicable, inventory financing and the value of usable remnants. A smaller delivered batch can leave less cash tied up even at a higher unit price. A large discount on a colour you rarely reorder can leave several jobs’ margin sitting in the rack.

Use three real examples where possible. Select a repeat job in a standard finish, an urgent replacement with a small material requirement and a large project with a specified finish. Ask for offered lead times and price validity for each basket. Record the action available if the nominated material misses the production slot. Where the agreement requires a single source, the fallback can be a revised date or finished-panel supply. Calculate the cost of that fallback instead of assuming that another merchant’s stock can go straight into the machine.

The calculation should show the margin from eligible orders after materials, production labour, travel, partner charges and the actual cost of finance. Compare the capital and fixed running costs over the same period. Give the programme credit for support that replaces a priced task your business would otherwise buy. On the independent route, include qualification and records for each proposed supply source and the cost of maintaining stock in more than one channel.

## Follow one coil from dispatch to site

Name the people who order the metal, receive it, release the correct lot and reconcile unused material. An urgent replenishment from a distant stock point can cost more than the original planned delivery. A just-in-time promise carries value only for a delivery point and date that the supplier has offered. If a portable former will serve several territories, repeat the landed-basket comparison at the least convenient regular destination.

## Two hypothetical mistakes on the purchase order

**The contractor counts a toll-forming client twice.** The model assumes a tied machine will replace subcontract costs on a customer’s own coil. The client retains its nominated metal supplier and declines a substitution. Reprice the job with the partner’s conversion rate and assign only the resulting margin to the business case.

**A low machine price is paired with an uneconomic finish.** The largest project specifies a custom colour. The equipment quote includes no custom coil basket, and the minimum proposed order leaves material without another identified use. Obtain the finish-specific offer and price the unallocated balance before choosing the equipment agreement.
