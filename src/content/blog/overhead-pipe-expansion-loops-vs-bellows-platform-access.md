Choose an engineered pipe expansion loop when its complete envelope fits the coordinated overhead route and the released support arrangement suits the building. Choose a bellows package when the required movement and available space justify that arrangement, with its anchors, guides and service access included in the accepted design. Compare installed pipework for the same operating duty, then select personnel access for its actual workfaces. A bellows unit price and a length of bent pipe are different purchase boundaries.

When both engineered arrangements fit the accepted duty and building, award the lower complete installed offer with the same acceptance boundary. A loop uses building space and additional installation positions. A bellows arrangement introduces a defined component and associated support interfaces. Include those items before ranking the totals.

The guarded deck below illustrates the crew work area that must be coordinated around the proposed pipe route. Put pipe handling, personnel access and temporary storage in separate scope lines before comparing equipment.

## The fitting is only one part of the expansion arrangement

[Spirax Sarco's pipe expansion guidance](https://www.spiraxsarco.com/learn-about-steam/steam-distribution/pipe-expansion-and-support) explains the space required by expansion loops and the role of supports, anchors and guides. It also identifies pressure thrust and restraint as important considerations for bellows. These are reasons to buy a coordinated engineered arrangement rather than exchange components by appearance.

Start both offers from one service specification issued by the piping designer. Identify the pipe route, operating conditions, movement basis and equipment connection boundaries by drawing revision. The designer selects and releases the expansion arrangement. The installer prices that released scope, and the building team accepts the associated structural interfaces. This guide concerns procurement and access, rather than the calculation or installation of pressure pipework.

Reject a comparison that gives the loop all its supports but prices the bellows without the specified anchors or guides. The reverse also distorts the decision: include the loop's complete fabrication, space reservation and installation work instead of counting only straight pipe. The same acceptance boundary must apply to both.

## Fill one sheet for each complete offer

Use building grid lines and the finished-floor datum consistently. The table is a bid comparison record; the responsible designers supply the technical values.

| Input and reference datum | Buyer entry | Responsible party | Decision affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service and operating duty, specification revision | Service ___; duty schedule ___ | Piping designer | Establishes a common technical comparison |
| Expansion envelope, building grids and elevations | Route ___; occupied volume ___ | MEP coordinator | Decides whether the loop fits the building |
| Accepted expansion arrangement and drawing | Loop or bellows ___; drawing ___ | Piping designer | Defines the priced installation |
| Support interfaces, marked structural locations | Anchors ___; guides ___; accepted attachments ___ | Piping and structural designers | Includes all building-side work |
| Crew positions relative to finished floor | Workface ___; height ___; obstruction ___ | Installation planner | Defines access coverage |
| Delivery path at actual installation stage | Opening ___; floor route ___; staging area ___ | Site logistics lead | Determines feasible equipment entry |
| Installed offer at identical handover boundary | Supply ___; supports ___; access ___; inspection ___ | Buyer and estimator | Compares complete eligible cost |

Keep supplier exclusions beside the table instead of hiding them in correspondence. If the building-side anchor work is outside the pipe contractor's offer, assign it a responsible package and an actual amount in the comparison. An exclusion does not make the cost disappear.

## Price the work positions, not just the route length

Ask the installation planner to mark every assembly, support and inspection position on the route. A loop around an obstruction can produce workfaces on different sides of the steelwork. A compact bellows location can still need several crew positions to reach its associated supports. Price access from those positions and the work sequence, using the proposed equipment's actual technical schedule.

The floor photograph shows why that sequence matters. Materials, uneven ground and existing plant occupy the route before a hall is finished. Record the condition at the scheduled installation stage, together with the floor or ground information supplied by the building team.

![An unfinished hall floor contains construction materials and access equipment beneath steel roof framing.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261102-hall-ground-route.webp)
*Budget the route and staging area as they will exist during pipework installation, rather than assuming an empty completed hall.*

Do not treat a broad personnel deck as the pipe lifting plan. Give the equipment provider the crew, tools and materials intended on the deck and their distribution. The installation contractor supplies a separate handling method for pipe sections and assemblies. Compare platform entry, positioning and working coverage against the released workfaces; keep lifting and supporting the pipe in the appropriate specialist package.

An accepted arrangement with lower access cost still loses if it requires an unpriced building change. Conversely, a higher component amount can produce a lower complete offer when the quoted design genuinely removes other priced work. Use actual bid lines and the same scope; avoid assumed percentage savings.

## Two hypothetical pipe-expansion failures

**A compact bellows is bought before its support interfaces are priced.** The purchasing team compares the bellows component with a fully installed loop. Structural attachment work then appears as a change after award. Correct the comparison by requiring the released anchors, guides, attachments and their package owners in both offers before selecting the arrangement.

**The loop fits the drawing but blocks the agreed access workface.** A contractor reserves the pipe volume and forgets the crew positions needed during assembly and later inspection. The access plan requires a different sequence and additional moves. Correct the coordinated layout before ordering fabricated sections, then obtain an equipment offer against the resulting work positions.

These examples describe purchasing failures, not reported ARCLIFT projects. For an actual job, retain the accepted drawings and scope comparison with the order so later changes have a clear reference.

## Eight attachments for the access and pipework enquiries

1. Service specification and designer's operating-duty schedule.
2. Released loop or bellows arrangement with drawing revision.
3. Coordinated plan and sections tied to building grids and finished floor.
4. Support, anchor and guide interface schedule with package owners.
5. Marked crew workfaces and the planned installation sequence.
6. Entry, floor-route and staging photographs with building-team floor information.
7. Deck crew, tool and material schedule, plus the separate pipe-handling scope.
8. Destination, equipment supply boundary and itemised installed-cost comparison.
