Choose the ambient construction period for large-area cold-store ceiling work when the building programme can complete installation and remove the equipment before cooldown. Shortlist a large-deck platform against that construction task. For maintenance inside an operating cold store, purchase an access arrangement whose actual equipment documentation covers the specified environment; do not carry the construction platform into that shortlist solely because it reached the ceiling before refrigeration began.

The two periods buy different capabilities. Construction needs access for assembling a defined ceiling system. The operating store needs access around its finished enclosure, controlled temperatures, contents and maintenance tasks. A single asset is a sensible purchase only when its documented configuration addresses both sets of requirements and the ongoing work justifies retaining it.

## Make two equipment briefs before comparing one-machine ownership

Write the construction brief first: ceiling workfaces, panel or component presentation, floor route, support positions and the sequence before the store closes. Include the removal route and the latest permitted equipment departure, not just the date when the final panel is fixed.

Then write the maintenance brief from the owner's actual tasks. Identify what must be inspected, adjusted or replaced, the finished access routes, operating temperature range and the duration and pattern of exposure. Record whether equipment remains in the controlled environment or passes between environments. The appointed equipment provider must address the exact proposed use in its applicable documents.

These briefs prevent an oversized construction purchase from becoming the default maintenance asset without a second assessment. They also prevent a compact maintenance proposal from silently replacing the shared working area needed during installation.

## Ambient commissioning is not low-temperature qualification

A machine completing work before cooldown has demonstrated a task in those recorded conditions. The operating store introduces another environment, so the supplier's temperature limits and required configuration matter. Fuel or electric drive labels alone do not establish suitability.

JLG's cold-condition guidance ties fluid selection to the applicable machine manual and describes specific cold-weather packages. Its separate freezer application report concerns one identified machine and test. These examples show why the buyer needs equipment-specific environmental information rather than a general assumption about electric platforms. No JLG temperature range, package or result is attributed to an ARCLIFT catalog model here. For the purchase, require a response stating the actual offered machine, documented environment, relevant options and conditions of use. If the offer covers only the ambient construction period, price it for that period. Obtain a separate operating-store access proposal instead of treating a future modification as an included capability.

## Put the cold boundary and the floor boundary on the worksheet

The refrigeration commissioning date is a programme decision. The floor assessment is an engineering decision. Neither can be supplied by an equipment salesperson's general assurance that the machine is suitable for warehouses.

| Purchasing input | Fill with the actual project value | Information owner | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Construction environment | Enter expected ambient range and dates for each work zone | Main contractor and commissioning lead | Defines the initial equipment duty |
| Operating environment | Record room temperature range, exposure pattern and transitions | Cold-store owner | Establishes the separate environmental qualification required |
| Ceiling work | Name component sizes, task positions and material presentation | Ceiling installer | Determines shared-deck demand during construction |
| Workface geometry | Enter surveyed ceiling elevation from the actual supporting-floor datum and the required horizontal offsets | Surveyor and ceiling installer | Connects the offered platform arrangement to the named workfaces |
| Finished access route | Enter room, door and turn dimensions from the issued layout | Building surveyor | Tests retained-equipment access after completion |
| Supporting floor | Attach finished floor build-up and structural records for named positions | Building engineer | Connects proposed machine reactions to the actual floor |
| Maintenance assignment | List inspection, adjustment and replacement tasks with visit frequency | Facilities team | Decides whether a construction-sized asset earns a permanent role |
| Completion boundary | Enter departure, cleaning and cooldown milestones and quoted costs | Programme owner and procurement lead | Compares retention with a separate later access package |

Surface protection is an additional scope, not a replacement for the floor assessment. Include floor finishes and hygiene requirements in the brief, with the parties responsible for the accepted protection and cleaning arrangements. A wide track or a clean-looking tyre is not the evidence needed for those decisions.

## Complete the work without trapping the platform in the finished store

Reserve a route for the actual removal configuration before final doors, partitions and services are completed. Obtain the supplier's dimensions for that configuration. An opening sized for goods handling after handover can differ from the route needed to remove construction equipment.

The low-position platform in the photograph helps frame this question: the complete machine includes its base and visible side assembly as well as the deck. Use the offered arrangement's actual drawings to define the removal envelope.

![A green folded platform assembly with a side boom, tracks and extended supports stands outside a workshop.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261029-deck-folded.webp)

Where later ceiling inspection requires access, price that task explicitly in the operating-store package. Retaining construction equipment simply because it is already inside is a poor substitute for selecting the maintenance arrangement. Conversely, a documented machine that serves both duties can be compared on its complete ownership and support scope, with the two periods kept visible.

## Two illustrative purchasing mistakes

**Mistake: a successful ambient handover is treated as freezer acceptance.** The installer completes the ceiling before cooldown and the owner keeps the platform for maintenance. The order contains no operating-temperature scope. Separate the two duties before the purchase and obtain the actual environmental response for any machine intended to remain.

**Mistake: the installed ceiling is finished before the exit is resolved.** The access equipment fits through a construction opening, but final insulated doors are installed before its departure. The buyer has priced equipment delivery without a workable removal package. Reserve the route and its release milestone, or select another access arrangement while the enclosure design is still being coordinated.

Attach eight items to the RFQ:

- Reflected ceiling plan and sections with surveyed workface elevations, supporting-floor datums and horizontal offsets.
- Component dimensions and the people, tools and materials involved in each task.
- Ambient construction conditions and the planned refrigeration commissioning date.
- Operating-room conditions and maintenance tasks if retention is being compared.
- Arrival and removal routes, including final doorway and partition drawings.
- Floor build-up, structural information and proposed support locations.
- Power, hygiene, cleaning and site-management requirements with named owners.
- Destination and separate quoted scopes for construction use and later access provision.
