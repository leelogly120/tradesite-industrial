Use an existing workshop when its professionally reviewed layout, infrastructure and permitted use serve the complete supported roof-panel business, including the actual product lengths and logistics. Commission dedicated premises when a documented continuing requirement cannot be met by suitable existing sites and the full site-development and production-launch plan is supported. Compare complete usable production premises, not rent against a bare building shell.

The property transaction and development decisions need the appropriate local professional review. The equipment buyer's contribution is a precise production, material and site brief that makes those reviews and quotations comparable.

## Begin with the longest product route, not the former footprint

Draw the complete operation: inbound material, coil storage, feeding, forming and cutting, panel receiving, inspection, packing, storage and loading. Use the actual delivered dimensions and intended installation arrangements of each machine and the dimensions of the supported customer products.

Mark columns, door openings, external vehicle routes, floor references and proposed equipment support positions. The competent site and equipment specialists determine the actual building, floor and machinery interfaces. Total floor area is not an answer to whether a long panel can reach dispatch without conflicting with another operation.

## Compare existing premises with the same finished endpoint

For an existing building, identify necessary alterations, utilities, handling resources, floor/site assessments and any restrictions relevant to the proposed operation. Obtain actual commitments for the work required before machinery installation and accepted production. Have the advisers establish the property arrangement, permissions and obligations applicable to that site.

For dedicated premises, include site selection, design, approvals, construction, utilities, external logistics and the handover sequence to machinery installation. The process ends with a usable production operation, not simply completion of the building structure.

The [U.S. Small Business Administration's location guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/) highlights that buying, renting or building premises still involves location requirements. It is a U.S. business guidance example, not an approval for another jurisdiction. Use the actual local professional and authority requirements for the intended property.

## Issue a production-premises worksheet

| Site requirement | Fillable buyer record and measurement basis | Owner | Effect on premises choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supported products | Section/material ___; longest and typical ordered dimensions ___ | Product and sales leads | Defines the real finished-product flow |
| Complete cell | Proposed equipment layout/revision ___; included and retained machines ___ | Integration lead | Sets the installation requirement |
| Material route | Receipt, storage, feed and handling positions ___ | Material/operations leads | Tests actual inbound movement and stock arrangement |
| Output route | Runout, inspection, packing, storage and dispatch dimensions ___ | Production/logistics leads | Identifies building geometry that limits accepted products |
| Building interfaces | Measured openings/columns ___; floor datum ___; assessed support/utility scope ___ | Site specialists | Establishes required enabling work |
| Property/development | Reviewed use and transaction arrangement ___; required work/providers ___ | Property and local professional advisers | Identifies obligations and permissions outside the machine quote |
| Launch dependencies | Site, utilities, machinery, trial and handover milestones ___ | Project manager | Compares actual readiness for accepted orders |
| Complete commitment | Premises, alterations/development, utilities, installation and operating resources ___ | Financial advisers and buyer | Compares matching usable-production endpoints |

Record both the largest supported order and the normal product mix. Do not let one exceptional uncommitted panel length dictate a permanent factory without a separate commercial case. Equally, do not advertise that length from premises whose documented route cannot serve it.

## Keep future expansion specific

Identify the actual expansion duty: another supported profile, more accepted output of the existing range, longer products, additional inspection or a different dispatch pattern. Place the corresponding equipment and resources on the candidate layout and identify which interfaces change.

“Room to grow” is not a measurable requirement by itself. A documented reserved area and an identified future duty are useful. An unpriced intention to move columns, change access or acquire neighbouring land is not an existing resource. Keep future development scenarios separate from the usable opening plant.

For a first market entry, compare purchasing accepted finished panels while the product business is tested. Dedicated construction is a separate commitment from ordering a former. The [service-centre make-or-buy guide](/blog/steel-service-centre-add-roof-panel-roll-forming-vs-buy-panels/) helps define that initial product and resource case.

## Work from the first accepted customer delivery

Set the defined accepted order and required delivery milestone. The planner places the actual site, machinery, staffing and trial commitments behind it. When the premises route cannot meet that commitment, use an identified outside supply route or revise the launch order rather than treating unfinished enabling work as available production.

The machine provider needs the actual site interface information when preparing its proposal. It does not own the property's legal use, building suitability or entire construction programme simply because its forming line appears on a layout.

## Two hypothetical premises mistakes

**Failure case: the former fits but the panels cannot leave.** A buyer chooses a building from machine footprint and area, then discovers columns or loading geometry prevent the ordered finished lengths reaching dispatch. Test the complete product route on a measured plan before committing the premises.

**Failure case: an existing shell is compared with a finished new factory.** The rental column omits alterations, utilities, handling and enabling work while the construction column includes them. Reprice both to the same usable production endpoint and have the relevant advisers review the actual commitments.

## Send a site-backed machinery brief

Attach eight items to the RFQ:

1. Supported products and actual order-length/quantity mix.
2. Measured candidate-site plans with openings, columns and reference datums.
3. Complete material-to-dispatch flow and handling routes.
4. Existing and proposed equipment identities and layouts.
5. Site-specialist floor, support, utility and interface information.
6. Defined buyer works and responsible providers.
7. Site, installation, staffing and acceptance programme.
8. Destination, delivered-equipment scope and product trial requirements.
