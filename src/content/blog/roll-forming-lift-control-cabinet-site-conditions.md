Prefer a sheltered cabinet position when the equipment designer can preserve the required controls, service access and cable arrangement there. If the cabinet must remain exposed beside the lift or travel with the former, buy a configuration assessed for that location's rain, dust, temperature and cleaning conditions. Reject an offer described only as "outdoor cabinet" when it gives no location-specific enclosure and environmental schedule.

The cover shows a cabinet low on a crawler arrangement, sharing the ground working zone while the former sits above it. Give each enclosure its own identifier and exposure record: lift, former and operator-station enclosures can occupy different environments on the same job.

## A dry location and an enclosure rating solve different problems

Coordinate the enclosure schedule with the [electrical and control interface guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-electrical-control-interfaces/) so environmental requirements and installation responsibilities describe the same equipment.

Compare the proposed location with a sheltered alternative. Reducing exposure can simplify the environmental duty, but relocation changes access, cable routes and the equipment interface. Have the equipment designer assess that trade-off before changing the layout.

An IP code describes enclosure protection against access to hazardous parts and ingress of solids and water. It must be selected for the installation conditions, as the [Swedish Electrical Safety Authority explains](https://www.elsakerhetsverket.se/en/professionals/manufacturers-importers-and-distributors/the-different-product-requirements/other-markings/ip-code/). Do not turn a generic "outdoor" description into an assumed IP number.

Treat heat and moisture as separate inputs. [Rittal's outdoor enclosure guidance](https://www.rittal.com/us-en_US/Solutions/Rittal-Product-Solutions/outdoor-enclosure-solutions) distinguishes covered and exposed environments, with temperature variation, humidity, UV and salt among selection factors. Use these factors to specify the proposed enclosure's environmental limits for your recorded site conditions.

Use the elevated forming photograph below to distinguish ground and elevated exposure. Mark every proposed enclosure on your drawing, including the position used after the shift.

## Fill the site schedule before choosing the cabinet package

Complete this table for each enclosure. The site team records conditions; the supplier returns the location and proposed response. Recheck affected rows after any location change.

| Site input to fill | Measurement datum or record | Responsible party | Decision changed by the answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cabinet ID: ___; proposed position: ___ | Plan grid, height above setup ground and face orientation | Site manager marks; equipment designer locates | Retain the proposed position or assess a sheltered alternative |
| Rain and splash exposure: ___ | Photos at the cabinet face; roof runoff and puddle paths on plan | Site supervisor | Select the enclosure duty and any designed shielding |
| Cleaning method: ___; chemicals: ___ | Cleaning contractor's method and product data | Buyer and cleaning contractor | Separate normal weather exposure from washing requirements |
| Dust source: ___; nearby cutting/grinding: ___ | Work-zone map and task schedule | Main contractor | Relocate exposure sources or select a compatible enclosure arrangement |
| Ambient range: ___; sun exposure: ___ | Site design weather record; orientation and shaded/unshaded periods | Project engineer | Define environmental limits and thermal assessment inputs |
| Humidity, condensation or salt exposure: ___ | Site environmental record and overnight storage conditions | Buyer supplies; cabinet designer evaluates | Include the necessary moisture and material-protection provisions |
| Access space: ___; service route: ___ | Door swing, ground access path and cable route on layout | Equipment designer and installation contractor | Preserve service access and an agreed installation route |

**Choose the sheltered arrangement** if the designer accepts the location and the site can keep that shelter and access available through every planned setup. Include the shelter provider and its arrangement in the order scope. A roof over one pad is insufficient if the next working position is open.

**Choose an exposed-duty proposal** if the cabinet's approved location remains outdoors. Compare the completed assembly, including cable entries and proposed ventilation, cooling or moisture-control provisions. Require its environmental limits, maintenance instructions and conditions of use in the offer.

The folded crawler unit below highlights another exposure stage: overnight parking. Name who provides storage protection and include the supplier's restart instructions in the handover.

![Folded red crawler lift standing outdoors on outriggers beneath a cloudy sky](/images/equipment/transport-real-stowed.webp)

*Describe the overnight parking location separately from the forming position and the transport arrangement.*

## Avoid these two location failures

The following examples illustrate scope mistakes rather than reported installations.

**Failure 1: the second setup pad loses the shelter.** A purchaser describes the cabinet as under cover because the first roof zone is beside a finished wall and canopy. The next zone sits beyond the canopy, but the same proposal is reused. Review all working positions on one plan. Specify the exposed condition from the outset, or make a designer-approved sheltered position part of every setup.

**Failure 2: cleaning is omitted from the weather brief.** The RFQ describes occasional rain, while the adjacent coil-handling lane is washed and splashes the lower cabinet. The cleaning contractor's method arrives after equipment selection. Add the cleaning path and method before ordering. If the proposed cabinet package excludes that duty, change the cleaning arrangement with the site team or select a suitable package through the equipment designer.

A sheltered-only proposal does **not fit** if the site cannot maintain its specified shelter at every position. Reject a layout when the site cannot provide controlled service access during maintenance; revise the route or work schedule before ordering. Return changes affecting the equipment arrangement to its designer for review and pricing.

## Send eight RFQ inputs for the environmental scope

Include this enclosure schedule with the wider [lift RFQ data package](/blog/high-altitude-roll-forming-lift-rfq-data/) so the site and equipment drawings use the same positions.

- Cabinet identification list separating lift, former and operator station, with proposed locations on a site plan.
- Roof section, outlet height datum and setup positions, including enclosure height above ground at each location.
- Photos showing rain paths, roof drainage, splashes, dust-producing work and the surrounding work zone.
- Site ambient temperature and humidity records, sun orientation, salt exposure and overnight storage arrangement.
- Cleaning method, washing route, cleaning frequency and chemical product information supplied by the contractor.
- Ground access drawing showing cabinet door space, outriggers, coil-handling routes and maintenance access.
- Power characteristics and cable-route plan, plus chassis scope and transport or storage protection responsibilities.
- Destination country, required documentation, enclosure schedule and the named buyer representative accepting the environmental scope.
