Write where the control cabinet will sit and what weather, dust, or wash-down reaches it. Do that on the RFQ, not in a separate essay. The eight-line file is the [RFQ data guide](/blog/high-altitude-roll-forming-lift-rfq-data/). Supply and interface boundaries stay in the [electrical interface guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-electrical-control-interfaces/).

| Condition you can point at | Write it as | If the location is still open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cabinet on the lift, on the former, or on the ground | Which enclosure the sentence is about | Confirm on RFQ |
| Rain, dust, or a wash-down lane | The one that actually hits that spot | Say unknown |
| Sun on a metal wall all afternoon | The face and the hours | Measure on site |
| A different cabinet for the former | Keep the two names apart | Do not copy one answer onto both |

Two ways this goes wrong. A brochure photo of an indoor cabinet is accepted for a machine that stands in the rain beside the eave. Or the former's enclosure is described, and everyone treats that sentence as cover for the lift cabinet that sits lower, closer to the coil and the wash-down.

![Long panel beside elevated forming equipment](/images/equipment/coil-real-panel.webp)

*Reference photograph of a panel path. It cannot locate the cabinet.*

Put these lines on the RFQ with the height class:

- Which cabinet, and whether a second one belongs to the former
- Rain, dust, or wash-down at that spot, or unknown
- Catalog screen: ARC-T18 at 18.5m, ARC-T25 or ARC-C25 at 25m
- Sheet class only if ARC-RF8 is in the same buy: 0.3–1.0mm is the catalog band, not your coil
- Destination country
- Who signs the exposure note
