A control cabinet shown in a roll-forming lift proposal needs to be assessed in the location where the equipment is actually intended to work. Give the provider a clear account of the site's exposure conditions and ask it to identify the offered enclosure and applicable equipment information. A cabinet photograph or a broad outdoor description cannot establish suitability for every site.

This guide produces a location-and-exposure brief. It specifies no enclosure rating, temperature limit or protection method. The [electrical interface guide](/blog/roll-forming-line-electrical-control-interfaces/) covers supply and system boundaries; here the buyer's useful output is an attributable description of the conditions the cabinet proposal must address.

## Identify the cabinet and its proposed location

### Name the actual equipment enclosure

Ask the provider which cabinet or enclosure its response describes and how it relates to the offered lift configuration. If the former has a separate cabinet, keep that identity distinct. A seller's answer about one enclosure should not become a statement about every electrical component in the forming package.

The [crawler forming-lift family](/products/#crawler-roll-forming-lifts) raises a compatible former toward the roof receiving level. That physical arrangement can involve equipment at different positions, so the buyer should identify the actual object whose exposure is being discussed rather than using the whole machine as a single location.

### Mark the location on the current proposal

Use the provider's actual arrangement and the site's layout to identify the proposed cabinet position. Distinguish a cabinet attached to the offered assembly from a separately positioned item if the provider describes it that way. Do not choose a new mounting location to solve a perceived exposure problem.

Where the location is undecided, state that clearly. The provider can explain what information it needs, while the buyer avoids obtaining an apparently definite suitability answer for a position no one has actually selected.

### Separate observation from interpretation

Describe visible or documented site conditions in ordinary terms: an exposed building edge, nearby work producing dust, a washdown activity described by the facility, or a proposed indoor storage area. Attribute the information to the site records or responsible owner. Do not translate those descriptions into an enclosure rating or conclude that a cabinet is protected.

A useful brief says what is known and where it applies. The equipment provider and responsible project reviewer must interpret the implications for the actual proposal.

<a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/introduction.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE's electrical-safety introduction</a> relates assessment to the equipment, its use and its environment. This Great Britain context explains why the actual building phase and exposure belong in the supplier brief. It does not select an enclosure rating or protection measure, or establish that the proposed cabinet suits the reported conditions.

## Describe exposure across the intended project phases

### Distinguish work from storage and delivery

The location considered during the forming task may differ from the place used for delivery, temporary storage or other project phases. Identify those phases as separate questions when they are part of the buyer's plan. Do not assume that a response for the working position also describes transport or storage conditions.

The [destination receipt guide](/blog/roll-forming-lift-destination-receipt-plan/) covers the broader arrival arrangement. The exposure brief should carry forward the actual receiving and storage information that affects the cabinet enquiry without prescribing protective coverings or handling steps.

### Record nearby activities that change the context

Ask the site team which surrounding activities are relevant to the proposed location and whether they change during the project. A building that appears enclosed in an early drawing may still have open areas during the planned equipment phase. A clean area during inspection may later host another trade's activity.

Keep these as attributable project conditions rather than predictions of equipment damage or performance. The provider needs the changed context to answer the actual enclosure question; the buyer should not invent a generic environmental limit.

### Identify missing records as missing

If the facility cannot yet provide a requested environmental record, identify the gap and its owner. Do not estimate a value merely because the supplier form has a numeric field. Ask the provider what information is necessary for its response and the site party how that information can be obtained through the project's arrangements.

The result can remain a useful preliminary brief with open items. A completed-looking form filled with unsupported assumptions is less informative than a short account of the actual location and the missing evidence.

## Compare enclosure answers against the same exposure brief

### Follow a hypothetical building-phase change

Imagine a roof project where the buyer initially describes the equipment as working inside a completed hall. The programme later places forming activity before one side of the building is enclosed. Provider A's earlier response refers to the completed-hall description. Provider B has only a generic cabinet photograph in its offer.

The buyer should send the changed site description to both providers. A needs to identify whether its earlier response still addresses the proposal. B needs to identify the actual enclosure and equipment information behind its offer. Neither photograph nor previous wording resolves the changed exposure context.

### Use a location-and-exposure matrix

| Brief field | Site information to provide | Equipment response to request | Unresolved question |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Cabinet identity | Proposed equipment and provider | Actual enclosure covered by the response | Is a separate former cabinet outside the answer? |
| Working location | Current layout and building phase | Conditions addressed by the offered equipment information | Does the reply assume a completed enclosure? |
| Nearby activities | Site-owner description of relevant work | Additional information needed for assessment | Which activities were absent from the original brief? |
| Storage context | Actual proposed storage location and period context | Applicable equipment information | Is storage outside the working-position response? |
| Delivery boundary | Receiving and transport arrangement | Relevant scope and exclusions | Which party owns the remaining destination question? |
| Changed conditions | Specific difference from the earlier brief | Revised or retained response with stated basis | Which earlier conclusion must remain open? |

This matrix identifies inputs and answers. It contains no IP classification, ingress claim, exposure threshold or recommended protection measure. A provider's actual technical record must define the equipment it offers.

### Interpret a conditional reply accurately

A provider may respond only after receiving a more precise location description or other site information. Record that dependency instead of treating the absence of a firm answer as proof of suitability or failure. If the offered configuration changes, ask for the revised identity and affected documents.

The trade-off is between comparing generic enclosure descriptions and comparing actual responses to a shared site brief. A more detailed answer can reveal useful limitations, but detail alone is not engineering acceptance. The responsible project review still needs to assess the actual proposal.

## Request a location-specific enclosure response

<!-- audit-section: limitations-not-fit -->
### Recognise an exposure assumption that remains unresolved

A proposal may not fit a final site decision when the cabinet identity or intended exposure is unknown, or when the supplier's response assumes a different building phase. Do not resolve the gap by proposing a cover, relocating the cabinet or selecting a rating from a general reference.

This guide supplies no environmental performance, enclosure limit, ventilation design or electrical protection method. The photographs do not show a verified enclosure specification. The buyer's role is to provide the real conditions and obtain the actual equipment response from the responsible provider.

<!-- audit-section: project-checklist -->
### Prepare the exposure brief

- Identify the lift cabinet and any separate former or feed-equipment enclosures, retaining each actual provider's scope.
- Mark the proposed cabinet location, roof receiving geometry and outlet height context on the current equipment and site layout.
- Describe relevant site exposure and nearby material-related activities using attributable observations rather than inferred enclosure requirements.
- Include ground or floor and access restrictions that affect the location discussion, without recommending relocation or protection work.
- State destination, transport receiving and storage context separately from the intended forming position.
- Ask for the offered power and control enclosure identity, applicable documentation, missing inputs and response to any changed building phase.

When a condition changes, highlight that difference rather than resending an unexplained set of photographs. The provider needs to know which earlier assumption the new information replaces so the answer remains traceable to the actual proposal.

## FAQ

#### Does a cabinet photograph establish its rating?

No. Ask the actual provider for the identity and applicable equipment information. Appearance cannot establish an enclosure classification or the conditions it addresses, and a photograph from another arrangement should not be treated as the proposed cabinet specification.

#### Can the buyer simply request an outdoor cabinet?

That phrase is too broad to describe the actual site by itself. Explain the proposed location, building phase and relevant activities, then ask what information the provider needs. This guide does not define a universal outdoor configuration or establish its availability.

#### Should storage be included in the same enquiry?

Include it as a distinct phase if it belongs to the buyer's actual plan. Identify the proposed location and ask for applicable equipment information. Do not assume that an answer for the working position automatically covers storage or transport conditions.

#### What if the building enclosure changes before arrival?

Identify the changed condition and ask the provider which earlier response remains applicable. Keep the new site description linked to the actual equipment identity. A revised building phase can require a new answer without implying any particular technical remedy.
