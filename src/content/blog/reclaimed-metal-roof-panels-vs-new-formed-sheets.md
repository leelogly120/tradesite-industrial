Choose reclaimed panels when the identified recovered material is accepted for the new roof, the usable quantity covers the planned zones, and recovery through installation costs less than the accepted new-sheet route. Choose new formed sheets when the recovered lot cannot meet those conditions. Buy forming equipment only for the new production workload that remains after this choice; reclaimed supply creates a recovery and integration task, not a coil-to-panel production order.

The decision starts with the destination roof. A low price for a dismantled lot becomes useful only when those particular pieces can serve the specified assembly. Keep the donor building, recovered inventory and receiving roof connected in one purchase record.

## Buy an identified usable lot, not a photographed area

A reclaimed offer should identify the material being sold, its source and the available condition records. Have the responsible roof specialist define the investigation and acceptance required for its intended reuse. The new roof designer then controls whether that material, geometry and connection arrangement belong in the receiving project.

Reuse is a real material route. [RHEINZINK describes intact titanium-zinc panels removed from the Kiasma Museum and intended for further use](https://www.rheinzink.com/sustainability/sustainable-planning-and-building/circular-economy/). That maker example concerns identified material from a particular building. Apply the same discipline to the buyer's actual lot: name its product, document its condition and define its accepted new purpose.

Separate pieces available before dismantling from pieces released after recovery. The purchase quantity should distinguish accepted full lengths, accepted shorter pieces assigned to particular zones, material requiring specified repair, and rejected material. Count only the accepted categories in the destination roof coverage.

Existing holes, seams, edges and handling damage belong in the assessment. The specialist determines the acceptable treatment and connections. Procurement then prices that work. A blanket allowance called “minor repairs” hides both the usable quantity and who must deliver it.

## Match recovery to the receiving roof before buying a former

Map each accepted length group to an actual roof zone. A recovered lot with enough total area still fails the supply plan if its lengths or mating details cannot serve the released layout. Where the designer accepts a mixed new-and-reclaimed solution, show its boundaries and components explicitly.

A small new-panel balance is usually a reason to obtain a finished-sheet quotation first. Compare external manufacture with ownership using that balance and other supported new orders, rather than the whole roof area. If recurring new production justifies a line, its tooling proposal must follow the accepted new profile and material.

This differs from [retaining an old roof for overcladding](/blog/metal-roof-overcladding-vs-tear-off-forming-lift-package/). Overcladding keeps the existing roof in its building and adds an accepted assembly above it. Reclaimed procurement removes material, sorts it and installs accepted pieces in a different defined arrangement. Those steps create their own yield and custody obligations.

## A lot-to-roof purchase schedule

Complete the entries with the recovery contractor, roof designer and receiving installer. Use one agreed finished roof boundary for both alternatives.

| Purchase field | Buyer entry to complete | Responsible owner | Decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material identity | Enter donor project, product records and lot IDs ___ | Seller and assessing specialist | Establishes the material offered for reuse |
| New roof acceptance | Record accepted system revision and permitted lot uses ___ | Receiving roof designer | Admits or excludes reclaimed material |
| Released inventory | Enter accepted quantities by length, profile and condition ___ | Recovery quality lead | Sets the real available supply |
| Integration work | List repairs, components and mixed-material boundaries ___ | Roof specialist and installer | Prices a complete fitted assembly |
| Recovery handover | Enter dismantling, sorting, packing and inspection responsibilities ___ | Buyer and recovery contractor | Allocates loss before acceptance |
| New-sheet balance | Record zone IDs, quantities and delivery dates ___ | Procurement lead | Defines bought-sheet or forming demand |
| Delivery route | Enter storage positions, receiving arrangement and release dates ___ | Logistics and site leads | Tests whether the usable lot arrives in sequence |
| Complete price | Enter recovery, treatment, transport, installation and new balance costs ___ | Commercial lead | Compares accepted roofs on the same basis |

Use the released inventory as a commercial milestone. If the seller quotes a lot before dismantling, specify how the final accepted quantity changes the price and what happens to shortfalls. The roof programme needs a dated alternative for missing pieces, particularly where a rare section prevents quick replacement.

## Price the two material streams separately

The reclaimed stream includes careful removal, segregation, inspection, any accepted treatment, packing, storage, delivery and installation. The new stream includes material, production, quality release, delivery and installation. Shared roof work, such as designated components and final completion, stays visible in both totals.

For an equipment investment, add the former, material handling, working space, staffing, trials and the route from output to the roof. A discounted machine does not eliminate the second material stream's operating costs. Conversely, a large recovered lot does not remove every new component or replacement piece.

![Long blue and orange forming-machine assemblies rest on a green-sided truck inside a workshop beneath crane hooks.](/images/equipment/buyer-20261110-former-truck-workshop.webp)
*Include mobilisation and handling in the new-production alternative before comparing it with delivered reclaimed supply.*

Set the decision at the usable lot release and the supported new-order schedule. Choose reclaimed supply where that complete case wins. If recovery leaves a large uncertain balance, buy new sheets for the required completion date rather than committing production equipment against an unmeasured shortfall.

## Two hypothetical purchasing failures

**Failure case: paying for the donor roof area.** A contractor buys all sheets visible on a donor building and uses that area as the receiving roof quantity. After dismantling, damaged lengths and unsuitable joints leave several zones uncovered. Purchase against assessed and released inventory, identify the shortfall supply, and price the accepted quantity rather than the original building area.

**Failure case: buying a line for the whole replacement roof.** Another buyer approves a reclaimed lot covering the main zones but still justifies a former using the total roof area. Only a small new balance reaches the line. Recalculate utilisation from actual new sheets and recurring orders; purchase the balance externally if ownership loses that comparison.
