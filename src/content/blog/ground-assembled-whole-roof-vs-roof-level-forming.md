**Choose ground assembly and a specialist whole-roof lift when the accepted erection design includes that route and the site can accommodate its complete assembly operation. Buy roof-level panel forming when the erected structure leaves a defined sheet-installation programme at height.** If the roof is lifted as a bare frame, retain the later cladding equipment in the budget. Moving structural assembly to ground level only removes the roofing tasks actually completed before the lift.

This is a decision about the building roof's erection sequence. It differs from suspending a forming machine from a crane during production. The object being lifted, the engineering package and the remaining site work all change.

## Buy the sequence before its individual machines

The main contractor needs two coordinated proposals: the roof designer and erection specialist's accepted sequence, and the roofing contractor's production and installation plan within it. Place both on the same building layout. A quotation for lifting a frame ends at a different point from a quotation for delivering a weathered roof.

Whole-roof lifting is an established specialist category. [Enerpac's documented roof-lifting application](https://www.enerpac.co.jp/products/integrat_solutions/strand_jacks/application/hsl_series04.html) describes steel roofs assembled at ground level and raised with strand jacks. That example supports the existence of the erection route. The project's engineering package defines which members, sheets and other components belong in its own lifted assembly.

The forming equipment then follows the issued sheet schedule. Ground assembly creates a ground-level production opportunity only where the planned roof package includes that sheet work before lifting. Where sheets remain a later trade, compare roof-level forming with the other accepted panel-supply routes for that later stage.

## Separate the three completion states

Mark the roof's state before lifting, immediately after permanent connection, and at roofing handover. This simple sequence prevents both missing and double-counting equipment.

Before lifting, the assembly specialist identifies the work included in the ground operation and the space it occupies. The roof contractor identifies accepted sheets, closures or interfaces installed at that stage. The lifting specialist prices the engineered lifting operation, temporary arrangements and handover boundary.

After connection, list every retained roofing task by location. These can include the project's specified closures, interfaces or entire sheet runs. Price access, material presentation and production against that actual list. Keep the specialists' responsibilities attached to those tasks rather than assuming the lifting contractor delivers the final roof.

At roofing handover, use the same accepted completion point for both routes. A proposal that finishes with the roof connected but leaves cladding to another contractor has a different scope from a complete roofing package. Reconcile those differences before comparing the equipment investment.

## Test whether the ground assembly area survives the programme

A whole-roof assembly footprint competes with foundations, deliveries, stored steel, other trades and the future building interior. Ask the main contractor to allocate the actual area and period. An empty site drawing at tender stage is insufficient if those spaces have already been assigned by the erection date.

For the elevated forming route, show the exterior setup, coil supply and panel receiving arrangement in the corresponding building stage. Identify which later works close that route. The relevant comparison is the real space available during each operation, with its required ground or floor provisions priced.

## Make the purchase follow the retained workload

A contractor delivering repeated projects with a substantial elevated sheet programme has a different equipment case from one buying a specialist roof lift for a single hall. Count the forming work retained by the business across its actual order book. Exclude sheets already included in another supplier's completed package.

For a ground-assembled project, obtain the complete ground production and handling proposal before deciding to own a former. For a project with retained high-level production, obtain the configuration-specific lift-and-former proposal. Both need the accepted profile, input material, receiving route and staff allocation.

The [crane-supported forming comparison](/blog/roof-forming-lift-vs-crane-supported-roll-former/) addresses a different purchase: the production machine's support system. Use it where that is the decision. A whole-roof erection order remains a separate specialist scope.

## Two hypothetical purchasing mistakes

**Hypothetical failure case: ground assembly removes the entire roofing allowance.** A buyer orders a frame lift and deletes the roof-forming package, although the accepted lifted assembly contains no roof sheets. The roofing contractor then inherits a full elevated installation without its production resources. Reconcile the pre-lift content and retained sheet schedule before removing equipment.

**Hypothetical failure case: an owned lift is justified by all roof area.** Another buyer counts sheets supplied within a completed ground package as future work for its forming lift. The purchased machine serves only the smaller retained closure programme. Base ownership on eligible work actually retained across the order book, then price external equipment for isolated needs.
