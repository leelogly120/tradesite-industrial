**Keep the continuous freeform roof in the tender when its uninterrupted appearance or geometry is an owner requirement. Price a faceted redesign when the owner will accept clearly defined planar faces and the design team can release their junctions, drainage and supports. Buy general roll-forming equipment only for the panel families the selected design assigns to it.** A lower machine price cannot settle a change to the building’s finished shape.

A bidder has two drawings with an apparently similar outline. On one, the skin runs through a changing curve. On the other, the same broad volume is enclosed by a series of planes. Stand back and the silhouettes look close. Along a junction, at a rooflight, or from a viewing gallery, the difference is visible. Ask the architect and owner which of those differences they are prepared to purchase before spending money on trials.

## Put the finished surface on the tender drawing

Define the two options at the outside face of the completed roof. Identify the viewing positions, the seam direction and the location of every intentional break in the surface. Mark architectural features that both bids must preserve. Ask the structural and drainage designers which support changes, water paths and adjacent openings each option requires. An option with an appealing rendering and no issued junction design is a design-development proposal. Put a price and a delivery milestone on that work.

A faceted support frame can carry a smooth outer roof. Curtis Moore’s Tesco project account describes a curved roof over a faceted substrate, using adjustable channels and clips to achieve the finished geometry. The contractor also describes the set-out work and numerous tapered sheet variations. Read the **outside** surface and the interface beneath it before assigning a regular sheet to the forming schedule. ## Ask what becomes simpler and what becomes more expensive

Give the freeform bidder the selected geometry, support survey requirements, setting-out responsibility and panel identification requirements. Request a finished package from measured supports to received, identifiable panels. Where site forming is offered, identify who controls the geometry data, operates the specialist process and reconciles each part with its position. A general section drawing is insufficient to price a panel whose width or longitudinal shape changes.

For the faceted bid, divide the roof into regular panel fields and junction work. List each field’s section, finished lengths, start and end widths and material. Then quantify the changes of plane, edge pieces, transition details, rooflight interfaces and any project-specific fabricated parts. A design with more planar faces can reduce specialist shaping but add short runs, junction fabrication and installation hours. Put those costs in the same bid. The useful comparison is an installed roof that the owner will accept from the agreed viewing points and that the design team accepts for service.

## Complete one design and fabrication return per option

| Project input | Fill in the two actual offers | Decision owner and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance | Drawing revision, agreed viewpoints and allowable visible breaks: ___ | Owner and architect identify acceptable options |
| Support geometry | Survey reference, outer-surface offsets and secondary support scope: ___ | Envelope and structural leads cost the finished surface |
| Water and openings | Junction IDs, actual drainage design and interfacing openings: ___ | Roof designer releases the details to price |
| Panel families | Panel IDs, section, end widths, lengths and quantities by roof face: ___ | Fabricator allocates regular and specialist work |
| Trial and sample | Enter representative field, junction or curved bay and acceptance owner: ___ | Architect and installer resolve the product to be delivered |
| Installation | Enter delivery sequence, set-out labour, temporary access and receiving locations: ___ | Contractor prices the programme and access package |
| Commercial endpoint | Record completed cost, outstanding design fee and owner decision date: ___ | Client compares offers at the same level of completion |

Start with the difficult piece, then use the regular fields to build the equipment case. A sample containing a change of plane reveals how adjacent runs terminate and whether the visual line is preserved. A representative specialist panel reveals who is responsible for its unique geometry and its location. Carry the accepted sample reference onto the procurement schedule so that a later substitution goes back to the right design authority.

## Two hypothetical bid mistakes

**The bidder prices the substrate instead of the finished roof.** Shop drawings show flat support segments, so an estimator budgets regular straight panels throughout. The specified weather surface still has to follow a smooth curve. Rebuild the quotation from the required skin, then compare an expressly authorised faceted alternative on its own drawing.

**The redesign saves panel cost and adds unpriced edge work.** A fabricator quotes material for every planar field and shows a substantial saving over specialist supply. The installer’s price excludes all transitions and the owner has not accepted the new visible breaks. Add the complete junction schedule, sample acceptance and installation labour before comparing the two totals.
