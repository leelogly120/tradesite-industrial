Choose the eligible emergency-lighting architecture with the lower complete installed-and-maintained cost over the same ownership period. Compare central batteries with self-contained fittings against identical accepted coverage, circuit, compartment and occupation requirements. If only one arrangement meets those requirements, select that qualifying route and fund its service visits. First obtain complete distribution and maintenance prices for both eligible designs.

This purchase fixes where emergency energy is stored after a new or complete emergency-lighting renewal has been selected. A large fitting count makes repeated battery visits important, but does not establish a central system's business case by itself. The buyer needs a qualifying design and a priced service plan for each architecture.

## Separate battery location from automatic testing

In self-contained equipment, the battery belongs at the individual luminaire or its adjacent enclosure. A central battery system supplies fittings from a separate battery installation. [Eaton's comparison of testing and battery options](https://www.eaton.com/de/en-gb/markets/buildings/how-we-drive-building-efficiency-and-safety/safe-evacuation/evacuate/basics-emergency-lighting-systems/testing-and-battery-options-for-emergency-lighting.html) also describes distributed central arrangements serving floors or fire sections. A central proposal therefore needs actual supply zones and plant locations, rather than a single symbol labelled battery room.

Automatic-testing and central-monitoring versions exist for both architectures; the offered package must state which functions it includes. Selecting central batteries solely to obtain remote fault reporting confuses two separate decisions. Hold the required reports and fault visibility constant, then compare battery location, distribution construction and physical maintenance. Automatic detection identifies a problem; a responsible person still arranges the repair and any high-level visit.

An overhead plan alone hides the access difference between a fitting beside an open aisle and one above production equipment. Give each luminaire an ID, mounting reference and service-face position measured from the finished floor. Identify the route to that position and the operating area that must be released. This inventory connects the electrical offer to an achievable maintenance event.

## Complete the architecture worksheet before comparing totals

Use the same building zones and ownership period in both columns of the quotation. The responsible designer establishes the emergency result and applicable installation requirements; the buyer prices accepted alternatives against that result.

| Fillable input and measurement datum | Responsible owner | How the answer changes the purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency coverage drawing revision ___; required occupied modes ___ | Emergency-lighting designer and facility lead | Sets equal coverage and continuity obligations |
| Fitting IDs ___; service-face heights from finished floor ___ | Surveyor and electrical contractor | Locates the actual high-access population |
| Central plant locations ___; distribution and compartment drawing references ___ | Electrical and fire designers | Establishes eligible central arrangements and construction scope |
| Self-contained fitting and battery references ___; accessible service faces ___ | Lighting provider and maintenance lead | Defines the distributed battery population and replacement work |
| Equivalent test reports ___; monitoring destinations and fault owner ___ | Facility manager and lighting specialist | Separates reporting capability from battery placement |
| Service and test occupation plan revision ___ | Facility lead and responsible designer | Prices the accepted arrangement during interruption and recovery |
| Replacement-event assumptions ___; priced access and labour references ___ | Maintenance lead | Converts component care into complete visits |
| Common ownership endpoint ___; installed and service quotation references ___ | Buyer | Compares qualifying offers over the same period |

Treat an unpriced distribution or service item as an incomplete offer. Do not assign it a zero value and let the spreadsheet decide the architecture. Resolve the missing scope with its owner before ranking the totals.

## Price the maintenance events over one ownership period

Build an event schedule for the comparison period. For a self-contained offer, connect the proposed battery and fitting schedule to the maintenance activities stated by that product provider. Count access mobilisation, permitted work windows, electrical work, replacement parts and return to service. Group visits only where the maintenance plan and facility access actually permit grouping. Link each priced visit to the fitting IDs and quotation lines it covers, and identify who authorises additional work discovered during that visit. This makes later service invoices traceable to the purchase assumptions.

For a central offer, price its battery installation, distribution, commissioning and future care. Retain high-level work for luminaire faults and other fitting maintenance. Moving the batteries does not move every service task to floor level. The relevant saving is the access work removed by the accepted central arrangement, less the work and infrastructure it adds. Include the agreed end-of-period replacement or disposal scope on the same basis in both offers.

Use quoted costs and stated assumptions throughout. If a replacement interval is uncertain, compare the offers under the same clearly identified alternative schedules. Keep the preferred architecture visible in each case. A decision that reverses under a small change in access mobilisation cost deserves a firmer site quotation before purchase.

The occupied-building plan belongs in this comparison too. Agree who releases a zone, how service affects the required emergency coverage, and who accepts reinstatement. Test and recovery arrangements must suit the accepted design and building use. The buyer should receive that plan with the offer rather than discover a production restriction after installation.

## Two purchasing mistakes to remove

**Mistake (hypothetical): buying central batteries to obtain automatic reports.** A hall owner rejects a self-contained proposal as labour intensive because its batteries sit overhead, while comparing it with a central-battery proposal that includes remote reporting. The self-contained alternative also offers central monitoring. Correct the comparison by specifying equivalent reports for both, then price the physical visits and infrastructure separately. The remaining access difference is useful evidence; the unequal reporting package is not.

**Mistake (hypothetical): accepting a central price without its distribution work.** A buyer multiplies a fitting count by avoided local battery visits and approves a central quotation. Its plant space and accepted compartment interfaces remain outside the price. Complete those construction and occupation items, then rerun the common-period comparison. Select central supply only if the fully scoped result still wins.
