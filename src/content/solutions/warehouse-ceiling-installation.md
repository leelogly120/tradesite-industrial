# Warehouse Ceiling Work Planning

Warehouse ceiling and service work must account for floor capacity, racks, conveyors, fire systems, traffic routes and the operating status of the building. A machine should not be selected from nominal height or deck size alone.

ARCLIFT can support early comparison of crawler platform concepts after receiving a dimensioned site package. Current configuration, load, travel and safety functions are confirmed only through the signed technical schedule and approved drawings.

## Define the work method first

The project team should map each work position and the route between positions. Existing racks or automation may create horizontal offsets that are more important than maximum height. The load schedule should separate people, tools, fixtures and materials. Floor information must cover the entire delivery and travel route, not just the final work area.

No generic page can substantiate daily coverage, project duration, downtime avoidance, floor protection or labour reduction.

## Project inputs to prepare

- Warehouse plan with racks, conveyors, doors, turns and work positions
- Clear height, required reach and obstruction dimensions
- Floor construction, allowable loads and protected zones
- Personnel, tool, fixture and material loads
- Required deck arrangement and access points
- Building operating status, traffic plan and exclusion zones
- Indoor power, ventilation and fire-system constraints
- Travel surface, gradient and emergency-recovery procedure
- Destination inspection, operator and documentation requirements
