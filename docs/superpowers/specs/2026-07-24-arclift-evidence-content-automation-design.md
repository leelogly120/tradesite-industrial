# ARCLIFT Evidence-First Content and Publishing Design

## 1. Objective and Scope

This design governs five initial English blog articles for `arclifteq.com`, their first-party visual assets, editorial review, production publishing, Google Search Console monitoring, and the conditional daily publishing workflow.

It applies to the existing ARCLIFT site only. It does not create a new site, redesign unrelated pages, or publish content that cannot pass the evidence and quality gates below.

## 2. Public Brand Positioning

ARCLIFT is presented publicly as an advanced integrated equipment supplier combining trade, technical selection, configuration coordination, documentation coordination, delivery coordination, and after-sales communication.

Public content must not:

- claim that ARCLIFT owns or operates the source factories;
- describe ARCLIFT as the manufacturer of equipment shown in local source files;
- use phrases such as `our factory`, `factory direct`, `factory price`, `our production line`, or `manufactured by ARCLIFT`;
- identify, name, locate, or otherwise expose any source factory;
- expose source-folder names, original filenames, internal documents, supplier names, customer names, addresses, phone numbers, vehicle plates, staff badges, faces, factory signs, workshop banners, or third-party logos;
- imply that an ARCLIFT watermark added for website presentation proves original manufacture.

Approved public descriptions include:

- `ARCLIFT integrated equipment supplier`
- `ARCLIFT equipment solutions supplier`
- `ARCLIFT technical selection and supply partner`
- `ARCLIFT coordinates project-specific configuration, documentation and delivery`

If existing site copy directly conflicts with this positioning, only the conflicting claim is corrected. Unrelated copy and design remain untouched.

## 3. Source and Evidence Rules

### 3.1 Source hierarchy

1. Verified product records in the private TradeSite knowledge vault.
2. Original equipment documents and media under the private local product archive.
3. Current product-page claims that already have a recorded evidence source.
4. Current official regulatory, standards-body, or Google documentation.
5. Editorial inference that is explicitly labeled as an inference or project question.

Commercial blogs, marketplaces, AI summaries, competitor claims, and unattributed search snippets are not acceptable evidence for technical facts.

### 3.2 Claim classification

Every non-obvious claim in a draft is classified internally as one of:

- `V1 — directly verified`: stated in a current source document or visibly demonstrated by an approved image;
- `V2 — cross-verified`: supported by two independent reliable sources;
- `C — conditional`: valid only under stated project conditions;
- `I — inference`: a reasoned editorial conclusion, clearly presented as such;
- `U — unverified`: prohibited from publication.

Each article has a private claim ledger containing the claim, classification, source path or URL, source date, and publication wording. Private paths and source identities never appear publicly.

### 3.3 Prohibited claims

The following require direct, current evidence and otherwise fail the article:

- performance gains, cost savings, labor reductions, completion times, or safety improvements;
- certifications, patents, standards compliance, approvals, or test status;
- current price, delivery time, stock, production capacity, or shipping cost;
- model height, load, outreach, dimensions, power, ground pressure, or container quantity;
- named customers, countries, projects, testimonials, or delivery results;
- claims that a crane, scaffold, crew role, permit, inspection, or engineering review is unnecessary.

## 4. Editorial Standard

### 4.1 Reader and intent

Each article answers one real decision faced by an overseas contractor, distributor, roofing-system integrator, project manager, or procurement engineer. It must help the reader decide what information to collect, what trade-offs to evaluate, and what must be confirmed before quotation.

No article is produced solely to target a keyword variation. There is no mandatory word count.

### 4.2 Required article components

Each article contains:

1. a direct opening that states the decision or project problem;
2. the conditions under which the topic matters;
3. an evidence-led explanation using visible or documented equipment facts;
4. trade-offs and cases where the proposed approach may not fit;
5. a project-information checklist;
6. a clearly bounded ARCLIFT call to action requesting the minimum information needed for technical selection;
7. factual image captions and descriptive Alt text;
8. relevant internal links without forced anchor text;
9. an editorial note explaining that final configuration and destination-market requirements are confirmed per project.

### 4.3 Anti-AI language rules

Drafts fail editorial review if they contain:

- generic openings such as `in today's fast-paced world`, `when it comes to`, or `in the ever-evolving landscape`;
- repetitive summary paragraphs that restate headings without adding information;
- inflated adjectives such as `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `best-in-class`, `unmatched`, or `ultimate` without evidence;
- fake first-person experience, invented anecdotes, anonymous quotations, fabricated interviews, or unsupported customer scenarios;
- mechanical heading patterns, uniform paragraph lengths, excessive em dashes, repeated triads, or formulaic conclusions;
- keyword stuffing, unnatural exact-match anchors, or headings written for crawlers rather than readers;
- confident answers where the available evidence supports only a question, range, condition, or uncertainty;
- paraphrased competitor or search-result content that adds no original decision value.

Sentence rhythm, paragraph length, and section form should follow the information rather than a reusable AI template. Technical terms are retained where they improve precision and explained where a buyer may not know them.

## 5. Visual Evidence Standard

Each initial article uses three to five relevant first-party images when suitable evidence exists.

### 5.1 Selection

Prefer field-operation, transport, setup, platform, finished-profile, and isolated equipment-detail views that directly support the surrounding paragraph. Factory interiors, production-floor views, factory gates, offices, workshops, staff groups, and any image whose context reveals a factory are excluded even if identifying text could be removed.

### 5.2 Privacy and identity gate

Reject or sanitize any image containing:

- factory names, signs, banners, addresses, uniforms, badges, or identifiable production information;
- customer, contractor, project, country, or location identifiers;
- faces without release, vehicle plates, shipping labels, QR codes, phone numbers, screens, documents, or serial numbers;
- third-party or competitor logos that are not necessary to understand the equipment.

### 5.3 Permitted processing

- non-destructive crop, straightening, exposure, white balance, contrast, noise reduction, and web compression;
- removal or masking of sensitive text, logos, faces, plates, and labels;
- a discreet ARCLIFT editorial watermark placed in an image corner, never painted onto equipment as proof of manufacture;
- a marketing cover derived from a real image only when it is labeled internally as an editorial asset and not used as technical proof.

Equipment structure, proportions, components, operating state, safety devices, and site conditions must not be invented or materially altered in an evidence image.

### 5.4 Public and private metadata

Public filenames are neutral and descriptive. EXIF, GPS, device, author, and location metadata are removed. Alt text describes only visible facts and does not infer model, height, capacity, certification, factory, customer, or location.

A private asset ledger records the original path, selected crop, privacy edits, output path, article usage, and whether the image is evidence or editorial presentation. The ledger is not deployed.

## 6. Five-Article Evidence Cluster

1. **Roof-Level Roll Forming for Long Panels: When It Fits and When Lifting Support Is Still Needed**
   - Evidence focus: crawler roof-level forming scenes and long-panel output.
   - Buyer decision: handling route, roof geometry, coil/former workflow, lifting boundaries.

2. **Crawler or Truck-Mounted Roof-Panel Forming System? A Site and Transport Decision Guide**
   - Evidence focus: crawler and truck-mounted structures, setup and mobility.
   - Buyer decision: site movement, road transfer, chassis rules, support area, transport plan.

3. **Planning a 40HQ Shipment for a Truck-Mounted Roll-Forming Lift**
   - Evidence focus: modular transport preparation and container-oriented configuration.
   - Buyer decision: disassembly, packing, local reassembly, chassis and destination compliance.

4. **How to Specify a Roll-Forming Line for Long-Span Roofing Panels**
   - Evidence focus: forming rolls, profile tooling, feed, cutting and finished profile.
   - Buyer decision: profile drawing, material, thickness, tolerance, cut method and acceptance documents.

5. **Pre-Project Data Checklist for Large Crawler Ceiling and Wall-Panel Platforms**
   - Evidence focus: large-deck crawler platform and wall-panel work scenes.
   - Buyer decision: reachable work zones, platform load, floor condition, stabilization, access and local work-at-height review.

These articles form one technical cluster and link to the relevant product family, application page, and contact form. They do not duplicate model product pages.

## 7. Review and Scoring

An article may publish only when it has no fatal failure and scores at least 95/100.

| Category | Points |
|---|---:|
| Evidence traceability | 25 |
| Original buyer utility | 20 |
| Technical accuracy and limitations | 15 |
| Natural editorial quality | 15 |
| Search intent and information architecture | 10 |
| Visual relevance, privacy and provenance | 10 |
| Page experience and technical SEO | 5 |

Fatal failures include:

- any `U — unverified` claim;
- any invented metric, customer, project, certification, price, lead time, or testimonial;
- any factory identity or claim of ARCLIFT factory ownership/manufacture;
- any exposed personal, customer, supplier, factory, location, or logistics identity;
- any materially altered evidence image;
- any broken image, broken internal link, wrong canonical, `noindex`, invalid frontmatter, or build failure;
- any article whose primary value is a generic summary or keyword variation.

Review order:

1. source and claim audit;
2. factory/privacy/identity audit;
3. technical and safety audit;
4. anti-AI editorial audit;
5. search-intent and internal-link audit;
6. image and metadata audit;
7. desktop/mobile rendering and production build.

## 8. Publishing and Automation

### 8.1 Initial release

Before publishing, the current contact-form presentation and inquiry route must pass local verification so unrelated unfinished work is not released with the articles.

The five articles and approved assets are then:

1. built and checked locally;
2. committed with their private audit records excluded from deployment;
3. pushed to the production branch;
4. verified on the public URLs for HTTP status, canonical, images, layout, schema, sitemap presence, and inquiry navigation.

### 8.2 Search Console observation

The five production URLs are monitored daily in Google Search Console. Indexing requests are not treated as guarantees. The daily-content trigger requires:

- at least three of the five URLs to show `URL is on Google`;
- no manual action, security issue, canonical conflict, crawl block, or page-quality issue affecting the set;
- production pages still matching the reviewed content and assets.

### 8.3 Conditional daily publishing

The daily workflow remains disabled until the 3/5 trigger is verified.

After activation, one article may be published per day only if:

- it addresses a distinct buyer decision and has sufficient first-party evidence;
- it passes every fatal gate and scores at least 95/100;
- it passes build, link, image, metadata, desktop, and mobile checks;
- it does not cannibalize an existing product or article page.

If evidence is insufficient, quality is below threshold, credentials fail, deployment fails, or Search Console reports a material problem, that day's publication is skipped and reported. The workflow never lowers the standard to maintain volume.

## 9. External Requirements

The production workflow requires working GitHub push authentication, Cloudflare deployment access, and durable Google Search Console access suitable for monitoring. Credentials are requested or repaired only when the corresponding step is reached and are never written to the repository, knowledge vault, article files, logs, or chat.

## 10. Acceptance Criteria

- Five articles satisfy the 95/100 threshold and all fatal gates.
- Every published image has a private provenance record and no public factory identity.
- ARCLIFT is consistently described as an integrated equipment supplier, not the source factory or manufacturer.
- Local build, desktop/mobile checks, inquiry checks, and production URL checks pass.
- The five URLs are monitored in Search Console.
- Daily publishing remains disabled until the verified 3/5 indexing threshold is met.
